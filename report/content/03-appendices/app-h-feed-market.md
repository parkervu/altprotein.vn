# H. Feed and aquafeed market

Feed is where a Vietnamese alternative protein is most likely to find its first large buyers. This appendix sets out the market it would enter: how much feed Vietnam makes and imports, how much aquafeed goes to each species, what fishmeal costs, how much fishmeal a novel protein could realistically displace, which substitutes are already in use, what feed trials show, how feed mills decide, and the prices a new product must beat. It ends with the counterfactual: Chinese microbial proteins that can already be imported. It supports [[ch01-why-vietnam]], [[ch09-economics]] and [[ch11-plays]]. Production costs are in [[app-i-costs]]; the rules for listing a new feed ingredient are in [[app-g-regulation]].

## How to read this appendix

- **Terms.** FM is fishmeal. SBM is soybean meal. SPC is soy protein concentrate. SCP is single-cell protein (microbial biomass). CAP is *Clostridium autoethanogenum* protein, a bacterial SCP made from steel-mill off-gas. BSF is black soldier fly. CP is crude protein. FCR is feed conversion ratio (kg of feed per kg of animal gained).
- **Protein replacement and functional effects.** "Protein replacement" means a novel ingredient supplies protein in place of fishmeal or soybean meal. A "functional effect" is a benefit such as better survival, immunity or palatability that protein supply does not explain. We keep the two apart, because they support very different prices.
- **Prices.** World Bank prices are nominal USD per tonne. We express them per tonne of protein at 65% CP for fishmeal and 46% CP for soybean meal. VND converts at 26,000 per USD.
- **Years.** USDA's "MY2024/25" for Vietnamese soybeans and soybean meal runs from January to December 2025, so it equals calendar 2025.
- **Our calculations** are marked and show their inputs. Several key inputs (aquafeed volumes by species, inclusion rates) are industry estimates or global averages, so the derived pool is Low confidence.

- **H1 2026.** Corn imports were 6.8 Mt (up 49%; Argentina 37%, Brazil 36%, United States 13%), wheat 4.8 Mt (up 58%; Argentina 58%) and DDGS 645 kt (down 24%; United States 81%) [@MAC-03] {VN-direct|Medium}. - **Tariffs.** Feed protein and energy imports are duty-free. The MFN duty on feed corn (HS 1005.90.99) and on soybean meal (HS 2304.00.29 and 2304.00.90) fell to 0% on 31 March 2025; soybeans (HS 1201.90) and fishmeal (HS 2301.20) were already at 0% in 2023 [@REG2-21; @REG2-23] {VN-direct|Medium}. Wheat has been at 0% since November 2021 [@MAC-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. So domestic novel proteins get no tariff shelter against soybean meal or fishmeal.

### H2.3 Soybean and soybean-meal balance, 2025

For calendar 2025 (USDA's MY2024/25) [@MAC-04] {VN-direct|Medium}:

- **Soybeans.** Supply: opening stocks 460 kt + production 42 kt + imports 2,609 kt = 3,111 kt. Uses: crush 1,900 kt + food 540 kt + feed and waste 220 kt + closing stocks 451 kt = 3,111 kt. The balance closes.
- **Soybean meal.** Supply: opening stocks 678 kt + production 1,482 kt (78% extraction from 1,900 kt) + imports 5,703 kt = 7,863 kt. Uses: exports 283 kt + food 95 kt + feed 7,200 kt + closing stocks 285 kt = 7,863 kt. The balance closes.
- **Reading.** Even if every domestic soybean were crushed, it would supply only about 2.2% of the crush (our calculation, 42 / 1,900). Domestic crushing shifts imports from meal to beans; it does not reduce dependence. About 99% of the soy protein in Vietnamese feed is imported.

> **Correction.** Earlier drafts mixed years: they combined 2026 soybean forecasts (41 kt produced, 3.2 Mt imported) with a 2025 forecast of 7.0 Mt of soybean-meal feed use. The reconciled 2025 figures are 2.61 Mt of soybeans imported (98.4% of supply), 5.70 Mt of soybean meal imported and 7.2 Mt of soybean meal fed [@MAC-04; @MAC-09].

US tariffs on Chinese tilapia have pushed northern feed millers, including Dabaco, into tilapia and carp feed [@MAC-06] {VN-direct|Low}.

### H3.2 Why the numbers do not close

- **Shrimp.** The industry source says 920 kt of shrimp feed supported "nearly 800,000 tonnes" of shrimp [@MAC-06]. NSO reports 1,291 kt of farmed whiteleg plus black tiger shrimp (our sum, 994.4 + 296.7) [@MAC-15] {VN-direct|High}. Black tiger shrimp is mostly semi-intensive and uses little pelleted feed. But whiteleg output alone (994.4 kt) would need more than 920 kt of feed at any FCR above 0.93 (our calculation, 920 / 994.4). Feed-based and statistical estimates diverge by about 40%.
- **Pangasius.** 2.0 to 2.5 Mt of feed for 1,938.8 kt of fish implies an FCR of only 1.03 to 1.29 (our calculation).
- **Possible explanations.** The industry feed estimates are low; part of the feed is farm-made; or official output is overstated. We cannot tell which.
- **Sensitivity.** If shrimp feed matched NSO whiteleg output at an assumed FCR of 1.2 to 1.4, shrimp feed would be 1.19 to 1.39 Mt (our calculation, 994.4 x 1.2 and x 1.4), not 920 kt.

> **Correction.** Earlier drafts gave aquafeed as 3.4 to 4.0 Mt. That matches only the capacity x utilisation arithmetic, not a production estimate. We report the industry species estimate (3.9 to 4.8 Mt) and the USDA estimate (6.5 Mt) side by side and do not average them. The displaceable pool below uses the industry species volumes, because only they give a split by species. Livestock and poultry compound feed was about 22 Mt in 2025 [@MAC-01; @MAC-06].

## H5. Vietnam's fishmeal trade and domestic supply

Vietnam is a net exporter of fishmeal by value, mostly to China [@FM-37; @FM-38] {VN-direct|Medium}:

| Flow, 2023 (HS 2301.20) | Value | Reported quantity | Main partners |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imports | USD 260.0 M | 138,676 t | India USD 128.4 M (49%), Korea 25.4 M, Brazil 14.9 M, Chile 12.6 M, Peru 11.9 M |
| Exports | USD 430.5 M | 226,644 t | China USD 402.3 M (93.5%), Korea 6.6 M, Thailand 6.3 M, Japan 6.2 M |

- **Quantities are imputed.** Every partner shows the same unit value (USD 1,875/t for imports, USD 1,899/t for exports), so the database estimated the tonnages. Quote values, not tonnes [@FM-37; @FM-38] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Net position.** About USD 170 M net exports in 2023 (our calculation, 430.5 - 260.0).
- **Reading.** Vietnam makes fishmeal from pangasius by-products, other processing waste and low-value fish, and sells most of it to China. It imports higher-grade meal, much of it from India, for shrimp and marine feeds. Pangasius processing by-products are 62 to 67% of the whole fish [@MAC-45] {VN-direct|Medium}; by-product fishmeal yields are 35.8% for African catfish waste and 54.8% for tilapia waste [@FM-44] {general|Medium}. See [[app-c-feedstocks]], sheet C26.
- **Gaps.** Domestic fishmeal production, grade mix and domestic prices (VND/kg by 55%, 60% and 65% CP) for 2024 to 2026 were not found.

## H7. The displaceable fishmeal pool

How much fishmeal is in Vietnamese aquafeed that a non-animal protein could, in principle, replace? We count only species with both a sourced feed volume and a sourced inclusion range. Feed volumes are the 2025 industry estimates [@MAC-06; @FM-04]; rates are from H6. {VN-direct|Low} for volumes, {general|Medium} for rates; the result is Low confidence.

| Species | Feed volume, kt | Fishmeal inclusion | Fishmeal, kt (our calculation) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shrimp | 920 | 6% (low bound) | 55 |
| Shrimp | 920 | 10% (central low) | 92 |
| Shrimp | 920 | 20% (central high) | 184 |
| Shrimp | 920 | 25% (high bound) | 230 |
| Pangasius | 2,000 to 2,500 | 1% to 2% | 20 to 50 |
| Tilapia | 400 to 500 | 1% to 2% | 4 to 10 |

- **Sourced bounds** (shrimp 6 to 25%): 55 + 20 + 4 = **79 kt** to 230 + 50 + 10 = **290 kt** a year (our calculation).
- **Central case** (shrimp 10 to 20%): 92 + 20 + 4 = 116 kt to 184 + 50 + 10 = 244 kt, which we round to **120 to 245 kt** a year (our calculation).
- **Hypothetical additions** (unsourced rates): snakehead up to 300 kt of feed x 20 to 40% = 60 to 120 kt; marine fish 80 to 100 kt x 15 to 30% = 12 to 30 kt. Even at the top of both hypotheses and the top of the sourced range, the pool is about 440 kt (our calculation, 290 + 120 + 30).
- **Sensitivity to shrimp volume.** If shrimp feed matched NSO whiteleg output (1.19 to 1.39 Mt at FCR 1.2 to 1.4; see H3), shrimp fishmeal alone would be 119 to 278 kt at 10 to 20% (our calculation).
- **Comparison.** Our wave 1 macro note gave about 80 to 270 kt, using 23% as the shrimp ceiling [@MAC-06; @MAC-41]. The wave 2 range (79 to 290 kt) allows 25% from trial control diets. The central range (120 to 245 kt) is the better single range to quote.
- **Realistic capture by a novel protein.** Trials show 25 to 60% of fishmeal can be replaced without growth loss (H9). Applying 25 to 50% to shrimp fishmeal at 10 to 20% inclusion gives 920 x 10% x 25% = 23 kt to 920 x 20% x 50% = 92 kt, which we round to **25 to 90 kt** a year of novel-protein demand (our calculation). That is 2 to 8 plants of Entobel's design size (11 kt/yr), and it is already contested by Entobel, Chinese SCP and poultry by-product meal.

> **Correction.** Earlier drafts gave a displaceable fishmeal pool of 350 to 450 kt a year. Sourced inputs do not support it. It is reachable only if the unsourced snakehead and marine-fish inclusion rates sit at the top of the hypotheses above. Use a central 120 to 245 kt, sourced bounds of 79 to 290 kt, and a realistic capture by a novel protein of 25 to 90 kt [@FM-04; @FM-05; @FM-07; @FM-08; @MAC-06].

### H9.2 Functional and immunity effects (separate from protein)

- CAP improved growth and survival after pathogen challenge in whiteleg shrimp across 15 to 45 ppt salinity [@FM-20] {general|Medium}.
- Mealworm meal at 50% replacement cut post-challenge mortality by 76.9% in whiteleg shrimp [@FM-26] {general|Medium}.
- FeedKind raised survival and antioxidant markers in largemouth bass [@FM-16] {general|Medium}.
- BSF protein hydrolysate at 1 to 2% of diet raised palatability in shrimp feed [@FM-45] {general|Medium}.
- Beta-glucan raised striped catfish survival under winter conditions from 78.3% to 95.0% [@FM-31] {general|Medium}. Yeast is broadly reviewed as an immunostimulant [@FM-32] {general|Medium}.

**Reading.** The growth data support partial replacement (about 25 to 60% of fishmeal) in the Vietnamese species that still use fishmeal: shrimp, snakehead and marine fish. Early life stages need their own trials (the barramundi fry result is a warning). In pangasius and tilapia there is little fishmeal left to replace, so a novel ingredient there must sell on function or on soybean-meal parity. A product sold on function can charge far more per tonne than one sold on protein (H11).

### H10.3 What fishmeal costs a shrimp mill

At 15% inclusion, fishmeal adds USD 256 per tonne of feed at the 2025 average price and USD 375 at the August 2026 price (our calculation, 0.15 x 1,706 and 0.15 x 2,500). That is roughly 13 to 30% of the derived shrimp-feed price of USD 1,270 to 1,920/t (our calculation). At USD 2,500/t, each percentage point of fishmeal removed saves about USD 25 per tonne of feed before paying for the replacement (our calculation, 0.01 x 2,500). This is why mills want to cut fishmeal.

### H10.4 Who buys

- **Shrimp feed.** Sheng Long, C.P. Vietnam, Uni-President Vietnam, Tongwei, Grobest and Gromax made a combined 640 kt in 2025, about 70% of shrimp feed [@FM-04; @IND-41] {VN-direct|Medium}. New high-protein capacity (C.P. Ca Mau, 124,800 t/yr; De Heus Vinh Long, 168,000 t/yr for 40 to 50% protein marine feeds) adds premium buyers [@IND-42; @VCO-10] {VN-direct|Medium}. See [[app-f-facilities]].
- **Pangasius.** Vertically integrated groups; Vinh Hoan is already an insect-meal buyer [@ECO-05] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Livestock feed.** De Heus, GreenFeed, Cargill, Japfa and C.P. lead [@MAC-25] {VN-direct|Low}. De Heus's purchase of CJ Feed & Care (closed 3 March 2026) concentrates buying power further [@VCO-08] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Certification pull.** Sheng Long and Uni-President cited ASC certification as a 2026 priority [@FM-04], and De Heus gained ASC Feed certification for pangasius and tilapia feeds in August 2026 [@VCO-09] {VN-direct|Medium}. Traceable, certifiable ingredients have an edge.

### H10.5 How a mill qualifies a new ingredient

A typical sequence, drawn from the trial literature and the published local cases (it is not a Vietnamese standard procedure):

1. Specification and safety data: proximate analysis, amino acids, contaminants.
2. Digestibility trial in the target species.
3. Dose-response growth trial in tanks, usually 8 to 12 weeks.
4. Pond or farm trial through a contract lab such as ShrimpVet in Ho Chi Minh City [@ECO-70].
5. Legal listing as a feed ingredient in Vietnam. For a new substance in livestock feed this means a feed trial under the Law on Animal Husbandry Art 37 and Circular 94/2025, with about 40 working days of agency steps (protocol approval 15 + 5 days, recognition 15 + 5 days) plus the trial itself [@REG2-08; @REG-29; @REG-32] {VN-direct|Medium}. Our estimate of the total is 6 to 18 months. For aquafeed, the documented steps are about 20 days of appraisal, the trial, then 10 working days [@REG2-01; @REG2-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. Whether an aquafeed trial is required for a new raw material is ambiguous (H8). See [[app-g-regulation]].
6. Supply-security and price agreement. The Entobel and Vinh Hoan offtake (at least 15,000 t over 2025 to 2027) is the local precedent [@ECO-05] {VN-direct|High}.

**Arithmetic for targets 2 and 2b (our calculation).** Maximum ingredient price = soybean-meal price + (performance gain x feed price) / inclusion rate. Inputs: soybean meal USD 403/t [@FM-01]; feed price levels derived in H10.2 [@FM-04]; performance gains of 3 to 5% are assumptions.

**Reading.** A Vietnamese feed protein sold on protein price alone does not clear fishmeal at 2025 prices and cannot touch soybean meal. At the August 2026 fishmeal price, the low end of the indicative cost comes within about 5% of parity, but that depends on the spike lasting. The investable thesis is a functional shrimp or pangasius ingredient with trial data, or a very low-cost by-product route. Pig and poultry feed (about 22 Mt) is ruled out by price, not by volume.

## H12. Outlook to 2030

- **Vietnamese feed demand.** USDA expects total feed demand (animal plus aquafeed) to rise from 28.6 Mt (2025) to 29.5 Mt (2026) and 30.6 Mt (2027), with aquafeed at 6.73 Mt (2026) and 7.12 Mt (2027) on its own, higher aquafeed basis [@MAC-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. It expects soybean-meal feed use of 7.4 Mt (2026) and 7.8 Mt (2027) [@MAC-04] and corn imports of 15 Mt in MY2026/27 [@MAC-03] {VN-direct|Medium}. Every increment of demand growth is met by imports.
- **The 2030 target.** Decision 1520/QD-TTg targets 30 to 32 Mt of industrial feed output from 40 to 45 Mt of capacity by 2030 [@MAC-23; @REG-54] {VN-direct|High}. The extract does not say whether aquafeed is included. Edition 1.1: the feed scheme, Decision 1625/QD-TTg, sets the same 30 to 32 Mt for industrial *livestock* feed, so the target appears to exclude aquafeed [@NTS-23] {VN-direct|Medium}; see [[ch17-protein-balance-2050]]. From about 22 Mt of livestock and poultry feed in 2025, the target needs 6.4 to 7.8% growth a year (our calculation, (30/22) and (32/22) to the power 1/5). Growth was about 3% in 2025 and 7.5% in H1 2026 [@MAC-01; @MAC-03]. Reaching the target would add 8 to 10 Mt of feed a year compared with 2025 (our calculation), a large protein-input requirement that imports or domestic novel inputs would have to meet.
- **Shrimp feed.** Sheng Long expects sales to grow about 15% in 2026 [@FM-04] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Price forecasts.** The World Bank's April 2026 forecasts stop at 2027: soybean meal USD 359/t (2026) and USD 363/t (2027); maize USD 211/t and USD 214/t. Maize had already reached USD 224/t by August 2026. There is no World Bank fishmeal forecast [@GT-14; @GT-15] {general|High}. Any 2030 feed-price path is therefore a scenario assumption; see [[app-o-outlook]].
- **Global fishmeal supply.** About 4.5 Mt of fishmeal was made in 2016, 69% of it used in aquafeed; global aquafeed was projected to grow from 49.7 Mt (2015) to 87.1 Mt (2025) [@FM-06] {general|Medium}. Aquaculture now takes about 87% (about 4 Mt a year), citing FAO 2024, and trimmings are a growing share of supply [@FM-35] {general|Medium}. Supply is flat and demand is rising, which supports structurally high fishmeal prices, but prices also swing with Peruvian anchovy seasons.
- **Leading indicator.** The fishmeal to soybean-meal price ratio (3.8 in 2024, 4.7 in 2025, 6.2 in August 2026) is the signpost to track monthly on the Pink Sheet [@GT-15] {general|High}.

## H13. The Chinese counterfactual

- **China is pushing soybean meal out of its own feed.** MARA issued a "Three-Year Action Plan" to reduce and substitute soybean meal in feed (2023), and a 2025 review reports that soybean-meal use in Chinese feed fell 8.0% in 2024 [@FM-36] {general|Low}. We could not read the plan's numeric targets. A 2021 Chinese plan also named single-cell protein [@RGN-22] {general|Low}. Earlier, China's fishmeal imports held at 1.0 to 1.5 Mt a year while its aquafeed grew to 19 Mt (2014) [@FM-09] {general|Medium}.
- **Chinese microbial protein is already at scale.** Calysseo's 20,000 t/yr FeedKind plant and CAP from steel-mill gas are in commercial use in China [@FM-13; @FM-15] {general|Medium}. Angel Yeast makes more than 11,000 t/yr of food-grade yeast protein [@RGN-24] {general|Low}. Chinese glutamic-acid biomass is already sold in Vietnam [@FM-12] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **The trade link runs both ways.** Vietnam sends 93.5% of its fishmeal exports (USD 402.3 M in 2023) to China [@FM-38] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **What it means for price (our reasoning, not a finding).** Chinese by-product SCP has near-zero marginal feedstock cost, so it can price anywhere between soybean-meal and fishmeal parity. The realistic landed price for a Vietnamese feed SCP is therefore at or below fishmeal-protein parity. A Vietnamese producer should plan for a realised price of 70 to 90% of fishmeal-protein parity: about USD 1,840 to 2,360 per tonne of protein at the 2025 average fishmeal price and USD 2,690 to 3,460 at the August 2026 price (our calculation, 0.7 and 0.9 x 2,625 and x 3,846).
- **Test.** Any Vietnamese feed-SCP business plan should be tested against a quote for Chinese SCP delivered to a Mekong Delta mill, not against fishmeal list prices.
