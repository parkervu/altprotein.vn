# Brief for policy makers

**The short version.** Vietnam produces animal protein at scale on imported plant protein: about 99% of the soy protein and three-quarters of the maize in its feed, roughly USD 10 billion of feed-ingredient imports in 2025. The 2030 livestock strategy targets 30 to 32 Mt of industrial feed, 8.5 to 10.5 Mt a year more than in 2024. Domestic alternative proteins can reduce that exposure where they meet a price or function. Vietnam has the carbohydrate, the fermentation skills and the buyers, but ranks joint last in the region (with Indonesia, Malaysia and the Philippines) on regulatory readiness. Most of the fix is cheap, and the most valuable step must be taken while the Food Safety Law is being revised, expected in the October to November 2026 session.

## What you need to know

1. **The strongest case is supply security, industrial value and trade** ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]). Environment and animal welfare are narrower motivations.
2. **Food rules: an open door but no gate.** New protein foods enter through self-declaration, yet "new-technology food not yet permitted for circulation" is recallable and no procedure grants permission. Firms face enforcement-discretion risk, not a ban ([[ch05-rules]]).
3. **Precision-fermented proteins probably count as GMO products** under Decree 43/2026; production with a GM strain needs a closed-production certificate with no published timeline or fee ([[ch05-rules]]).
4. **Feed rules are more open but have two gaps.** The aquafeed list has no raw-material section, and the livestock list has no entry for bacterial or fungal biomass. Since Decree 211/2026, unlisted use draws fines of VND 10 to 20 million per material and one to three months' suspension ([[ch05-rules]]).
5. **The region is moving.** Singapore has approved 18 novel foods; Thailand has a novel-food route with recognised assessment units and a 2026 to 2035 biotechnology framework that cites the same regional study that scores Vietnam 0 on rules; Korea amended an existing rule in about nine months ([[ch08-regional]]).
6. **Tax holidays are the weakest incentive for this industry.** Capital is most of the cost; a regional incentive study ranks capital grants and loan guarantees far above tax holidays ([[ch09-economics]]).

## Alignment with national strategy

The options serve Resolution 36-NQ/TW on biotechnology (biotech 7% of GDP by 2030; replace imported biotech products), Resolution 57 on science and innovation (R&D 2% of GDP), and the livestock strategy (Decision 1520: feed and by-product use). None of these names alternative protein; naming it in biotechnology programmes and state venture-fund priorities is low-cost ([[ch05-rules]], [[ch07-capital]]).

## Trade-offs to weigh

Speed against caution in a reliance lane; the risk that a new gate slows products that are legal today (hence transition rules); tariffs that help food makers but weaken domestic ingredient makers; public spending on a pilot line that needs an anchor host to avoid becoming idle ([[ch12-policy-options]]).

## For provinces

Tay Ninh and the Central Highlands hold the cassava-starch industry that could host fermentation-sugar hubs (T4). Ho Chi Minh City, Dong Nai and Hanoi hold the industrial parks, research institutes, city venture funds and the sandbox. Mekong Delta provinces hold the aquafeed buyers. The provincial map changed on 1 July 2025 ([[app-p-admin-map]]).

## Looking to 2050 (edition 1.1)

- **Vietnam's 2050 plans cover inputs, not protein.** Power, hydrogen and emission caps have 2050 numbers; meat, feed and aquaculture targets stop at 2030. The successor livestock and fisheries strategies and the national biotechnology strategy are the natural homes for protein targets ([[ch15-drivers-2050]], [[app-y-targets-hubs]]).
- **The 2030 feed target appears to cover livestock feed only** (Decision 1625/QD-TTg) and looks out of reach on our model: 26.4 Mt against 30 to 32 Mt.
- **Feed efficiency does about twice the work of alternative protein** in cutting 2050 soybean-meal imports; the two add up ([[ch17-protein-balance-2050]]).
- **Nine no-regret moves pay off in all four 2050 worlds.** Eight can start by 2028 with existing institutions: a new-food route with deadlines, a feed-efficiency programme, climate-proof siting rules, a skills programme, a national protein and feed balance, residue-carbon rules, a protein-quality laboratory with an open price series, and an omega-3 sourcing plan. One cheap option belongs alongside them: naming fermentation and feed-protein sites in the MAE biotechnology-cluster plan (December 2026) ([[ch20-robust-moves]]).
- **A vision to debate, not a forecast:** 7 to 9 Mt of imported soybean meal in 2050 instead of 10.4 Mt, 4 to 8% microbial feed protein and 15 to 50% non-marine aquafeed omega-3, reviewed at every five-year plan ([[ch19-vision-2050]]).
