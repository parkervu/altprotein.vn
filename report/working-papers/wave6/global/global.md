# Global and Asian benchmarks: how demand for alternative protein actually behaves

Agent: GLOBAL (wave 6). Source prefix: GLB. Date: 24 September 2026.

Purpose: to calibrate the Vietnam demand sizing model in Part VI and to tell frontier actors what has and has not worked elsewhere. Almost all evidence here is `general` (outside Vietnam) or `VN-adjacent` (Southeast or East Asia). Tags follow the brief: `[evidence label, confidence, source IDs, demand evidence type]`. Supply-study context is cited by its existing IDs (FORM-01, ECO-24).

---

## Headline findings

1. **Modern plant-based meat peaked in 2020 to 2021 and has since plateaued or shrunk in every large market with good data.** US retail sales fell from USD 1.4 billion (2021) to USD 1.0 billion (2025) and units from 281 million to 173 million; its share of total meat dollar sales halved from 1.4% to about 0.7%. UK volume fell 22% in the two years to January 2025 and Dutch volume fell 13% from 2022 to 2024 and another 11% in 2025. Germany, the largest European market, was flat (+6.2% in 2024, -1.7% in 2025). Euromonitor puts peak growth in North America and Western Europe in 2020 and in Asia Pacific in 2021. France is the one large exception in 2025 (+17% volume). `[general, High, GLB-01, GLB-02, GLB-08, GLB-09, GLB-10, GLB-11, GLB-47, revealed]`

2. **Realistic ceiling after about ten years in the most favourable markets: 3 to 4% of pre-packaged retail meat volume, and under 1% of total meat value.** Germany reached 3.1% (2024) and the Netherlands 4.1% (2024, down from 4.6% in 2022). The US share of total meat value is about 0.7% (2025). Plant milk, the mature analogue, settled at about 7 to 13% of milk sales: UK 6.7% by volume, Germany 9.9% by value, US 13% by value (down from 16% in 2021). `[general, High, GLB-01, GLB-02, GLB-09, GLB-10, GLB-11, revealed]`

3. **The failure is in turning trial into habit, not in losing loyal buyers.** US household penetration fell from 19 to 20% (2021) to 11% (2025) while repeat rates stayed near 60 to 64%. In a 2018 to 2020 US panel, 40% of buyers bought only once. Household spending fell by over 75% after the first month of purchase. Frequent buyers (at least monthly) were only 8.6% (UK) and 9.7% (Germany) of households in 2024. `[general, High, GLB-01, GLB-02, GLB-17, GLB-18, GLB-19, GLB-09, GLB-11, revealed]`

4. **Price matters a lot, but price parity on its own has not produced growth.** Scanner studies put the own-price elasticity of plant-based meat at about -0.8 to -1.6 (US), so price cuts raise volume. Yet in the Netherlands, where several chains promised price parity and set protein targets, plant-based meat volume fell 6% (2024) and 11% (2025) and the supermarket plant share of protein stayed flat at 42.0%, 42.7% and 42.6% (2023 to 2025). In the UK, dearer branded products lost less volume than cheap private label. `[general, High, GLB-16, GLB-03, GLB-10, GLB-08, GLB-12, GLB-11, revealed]`

5. **Taste parity has not been shown under test conditions.** In a combined blind and informed tasting with US consumers, beef burgers beat every alternative; a 70% beef and 30% mushroom blend won among alternatives when tasted blind but lost demand once people were told what was in it. A review of the "price, taste and convenience" hypothesis concludes that matching meat on all three would still not make most consumers switch. `[general, Medium, GLB-30, GLB-32, tested]`

6. **Plant-based meat has not measurably displaced meat; plant milk has partly displaced dairy.** 86 to 98% of US plant-based meat buyers keep buying meat, and household ground-meat purchases did not fall after a first plant-based purchase. A UK retailer's Veganuary push raised plant-based unit sales by 57% with no significant change in meat sales. By contrast, each extra unit of plant milk bought is associated with 0.43 to 0.93 fewer units of dairy milk. A calibrated US model finds a 10% cut in plant-based meat prices lowers cattle output by only about 0.15%. `[general, High, GLB-01, GLB-02, GLB-19, GLB-33, GLB-28, GLB-22, GLB-27, revealed]`

7. **Formats that hold up are cheap, ingredient-like and fit existing dishes; branded burgers are losing ground.** Mince, pieces, strips and sausages lead in Europe; burgers fell from 17.2% to 14.0% of Dutch plant-based meat volume (2022 to 2024) and are 11% in the UK. US "shreds, chunks and strips" grew 8% in units in 2025 while the category fell 11%. Tofu, tempeh and seitan grew where they were cheaper than both analogues and meat (Germany +16.7% volume in 2024 at EUR 6.82 per kg, versus EUR 14.09 for plant-based meat and EUR 9.68 for meat). `[general, High, GLB-09, GLB-10, GLB-11, GLB-01, revealed]`

8. **Institutional routes reliably change the meal served; lasting diet change is not proven.** Doubling the share of vegetarian options in UK university cafeterias raised vegetarian sales by 41 to 79% with no rebound at other meals; a vegetarian default at conferences moved uptake from 2 to 12% to 86 to 89%. But a 2025 meta-analysis of randomised trials that measured eating at least a day later finds a pooled effect of only SMD 0.07. `[general, High, GLB-35, GLB-37, GLB-38, GLB-36, GLB-39, tested]`

9. **Asian markets with strong vegetarian and soy traditions did not become large markets for Western-style analogues; traditional products dominate.** Thai meat alternatives were USD 20.8 million in 2020 against USD 803 million of dairy alternatives (mostly soy milk). Asia Pacific holds over a third of global plant-based dairy sales but Europe and North America hold about 83% of plant-based meat sales. Japanese soy milk output is near its 2020 record (410,712 kL in 2024) while soy meat contracted. Taiwan, with about 3 million vegetarians, had only USD 36 million of plant-based food retail sales in 2021. `[VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-52, GLB-49, GLB-05, GLB-55, GLB-56, GLB-57, revealed]`

10. **Where Western-style products did sell in Asia, they were hidden inside familiar dishes at low prices per serving.** OmniPork dumplings at Taiwan's Bafang Yunji chain sold over 1 million a week across about 1,000 outlets at NT$6 each (launch period, 2020), while the same mince sold at about three times the price of pork at retail. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-59, GLB-58, revealed]`

11. **Vietnam's price gap is roughly an order of magnitude larger than the gaps that stalled Western markets.** Imported analogues in the supply study's retail audit cost about 12 to 18 times eggs or chicken breast per gram of protein. The premiums that coincided with US and European decline were 35 to 100% per kg; Thailand's was 2 to 8 times. `[VN-direct and general, Medium, FORM-01, GLB-03, GLB-09, GLB-10, GLB-53, inferred]`

12. **Long-range market forecasts made in 2019 to 2021 overshot by one to two orders of magnitude.** Kearney (2019) forecast plant-based meat at 10% of the global meat market in 2025; the actual figure is about 0.55% (USD 6.6 billion retail against Kearney's USD 120 billion). Barclays' USD 140 billion by 2029 would need 115% annual growth from 2025. BCG and Blue Horizon's plant-based price parity "by 2023" was not met at category level. `[general, Medium, GLB-45, GLB-44, GLB-43, GLB-42, GLB-05, inferred]`

13. **Cultivated and fermentation-derived foods have negligible consumer sales so far.** Cultivated meat has been sold in a handful of restaurants and one Singapore butchery, where the 2024 retail product contained 3% cultivated cells. Investment fell to USD 74 million (cultivated) and USD 357 million (fermentation) in 2025. We found no published sales volumes. `[general, Medium, GLB-60, GLB-61, GLB-07, GLB-63, revealed]`

14. **For Part VI, the S-ALT pathway (1% of meat demand replaced by 2030) should be labelled exploratory, not a forecast.** No market has shown modern plant-based or fermented products replacing a measurable share of meat; the best retail share anywhere is 3 to 4% of pre-packaged meat after a decade of high-income support, with little evidence of displacement. `[general, Medium, GLB-09, GLB-10, GLB-01, GLB-19, inferred]`

Plant-based foods overall are 1.1% of US retail food and beverage dollar sales (USD 7.9 billion, 2025), and 60% of households buy something plant-based in a year, mostly milk. `[general, High, GLB-01, revealed]`

In the UK, the self-reported share of people eating any plant-based alternative food doubled from 6.7% (2008 to 2011) to 13.1% (2017 to 2019), which shows the pre-boom build-up that retail data later confirm peaked. `[general, High, GLB-40, revealed]`

### 1.3 Global and Asia Pacific

Global retail sales of plant-based meat and seafood were about USD 6.6 billion in 2025 (+8% nominal, about +4% real, +3% volume), roughly three times 2015. Europe and North America account for about 83%; Asia Pacific, Latin America and the Middle East and Africa share the remaining 17%. `[general, Medium, GLB-05, GLB-06, revealed]` The wider plant-based total (milk, yoghurt, cheese, meat and so on) was USD 28.9 billion, with Asia Pacific at USD 9.2 billion, largely plant milk. `[general, Medium, GLB-07, GLB-06, revealed]` Asia Pacific holds over a third of global plant-based dairy sales. `[VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-49, revealed]`

Euromonitor reports that growth in meat and seafood substitutes peaked in 2020 in North America (46%) and Western Europe (36%) and in 2021 in Asia Pacific (42%). It notes that Asia faces "an additional challenge" because meat alternatives and tofu products are already established. `[VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-47, revealed]`

Two lessons carry to Vietnam. First, even the most successful plant analogue in the West stopped at about 7 to 16% of its category and has slipped in the US since 2021. Second, in Asia the plant "milk" that sells is mostly traditional soy milk, which grew as a food in its own right. In Japan, growth since 2023 is led by unsweetened soy milk bought for protein and isoflavones. `[VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-55, revealed]` The supply study already records Vinasoy with about 80% of Vietnamese soy milk; this is consistent with the Asian pattern. `[VN-direct, Medium, supply study context, revealed]`

### 1.5 Realistic ceilings after 5 to 10 years

On the evidence, a realistic ceiling for novel plant-based meat in a supportive, high-income market after 5 to 10 years is:

- **3 to 4% of pre-packaged retail meat volume**, and well under 1% of total meat value once fresh counters and foodservice are included. `[general, High, GLB-09, GLB-10, GLB-01, revealed]`
- **About 30 to 39% of households trying in a year at peak, but only about 9 to 10% buying monthly (8.6% UK, 9.7% Germany in 2024).** `[general, High, GLB-09, GLB-11, revealed]`
- **Plant milk-type categories: 7 to 16% of the category.** `[general, High, GLB-01, GLB-02, GLB-09, GLB-11, revealed]`

We found no market where modern plant-based meat exceeded 5% of meat by any measure. `[general, Medium, all adoption sources, inferred]`

### 1.6 Gaps

We found no retrieved Circana, SPINS, NielsenIQ or Kantar series for Sweden, Israel, Singapore, Hong Kong or South Korea. One Korean review paper was found but could not be read (rate limit). These are logged as open questions. `[general, Low, none, inferred]`

## 2. Price: elasticities, parity experiments and taste

### 2.1 Price elasticities from scanner data

| Study | Market and data | Own-price elasticity | Cross-price with meat | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zhao et al. 2023 | US store scanner, 2017 to 2020 | -0.84 before COVID-19, -1.55 after | Complement to beef (-1.41) and pork (-0.39); substitute for chicken (+2.27), fish (+0.34), turkey (+0.12) | `[general, High, GLB-16, revealed]` |
| GFI and NIQ 2024 | US store-level model, periods to July 2023 | -1.23 for plant-based beef | 53% of the response is to other products' prices; conventional chicken and turkey are the main outside competitor | `[general, High, GLB-03, revealed]` |
| Nouve et al. 2025 | US household panel, 136,553 households, 2019 to 2021 | More elastic than animal meat | Plant-based beef patties substitute for beef patties; plant-based bacon complements bacon | `[general, Medium, GLB-20, revealed]` |
| Liu and Ansink 2024 | Dutch store scanner, 884 stores, 2015 to 2018 | +1.52 (positive; authors link it to fast variety growth) | Complements to beef, poultry and fish | `[general, Medium, GLB-21, revealed]` |
| Dogbe et al. 2024 | Scottish household panel (Kantar), 2021 | Non-dairy milk -1.63; beans -1.00; peas -0.83 | Near zero | `[general, Medium, GLB-23, revealed]` |
| Lusk et al. 2022 | US calibrated model | About -2.3 implied (10% price cut raises plant-based consumption about 23%) | 10% price cut lowers cattle output about 0.15% | `[general, High, GLB-27, inferred]` |

Reading across: plant-based meat demand is price elastic (a 10% price cut raises volume by roughly 8 to 16%), but cross-price effects with meat are small or of mixed sign. Price cuts grow plant-based sales without shrinking meat much. `[general, High, GLB-16, GLB-03, GLB-27, GLB-26, revealed]` Stated and choice studies agree on the direction: lower plant-based prices raise choice, while changing meat prices barely moves plant-based demand (2,126 US adults, hypothetical restaurant setting). `[general, Medium, GLB-25, stated]` About a third of Swedish meat-preferers say they would switch only if the substitute cost two thirds of the meat price or less. `[general, Medium, GLB-31, stated]`

Promotions work mainly through visibility: a 10% discount on US plant-based beef lifted units 20%, and 80% when paired with a feature advertisement. `[general, High, GLB-03, revealed]`

### 2.2 The price parity natural experiments

- **Lidl Germany** priced most of its Vemondo private-label vegan range at parity with comparable animal products from October 2023 and reported vegan sales up more than 30% in six months. This is a company claim without a control group, and no meat-sales effect was reported. `[general, Low, GLB-13, GLB-14, revealed]` Across all German retailers, plant-based meat volume rose 6.2% in 2024 and fell 1.7% in 2025, and the sales-weighted average premium was still 45% per kg in 2024. `[general, High, GLB-09, GLB-08, revealed]` The likeliest reading is that parity at one chain shifted share between retailers more than it grew the category. `[general, Medium, GLB-09, GLB-13, inferred]`
- **Netherlands (Lidl, Aldi, Albert Heijn, Jumbo and others).** Several chains set 60% plant protein targets for 2030 and 50% for 2025, and Aldi says its alternatives are always the same price or cheaper than the animal equivalent. The national plant share of protein sold was 42.0% (2023), 42.7% (2024), 42.6% (2025); every chain with a 2025 target of 50% missed it. The tracker's authors say individual retailers cannot go further "without losing market share". `[general, High, GLB-12, revealed]` Over the same period Dutch plant-based meat volume fell. `[general, High, GLB-10, GLB-08, revealed]`
- **Shelf versus basket.** A shelf-price study of about 2,600 substitutes in six European countries found meat substitutes 24 to 115% dearer than meat except in Germany, where parity had been reached in the sample. `[general, Medium, GLB-15, revealed]` Sales-weighted data show a 35 to 46% premium in Germany and the Netherlands. The cheap items exist, but they are not what the average buyer puts in the basket. `[general, High, GLB-09, GLB-10, revealed]`

**Assessment of the "price parity tipping point".** The claim that plant-based meat will take off once it matches meat on price is not supported by revealed evidence so far. Where parity was offered on private label, volumes did not take off. Survey figures such as "76% of interested Southeast Asian consumers would buy at price parity" are stated intentions. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-48, stated]` Price is a necessary condition in poorer markets, not a sufficient one. GFI Europe itself concludes that "low prices aren't sufficient". `[general, Medium, GLB-08, revealed]`

### 2.3 Taste parity and blind tasting

- A US sensory and choice study compared a beef burger, a pea-protein burger, a burger with "animal-like" protein (heme type) and a 70% beef and 30% mushroom blend under blind and informed tasting. Beef was preferred over all alternatives. The blend beat the plant-based options when tasted blind, but demand fell once ingredients were disclosed. The animal-like protein burger beat the pea burger. `[general, Medium, GLB-30, tested]`
- GFI reported in 2023 that 53% of consumers said plant-based products should taste indistinguishable from meat and that taste was the top reason for not trying or for stopping. `[general, Low, GLB-04, stated]`
- A review of the price, taste and convenience ("PTC") hypothesis, drawing on surveys, choice experiments, a field experiment and company cases, concludes that PTC-competitive plant-based meat "would not currently replace meat" for most consumers, because social and psychological factors also drive choice. `[general, Medium, GLB-32, tested]`
- In the UK, branded products lost less volume than cheaper private label in the year to January 2025, which GFI Europe reads as quality and taste mattering more than price for remaining buyers. `[general, Medium, GLB-11, revealed]`

We did not find a published blind test in which a commercially available plant-based meat matched meat in overall liking among meat eaters. `[general, Medium, GLB-30, GLB-32, inferred]`

## 3. Why the US category declined after 2021

The evidence points to several causes acting together. We rank them by the strength of evidence, not by how often they are asserted.

1. **Trial without habit (strong, revealed).** Most households tried once. Over 2014 to 2019, 68% never bought and 11% bought in only one month; spending fell from about USD 8 in the first month to under USD 2 a month afterwards. `[general, High, GLB-18, GLB-17, revealed]` In 2018 to 2020, 40% of buyers bought once only. `[general, High, GLB-19, revealed]` Penetration then halved while repeat stayed flat, so the pool of triers shrank. `[general, High, GLB-01, GLB-02, revealed]`
2. **Price premium and inflation (strong, revealed).** The premium over meat widened from 65% (2022) to 77% per lb (2023). `[general, High, GLB-03, revealed]` Demand is elastic (section 2.1). The share of meat-reducers citing price as key rose from 16% (2020) to 52% (survey cited by GFI in April 2023). `[general, Low, GLB-04, stated]` Price cannot be the whole story: in 2022 plant-based meat prices rose less than meat prices (+8% versus +12%) and volumes still fell. `[general, Medium, GLB-04, revealed]`
3. **Taste and texture short of meat (medium, tested and stated).** See section 2.3. `[general, Medium, GLB-30, GLB-04, tested]`
4. **Novelty fading (medium, revealed).** Growth peaked in the same year or two across North America, Europe and Asia, which fits a fad cycle more than country-specific shocks. `[general, Medium, GLB-47, revealed]` Advertising for the Impossible Burger lifted purchase among meat-burger buyers by only about a fifth as much as among vegan-burger buyers, so marketing mainly recruited people already close to the category. `[general, Medium, GLB-41, revealed]`
5. **Health and "ultra-processed" concerns (weak evidence as a cause).** Barriers citing processing appear in Taiwan (over 40% cite dislike of processed foods), Thailand ("excessive processing") and Japanese commentary. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-57, GLB-54, GLB-56, stated]` Shelf audits show meat substitutes have lower protein quality and more salt and sugar than meat on average. `[general, Medium, GLB-15, revealed]` We found no study that isolates the health narrative's causal effect on sales. `[general, Low, none, inferred]`

Note also that growing meat avoidance in the US (the share of households not buying meat in a month rose about 10% from 2004 to 2020) mostly predates Beyond and Impossible, so modern analogues rode a trend rather than created it. `[general, Medium, GLB-22, revealed]`

**Lessons for a market like Vietnam** (our inference): a launch spike tells you little; measure repeat and monthly buyers. Do not rely on novelty, celebrity or climate messaging. A premium product that asks meat eaters to pay more for a worse-tasting copy of a familiar meat will lose repeat buyers. `[general, Medium, sections 1 to 3, inferred]`

## 4. Asia: what happened, with the Buddhist vegetarian analogues

Transfer assumption for all `VN-adjacent` claims in this section: Vietnam shares with Thailand, Taiwan and southern China a Buddhist-influenced vegetarian tradition (*ăn chay*, eating vegetarian, on lunar days and festivals), an established industry of soy, gluten and konjac mock meats (*đồ chay*, traditional vegetarian food), and meat prices that are low relative to Western analogues. Income and retail structure differ, especially from Taiwan and Japan.

### 4.1 China

Revealed evidence is thin but consistently negative since 2021. By mid-2023, Shanghai start-up Hey Maet had its licence revoked, Zhenmeat had launched nothing new since 2020, Beyond Meat was selling under 600 units a month on Tmall, and plant-based patties cost about three times conventional ones online. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-50, revealed]` In contrast, 60.1% of an online sample matched to China's adult population said they had eaten plant-based meat at least once, and perceived healthiness was the strongest predictor of purchase intent. `[VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-51, stated]` This gap between stated trial and revealed sales is a warning for Vietnamese survey data. We found no reliable Chinese market size by year (see rejected claims).

### 4.2 Thailand (closest ASEAN comparator with a large meat industry)

- Euromonitor data: meat alternatives USD 13.7 million (2016), USD 19.3 million (2019), USD 20.8 million (2020); dairy alternatives USD 604 to 823 million over the same years. `[VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-52, revealed]`
- Meat and seafood substitute volumes grew about 30% from 2021 to 2024 from that small base, as average prices fell 8% from a 2022 peak. In 2024 substitutes averaged 736 THB per kg against 94 for chicken, 179 for pork and 348 for beef: about 8, 4 and 2 times. `[VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-53, revealed]`
- Large meat companies entered (CP Foods "Meat Zero", Thai Union "OMG Meat", NR Instant Produce's "Phuture" and co-manufacturing, Nithi Foods' "Let's Plant Meat"). Entry was mostly through partnerships with restaurant chains and quick-service brands. `[VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-52, revealed]` We found no brand sales figures.
- Drivers cited are health and the Buddhist ethic of not killing; the annual nine-day vegetarian festival is served mainly with textured soy products. Local firms were said to have an advantage over US brands by making pork and seafood styles for Thai dishes. `[VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-52, stated]`
- Two thirds of Thais in a 2023 survey said they want to eat less meat within two years; sample details are not published. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-54, stated]`

### 4.3 Japan

Soy milk production reached 410,712 kL in 2024, close to the 2020 record of about 430,000 kL, with unsweetened soy milk at a record 137,052 kL. `[VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-55, revealed]` By contrast, many companies that entered soy meat (*daizu* meat) have slowed, contracted or withdrawn, while tofu purchases stay stable. Japanese commentary frames soy foods as an independent food culture, not meat substitutes. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-56, revealed]` We could not retrieve a soy meat market-size series (open question).

### 4.4 Taiwan: the closest cultural analogue

- Taiwan has about 3 million vegetarians (commonly described as over 10% of the population) and a labelling system with vegan, ovo, lacto, ovo-lacto and "five pungent" categories. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-57, GLB-58, stated]`
- Yet plant-based food retail sales (a Euromonitor-type measure of modern products) were only USD 26.8 million in 2017 and USD 36.0 million in 2021. Among non-vegetarians, 27.3% had tried plant-based meat versus 50% of vegetarians; over 40% cited dislike of processed foods and about half would not pay a premium. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-57, stated]`
- Western brands launched in 2019. Beyond Meat reported about NT$10 million a month (about USD 0.33 million at 30 NT$ per USD, our conversion); over half its buyers were vegetarians, and its patty cost about five times conventional burger meat. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-58, revealed]`
- The most striking sales figure is from foodservice in a local format: OmniPork dumplings at the Bafang Yunji chain, over 1 million a week across about 1,000 outlets at NT$6 each in the launch period (2020). We found no evidence on whether this lasted. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-59, revealed]`

Interpretation (our inference): in a culture that already has a large vegetarian population and a mature mock-meat industry, Western-style analogues mostly sold to existing vegetarians and took share within vegetarian eating rather than converting meat eaters. The traditional industry's own size and response are the key unknown; we found no reliable figure (see rejected claims). `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-57, GLB-58, inferred]`

### 4.5 Singapore, Hong Kong and Korea

Singapore approved the world's first cultivated meat sale (2020) and in 2025 approved two more products, but consumer evidence is mostly stated: 58% say they are likely to buy "blended meat". `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-49, GLB-07, stated]` Across six Southeast Asian markets including Vietnam, a 2024 GFI APAC survey found only 5% regularly eat plant-based meat and 44% had heard of it but never tried it; this is probably the survey the supply study cites as ECO-24. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-48, stated]` We found no retrieved sales series for Hong Kong (OmniFoods, Green Monday) or Korea.

### 5.2 Foodservice versus retail

US foodservice sales of plant-based proteins were about USD 291 million in 2025 (-7%), while plant milk in foodservice grew 14%. `[general, Medium, GLB-07, revealed]` In Asia, foodservice was the main entry route for Beyond, OmniFoods and Thai producers. `[VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-52, GLB-58, GLB-59, revealed]` Choice experiments suggest that adding a plant-based burger to a menu cuts beef orders about as much as adding a chicken wrap, i.e. it competes as one more option. `[general, Medium, GLB-24, tested]`

## 6. Displacement and rebound

- **Plant-based meat.** 86% of 2018 to 2020 buyers also bought ground meat, and ground meat purchases did not fall after a household's first plant-based purchase. `[general, High, GLB-19, revealed]` Meat spending did not change in the month of first plant-based purchase. `[general, High, GLB-17, revealed]` Household-level results for 2019 to 2021 show mixed substitution and complementarity and "not yet ... promising" displacement. `[general, Medium, GLB-20, revealed]` Retail-level results show complementarity with beef and pork. `[general, High, GLB-16, revealed]` A 2024 working paper finds plant-based meat effects on meat "noisy". `[general, Medium, GLB-22, revealed]`
- **Plant milk.** One extra gallon of non-dairy milk is associated with 0.43 to 0.60 fewer gallons of dairy milk (US, 2004 to 2018) and a working paper finds 0.73 to 0.93. In both cases plant milk explains only a small part of the long decline in dairy milk. `[general, High, GLB-28, GLB-22, GLB-29, revealed]`
- **Supply-side modelling.** Even large plant-based price cuts barely move US cattle numbers (10% price cut, -0.15% cattle; 20%, -0.29%), because cross-price elasticities are small and cattle supply is inelastic. `[general, High, GLB-27, inferred]` A food-away-from-home choice model reaches the same conclusion for poultry and livestock. `[general, Medium, GLB-26, tested]`
- **Rebound within the day.** In Cambridge cafeterias, extra vegetarian lunches were not offset by less vegetarian choice at dinner. `[general, High, GLB-35, tested]` Rebound outside the cafeteria was not measured.

For Vietnam's supply-security and animal-welfare case this matters: if displacement is weak, plant-based products add protein supply without cutting meat demand one for one. The supply case (import substitution of soy protein) does not depend on displacement; the welfare case does. `[general, Medium, sections above, inferred]`

## 7. Cultivated and fermentation-derived products (short)

- **Cultivated meat.** First sold in Singapore in December 2020 (restaurant 1880); sold in the US from 2023 in two restaurants; Huber's Butchery in Singapore sold GOOD Meat chicken through 2023, and the May 2024 retail product contained 3% cultivated cells and 97% plant-based ingredients. `[general, Low, GLB-60, GLB-61, revealed]` Approvals now exist in Singapore, the US, Israel and Australia; Italy, Florida and Alabama have bans. `[general, Low, GLB-62, GLB-07, revealed]` Eat Just's planned Singapore plant was shuttered by 2024. `[general, Low, GLB-61, revealed]` We found no published sales volumes; consumer demand to date is best described as tasting-scale. Investment fell from USD 226 million (2023) to USD 74 million (2025). `[general, Medium, GLB-60, GLB-07, revealed]` Another wave 6 stream covers Asian cultivated demand.
- **Fermentation.** More than 163 companies; investment USD 357 million in 2025 (down from about USD 632 to 651 million in 2024; the two GFI summaries differ) amid "high-profile closures and restructurings". Cost claims exist (for example a threefold productivity gain and 50% cost cut at 3,000 L for one dairy-protein process) but we found no published B2B sales volumes or price per kg for precision-fermented dairy proteins. `[general, Low, GLB-63, GLB-07, revealed]`

Three patterns stand out. First, forecasts were made at or near the 2019 to 2021 peak and extrapolated launch-year growth. Second, several came from parties with an interest (investors, consultancies selling transformation work); Kearney describes its own method as "qualitative", based on expert conversations. `[general, High, GLB-45, revealed]` Third, the "price parity" milestones were treated as tipping points, but where parity partly arrived, growth did not follow (section 2.2).

**Recommendation for Part VI labelling (our inference):**

- Call any pathway that assumes a replacement share of meat an **exploratory scenario**, not a forecast, and state that no market has yet shown measurable replacement.
- Include a **benchmark-anchored scenario** built from observed values: peak trial of about 20 to 39% of households, monthly buyers of about 9 to 10% (8.6 to 10.4% observed), repeat of about 60%, and a retail share ceiling of 3 to 4% of pre-packaged meat after ten years in rich markets with premiums of 35 to 100%.
- Treat any figure above those anchors as a **stretch scenario** that requires conditions not yet seen anywhere (taste parity among meat eaters plus price at or below meat, in local formats).
`[general, Medium, sections 1 to 8, inferred]`

## 10. Implications for frontier actors

**Founders and start-ups.**
- Do not copy the Western branded-burger model. The categories that held up are mince, strips, pieces, sausages and tofu-type products used inside dishes. In Vietnam that means fillings and components for existing dishes (for example *chả*, Vietnamese pressed meat loaf; dumpling and spring-roll fillings; stir-fry strips), not a premium patty. `[general and VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-09, GLB-10, GLB-11, GLB-59, inferred]`
- Price per gram of protein is the first gate: design for tofu to chicken-breast parity per 100 g of protein (VND 34,000 to 66,000 in the retail audit), not for "premium" positioning. `[VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01, GLB-16, inferred]`
- Measure repeat purchase and monthly buyers from the first month; ignore launch spikes. `[general, High, GLB-17, GLB-19, revealed]`
- Consider foodservice and B2B supply to existing *đồ chay* makers first; in Asia, analogues that worked were served inside local dishes by chains. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-52, GLB-59, inferred]`

**R&D bodies and universities.**
- Run informed and blind taste benchmarks of Vietnamese products against meat and against tofu with local meat eaters, and repeat them over time. `[general, Medium, GLB-30, GLB-32, inferred]`
- Work on texture and cost of textured protein from local raw materials; every local product with 10 g or more protein per 100 g currently relies on imported textured soy or gluten (FORM-01). `[VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01, inferred]`
- Address nutrition critiques early (salt, protein quality, fortification) because "processed" is a stated barrier across Asia. `[VN-adjacent, Low, GLB-15, GLB-57, stated]`

**Investors and development finance.**
- Discount market-size decks built on 2019 to 2021 forecasts; they overshot by 10 to 100 times. `[general, Medium, GLB-45, GLB-44, GLB-43, inferred]`
- Underwrite on repeat rates, cost per kg of protein and B2B contracts, not on consumer surveys of intent. `[general, Medium, GLB-48, GLB-51, GLB-12, inferred]`
- The supply case (import substitution of soy protein for food and feed) does not depend on consumers switching from meat; the consumer-switch case has weak evidence. `[general, Medium, section 6, inferred]`

**Funders and philanthropies.**
- Fund the missing Vietnamese evidence: panel data on tofu, soy milk and *đồ chay*; a scanner-based elasticity; a canteen default trial with delayed outcomes; a displacement study. These are cheap relative to their value (see open_questions.csv). `[general, Medium, GLB-39, inferred]`

**Policy makers.**
- The best-evidenced lever is what institutions serve: availability and defaults in canteens, schools and hospitals change meals reliably. Pair any programme with measurement over days, not just the next meal. `[general, High, GLB-35, GLB-37, GLB-39, tested]`
- Do not set national targets from consultancy forecasts. The Dutch retail sector set 50% plant protein for 2025 and stayed at 42.6%. `[general, High, GLB-12, revealed]`
- Price support alone (subsidy or parity pledges) grows plant-based volume but does not reduce meat much. `[general, High, GLB-27, GLB-12, revealed]`

**Food and feed manufacturers.**
- Private label grew volume in Germany (across six plant-based categories, private-label volume +41% from 2022 to 2024 while branded fell 8%); incumbents with scale (Vinasoy, the industrial *đồ chay* makers) are best placed to compete on price. `[general, High, GLB-09, revealed]`
- Soy milk's Asian trajectory (Japan's unsweetened soy milk at a record) suggests "protein-forward traditional soy" is a stronger positioning than "meat replacement". `[VN-adjacent, Medium, GLB-55, GLB-56, inferred]`
- Hybrids (part meat, part plant or mushroom) can win on taste; do not lead with a "less meat" claim. `[general, Medium, GLB-30, tested]`

**What not to do.** Do not launch premium branded analogues at 5 to 18 times the price of meat and expect repeat. Do not size demand from stated intent. Do not assume plant-based sales mean less meat eaten. `[general, Medium, sections 1 to 6, inferred]`

## 11. What would change this view

- Vietnamese or Thai panel data showing plant-based or *đồ chay* buyers with repeat rates well above 60% and rising monthly buyers.
- A product that matches meat in informed tastings among meat eaters and sells at or below meat price, with volumes growing for two years or more.
- A scanner-panel study in Asia showing meat purchases fall when households start buying plant-based products.
- Evidence that Lidl-style price parity grew national category volume (not only retailer share) once controlled for.
- Retrieved data showing Taiwan's traditional vegetarian industry grew faster after Western analogues entered (would suggest complementarity rather than cannibalisation).
- Verified cost and sales data showing fermentation-derived proteins at or below animal protein prices in B2B contracts.
- France's 2025 growth (+17% volume with falling prices) spreading to other markets, which would indicate the plateau was price-driven and reversible.

## 13. Research limits for this stream

- The shared web-search budget ran out part-way through this work. Later retrieval used direct document fetches and the Scite and OpenAlex academic databases. As a result, Korea, Hong Kong, Sweden, Israel and Singapore retail data are missing, and some Asian evidence rests on press sources.

(Wiley, HTTP 403); Japanese MAFF milk statistics (HTTP 403); GFI's State of the Industry PDFs (behind a form). Figures from these rely on abstracts or summaries and are tagged Medium or Low.
- Several numbers were read through an automated page summariser rather than verbatim; where the exact wording mattered (Thai prices, US penetration, Protein Tracker) we re-read the source or quoted it.
- No retractions or editorial notices were found for the peer-reviewed papers checked in Scite.
