# The incumbent: chay, tofu and plant eating as the demand base for novel protein in Vietnam

Agent: CHAY (wave 6). Source prefix: CHY. Date: 24 September 2026.

This paper studies *chay* (Buddhist-rooted vegetarian eating) as the incumbent that any novel protein food in Vietnam must either win against or build on. It treats chay as a baseline and a channel. It does not promote it. Related terms: *đồ chay* (traditional vegetarian foods, including industrial mock meats), *giả mặn* (vegetarian food that imitates meat), *ngày rằm* and *mùng một* (the 15th and 1st days of the lunar month), *Vu Lan* (the festival on the 15th day of the 7th lunar month; 27 August 2026), *ngũ vị tân* (the "five pungent plants", alliums avoided in strict Buddhist cooking), *đậu phụ* or *đậu hũ* (tofu).

Evidence tags follow the wave 6 brief: [scope, confidence, source, demand evidence type]. Source IDs FORM-01 (the September 2026 retail audit) and ECO-24 (GFI APAC 10 charts) belong to the supply study and are cited by ID. All 69 CHY sources concern Vietnam; ECO-24 is regional.

## Headline findings

1. **Nobody has measured how many Vietnamese eat chay, or how often.** We found no probability survey with a diet question. The only representative religious survey, Pew Research Center's face-to-face sample of 2,255 adults (June to September 2023), finds 38% identify as Buddhist, 48% as having no religion and 10% as Christian, and 86% offered food or drink to ancestors in the past year. It asked nothing about diet [VN-direct, High, CHY-06, stated]. Official counts of Buddhists differ threefold: 4.6 million in the 2019 census against more than 14 million registered followers in the 2023 government White Paper [VN-direct, Low to Medium, CHY-08; CHY-07, stated]. The widely repeated "about 10% of Vietnamese are vegetarian" has no survey behind it [VN-direct, Low, CHY-15; CHY-18, stated].

2. **Online panels say chay-type eating is near universal, and they overstate it.** Rakuten Insight (online panel, November 2021, n=4,018, aged 16 and over): 86% have eaten "plant-based alternatives", the highest of 13 markets; 10% say they eat only plant-based food; 44% eat plant-based alternatives several times a week [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-02, stated]. W&S (online, 2012, n=659): 59% "regularly" eat vegetarian, mainly on lunar days [VN-direct, Low, CHY-01, stated]. Vietnamese respondents count tofu, soy milk and đồ chay as "plant-based", so these figures measure familiarity with the incumbent, not demand for new products.

3. **Periodic, calendar-driven eating is the norm; full-time vegetarianism is rare and unmeasured.** The schedules described are 2 days a month (1st and 15th), 4 days (1st, 14th, 15th and the last day), 8 or 10 days, and whole months (the 1st and 7th lunar months) [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-09; CHY-10, stated]. Every Vietnamese academic survey we found recruited at chay restaurants, pagodas or universities, so none gives a prevalence [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-09; CHY-10; CHY-11, stated].

4. **Revealed demand spikes are large, regular and predictable.** Chay sellers report 2 to 3 times normal sales in the 7th lunar month in 2024, 2025 and 2026; a hypermarket chain's chay sales rose 20 to 40% month on month (Go!, 2024); one manufacturer's volume was 3.5 times the previous year [VN-direct, Low, CHY-16; CHY-17; CHY-18; CHY-20, revealed]. Staples react differently: a Ho Chi Minh City tofu maker raised output 15 to 20% with prices unchanged, while straw mushroom prices rose from VND 80,000 to 100,000 to about VND 160,000 per kg [VN-direct, Low, CHY-19; CHY-22, revealed]. Chay Garden charges 8 to 13% more for its buffet on holy days [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-33, revealed].

5. **The 2026 season was soft and price sensitive.** A Hai Phong caterer reported chay offering-tray orders down 20 to 30% on 2025 as input costs rose 15 to 20%; the national news agency described Ho Chi Minh City's Vu Lan buying as "slightly up" [VN-direct, Low, CHY-21; CHY-19, revealed].

6. **A dense but fragmented restaurant network with no scaled chain.** Foody lists more than 1,873 chay places in Ho Chi Minh City (old boundary), 401 in Hanoi, 201 in Da Nang and at least 3,275 across the 23 former provinces that show a count (September 2026) [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-31, revealed]. The largest chain we could count, Loving Hut, has 14 outlets; Chay Garden has 2; Hum had 3 in 2018 [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-32; CHY-34; CHY-36, revealed].

7. **Chay is cheaper than meat, except at the premium end.** Street chay cost VND 15,000 to 30,000 a plate in 2024 to 2026, against VND 29,000 to 35,000 for a meat rice plate in Ho Chi Minh City in 2026 [VN-direct, Low, CHY-38; CHY-39; CHY-42; CHY-40, revealed]. In modern retail, same-brand frozen chay was 17% (CJ Cau Tre) and 38% (Vissan) cheaper than the meat version [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01, revealed]. Premium chay dining costs VND 239,000 to 400,000 a head [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-33; CHY-37, revealed].

8. **Mock meat is mainstream and religiously tolerated, but the fashion is tilting to "natural".** Monks quoted in Buddhist media call giả mặn a skilful means that reduces killing; a minority of Buddhist writers call it harmful to compassion; we found no binding Sangha ruling against it [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-53; CHY-54; CHY-55, stated]. Mock meats were the best sellers in Ho Chi Minh City shops in Vu Lan 2024 [VN-direct, Low, CHY-26, revealed], while northern sellers in 2026 report buyers moving to mushrooms, lotus, beans and vegetables over "industrial" chay [VN-direct, Low, CHY-20; CHY-21, stated].

9. **The five pungent plants rule is a niche requirement, not a mass-market gate.** The Sangha newspaper's advisory panel says avoiding ngũ vị tân binds people who have taken Bodhisattva precepts, not lay people keeping lunar days [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-52, stated]. Mainstream industrial chay ignores it: Vissan, CJ bibigo, CJ Cau Tre and Saigon Co.op private-label chay list onion, garlic, shallot or leek, and one Co.op chay product contains egg [VN-direct, High, FORM-01, revealed].

10. **Trust, not taste, is the category's weakest point.** Unlabelled mock meat at VND 60,000 to 250,000 per kg is sold openly in Ho Chi Minh City markets (August 2026); inspectors have seized a tonne of untraceable *chả chay* (vegetarian sausage) and 500 kg of unlabelled Chinese textured soy sold as "chay spare ribs" [VN-direct, Low, CHY-23; CHY-59; CHY-60, revealed]. Certification is voluntary (TCVN 13625:2023, equivalent to ISO 23662:2021; V-Label via SGS), and we found no national or Buddhist chay certification scheme in use [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-57; CHY-58, revealed].

11. **The incumbent's size is known only for soy and mushrooms.** Vietnam uses about 550,000 tonnes of soybeans a year for food (2025/26), roughly 5 kg per person [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-45, revealed; per-person figure Low, inferred]. Mushroom output is 250,000 to 270,000 tonnes [VN-direct, Low, CHY-51, revealed]. We found no audited sales figure for industrial đồ chay and no chay target in Vissan's 2026 plan [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-30, revealed].

12. **Motives: health first, religion as the calendar, ethics a thin layer.** Rakuten 2021: health 61%, animal welfare 40%, environment 32% of those choosing plant-based alternatives [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-02, stated]. In group discussions with 44 self-described green consumers, health and food safety dominated and only 3 raised the environment [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-12, stated]. The organised vegan movement is small and online [VN-direct, Low, CHY-62; CHY-63, stated].

13. **The protein gap is the opening.** Chay products are protein-poor: the median label protein of the 43 ready-to-eat or ready-to-cook foods in the retail audit is 4.7 g per 100 g, and a Saigon Co.op chay gyoza carries 4.2 g [VN-direct, High, FORM-01, revealed]. At typical prices, market mock meat costs roughly VND 120,000 to 200,000 per 100 g of protein, above tofu's VND 66,000 [VN-direct, Low, FORM-01; CHY-20, inferred]. A product that lifts protein inside existing chay formats at today's chay price fits the demand as it is.

14. **Selling into chay days does not by itself displace meat.** Chay days are already inside the meat baseline. A novel protein sold into them replaces tofu, gluten, mushrooms or imported textured soy, not pork. Displacement needs more chay occasions or entry into ordinary meals [VN-direct, Low, our inference, inferred].

## 1. What this paper covers

The brief asks seven questions: prevalence and frequency of chay; revealed demand and the restaurant network; the size of the incumbent categories; what chay buyers value and reject; price benchmarks; the vegan movement; and whether chay is a channel or a separate logic. Sections 2 to 8 answer them in order. Section 9 turns the evidence into design inputs. We searched in Vietnamese and English, read primary documents where they existed (Pew, Rakuten, USDA, company pages, Foody listing pages, academic papers), and used press only where no better source exists, tagged Low.

A structural point shapes everything below. In Vietnamese usage "chay" is a practice (not eating animal flesh on set days), a cuisine (cơm chay, bún chay, đồ chay) and a product category (the frozen "thực phẩm chay" aisle). Surveys that ask about "plant-based" or "vegetarian" food catch all three. That is why Vietnam tops multi-country plant-based surveys and why those numbers say little about novel products.

## 2. How many people eat chay, and how often

### 2.1 The religious base

Chay is anchored in Mahayana Buddhism and in folk practice around the lunar calendar. The size of that base depends on the definition.

| Estimate | Figure | Method | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adults who identify as Buddhist | 38% (no religion 48%, Christian 10%, other 3%) | Pew, face-to-face probability sample, n=2,255, June to September 2023; margin of error 3.03 points; about 11% of population inaccessible | [VN-direct, High, CHY-06, stated] |
| Adults who offered food or drink to ancestors in the past year | 86% | Same Pew survey | [VN-direct, High, CHY-06, stated] |
| Registered religious followers | 26.5 million (27% of population), of which Buddhists more than 14 million | Government White Paper, March 2023, administrative count | [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-07, revealed] |
| Buddhists in the census | 4.6 million | 2019 Population and Housing Census, as reported; contested by the Sangha | [VN-direct, Low, CHY-08, stated] |
| Buddhist places of worship | 18,544 | White Paper 2023 | [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-07, revealed] |

We prefer Pew's 38% as the measure of Buddhist self-identification because it is the only probability sample. For chay behaviour, none of the three is the right number: lay people who keep lunar chay days include many who call themselves "no religion" and practise ancestor rites. We found no source that measures chay-day observance by religious group.

What is missing matters as much. We found no public Q&Me, Decision Lab, Kantar Worldpanel, NielsenIQ or Cimigo survey that reports chay frequency. Q&Me's photo-diary study of 6,000 dinners (April 2023, n=300 home cooks in Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi) and its July 2026 new-foods survey (n=1,000) publish no chay or protein-source split [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-66; CHY-69, revealed]. The GFI APAC regional survey of nearly 6,000 people across six Southeast Asian countries gives no Vietnam split on its public page [VN-adjacent, Medium, ECO-24, stated]. No survey from the Vietnam Buddhist Sangha was found.

**Stated versus revealed.** All prevalence figures above are stated, from online panels or self-selected samples. Online panels over-represent young, urban, connected people and those who like to answer food surveys. The Rakuten question lists Western examples (nut milk, plant-based burgers, vegan gummies) but Vietnamese respondents almost certainly include soy milk and tofu: 87% of Vietnamese plant-based consumers named plant milk [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-02, stated]. The revealed checks in Section 3 (sales spikes, price moves, listings) confirm that the practice is widespread and calendar-bound, but they cannot give a share of the population.

### 2.3 The chay calendar

The pattern described in academic and press sources is consistent [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-09; CHY-10; CHY-25; CHY-26, stated]:

- **Monthly lunar days.** The 1st and 15th are the core; stricter households add the 14th and the last day (4 days) or keep 8 or 10 days.
- **Whole months.** The 1st lunar month (after Tet, peaking at the first full moon) and the 7th lunar month (Vu Lan and the "wandering souls" period, peaking on the 15th).
- **Life events.** Funerals, death anniversaries and pagoda visits.

A market vendor in Can Tho put it simply: in the first month "many families choose to eat vegetarian meals on the full moon day, or for half a month or even the whole month" [VN-direct, Low, CHY-25, stated]. A Ho Chi Minh City vegan restaurant owner said customers crowd in on the 1st and 15th, but during the Vu Lan month traffic stays high from start to finish [VN-direct, Low, CHY-26, stated].

### 2.4 Is chay growing among young urban people?

Stated evidence says yes; revealed evidence is weak.

- **Stated.** Press pieces from 2023 to 2026 describe young urban people eating chay for health, lightness, weight control, calm and the environment, alongside religion [VN-direct, Low, CHY-64; CHY-65; CHY-43; CHY-15, stated]. Rakuten's 2021 reasons put health first (61%), then animal welfare (40%), following a vegetarian diet (38%), trying a trend (36%) and environment (32%) [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-02, stated]. A 2019 southern sample found environment had no significant effect on choice once health, "understanding of the body", variety and spirituality were in the model [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-09, stated].
- **Revealed.** Foody listings rose from 2,737 nationwide in May 2024 (as reported by B-Company) to at least 3,275 in 23 former provinces in September 2026 (our read) [VN-direct, Low to Medium, CHY-14; CHY-31, revealed]. Listings are cumulative and include closed venues, so this is weak evidence of growth. B-Company reports HappyCow listings in Vietnam rising from about 1,400 (2022) to about 1,894 (2024) [VN-direct, Low, CHY-14, revealed]; we could not verify this because HappyCow blocked access. Chay Garden and Hum Garden held Michelin Bib Gourmand status in May 2024, a signal of premium urban demand [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-35, revealed].
- **Our reading.** We cannot say whether the share of people keeping chay days is rising. We can say that urban chay supply (listings, premium restaurants, delivery) has grown and that the stated motive mix among the young has shifted toward health. We found no panel data that tracks the same people over time.

We found no retailer scan data, no Kantar Worldpanel release and no official statistic on lunar-day sales. Every figure above is a quote to the press, so each is Low on its own. Taken together, across four years, three regions and several independent sellers, they support a Medium-confidence conclusion: chay demand roughly doubles in specialist channels in the 7th lunar month, rises 15 to 40% in mainstream retail, and moves prices of supply-constrained items (fresh mushrooms) but not of tofu [VN-direct, Medium, synthesis of the rows above, inferred].

Two design lessons follow. First, tofu makers flex volume, not price, so tofu remains the price anchor even at the peak. Second, fresh mushrooms cannot flex supply, so their prices roughly double; a shelf-stable protein that holds price through the peak has a natural opening.

## 5. What chay buyers value and dislike

### 5.1 Giả mặn: imitation meat

Buddhist opinion is divided, and permissive on balance.

- **For.** Thich Thanh Huan of the Central Sangha's Office I: "even if a chay dish is shaped like meat food, it is still chay, and it still reduces killing" (*"dù là món chay được làm theo hình dáng của thức ăn mặn thì đó vẫn là đồ chay, vẫn giảm thiểu được sự sát sinh"*). He treats meat-like names as a legitimate trade technique that helps people start eating chay [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-53, stated]. Thich Minh Thanh, an abbot in Nam Dinh (now Ninh Binh), called it "using the false to find the true" [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-55, stated].
- **Against.** A 2020 essay on the main Buddhist portal argues that mock meat keeps the mind attached to meat, "damages compassion" and contains harmful chemicals, while conceding it would save animals if widely adopted [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-54, stated].
- **Science and nutrition voices.** A food technologist says meat-like appearance forces makers to add colourings, flavourings and texturisers, and calls for oversight; a nutritionist advises long-term vegetarians to eat fresh foods and limit mock meat [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-55, stated].

We found no Sangha directive against giả mặn. Revealed behaviour matches the permissive view: mock meats were the fastest sellers in Ho Chi Minh City chay shops and Bach Hoa Xanh stores in Vu Lan 2024 [VN-direct, Low, CHY-26, revealed]; a busy VND 15,000 chay canteen offers 6 mock meat dishes among about 30 [VN-direct, Low, CHY-38, revealed]. The 2019 southern study observed that "semi-vegetarians" (periodic eaters) prefer processed foods in their chay meals while vegans and lacto-ovo vegetarians mostly eat unprocessed plant foods [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-09, stated]. That is the key segmentation: the periodic majority buys mock meat; the strict minority buys whole foods.

The 2026 counter-signal comes from the north. A Hanoi online chay seller: "Instead of choosing too many industrial chay products, many people prefer dishes made from lotus, mushrooms, vegetables and natural ingredients." A Hanoi caterer: customers "increasingly care about ingredient composition, preferring mushroom, lotus seed, bean and vegetable dishes over too many ready-made chay foods" [VN-direct, Low, CHY-20; CHY-21, stated]. This may be a regional difference (southern cities are the heartland of giả mặn) or a trend; we log it as a disagreement.

### 5.2 Ngũ vị tân, eggs and dairy

**The rule.** Giac Ngo's advisory panel lists the five as *hành, hẹ, tỏi, nén, kiệu* (onion or scallion, Chinese chives, garlic, and two small alliums, *nén* and *kiệu*). It says only those who take the Bodhisattva precepts of the Brahma Net Sutra eat chay permanently and avoid the five; lay people keeping the eight precepts on lunar days are not bound by it [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-52, stated]. An academic paper describes the Buddhist vegetarian diet as excluding the five pungent plants in general terms [VN-direct, Low, CHY-10, stated]; we treat that as describing the strict ideal.

**Revealed practice.** In the Nha Trang shelf log (part of FORM-01), mainstream industrial chay uses alliums freely [VN-direct, High, FORM-01, revealed]:

- Vissan chả giò chay: onion (*hành*).
- CJ bibigo Healthy Mandu chay: onion, leek, shallot, spring onion and garlic.
- CJ Cau Tre há cảo chay: leek.
- Saigon Co.op chay gyoza with noodles: garlic in the filling and hen's egg in the noodles.
- SG Food chay Thai hotpot kit: onion in the broth.
- Vissan canned chay beef and bean stew: onion.

**Eggs and dairy.** A Buddhist portal accepts unfertilised eggs for lay chay eaters because they carry no life [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-56, stated]. The national standard TCVN 13625:2023 recognises lacto-ovo, ovo, lacto and vegan categories [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-57, stated]. So "chay" on a pack does not guarantee egg-free or dairy-free.

**Implication.** Allium-free and egg-free are requirements for a definable minority (monastics, pagoda kitchens, strict lay vegetarians, some offering trays), not for the periodic mass market. A product line aimed at pagodas and strict buyers needs allium-free and egg-free variants, clearly labelled; a mass-market product does not.

### 5.3 Additives, adulteration and "chay bẩn"

The concern is real and documented mainly through enforcement cases and press market checks.

- **Unlabelled goods.** Tien Phong found "three-without" (*ba không*: no label, no origin, no expiry) mock meats sold loose in Ho Chi Minh City markets at VND 60,000 to 250,000 per kg in August 2026. The vice chair of the city's Nutrition Association: "consumers have almost no basis for knowing the product" [VN-direct, Low, CHY-23, revealed].
- **Seizures.** One tonne of chả chay with no proof of origin, stopped on a truck and destroyed in former Phu Yen (now Dak Lak), May 2025 [VN-direct, Low, CHY-59, revealed]. 500 kg of Chinese "Textured Soy Protein" sold as *sườn non chay* (chay spare ribs), with no Vietnamese label, Dong Thap, June 2022; declared value implies about VND 68,000 per kg [VN-direct, Low, CHY-60, revealed]. Hanoi market surveillance reported 20 food safety cases with fines above VND 703 million in July 2026, not chay-specific [VN-direct, Low, CHY-24, revealed].
- **Meat in chay.** A Can Tho University team built a multiplex PCR kit to detect pork, beef and chicken DNA in vegetarian food, citing "many cases" of meat found in chay foods [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-61, stated]. We found no published survey of how often chay products contain meat. This is an open question, not a finding.
- **Additives.** Emmay's founder claims 3 to 5 additives in its mycelium products against 15 to 23 in other brands [VN-direct, Low, CHY-68, stated]. Industrial chay labels commonly list MSG (621) and stabilisers [VN-direct, High, FORM-01, revealed].
- **Buyers respond.** In 2026 buyers asked for origin, production and expiry dates and preferred items with clear sourcing [VN-direct, Low, CHY-21; CHY-24, stated].

### 5.4 Certification and trust marks

- **National standard.** TCVN 13625:2023, fully equivalent to ISO 23662:2021, defines vegetarian and vegan food and labelling claims. It is voluntary [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-57, stated]. The press summary does not mention alliums; ISO 23662 does not address them.
- **V-Label.** SGS has audited for V-Label since 2021 and offers it in Vietnam; the page gives no count of Vietnamese licensees [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-58, revealed].
- **Buddhist certification.** We found no Sangha or pagoda certification scheme for chay products.
- **Consultancies** advertise Vegan Society registration for Vietnamese exporters, mainly cosmetics; we did not verify any food licensee.

The trust gap is an opportunity for a supplier that can prove what is and is not in the product.

## 7. The vegan movement and civil society

- **Sống Thuần Chay** ("Living Vegan"), founded 2018, claims more than 250,000 followers across TikTok, Facebook and Instagram and ran Vegfest 2019 with 3,500 attendees; its founder named three other Vietnamese vegan social channels of similar size [VN-direct, Low, CHY-62, stated]. Self-reported and undated; we could not reach its site.
- **Animal protection NGOs.** Humane World for Animals (formerly Humane Society International) works in Vietnam on the dog and cat meat trade, cage-free eggs (it reports ending cage confinement for over 90,000 hens in 2024), wildlife and disasters. Its Vietnam page lists no plant-based or meat-reduction programme [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-63, revealed].
- **Associations.** We found no national vegetarian or vegan association with a public membership figure. Facebook group sizes could not be verified without platform access.
- **Motives.** Qualitative research with green consumers finds health and food safety first, environment rare, and meat seen as irreplaceable at family meals: "Meat cannot be replaced; it can only be limited" (man, 40) [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-12, stated].

**Relative size.** No source measures the ethics or environment segment. Our judgement: the organised ethical vegan segment is at most in the low hundreds of thousands of engaged followers, while religious and folk chay reaches tens of millions of people on some days of the year [VN-direct, Low, CHY-62; CHY-06, inferred]. The ethical segment is a useful early-adopter and media channel for novel products; it is not the volume market.

## 8. Channel or separate logic?

### 8.1 What sellers and makers say

- NFC Food's chairwoman: Vu Lan "is one of Buddhism's major festivals. On this day, besides visiting temples, many people eat vegetarian to express filial piety" [VN-direct, Low, CHY-17, stated].
- Saigon Chay's chairman: demand in the season rises two to three times [VN-direct, Low, CHY-18, stated].
- A Bach Hoa Xanh store manager (2024): promotions make customers buy a lot, and chay stock sells out before new deliveries arrive [VN-direct, Low, CHY-26, stated].
- A Ho Chi Minh City tofu maker (2026): *"Giá đậu vẫn giữ như ngày thường, không tăng"* (tofu prices stay as on normal days, no increase) [VN-direct, Low, CHY-19, stated].
- The family running a VND 15,000 chay canteen: margins per dish are thin and volume carries the business; raising prices would lose customers [VN-direct, Low, CHY-38, stated].
- Hanoi sellers (2026): buyers move away from industrial chay toward lotus, mushrooms, beans and vegetables [VN-direct, Low, CHY-20; CHY-21, stated].
- Vissan, launching chay dumplings for Vu Lan 2026: the aim is to give households more ready-made options to build chay menus at home without long preparation [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-29, stated].
- Hum's owner (2018): the restaurant lost money for three years and runs on much lower margins than meat restaurants [VN-direct, Low, CHY-36, stated].

We found no public interview in which a chay manufacturer or shop owner asks for more protein, a new protein ingredient or a meat-like texture upgrade. Their stated concerns are price, convenience, freshness, safety and "natural" ingredients.

### 8.2 Where chay is a ready channel

- **Shelf and cold chain exist.** The supermarkets in the retail audit had signed frozen chay sections with several brands, and markets carry fresh and loose chay [VN-direct, High, FORM-01; CHY-26, revealed].
- **Meat-likeness is accepted.** Buyers already pay for chay chicken, fish, shrimp, ham, sausages and spare ribs. A higher-protein analogue does not ask anyone to accept a new idea [VN-direct, Medium, CHY-26; CHY-53, revealed].
- **Demand is predictable.** The calendar gives producers known peaks every fortnight and two big months a year.
- **Price headroom per gram of protein.** Incumbent mock meat is an expensive protein source (Section 6), so a better-formulated product can beat it on protein per đồng without raising the shelf price.
- **Imported inputs are already there.** The high-protein chay products on the shelf use imported Chinese textured soy or gluten (supply study; CHY-60), so a domestic replacement ingredient is a supply substitution, not a consumer change.

### 8.3 Where the logic resists

- **Price ceilings are low.** Street chay sells below meat; mass buyers are price sensitive, and 2026 showed volume falling when costs rose [VN-direct, Low, CHY-21; CHY-38, revealed]. A premium novel ingredient that raises the shelf price will be refused.
- **"Industrial" is becoming a negative word** among some buyers, and additives and origin are the main fears [VN-direct, Low, CHY-20; CHY-21; CHY-23, stated]. A novel protein framed as high-tech, lab-made or "the future of meat" collides with that. A precision-fermented ingredient that counts as a GMO product (supply study) would face a religious and trust question we cannot yet answer.
- **The strict segment wants whole plants.** Premium restaurants and devout eaters prize fresh vegetables, mushrooms and monastic cooking, not analogues.
- **The ritual market values form over nutrition.** Offering trays are bought for presentation, freshness and timely delivery [VN-direct, Low, CHY-24, stated]. Protein content is irrelevant there.

### 8.4 The displacement arithmetic

Suppose, for illustration only, that 20 to 50% of people keep 2 to 4 chay days a month. Chay person-days are then 1.3% to 6.7% of all person-days (share times days divided by 30), before counting the whole-month observers [VN-direct, Low, our arithmetic on CHY-06 and CHY-09, inferred]. That is the same order as the supply study's S-ALT assumption of 1% of meat demand replaced in 2030. But these chay days are already in the meat consumption baseline. Three consequences:

1. A novel protein that captures chay-day protein replaces tofu, textured soy, gluten and mushrooms. It improves nutrition and import substitution. It displaces no meat.
2. Meat displacement requires either more chay occasions (a third or fourth day a month, chay breakfasts, chay canteen days) or entry into ordinary meals.
3. We found no evidence on compensation: whether people who eat chay on the 1st and 15th eat more meat on other days.

The chay base is therefore the cheapest place to launch and learn, and the wrong place to count impact.

## 9. Implications for frontier actors

**Founders.**
- Sell an ingredient, not a consumer brand. The winning first product is a protein base (textured, fungal or blended) that existing chay makers put into chả, giò, nem, há cảo, bò viên and chả giò chay.
- Design target: lift product protein from about 4 to 5 g to 10 to 12 g per 100 g while keeping the shelf price inside today's range (supermarket VND 56,000 to 173,000 per kg; market VND 60,000 to 90,000 per kg for giò and nem). That puts protein cost near tofu's VND 66,000 per 100 g of protein.
- Replace the imported Chinese textured soy and gluten already in the category; that is a purchasing decision for manufacturers, not a behaviour change for consumers.
- Make allium-free and egg-free variants for pagoda kitchens, offerings and strict buyers, and say so on the pack. Do not assume the mass market needs them.
- Build for the calendar: capacity and frozen stock for 2 to 3 times normal demand in the 1st and 7th lunar months and fortnightly peaks.
- Lead with safety, origin, short ingredient lists and taste. Avoid "lab", "future of meat" and environmental claims as the main message.

**R&D bodies and universities.**
- Publish a composition table for chay foods: measured protein, additives and meat DNA for street, market and supermarket chay. The Can Tho PCR kit is a starting tool.
- Develop allium-free flavour systems (mushroom, fermented soy, seaweed) that work in Vietnamese chay formats.
- Run sensory and choice tests of higher-protein chay products among periodic eaters and strict eaters separately; stated interest will not predict purchase.

**Investors.**
- The channel is thousands of independents plus a few frozen-food makers (Vissan, CJ Cau Tre, SG Food, Au Lac, An Nhien, Thoai An) and retailer private labels. Value capture is B2B supply to these makers.
- Underwrite on per-kg price parity with existing đồ chay and on repeat orders from manufacturers, not on market-size claims. The USD 103 million, USD 500 million and USD 1 billion figures in circulation have no visible method.
- Treat demand as seasonal and price sensitive.

**Funders and philanthropies.**
- Fund measurement: a chay-day question in a probability survey, and household purchase data by lunar day. These are cheap and would settle the size of the base.
- Fund a chay authenticity and composition survey; it serves consumers and creates the case for trustworthy suppliers.
- Count impact only for added chay occasions or entry into ordinary meals, not for upgraded chay days.

**Policy makers.**
- The new Food Safety Law is a window to define the claims "chay", "thuần chay" and "phù hợp cho người ăn chay" by reference to TCVN 13625, and to make meat-free claims enforceable.
- Target inspections before the 1st and 7th lunar months, when unlabelled chay floods markets.
- If precision-fermented proteins are treated as GMO products, chay buyers will need clear labelling to decide for themselves.

**Food manufacturers.**
- A declared protein content ("giàu đạm", high protein) would be new on the chay shelf; the category currently competes on price and taste only.
- Test higher-protein reformulations of top chay SKUs during the Vu Lan and first-month peaks, when trial is highest.
- Offer clean-label and allium-free lines to meet the 2026 "natural" shift and the strict segment.

**What not to do.**
- Do not price novel chay products at modern plant-based levels (imported analogues at VND 542,000 to 622,000 per 100 g of protein in the supply study).
- Do not treat online-panel "86% eat plant-based" or "10% vegetarian" as demand for novel products.
- Do not plan distribution through a chay restaurant chain; none has scale and margins are thin.
- Do not claim meat displacement from sales into existing chay days.

## 10. What would change this view

- **A probability survey with a chay question.** If fewer than 10% keep any chay days, the channel is smaller than we assume; if over half keep 4 or more days, it is larger and the displacement arithmetic changes.
- **Retail scan or household panel data** showing lunar-day uplift in modern trade is small would weaken the "predictable peaks" point.
- **Evidence that young urban people are adding chay days** (panel data) would make chay a displacement route, not only a channel.
- **A tested finding that chay buyers reject higher-protein or fungal ingredients** in blind tastings or at the shelf would weaken the ingredient strategy.
- **A Sangha statement against mock meat or requiring allium-free food for lay chay** would split the market.
- **A DNA survey showing widespread meat in chay** would raise the value of verified supply and certification.
- **Evidence of compensation** (more meat on non-chay days) would reduce the impact value of added chay days.
