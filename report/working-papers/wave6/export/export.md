# Export demand for Vietnamese alternative protein: who would buy, on what terms, and which export strengths could carry it

Agent: EXPORT (wave 6). Source prefix: EXP. Date: 24 September 2026.

Scope: this paper asks which foreign buyers would pay for Vietnamese-made alternative protein products and ingredients, on what terms (price, format, label, tariff, certification), and which of Vietnam's existing export businesses could carry them. It treats export demand as a design input for what to make. Traditional *đồ chay* (traditional vegetarian food) is studied as the incumbent export, not promoted. Insect protein appears only as an incumbent benchmark in feed. Every substantive claim carries `[evidence label, confidence, source ID, demand evidence type]`. Trade values are Vietnam-reported UN Comtrade data unless stated; Vietnam had not reported 2024 detail to Comtrade when we queried it (24 September 2026), so 2023 is the latest year at HS 6-digit level [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed].

## Headline findings

1. **Vietnam exports almost no protein ingredients today.** Vietnam exported USD 0.52 million (about 67 t, weight estimated by Comtrade) of protein concentrates and textured protein (HS 2106.10) in 2023, mostly to Japan, the US and Korea [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed], against about USD 34 million of imports of the same code in 2023 reported in the supply study (TF-002). Mirror data tell the same story: the US imported USD 1.45 million of HS 2106.10 from Vietnam in 2024 (1.0% of its imports of the code) and extra-EU imports from Vietnam were USD 31,521 [VN-direct, High, EXP-40, revealed]. China exported USD 380 million (178,588 t) of HS 2106.10 in 2024, Malaysia USD 7.8 million, Thailand USD 4.2 million and Indonesia USD 0.24 million [general, High, EXP-40, revealed]. Export demand for Vietnamese textured protein is untested, and China sets the price.

2. **The protein-ingredient export that does exist comes from seafood by-products.** Vietnam exported USD 13.0 million of HS 3504 (peptones and other protein substances) in 2023, led by Spain, the UK, Korea and Denmark [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed]. Vinh Hoan sells pangasius collagen peptides and gelatin under its Vinh Wellness unit [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-49, revealed], so much of this code is probably fish collagen peptide [VN-direct, Low, EXP-39; EXP-49, inferred]. The lesson for founders: Vietnamese B2B protein ingredients can already sell into European specification-driven buyers when they come from a certified, traceable seafood chain.

3. **The export platforms that could carry alternative protein are cashew, seafood processing, rice and cassava, not plant-based brands.** In 2025 Vietnam exported USD 5.23 billion (766,585 t) of cashew [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-18, revealed], USD 11.32 billion of fisheries products and USD 8.6 billion of fruit and vegetables [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-16, revealed], and 7.5 million t of rice worth USD 3.83 billion in the first 11 months [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-43, revealed]. In 2023 it exported USD 1.06 billion of cassava starch, 93.5% to China [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed], and USD 268 million of instant noodles [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed]. These businesses already hold buyers, certifications and cold or dry export logistics.

4. **Retail demand for finished plant-based meat is shrinking in the US and flat in Europe; growth is in plant milk, yoghurt and minimally processed proteins.** US plant-based meat and seafood retail sales were USD 1.0 billion in 2025, down 10% in value and 11% in units, and down 17% in value since 2023 [general, High, EXP-02, revealed]. In six large European markets, plant-based food sales rose 3.3% to EUR 4.75 billion in 2025 and volume rose 4.8%, but plant-based meat volume fell in the UK (9.4%), the Netherlands (10.7%) and Spain (7.0%) and grew only where prices fell (France, up 16.8% with prices down 2.8%) [general, High, EXP-05, revealed]. Tofu, tempeh and seitan grew by almost 30% in Germany and the Netherlands [general, High, EXP-05, revealed].

5. **Plant-based seafood is the weakest category in the richest markets.** German retail sales of plant-based seafood were EUR 14.1 million in 2025, down 22% in value and 29% in volume, and down 41% in value since 2023 [general, High, EXP-05, revealed]. We found no evidence that importers of Vietnamese seafood ask their suppliers for plant-based seafood. Vietnamese seafood processors are innovating in value-added fish formats, not plant-based ones [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-49, revealed].

6. **Asian import markets buy protein ingredients rather than finished analogues.** Japan's plant-based meat market was about USD 18 million in 2022, forecast at USD 29 million in 2025, under 0.5% of the meat products market; Japan imported 35,059 t (USD 149 million) of plant-derived protein ingredients in 2022 [general, Medium, EXP-07, revealed]. Korea's alternative meat market was KRW 21.2 billion (USD 16.9 million) in 2022 per Euromonitor [general, Medium, EXP-09, revealed] and about KRW 25.2 billion in 2023 per aT [general, Medium, EXP-08, revealed]. Europe and North America hold about 83% of global plant-based meat and seafood retail sales (USD 6.6 billion in 2025) [general, High, EXP-10, revealed].

7. **Vietnam's clearest structural advantage is tariffs into Europe, the UK and Japan.** Vietnamese textured protein, food preparations, plant drinks, soy sauce, stuffed pasta and prepared nuts enter the EU at 0% under EVFTA, against EU MFN rates of 12.8% (HS 2106 10 20), 9% plus an agricultural component (2106 90 98), 9.6% (plant drinks, 2202 99, duty-free for Vietnam since 1 January 2025) and 7 to 10.2% (prepared nuts) that Thai and Chinese goods pay [VN-direct, High, EXP-23, revealed]. The UK applies 0% to the same Vietnamese lines against 6 to 12% MFN [VN-direct, High, EXP-24, revealed]. Japan admits Vietnamese textured vegetable protein free, against 10.6% MFN and 6.6% for Chinese goods under RCEP [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-41, revealed]. The EU and Thailand are still negotiating; the EU and Indonesia finalised their agreement in September 2025 but it is not in force [general, High, EXP-47; EXP-48, revealed].

8. **The US is the least attractive large market for Vietnamese plant-based food in 2026.** Since 24 July 2026 the US charges an extra 12.5% Section 301 tariff on Vietnamese goods, against 10% for Malaysia, Indonesia and India and 12.5% for Thailand; cashews and coconuts are exempt, prepared plant-based foods are not [VN-direct, High, EXP-22, revealed]. This follows the Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling against IEEPA tariffs and a temporary 10% Section 122 tariff [general, High, EXP-21, revealed]. Combined with falling US plant-based meat sales, this argues against US-first plant-based meat exports.

9. **Labels, not tariffs, are the new barrier in Europe.** Since 19 August 2026 the EU restricts 32 meat terms on plant-based foods, including "meat", "chicken", "pork", "beef", "bacon" and "steak"; "burger", "sausage" and "nuggets" remain allowed but are again under attack, with a committee vote due in December 2026 [general, High, EXP-25, revealed]. Seafood names are not on the list [general, Medium, EXP-25, inferred]. Plant products may not use dairy names such as "milk" or "cheese" in the EU since the 2017 TofuTown judgment [general, Medium, EXP-26; EXP-25, revealed].

10. **Novel ingredients need years of pre-market approval before they can be exported; conventional plant ingredients do not.** The EU treats as novel any food not consumed to a significant degree before 15 May 1997 [general, High, EXP-46, revealed], and Singapore requires pre-market assessment for foods without a 20-year history of safe use, including cultivated meat and precision-fermentation proteins [general, High, EXP-34, revealed]. The US FDA proposed tighter oversight of self-affirmed GRAS on 10 August 2026 [general, High, EXP-45, revealed]. A Vietnamese mycoprotein or precision-fermented protein could not be sold in these markets without its own dossier.

11. **Diaspora demand is real, visible on shelves, and served by incumbent *đồ chay* and convenience-food makers.** About 2.3 million Vietnamese Americans lived in the US in 2023, 37% of Vietnamese-alone adults Buddhist [general, High, EXP-35, revealed], and 326,630 Vietnam-born people lived in Australia in June 2025 [general, High, EXP-36, revealed]. On 24 September 2026 the US Asian grocer Weee! listed Vietnam-made vegetarian net spring rolls (USD 4.99 for 14 oz), vegan lemongrass "chicken" jerky (USD 4.99 for 4.59 oz), vegetarian oyster sauce, vegetarian instant noodles, frozen vegetarian soups and fermented tofu [VN-direct, High, EXP-37, revealed]. Vietnam's 2023 instant noodle exports went mostly to the US, Cambodia, Germany and Poland [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed], all countries with established Vietnamese communities [VN-direct, Low, EXP-39, inferred]. This channel is a route for protein-upgraded *chay* (vegetarian) products, not a volume market [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-37; EXP-39, inferred].

12. **The most frontier-relevant export pull is on feed, through seafood buyers' standards.** The ASC Feed Standard (version 1.1 text; version 1.2 has been mandatory since 2 February 2026) requires certified feed mills to commit to deforestation- and conversion-free soy and palm with a cutoff no later than 31 December 2020, to improve marine-ingredient sourcing over time, to calculate greenhouse gas emissions per tonne of feed and to disclose species-specific emissions to ASC farms on request [general, High, EXP-29; EXP-30, revealed]. Vietnam has 20 feed mills in the ASC programme (13 certified), is the largest producer of ASC-certified pangasius and ranks third for ASC-certified shrimp [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-32, revealed]. UK retailers including Tesco, Sainsbury's, Aldi and Lidl, and prawn importers Seafresh and Lyons Seafoods, committed to deforestation- and conversion-free soy, including soy embedded in aquaculture feed, by the end of 2025 [general, High, EXP-33, revealed]. Vinh Hoan contracted at least 15,000 t of insect protein for pangasius feed for 2025 to 2027 [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-50, revealed]. None of the buyer rules we retrieved requires novel protein as such; they reward traceable, low-footprint, non-soy protein [general, Medium, EXP-29; EXP-33, inferred].

13. **The buyer mix for Vietnamese seafood is shifting towards markets that ask less about feed.** In January to August 2026, seafood exports to China and Hong Kong rose 33.4% to USD 2.03 billion, while the US was flat at USD 1.24 billion and the EU fell 1.6% to USD 750 million [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-31, revealed]. The standards-driven pull on feed (EU, UK) is therefore concentrated in a shrinking share of Vietnam's seafood sales [VN-direct, Low, EXP-31; EXP-33, inferred].

**Cashew is the plant-based platform with the widest reach.** Vietnam's cashew exports reached a record USD 5.23 billion in 2025, up 30.2% in value and 5.7% in volume, with China overtaking the US as the largest buyer [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-18, revealed]. In January to July 2025 exports to the US fell 27.5% in volume, which the source attributes to the 20% US reciprocal tariff then in force [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-17, revealed]. About 90% of the raw nuts are imported, mostly from Cambodia and West Africa [VN-direct, Low, EXP-18, revealed]. Vietnamese commentators now pitch cashew milk to the US [VN-direct, Low, EXP-19, stated], but we found no Vietnamese exporter of cashew milk or cashew cheese with verifiable export volumes. The only Vietnamese cashew cheese maker in the supply study, Kashew, sells in Ho Chi Minh City (supply study company register, CO-053). Cashew kernels enter the EU at 0% MFN, so Vietnam's EVFTA advantage for cashew applies only to processed forms (prepared nuts, drinks, spreads) [VN-direct, High, EXP-23, revealed].

**Coconut is visible in trade data only as raw material.** Vietnam's coconut belt is in the Mekong Delta, centred on former Ben Tre province (part of Vinh Long province since 1 July 2025, per the supply study's province register). In HS data we could only isolate desiccated coconut (USD 38 million in 2023, led by Egypt, China and Thailand) and fresh coconuts (USD 32 million, led by Thailand) [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed]; coconut milk and cream fall into broader codes (2106.90, 2008.19 or 2202.99) that we could not split, and we did not retrieve industry association totals for coconut products. Thailand, the largest buyer of Vietnamese fresh coconuts, is itself a major coconut-milk exporter, so some Vietnamese coconut probably ends up in Thai-finished coconut products [VN-direct, Low, EXP-39, inferred]: Vietnam may sit upstream of a dairy-alternative value chain that another country finishes.

**Rice is under price pressure.** Rice export value fell 24.7% in the first ten months of 2025, which the statistics office attributes to price competition from Thailand, India and Pakistan [VN-direct, High, EXP-42, revealed]. Adding value to broken rice or bran (for example rice protein) would be a response to this, but we found no Vietnamese rice protein exports or producers.

**Seafood processors have the skills plant-based seafood needs but are not using them for it.** Vinh Hoan launched a pangasius fish-cake *bánh mì* (Vietnamese baguette sandwich) and a cured pangasius croquette for retailers in April 2026 and showed formed "seafood family" shapes at Thaifex in May 2026 [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-49, revealed]. Forming, coating, retail packing and certification are exactly what a plant-based or hybrid seafood line would need, but we found no Vietnamese seafood exporter selling plant-based seafood abroad.

**Processed-food exporters are moving to their own brands.** Vietnam's food processing industry reached USD 88 billion in 2025; USDA FAS reports that processors are shifting from re-exporting under client labels to their own brands, and import higher-quality ingredients to supply Japan, Korea, the US and Europe [VN-direct, Medium, MAC-31, revealed]. The same report notes 2025 food-safety scandals at a dairy-substitute and nutritional-beverage processor [VN-direct, Medium, MAC-31, revealed], a reputational risk for any "Made in Vietnam" plant-based beverage.

**Incumbent *chay* exporters exist but disclose no volumes.** Au Lac Vegan Food claims exports to the US, Canada, Australia, Germany and the UK, with halal, ISO 22000 and FSSC 22000 certification [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-38, stated]. The supply study's company register lists claimed exports by An Nhien (Korea, Europe, North America), SG Food (Japan), Agrex Saigon (a vegetarian shiitake dumpling for export) and Emmay (about 10% of revenue to the UK, Australia, India and the Netherlands in 2023, company-reported) (supply study company register, CO-001, CO-030, CO-032, CO-036). None of these is revealed trade data.

**United States: falling.** GFI's SPINS-based data show 2025 US plant-based retail sales of USD 7.9 billion, down 2% in value and 3% in units, with plant-based meat and seafood at USD 1.0 billion, down 10% in value and 11% in units; from 2023 to 2025 the category lost 17% of value and 21% of units [general, High, EXP-02, revealed]. Average US stores carried 9.7 plant-based meat items in April 2025, 31% fewer than in early 2021 [general, Medium, EXP-44, revealed]. Only 11% of households bought plant-based meat or seafood in 2025, against 38% for plant-based milk [general, High, EXP-02, revealed]. Soy milk (+4%), coconut milk (+27%) and "chunks, shreds and strips" (+8% units) grew [general, Medium, EXP-01, revealed]: plain, versatile formats and coconut-based drinks, both closer to what Vietnam can make.

**Europe: resilient but price-driven.** GFI Europe's Circana data for France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Spain and the UK show 2025 sales of EUR 4.75 billion (up from EUR 4.60 billion) and volume of 1,573 million kg (up from 1,500 million kg) [general, High, EXP-05, revealed]. Plant-based meat grew only where prices fell: French volume rose 16.8% as prices fell 2.8%, and Italian volume rose 4.1% as prices fell 2.0%, while volume fell where prices rose (UK, Netherlands, Spain) [general, High, EXP-05, revealed]. Private label drives the milk category: in Germany, private-label plant milk is now cheaper than private-label dairy milk and made up 60% of plant milk volume; in the Netherlands, branded plant-based meat volume fell 15.8% while private label fell only 2% [general, High, EXP-05, revealed]. In France, plant-based meat still cost 25% more per kg than animal meat and plant milk 68% more per litre than dairy [general, High, EXP-05, revealed]. This is the market segment where a low-cost, tariff-free Asian contract manufacturer could compete: private-label, price-led, for retailers, in formats with simple labels.

**Japan and Korea: small finished markets, real ingredient demand.** USDA FAS reported that industry sources put Japan's plant-based meat market at about USD 18 million in 2022, forecast to reach USD 29 million in 2025, and that plant-based meat is less than 0.5% of the traditional meat product market by value [general, Medium, EXP-07, revealed]. Japanese manufacturers historically used soy protein to extend and cheapen processed meat, and "hybrid" products remain common [general, Medium, EXP-07, revealed]. Japan imported 35,059 t of plant-derived protein ingredients worth USD 149 million in 2022, up 66% in volume since 2010 [general, Medium, EXP-07, revealed]. In Korea, Euromonitor estimated the alternative meat market at KRW 21.2 billion (USD 16.9 million) in 2022, forecasting KRW 32.1 billion in 2025 [general, Medium, EXP-09, revealed]; aT put it at about KRW 25.2 billion in 2023, rising to KRW 29.5 billion in 2025 [general, Medium, EXP-08, revealed]. GFI APAC expects large-scale Asian rollouts of conventional meat blended with plant proteins in 2026 [general, Low, EXP-11, inferred]. Together these point to B2B demand for functional plant proteins in Japan and Korea, not to demand for finished Vietnamese analogues.

**Private label and contract manufacturing.** Brand owners already outsource plant-based production to Southeast Asia: Thailand's NRF describes itself as a listed "global Asian food export platform" making private-label products for major retailers [VN-adjacent, Low, EXP-52, stated], and GFI APAC argued in 2023 that "competitive costs, specialty skills, and relatively free access to Western and Asian markets" make Southeast Asia "a prime candidate for manufacturing scale-up" [VN-adjacent, Medium, ECO-23, stated]. We found no verified case of a Western plant-based brand manufacturing in Vietnam.

**Gaps.** We did not retrieve 2023 to 2026 revealed sales data for China, Singapore, Australia or the Gulf states (Food Frontier's Australian reports were blocked to us). We found no primary data on halal plant-based demand. These are logged as open questions.

## 3. Diaspora and ethnic markets

**Size of the diaspora buyer base.** Pew counted about 2.3 million people of Vietnamese origin in the US in 2023 (60% immigrants), with 350,000 in Los Angeles, 145,000 in San Jose and 140,000 in Houston; 37% of Vietnamese-alone adults are Buddhist [general, High, EXP-35, revealed]. Australia had 326,630 Vietnam-born residents at 30 June 2025 [general, High, EXP-36, revealed]. We did not retrieve European diaspora counts.

**Revealed shelf presence.** On 24 September 2026 a search for "vegetarian vietnamese" on Weee!, a US online Asian grocer, returned 485 results, and the product pages we checked listed these Vietnam-made vegetarian items [VN-direct, High, EXP-37, revealed]:

These are *chay* staples and convenience foods. None is marketed on protein. The last row matters: diaspora firms in the US also make *chay* bases locally, so Vietnam-made goods compete with US-made ones inside the ethnic channel [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-37, revealed].

**Revealed trade through the ethnic channel.** Vietnam's instant noodle exports (USD 268 million in 2023) went to the US (23.6%), Cambodia (19.5%), Germany (8.1%) and Poland (7.2%) [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed]. Germany and Poland have long-established Vietnamese communities, so this pattern is consistent with a diaspora and Asian-grocer channel [VN-direct, Low, EXP-39, inferred]. Sauces and condiments (HS 2103.90, which includes fish sauce and vegetarian sauces) went mainly to Japan and the US [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed]. We found no revealed data on vegan fish sauce exports.

**Design implication.** The diaspora channel already accepts Vietnamese vegetarian products at low price points (USD 3.50 to 9.00 a pack). It could take protein-upgraded *chay* products (for example spring rolls or *chả lụa chay*, vegetarian pork-style sausage, with 10 g or more protein per 100 g) at similar prices, with frozen logistics already in place. It will not absorb volumes large enough to justify a novel-protein plant on its own [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-37; EXP-39, inferred].

**Tariffs.** The EU TARIC measures for Vietnamese origin on 24 September 2026 show zero preferential duty on every plant-based food line we checked, while third countries without an EU agreement pay 7% to 12.8%, plus agricultural components or specific duties on some lines [VN-direct, High, EXP-23, revealed]. The UK tariff shows the same pattern for UKVFTA [VN-direct, High, EXP-24, revealed]. Japan's tariff schedule gives Vietnam duty-free entry for textured vegetable protein and plant drinks, while China still pays 6.6% on textured vegetable protein under RCEP [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-41, revealed]. We read these lines from the customs schedule table programmatically and they should be confirmed line by line before use. TARIC also shows a new EUR 3 low-value consignment customs duty from 1 July 2026, relevant to small parcel exports to diaspora buyers [general, Medium, EXP-23, revealed]. We did not verify Korean (VKFTA, RCEP), Chinese (ACFTA, RCEP) or Gulf tariffs on these lines.

**US tariffs in 2026.** The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on 20 February 2026; the administration imposed a 10% Section 122 tariff on most imports from 24 February 2026 for 150 days, which the Court of International Trade ruled unlawful for three plaintiffs on 7 May 2026 [general, High, EXP-21, revealed]. USTR's final Section 301 action, effective 24 July 2026, imposes 12.5% on Vietnamese goods, with exemptions in Annexes I and II that include cashews (0801.31, 0801.32) and coconuts (0801.11 to 0801.19) [VN-direct, High, EXP-22, revealed]. Earlier, the November 2025 agricultural exemption covered coffee, tea, tropical fruit and juices, cocoa, spices, bananas, oranges, tomatoes and beef [general, Medium, EXP-20, revealed].

**Novel food rules.** A Vietnamese plant-based product made from soy, wheat gluten, cashew, coconut, rice, mushrooms or jackfruit uses ingredients with a long history of use and needs no pre-market approval in the EU or Singapore; a new fungal strain, a precision-fermented protein or cultivated meat does [general, Medium, EXP-46; EXP-34, inferred]. The EU lets a business consult the member state where it plans to launch when novel status is unclear [general, High, EXP-46, revealed]. This matters because the supply study found no novel-food route in Vietnam itself: an exporter would have to file its first dossier abroad.

**Labels.** The EU's March 2026 agreement restricts 32 terms: meat, beef, veal, pork, poultry, chicken, turkey, duck, goose, lamb, mutton, ovine, goat, drumstick, tenderloin, sirloin, flank, loin, steak, ribs, shoulder, shank, chop, wing, breast, liver, thigh, brisket, ribeye, T-bone, rump and bacon [general, High, EXP-25, revealed]. It took effect on 19 August 2026, but members of the European Parliament tabled new amendments days later to restrict "burger", "sausage" and blended products; a committee vote is due in December 2026 and a plenary vote in early 2027 [general, High, EXP-25, revealed]. A Vietnamese exporter should therefore design EU packs around descriptive names ("soy protein strips", "shiitake dumplings") rather than meat names.

**Deforestation rules.** EUDR covers soy and some derived products; application starts on 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators and 30 June 2027 for micro and small ones, and the July 2026 scope update removed some products and added soluble coffee, some palm derivatives and frozen cattle tongues from 30 December 2027 [general, High, EXP-27; EXP-28, revealed]. The update did not add soy protein or food preparations [general, Medium, EXP-28, revealed]. We did not retrieve Annex I itself; our reading is that Vietnamese plant-based foods made from imported soy protein are outside EUDR unless they are exported as soybeans, soy flour, soy oil or soy cake, which should be confirmed [general, Low, EXP-28, inferred]. The stronger pressure on imported soy comes from private buyer standards on feed (section 6).

**Halal.** Au Lac claims halal certification [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-38, stated], and the supply study's company register records a halal claim by An Nhien (CO-030). Malaysia declared cultivated meat halal in December 2025, the first Muslim-majority country to do so [general, Medium, EXP-11, revealed]. Vietnam already ships cashew kernels to the UAE (USD 98 million) and Turkey (USD 115 million) and desiccated coconut to Egypt, Algeria and Saudi Arabia (2023) [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed], so buyers in Muslim-majority markets already source Vietnamese plant ingredients. We found no data on halal plant-based meat demand.

## 5. B2B export demand for ingredients

**Textured and concentrated plant protein.** Revealed trade says Vietnam is a buyer, not a seller: USD 0.52 million of HS 2106.10 exports in 2023 [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed]. The US recorded USD 1.45 million (195 t) of HS 2106.10 imports from Vietnam in 2024, at about USD 7.40 per kg against about USD 3.97 per kg from China, which suggests finished vegetarian products rather than bulk ingredient [VN-direct, Low, EXP-40, inferred]. The EU recorded only USD 31,521 from Vietnam against USD 35.8 million from China [VN-direct, High, EXP-40, revealed]. China's exports of the same code were USD 380 million in 2024 at about USD 2.13 per kg [general, High, EXP-40, revealed]. A Vietnamese textured protein made from imported soy would compete with Chinese product at Chinese prices, and its only edge would be tariffs in the EU, UK and Japan (section 4) [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-40; EXP-23, inferred].

**Rice protein.** We found no Vietnamese rice protein exports in trade data (rice protein has no separate HS code) and no Vietnamese producer. Vietnam exports rice flour worth USD 13 million (2023), mainly to the Philippines and the US [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed]. Global rice protein market sizes come from market-research firms without disclosed methods; we did not use them.

**Cassava.** China bought 93.5% of Vietnam's USD 1.06 billion cassava starch exports in 2023 [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed]. The export demand is for starch, not protein or fermentation products; converting starch to protein at home would redirect an existing export, not meet a new foreign demand [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-39, inferred].

**Mushrooms and microalgae.** Vietnam's dried mushroom (0712.39) and prepared mushroom (2003.90) exports were USD 0.81 million and USD 7.5 million in 2023, 86 to 88% to the US [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed]. We found no trade data that separates spirulina or other microalgae. Neither is an established export demand.

**Industrial fermentation products.** Vietnam exported USD 106 million (about 62,900 t, weight estimated) of glutamic acid and its salts, mainly MSG, in 2023, 46% to Japan and 20% to the US; lysine exports were only USD 1.3 million [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed]. Export-scale fermentation on starch and molasses already exists and already meets Japanese buyers' specifications, which is relevant for biomass fermentation proteins, but it sells amino acids and flavour, not protein [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-39, inferred]. Vietnam also exported USD 13.3 million of active yeasts (mainly to Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand) and USD 11.2 million of inactive yeasts and dead single-cell micro-organisms (HS 2102.20, the closest code to single-cell protein) to Japan, the US, Singapore and Finland in 2023 [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed].

**Marine by-product proteins.** HS 3504 exports of USD 13.0 million in 2023 to Spain, the UK, Korea, Denmark and the Netherlands [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed] show that European ingredient buyers already accept Vietnamese protein ingredients from certified seafood chains. This is the nearest existing template for a B2B novel protein export.

**Japan and Korea as ingredient buyers.** Japan's plant protein imports (35,059 t, USD 149 million in 2022) and its use of soy protein in processed and hybrid meats [general, Medium, EXP-07, revealed], together with Vietnam's duty-free access for textured vegetable protein [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-41, revealed], make Japanese food manufacturers the most plausible first foreign buyers of a Vietnamese functional plant or fungal protein, if it meets Japanese specifications [VN-adjacent, Low, EXP-07; EXP-41, inferred].

## 6. Seafood buyers as a demand signal for novel feed protein

**What buyers actually require.** The ASC Feed Standard asks certified feed mills to (a) publicly commit to deforestation- and conversion-free supply chains for all plant ingredients, with cutoff dates no later than 31 December 2020 for soy and palm; (b) move the majority volume of whole-fish marine ingredients up one sustainability level every three years, with a stated long-term aim of 100% MSC-certified or equivalent sources; and (c) calculate greenhouse gas emissions in kg CO2 equivalent per tonne of feed, report them annually and disclose species-specific figures to ASC-certified farms on request [general, High, EXP-29, revealed]. These are the version 1.1 requirements; version 1.2, released on 19 December 2025, has been mandatory since 2 February 2026 and we did not read its text [general, High, EXP-30, revealed]. The standard does not require novel ingredients; it makes soy traceability, fishmeal sourcing and carbon footprints visible to farms and their buyers [general, High, EXP-29, inferred].

**Vietnam's exposure.** VASEP reported in September 2026 that 20 Vietnamese feed mills take part in the ASC programme (13 certified, 7 in assessment), out of 148 mills in 34 countries, and that Vietnam is the largest producer of ASC-certified pangasius and the third largest of ASC-certified shrimp [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-32, revealed].

**Retailer commitments.** The UK Soy Manifesto commits 51 signatories, including Tesco, Sainsbury's, Marks & Spencer, Waitrose, Morrisons, Co-op, Aldi, Lidl, Iceland and Ocado and seafood firms Seafresh Group and Lyons Seafoods, to deforestation- and conversion-free soy with a 2020 or earlier cutoff, fully implemented by the end of 2025, covering soy embedded in animal and aquaculture feed for UK products [general, High, EXP-33, revealed]. Vietnamese shrimp and pangasius sold in UK supermarkets therefore need traceable feed soy [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-33, inferred]. We did not retrieve comparable feed requirements from Japanese buyers (Aeon's public pages did not state them) or from US retailers.

**Revealed buying of novel feed protein.** Vinh Hoan, which describes itself as the largest producer of pangasius [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-49, stated], agreed in February 2024 to take at least 15,000 t of insect protein from Entobel for pangasius feed over 2025 to 2027 [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-50, revealed]. We cite this as an incumbent benchmark only: it shows that one export-facing buyer is willing to contract a non-soy, non-fishmeal feed protein at scale, which is the demand a Vietnamese single-cell or fungal feed protein would have to win.

**What this means.** The export pull for novel feed protein is indirect. Buyers reward traceability, lower deforestation risk and lower feed carbon footprints; a domestically made microbial or fungal protein from Vietnamese starch or by-products would score well on all three, but only if it is priced near soybean meal or fishmeal, because feed mills compete on cost and the fastest-growing seafood buyer, China, is less standards-driven [VN-direct, Low, EXP-29; EXP-31; EXP-33, inferred]. We found no buyer in any market that pays a documented price premium for seafood raised on novel feed protein.

**Feed exports as a channel.** Vietnam exported USD 350 million of feed preparations (HS 2309.90) in 2023, a third to Malaysia and most of the rest to Cambodia, the Philippines, Taiwan and Thailand, and USD 430 million of fishmeal, 93.5% to China [VN-direct, High, EXP-39, revealed]. A domestic novel feed protein would therefore have two export routes: inside compound feed sold to neighbouring countries, and inside seafood sold to standards-driven buyers. Both are price-led [VN-direct, Low, EXP-39; EXP-29, inferred].

**Alternative seafood.** We found no retrieved evidence that major importers of Vietnamese seafood ask for plant-based or cultivated seafood. The only market data we found point the other way (German plant-based seafood sales down 22% in 2025) [general, High, EXP-05, revealed].

**Thailand.** Thailand's future food exports were THB 144 billion in 2023, ranked 12th in the world, but 89% were functional foods rather than alternative protein [VN-adjacent, Medium, EXP-13, revealed]. In January 2025, alternative protein exports were THB 384 million, and future food exports went 50% to ASEAN, 21% to China, 18% to the US and 10% to the EU [VN-adjacent, Low, EXP-12, revealed]. Thai trade officials put alternative protein exports at about THB 4.5 billion in 2021 and projected THB 5.67 billion for 2024 [VN-adjacent, Low, EXP-15, stated]. Thailand has no EU trade agreement in force, so its plant-based foods pay 9 to 12.8% more in the EU than Vietnamese ones [VN-adjacent, High, EXP-47; EXP-23, revealed].

**What would make Vietnam a preferred supplier** (our synthesis from the sources above, [VN-direct, Low, EXP-23; EXP-24; EXP-41; EXP-29; EXP-22, inferred]):
- For EU and UK private-label buyers: zero tariffs (a 6 to 13 point margin over Thailand and China), seafood-grade food safety and audit culture (ASC, BRC-type certifications), frozen logistics already used by seafood exporters, and simple-label products with protein claims.
- For Japanese and Korean manufacturers: duty-free functional plant or fungal proteins made to their specifications, especially for hybrid meat products.
- For seafood buyers: a traceable, deforestation-free, low-carbon domestic feed protein that helps Vietnamese exporters meet ASC and UK Soy Manifesto requirements without paying more.
- Against: the US 12.5% tariff, no domestic protein ingredient base, no novel-food approvals abroad, and Vietnam's reliance on imported soy and gluten for every high-protein local product (supply study retail audit, FORM-01).

## Implications for frontier actors

**Founders.** Do not start with branded plant-based meat for the US: demand is falling and Vietnamese goods now pay an extra 12.5% Section 301 tariff, more than Malaysian, Indonesian or Indian goods. The best-evidenced first export products are (a) private-label or co-packed plant-based foods for EU and UK retailers that use the zero-tariff lines (dumplings, spring rolls, strips, cashew or coconut drinks and spreads), priced near private-label parity and named without restricted meat or dairy terms; (b) protein-upgraded *chay* for the diaspora channel at USD 3.50 to 9.00 per pack; (c) B2B functional plant or fungal protein for Japanese and Korean manufacturers. Plant-based seafood for export is the weakest bet on current demand evidence.

**R&D bodies and universities.** Work that raises protein content in Vietnamese formats (spring rolls, *chả* (Vietnamese sausages and patties), dumplings, noodles) to 10 g per 100 g or more without imported textured soy would serve both the domestic and diaspora markets. For feed, the export-relevant test is not growth performance alone but documented traceability and a per-tonne carbon footprint that ASC feed mills can report.

**Investors and development finance.** Export demand justifies processing and co-packing capacity with EU and Japanese food-safety certification more than it justifies a greenfield branded play. Price the US tariff risk explicitly. Novel-protein ventures aimed at export need a budget and timeline for EU novel food or Singapore approval, since Vietnam has no route of its own.

**Funders and philanthropies.** Fund the missing evidence: a buyer survey of EU and UK private-label and seafood buyers on plant-based sourcing and feed requirements; a first EU novel food or Singapore dossier for a Vietnamese fungal or single-cell protein; a feed carbon-footprint benchmark for Vietnamese shrimp and pangasius feed.

**Policy makers.** Protect and use the EVFTA, UKVFTA, CPTPP and Japanese preferences, which are worth 6 to 13 percentage points on plant-based lines. Help exporters with origin rules for products made from imported soy protein (we did not verify whether they qualify for preference). Build a domestic novel-food route that mirrors EU and Singapore evidence requirements so that one dossier can serve several markets. Support ASC-ready feed traceability as an export-competitiveness measure.

**Food and feed manufacturers.** Cashew and seafood processors already own the buyers, certifications and cold chain that an export plant-based or hybrid line needs. Feed mills supplying ASC-certified farms will face growing requests for soy traceability and carbon data; a domestic, non-soy protein is one answer, but only at soy-meal-like prices.

**What not to do.** Do not size a Vietnamese export business on global plant-based market forecasts; do not use meat or dairy names on EU packs; do not plan US exports on pre-2026 tariff assumptions; do not assume seafood buyers will pay a premium for novel feed.

## What would change this view

- A reversal in US plant-based meat sales (two consecutive years of unit growth in SPINS or Circana data), or a US tariff deal that removes the 12.5% Section 301 rate for Vietnam.
- An EU and Thailand FTA in force, or the EU and Indonesia CEPA entering into force, which would erode Vietnam's EU tariff edge.
- A final EU decision to extend the name ban to "burger" and "sausage" (committee vote December 2026), which would raise relabelling costs.
- An ASC farm-level requirement or a major retailer policy (UK, EU or Japan) that explicitly sets targets for novel or non-soy feed proteins, or a documented price premium for seafood raised on such feed.
- Revealed evidence of Western brands contracting plant-based production in Vietnam, or of Japanese or Korean manufacturers sourcing Vietnamese plant protein.
- Vietnamese 2024 and 2025 Comtrade data showing growth in HS 2106.10, 2106.90 or 3504 exports.
