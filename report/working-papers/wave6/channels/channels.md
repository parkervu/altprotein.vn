# Where protein is bought and eaten in Vietnam: retail, foodservice, institutions and tourism, and which channels could carry novel protein first

Agent: CHANNELS (wave 6). Source prefix: CHN. Date: 24 September 2026.

Scope: the channels through which Vietnamese households, workers, pupils and visitors obtain protein, how large and how concentrated each channel is, what a new protein product must survive to enter it, and which channel is the most plausible first route for five product archetypes. Demand is treated as a design input. Tags follow the Wave 6 brief: `[evidence label, confidence, source ID, demand evidence type]`. "Our calculation" marks every derived number. VND is converted at 26,000 VND per USD where stated.

> **Method note.** 58 sources were read (USDA FAS GAIN reports in full, a Q&Me store enumeration in full, company results, government portals, Vietnamese press, peer-reviewed papers via Scite and OpenAlex). The session's web search budget ran out part way through, and HappyCow, the USDA HRI sub-sector attachment and a Vietcap sector report could not be retrieved. Those gaps are logged in `open_questions.csv` rather than filled from memory.

## Headline findings

1. **Most food money still moves through traditional trade, but the shift to modern grocery is now fast.** Euromonitor puts traditional channels (small grocers and *chợ*, fresh-food markets) at 83% of USD 55.5 billion food retail sales in 2024, supermarkets 9%, hypermarkets 3%, food and drink specialists 3%, convenience 1% and food e-commerce about 1% [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-01, revealed]. WinCommerce's chief executive says modern trade rose from 12% to 14% of grocery in one year, 2024 to 2025, a shift that used to take four years [VN-direct, Low, CHN-15, revealed]. The 2025 tax and invoicing reform is expected to favour modern trade further [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-05, inferred].

2. **Two chains now form the gate to modern grocery.** Bach Hoa Xanh had 3,612 stores at the end of August 2026 and WinCommerce 5,141 at mid-2026, each opening about 1,000 stores a year, now mostly in rural areas and the north [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-12, CHN-15, revealed]. Their 2025 revenues were about VND 46,900 billion and VND 39,000 billion, together roughly USD 3.3 billion (our calculation) [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-10, CHN-14, revealed]. Fresh food was about 40% of Bach Hoa Xanh sales in Q3 2024 [VN-direct, Low, CHN-13, revealed].

3. **Fresh meat is still a wet-market product.** About 80% of fresh meat value is sold through traditional channels (Euromonitor, cited by B&Company, year not stated) [VN-direct, Low, CHN-18, revealed], and a systematic review reports that over 95% of pork retailers sell at wet markets [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-19, revealed]. A chilled or frozen meat alternative cannot follow meat into the *chợ*. It will be sold where the cold chain already exists (modern frozen sections, where *đồ chay*, traditional vegetarian food, already sits) or through kitchens.

4. **Foodservice is large, fragmented and churning.** Euromonitor values it at USD 26.6 billion in 2024 across about 323,000 outlets: full-service restaurants 69% of sales from 121,800 outlets, cafés and bars 18%, street stalls and kiosks 10% of sales from about 152,000 outlets, limited-service (fast food) 3% from about 11,000 [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-03, revealed]. More than 50,000 operators closed in the first half of 2025 [VN-direct, Low, CHN-03, revealed].

5. **Chains have trialled meat-free items for eight years and none has disclosed a success.** KFC Vietnam sold a *chay* (vegetarian) chicken burger at VND 39,000 for lunar July 2018 [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-22, revealed]; Starbucks ran OmniFoods items in summer 2023 (supply study, ECO-20); Lotteria launched "Burger Zero Meat" with a vegetable patty at VND 39,000 in seven cities including Nha Trang in July 2025 [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-24, revealed]. The pattern is price parity with core items, lunar-calendar timing, and no published sales outcome.

6. **Industrial canteens are the largest price-set protein channel and the least studied.** About 4.15 million people worked in industrial parks and economic zones at end-2023 [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-37, revealed]. Shift meals (*bữa ăn ca*) now cost VND 25,000 to 35,000 in foreign-invested factories in Hai Phong, against a union floor of VND 20,000 [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-39, revealed]. The contract catering market is about USD 2 billion, food is 60% to 80% of the meal price, and the largest caterers serve 30,000 to 120,000 meals a day [VN-direct, Low, CHN-41, revealed]. Up to about 1.2 billion factory meals a year is our upper-bound calculation [VN-direct, Low, CHN-37, inferred].

7. **School meals are large, price-capped and in a trust crisis.** Hanoi subsidises lunch for about 768,000 primary pupils at VND 20,000 to 30,000 a day in 2025 to 2026, a VND 3,063 billion budget [VN-direct, High, CHN-29, revealed]. Its fee ceiling of VND 35,000 is proposed to rise to VND 50,000 [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-28, revealed]. In April 2026 about 300 tonnes of African swine fever pork reached 26 Hanoi public schools and private kindergartens [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-36, revealed], and the Prime Minister's dispatch 64/CĐ-TTg of 13 September 2026 requires traceability and bars selecting suppliers on lowest price alone [VN-direct, High, CHN-30].

8. **The school milk programme shows how state-driven demand works, and it points towards animal protein.** Decision 1340/QĐ-TTg (8 July 2016) set a target to raise animal protein above 40% of total protein in preschool and primary diets by 2020 [VN-direct, High, CHN-33]. Hanoi's tender (VND 3,828 billion for 2019 to 2020; VND 6,286 per 180 ml box; state 30%, supplier 23%, parents 47%; milk made entirely in Vietnam) is the template: a nutrition rationale, a domestic-production condition and cost sharing [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-34, revealed]. A plant protein product pitched as replacing animal protein in schools would run against the stated policy target.

9. **Tourism is at a record, with the mix moving towards Russia, India and Europe.** Vietnam received 21.17 million international visitors in 2025 (up 20.4%) and 15.9 million in the first eight months of 2026 (up 14.4%), against a 2026 target of 25 million [VN-direct, High, CHN-45, CHN-44, revealed]. China was flat and Korea down 5.2% in 2026, while Russia rose 166% to over 1 million and India 38% to 612,000 [VN-direct, High, CHN-45, revealed].

10. **The Nha Trang HoReCa hypothesis is weak on volume and untested on demand.** Khanh Hoa received 5.5 million international visitors in 2025 and has 70,500 rooms, over 40% of them four and five star [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-47, revealed]. But its visitors come mainly from Korea (about 20 flights a day in March 2026), Russia and China [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-48, revealed], not from India, whose direct flights land in Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City and Da Nang [VN-direct, Low, CHN-52, revealed]. Our rough calculation gives 1 to 2 million vegetarian or vegan meal occasions a year from international visitors to Khanh Hoa, two orders of magnitude below Hanoi's subsidised primary school lunches alone [VN-direct, Low, CHN-47, inferred]. We found no direct HoReCa evidence either way; the September 2026 audit covered retail only (FORM-01).

11. **Channel economics are mostly undocumented.** We found no retrievable source on Vietnamese listing fees, retailer margins or distributor margins. The only figures are from catering: food at 60% to 80% of meal value and informal commissions of VND 500 to 1,000 per meal paid to clients' purchasing staff [VN-direct, Low, CHN-41, revealed]. The supply study already found retail prices of dried soy analogues at 5 to 17 times their ex-factory protein cost (supply study, appendix I).

12. **First routes differ by product.** A textured plant protein ingredient enters through B2B buyers (the *đồ chay* and processed-meat makers, then contract caterers); a higher-protein *chay* product through modern frozen sections and lunar-calendar foodservice; a premium analogue through premium HoReCa and import-led retail in Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi; a hybrid through integrated meat-and-retail groups (Masan's MEATDeli in WinCommerce, C.P. Vietnam's more than 1,000 shops) and canteens; fermentation or cultivated products later, as ingredients and showcase dishes [VN-direct, Low, CHN-12, CHN-15, CHN-17, CHN-41, FORM-01, inferred]. Schools and wet markets are not first routes for anything novel.

13. **The evidence base is structural, not behavioural.** Almost everything above is revealed structure (store counts, prices, arrivals, budgets). There is no tested evidence in any Vietnamese channel on acceptance of a novel protein, and no disclosed outcome from any chain trial. The cheapest next step is channel trials, not more desk research [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-22, CHN-24, inferred].

### 1.3 Fresh meat and the wet market

- Traditional trade holds "around 80%" of the fresh meat market and modern retail "only 20%"; premium traceable meat is "about 10%" (Euromonitor as cited by B&Company, June 2025, year of data not stated) [VN-direct, Low, CHN-18, revealed].
- "Over 95% of retailers sell fresh pork at the wet market" (Unger et al. 2018, cited in a 2020 systematic review of Vietnamese pork value chain studies) [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-19, revealed]. Consumers buy from acquaintance retailers whom they consider safe [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-19, stated].
- In Dien Bien, almost 90% of Kinh and Thai respondents preferred buying beef in wet markets rather than convenience stores or supermarkets, for fresh, warm meat [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-20, stated]. This is a rural northern province; urban shares will be lower.
- In northern Vietnam, "modern urban" pork consumers trusted wet markets and street vendors least, while "traditional urban" consumers trusted them more [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-58, stated].
- The modern exceptions are vertically integrated: C.P. Vietnam runs over 1,000 shops (C.P. Fresh Shop, Fresh Mart, Five Star), MEATDeli chilled pork is sold in WinMart and WinMart+ [VN-direct, Low, CHN-17, revealed], and fresh food made up about 40% of Bach Hoa Xanh sales in Q3 2024 [VN-direct, Low, CHN-13, revealed].

Implication: the wet market is where protein is bought, but it is not a channel a packaged, chilled or frozen alternative can use at scale today. The one wet-market route open to plant protein is the one it already uses: dry and ambient *đồ chay* ingredients (dried textured soy, tofu skin, gluten) sold by market stalls to households and street vendors. We found no data on that trade's volume (open question).

### 1.4 Cold chain, freezers and frozen food

- Only one data point on home refrigeration was retrieved: 59.0% of households used a refrigerator in the 2014 living standards survey (GSO, 2015, as quoted in Ha-Duong 2021) [VN-direct, Low, CHN-21, revealed]. This is twelve years old, measures refrigerators not freezers, and must not be used as a current figure. We found no current chest-freezer ownership figure.
- GAIN reports that frozen categories "are expanding in both quantity and variety" in supermarkets as consumers seek ready-to-cook and ready-to-eat products [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-01, revealed], but give no frozen share of retail. We found no frozen-food share.
- Lack of cold storage and high logistics costs restrict imported food products "to food service outlets in third-tier and smaller cities" [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-03, revealed].
- The September 2026 retail audit found dedicated frozen *đồ chay* sections of two or more door units in Lotte Mart and Co.opmart Nha Trang, multi-brand (SG Food, Vissan, CJ Cau Tre, bibigo, private label) (FORM-01).

Implication: a frozen format is viable in the modern chains that already hold frozen *chay*, and nowhere else. Formats that need no cold chain (dry textured protein, retort pouches, canned) are the only way into the 650,000 traditional outlets.

### 1.5 Online grocery and quick commerce

- Food e-commerce was about 1% of food retail value in 2024, growing 29% [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-01, revealed].
- Bach Hoa Xanh's online channel earned about VND 2,300 billion in 2025, nearly 5% of its revenue, up 150% [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-10, revealed].
- Food delivery spending reached USD 1.4 billion in 2023, up 30% (Momentum Works, via GAIN) [VN-direct, Low, CHN-04, revealed]. KFC sells through virtual stores on ShopeeFood and GrabFood and via TikTok livestreams [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-04, revealed].
- The platforms named by GAIN are Shopee, Lazada, Tiki, Sendo and TikTok Shop, and GrabMart, ShopeeFood, AhaMove and Be for delivery [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-01, revealed]. We found no category data for frozen or protein foods on these platforms.

E-commerce is a test and premium channel, not a volume channel, for a protein product before 2030 [VN-direct, Low, CHN-01, inferred].

## 3. Institutions: canteens, schools, hospitals, military

### 3.1 Industrial canteens (*suất ăn công nghiệp*, industrial catering)

- **Workers.** About 4.15 million workers in industrial parks and economic zones at end-2023; 416 industrial parks, 296 operating [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-37, revealed]. We found no 2025 update.
- **Meal price.** Hai Phong unions set a floor of VND 20,000 per shift meal; typical prices are VND 25,000 to 30,000, and Toyota Boshoku, Toyoda Gosei and Sumiden now pay VND 30,000 to 35,000, up from VND 23,000 to 25,000 [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-39, revealed]. In northern garment firms (Duc Giang, Hung Yen Garment) and the Ho Guom group, VND 17,000 to 25,000 is typical, with proposals of VND 25,000 to 29,000; Ho Guom moved from VND 17,000 (2024) to 20,000 (2025) to 25,000 (2026) [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-38, revealed]. The 2016 minimum was VND 15,000 and the 2022 recommendation VND 18,000 to 20,000 [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-38, revealed].
- **Menu.** A typical Hai Phong factory meal is two to three main dishes, one vegetable dish, a soup and dessert [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-39, revealed]. We found no protein portion standard.
- **Delivery model.** About 62% of unionised firms in Hai Phong use collective kitchens, contracted caterers, vouchers or cash [VN-direct, Low, CHN-39, revealed]. Employer-organised meals are fully exempt from personal income tax; cash meal allowances are exempt up to VND 1.2 million a month from 1 July 2026 (Decree 253/2026/NĐ-CP) [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-40].
- **Caterers.** The contract catering market is about USD 2 billion a year, growing 7%, projected at about VND 52,600 billion in 2026. Vietnam EOC serves about 120,000 meals a day, Hoang Kim 50,000, Vinastory 38,000, Dussmann and Thao Ha about 30,000 each; a "standard" meal is VND 29,000, the range VND 20,000 to 50,000; food is 60% to 80% of the meal value; suppliers pay VND 500 to 1,000 per meal in "commissions" to clients' purchasing staff [VN-direct, Low, CHN-41, revealed]. The market remains fragmented with "hundreds of suppliers" and education as its largest pillar (B&Company, September 2025) [VN-direct, Low, CHN-35, revealed].

**Order of magnitude (our calculation).** 4.15 million workers × about 300 meal days = about 1.2 billion shift meals a year, worth VND 31,000 to 44,000 billion (USD 1.2 to 1.7 billion) at VND 25,000 to 35,000 a meal [VN-direct, Low, CHN-37, CHN-39, inferred]. This is an upper bound: some firms pay cash instead, and workers outside industrial parks are not counted. It is consistent with a USD 2 billion catering market that also serves schools and offices.

**What a protein ingredient must hit (our calculation).** With food at 60% to 80% of a VND 25,000 to 35,000 meal, the food budget is VND 15,000 to 28,000 per meal. If the protein dish takes about half (assumption), it has VND 7,500 to 14,000. At the retail benchmark of VND 34,000 to 46,000 per 100 g of protein for eggs and chicken breast (supply study, COST-39 to COST-41), 20 g of protein costs VND 6,800 to 9,200. The supply study's indicative ex-factory cost of textured plant protein, VND 3,700 to 10,700 per 100 g of protein, puts 20 g at VND 740 to 2,140. Retail textured or gluten analogues at VND 112,000 per 100 g of protein (FORM-01) put it at VND 22,400, above the whole protein budget. So the route is an ingredient sold to caterers and manufacturers, not a branded analogue sold at retail prices [VN-direct, Low, CHN-41, inferred].

### 3.2 Schools (*bán trú*, school lunch and midday care)

- **Scale.** About 15.5 million primary and lower secondary pupils; most primary pupils eat lunch at school [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-31, revealed]. B&Company counts over 25 million students and about 26,000 public schools in 2024 to 2025 [VN-direct, Low, CHN-35, revealed]. We found no national count of pupils eating *bán trú* (open question).
- **Hanoi, 2025 to 2026.** 703 schools (90.4%) provide *bán trú*. The city subsidises about 768,000 primary pupils (707,727 public, 60,273 private) at VND 20,000 a day, or VND 30,000 in mountainous and island areas, for up to nine months, a budget of about VND 3,063 billion; the minimum meal cost is VND 30,000 and parents pay any difference [VN-direct, High, CHN-29, revealed]. That is about 100 to 150 million subsidised pupil meals a year (our calculation: budget divided by VND 20,000 to 30,000).
- **Price ceilings.** Hanoi's current ceiling is VND 35,000 for lunch and VND 20,000 for breakfast; the city proposed VND 50,000 and VND 25,000 (preschool VND 40,000) on 23 September 2026, to cover labour, equipment, transport and quality assurance [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-28, revealed]. Public school meals nationally run VND 25,000 to 40,000, with a minimum of VND 30,000 in major cities; international schools charge VND 120,000 to 140,000 a day for three meals [VN-direct, Low, CHN-35, revealed].
- **Delivery.** Schools either run their own kitchens or receive packed meals from industrial caterers [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-31, revealed].
- **National policy.** The National Assembly agreed in June 2025 to fund lunches for primary and lower secondary pupils in land-border communes from 2025 to 2026 [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-32].
- **Trust shock.** In April 2026 police broke a chain that relabelled pork from pigs with African swine fever as clean; about 300 tonnes reached 26 Hanoi public schools through Hanoi Industrial Meal Service Company and private kindergartens through a second caterer; over 2,900 schools reviewed their supply chains and were ordered to publish ingredient sources [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-36, revealed].
- **A wider supplier-trust problem.** In January 2026 a food-safety case at Ha Long Canned Food (Canfoco) forced Highlands Coffee and The Coffee House to state which of their products used its ingredients; The Coffee House withdrew two drinks and changed supplier [VN-direct, Low, CHN-57, revealed]. Chains and schools are both now auditing suppliers, which raises the documentation bar for any new ingredient maker.
- **Rules after the shock.** Dispatch 64/CĐ-TTg (13 September 2026) requires public, transparent procurement "not based solely on lowest price", suppliers that prove food-safety capability and traceability, and menus with adequate energy and balanced nutrients suited to age, school hours and meal budget [VN-direct, High, CHN-30].

### 3.3 The school milk precedent

- Decision 1340/QĐ-TTg (8 July 2016) set targets to 2020: 100% of preschool and primary children in poor districts and 70% elsewhere; energy needs met at 90% to 95%; and "increase the ratio of animal protein to total protein in the diet of preschool and primary children to over 40%" [VN-direct, High, CHN-33].
- Funding was to come from enterprises, organisations and individuals, family and community contributions, and local budgets [VN-direct, High, CHN-33].
- Hanoi's tender (November 2018) went to Vinamilk: over VND 3,828 billion for 2019 to 2020, VND 6,286 per 180 ml box of pasteurised milk "entirely manufactured in Vietnam", cost split state 30% (50% for disadvantaged families), Vinamilk 23% (above the required 20%), parents about 47%; it covered over 90% of preschool and primary children in the city [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-34, revealed].

What it teaches: state-driven demand for a protein food in Vietnam has come with (1) a stated nutrition target, (2) a domestic manufacturing condition, (3) a supplier co-payment and (4) a public tender that one large incumbent won. The stated target favoured animal protein. A novel protein could only enter a programme like this through a protein-quality and cost argument (more protein per VND at equal quality), probably as a blend in a familiar food, and after food-safety trust is re-established.

### 3.4 Hospitals, military and procurement rules

We found no retrievable source, within this session, on hospital patient meal budgets, the military daily ration value or military food procurement (open questions). Dispatch 64/CĐ-TTg is the only procurement rule we read directly [VN-direct, High, CHN-30]; we did not read the Law on Bidding text. These channels are price-set and centrally specified, like schools, and deserve a direct enquiry to the Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Defence logistics general department.

### 4.3 Vegetarian, vegan and halal demand by market

- **India.** 39% of Indians say they are vegetarian, varying from 8% to 92% by religion and 9% to 90% by region [general, Medium, CHN-53, stated]. - **Western markets.** Nationally representative surveys put vegetarians at 1.3% and vegans at 0.3% of Canadians (2015), with other estimates of 2.5% in Germany (2014) and 4.0% in the United States (2012) [general, Medium, CHN-54, stated].
- **Korea, China, Russia.** We found no robust recent prevalence figure for these markets in this session. Nothing we read suggests that vegetarian demand is a defining feature of these visitors; Soha's account of Russian visitors to Nha Trang mentions mud baths and local cuisine, not dietary needs [VN-direct, Low, CHN-50, stated].
- **Halal.** Malaysia (573,000 in 2025) and Indonesia (growing 26.7% in 2026) are the Muslim-majority markets [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-46, CHN-51, revealed]. We found no count of halal-certified restaurants or hotel kitchens (open question). ### 4.4 The Nha Trang HoReCa hypothesis: evidence for and against

The project's hypothesis is that Nha Trang's tourist-driven hotels and restaurants are a channel for plant-based products.

**For.**
- Scale and quality of hospitality: 5.5 million international visitors, 70,500 rooms, over 40% four and five star [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-47, revealed]. International hotels run buffets that must offer vegetarian options (our inference, untested).
- Lotteria chose Nha Trang as one of seven launch cities for its meat-free burger [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-24, revealed].
- International growth is strong (up 29% in early 2026) and increasingly European and Russian [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-48, CHN-49, revealed].
- Domestic tourists (about 10.9 million, our calculation from CHN-47) include many who eat *chay* on lunar days; this domestic *chay* demand may be larger than foreign vegetarian demand (inferred, untested).

**Against.**
- The international mix is Korean, Chinese and Russian, not Indian or Western vegetarian [VN-direct, Medium, CHN-48, CHN-49, revealed]; Indian flights go elsewhere [VN-direct, Low, CHN-52, revealed].
- Volume is small: about 1 to 2 million vegetarian or vegan meal occasions a year from international visitors (our calculation), against about 100 to 150 million subsidised school lunches in Hanoi primary schools alone and up to about 1.2 billion factory meals nationally.
- Volatility: China flat and Korea down in 2026 [VN-direct, High, CHN-45, revealed]; the top market can move by a third in a year.
- The September 2026 audit found no modern plant-based analogues in Nha Trang modern retail; the nearest thing was Chinese canned soy "meat" (FORM-01). There is no local supply base for HoReCa to draw on.
- Imported premium analogues cost VND 542,000 to 622,000 per 100 g of protein (FORM-01), which only a premium hotel menu can absorb.

**Verdict.** Not supported as a volume channel; plausible as a small showcase and test market for premium analogues and higher-protein *chay* dishes in four and five star hotels [VN-direct, Low, CHN-47, CHN-48, inferred]. The hypothesis is testable cheaply: interview 20 hotel and restaurant F&B managers in Nha Trang and audit 30 menus (open question, High).

## 7. Implications for frontier actors

**Founders.**
- Sell to kitchens and factories before shelves. The largest price-set protein demand is in factory canteens (up to about 1.2 billion meals a year) and in manufacturers that already buy imported textured soy and gluten. A textured protein ingredient at about VND 740 to 2,140 per 20 g of protein (supply study cost, our calculation) fits a canteen protein budget of VND 7,500 to 14,000 per meal; a branded retail analogue at VND 22,400 per 20 g does not.
- If you make a consumer product, design it for the frozen *chay* section of Bach Hoa Xanh and WinCommerce: at or below the meat price, 10 g or more protein per 100 g, familiar formats. Two listings reach about 8,700 stores.
- Price meat-free foodservice items at parity (VND 39,000 at QSR) and time launches to *rằm* and *mùng một* (full moon and first day of the lunar month) and to *Vu Lan*.
- Do not build a business on Nha Trang tourists. Use hotels there as a showcase if you already supply Ho Chi Minh City.

**R&D bodies and universities.**
- Work on bulk-cooking functionality (holding at temperature for two hours, reheating, broth absorption) and on dry, shelf-stable formats that can move through the 650,000 traditional outlets without a cold chain.
- Develop hybrid formulations for the dishes canteens actually serve (braised pork, meatballs, stuffed tofu), with protein and cost per portion as the design targets.
- Run the first tested evidence: blind canteen trials with a caterer.

**Investors and development finance.**
- Channel access in Vietnam is concentrated: two grocery chains (MWG, Masan), a few integrated meat groups (Masan, C.P., Vissan), a few caterers serving 30,000 to 120,000 meals a day. Back companies with a signed channel partner, not a consumer brand plan.
- Treat foodservice churn (50,000 closures in H1 2025) and chain trial histories as risk: no meat-free item at a chain has a disclosed success.

**Funders and philanthropies.**
- Fund channel trials that produce tested evidence: a canteen trial with one large caterer, an in-store test in 20 Bach Hoa Xanh or WinMart+ stores, a menu test in 10 Nha Trang hotels. Each costs less than one more desk study and closes a High open question.
- Fund the missing data: listing fees and margins, frozen distribution cost, national *bán trú* coverage, military and hospital meal budgets.

**Policy makers.**
- The only national protein programme for children (school milk) set a target to raise animal protein above 40% of children's protein. If plant or blended protein is ever to enter public meals, nutrition guidance needs a protein-quality basis (for example DIAAS per VND), not an animal share.
- Dispatch 64/CĐ-TTg's rule against lowest-price-only procurement and its traceability requirement are the right frame for any new protein supplier to schools; publish what documents a supplier needs so that small domestic producers can qualify.
- The informal commissions reported in catering are a barrier to any new, cheaper supplier; transparent procurement in canteens helps new entrants more than subsidies.

**Food and feed manufacturers.**
- Incumbent *đồ chay* makers (SG Food, Vissan, CJ Cau Tre, An Nhien, Au Lac) already hold the frozen sections, convenience contracts and export channels. They are the natural first customers for a domestic textured protein and the natural makers of a higher-protein *chay* line.
- Integrated meat groups are best placed to launch hybrids: they own the brand trust, the cold chain and, for Masan, the stores.

**What not to do.**
- Do not use the published Vietnamese plant-based market sizes (supply study rule).
- Do not lead with school meals: they are price-capped, just hit by a pork scandal, and policy targets animal protein.
- Do not rely on wet markets for chilled or frozen branded goods.
- Do not read chain launches as demand evidence until outcomes are known.

## 8. What would change this view

- A household panel (Kantar Worldpanel) showing that modern trade already takes more than 30% of urban fresh protein spending would move modern retail ahead of canteens for hybrids and *chay*.
- Caterer interviews showing that commissions or single-supplier contracts block new ingredient suppliers would push canteens down and manufacturers up for archetype (a).
- A disclosed outcome from Lotteria's Burger Zero Meat (for example, still on the menu after a year with stable sales) would raise QSR for (b) and (d).
- A hotel and menu audit in Nha Trang finding strong, unmet vegetarian demand among Russian and Korean guests would rescue the hypothesis as a niche.
- A novel-food approval route in the new Food Safety Law (expected October to November 2026) would bring (e) forward into HoReCa and ingredients.
- A national nutrition document reframing protein targets around quality rather than animal share would open public meals to blends.
- Current VHLSS data showing high freezer ownership outside the big cities would widen the frozen route.
