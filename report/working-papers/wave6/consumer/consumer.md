# Vietnamese consumers and plant-based and fungal protein: attitudes, barriers, segments, willingness to pay and what they actually buy

Agent: CONSUMER (wave 6). Source prefix: CON. Date: 24 September 2026.

Scope: end consumers (households) for plant-based and fungal protein foods and plant milks. Cultivated meat, precision fermentation and GMO attitudes belong to the NOVEL stream and appear only in passing. Traditional *chay* (vegetarian, meatless) food is treated as the incumbent baseline. Every demand claim carries an evidence tag: `[evidence label, confidence, source IDs, demand evidence type]`.

Method note. We read the primary GFI APAC survey charts image by image, both Rakuten Insight report PDFs, 12 Vietnamese and Southeast Asian academic papers (Scite and OpenAlex), broker notes and press on Vinasoy and Oatside, and the project's own September 2026 retail audit (FORM-01). We extracted Google Trends series for 18 Vietnamese terms ourselves (CON-37). The session's web search quota ran out part way through this stream. After that we read only known URLs, academic databases and publishers' own index pages. Some checks in the brief were therefore not done: chain menus (KFC, Burger King, Pizza 4P's, Highlands, The Coffee House, Phuc Long, Katinat), Metric.vn category sales, and consumer studies by Decision Lab, Ipsos, YouGov, Mintel and NielsenIQ. Each is logged as an open question and none is reported as a finding.

## Headline findings

1. **Only one large study gives Vietnam numbers on plant-based meat, and it shows a small, trial-stage market.** GFI APAC's "Decoding demand" survey was run by The Good Growth Co. in 2023 and paid for by GFI APAC. It covered 5,971 people in six countries, 1,049 of them in Vietnam, all from an online panel in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. In Vietnam, 90% had heard of plant-based meat, 42% had ever tried it, 20% eat it monthly or more and 3% eat it weekly. The six-country averages are 91%, 47%, 20% and 5%. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; CON-03; ECO-23, stated]
2. **Vietnam is the least willing of the six countries to cut meat.** Only 15% of Vietnamese respondents intend to reduce meat (six-country average 21%, range 15% to 26%). They report 43.3 meat-eating occasions a month, second only to Thailand, and the highest beef frequency. A separate urban survey (n=616, 2022) found only 4% flexitarians among meat eaters, each eating 0.74 kg of meat a week. Demand is for more kinds of protein, not for a meat replacement. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; CON-08, stated]
3. **Vietnamese respondents say they are the most willing to buy at parity, but real analogues cost far more than any premium they say they would accept.** At the same price as meat, 74% of Vietnamese respondents would choose plant-based meat, the highest of the six (average 62%). The share falls to 64% at 20% above the meat price, 47% at 50% above and 26% at double. The only imported analogue on Vietnamese shelves in September 2026 costs VND 542,000 to 622,000 per 100 g of protein. Eggs and chicken cost VND 34,000 to 46,000 for the same protein, so the analogue is 12 to 18 times dearer. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; FORM-01, stated and revealed]
4. **The "5% to 63%" finding is a regional, hypothetical number, not a forecast.** It compares the 5% of all six-country respondents who eat plant-based meat weekly now with the 63% who say they would choose it "always" (15%) or "often" (48%) "if all your concerns were resolved". No Vietnam split of this item is public. GFI's own press release describes the same result as "three-quarters would regularly consume", which the charts do not support. [VN-adjacent, Medium, ECO-23; ECO-24; CON-02, stated]
5. **Vietnam's barrier profile is different from the region's.** Asked "what would make you eat more?", Vietnamese respondents chose affordability less often than any other country (38% against an average of 47%). They chose availability in shops (31% against 27%), variety (29% against 23%), fewer additives (27% against 21%, the highest of the six) and knowing how to cook it (22% against 16%, also the highest) more often. They were the least likely to ask for a product that "tasted more like meat" (23% against 28%). Rakuten's 2025 panel agrees: "difficult to find where I live" is the top Vietnamese barrier at 33%, and cost is cited by only 21%, the lowest of six Southeast Asian markets. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; CON-07, stated]
6. **Health comes first as a driver, food safety is the driver specific to Vietnam, and environmental concern is weak.** Health is the top reason in every source: 55% of Rakuten's Vietnamese panel say plant-based products are "healthier". Urban Vietnamese rate food safety as important (5.35 on a 6-point scale, against 4.59 in Switzerland), with specific fears of hormones and drug residues in meat. Environment ranks sixth of seven reasons and has a negative or unstable effect in small academic samples. Religion drives *chay* eating (35% "follow a vegetarian diet") but did not predict intention among Hanoi students. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-07; CON-08; CON-10; CON-13, stated]
7. **Plant milk is the only modern plant-based category that sells at scale, as sales data show.** Vinasoy's soy milk revenue was VND 3,596 billion in the first nine months of 2025 (up 13%) and about VND 1,200 billion in Q4 2025 (up 22%). Vinasoy held 90.6% of branded soy milk in 2024. Together these imply a branded soy milk market of about VND 5,300 billion (about USD 204 million) in 2025 (our derivation). Plant milks were 51 of the 186 SKUs in the retail audit, across 14 brands. [VN-direct, Medium (derivation Low), CON-26; CON-27; CON-28; FORM-01, revealed]
8. **Search behaviour tells the same story.** Across 2019 to 2026, Google search interest in *sữa hạt* (nut or seed milk) averaged 17 times that in *thịt thực vật* (plant meat). Interest in *sữa hạt* has roughly tripled since 2019. Interest in *thịt thực vật* peaked in 2021 to 2022 and has since fallen by about 60%, and its top query is "what is plant meat?". The top *sữa hạt* queries are about home nut-milk machines, so much nut-milk demand is homemade rather than bought. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-37, revealed]
9. **Modern plant-based meat has not found a commercial footing.** Mintel logged no plant-based meat launches in Vietnam from 2022 to Q2 2023. Imported brands arrived through restaurants and gourmet shops: Beyond at BiaCraft in 2019 and OmniFoods at Starbucks Vietnam in summer 2023. In the September 2026 audit, the only extruded modern analogue was one Italian brand in one gourmet store. Beyond, Omni, Next Meats and Heura were absent, including from the two gourmet chains that stocked them in 2022. [VN-direct, Medium, ECO-23; ECO-16; ECO-20; ECO-15; FORM-01, revealed]
10. **Plant milk worked where plant meat failed for five reasons (our inference).** Plant milk is priced at or below dairy: Fami costs about VND 22,900 per litre against VND 33,000 to 45,000 for cow milk. It is made at scale by local incumbents with national distribution. It is a familiar format, sold as a healthy addition to the diet rather than a replacement. It also fits coffee culture: about 100 cafes used Oatside by 2023 and charged VND 10,000 to 15,000 more per cup. Plant-based meat was imported, Western in format, priced 10 or more times above meat per unit of protein, sold only in gourmet shops, and up against cheap *đồ chay* (traditional vegetarian food). [VN-direct, Medium, CON-35; FORM-01; CON-26; CON-37, inferred]
11. **The likely first buyers are urban 25 to 44 year olds who eat a lot of meat and want variety, plus health- and safety-anxious urban households.** These are not students. GFI's "Curious" segment (aware, not yet tried, leaning towards cutting red meat) is 43% of the Vietnamese sample. Regionally, 18 to 24 year olds are the least aware of plant-based meat, and about two-thirds of Enthusiasts are over 35. The ceiling is loose: if the 3% weekly rate held for all 39.4 million urban residents, weekly eaters would number at most about 1.2 million. [VN-direct, Low to Medium, CON-02; ECO-23; MAC-19, inferred]
12. **No tested willingness-to-pay evidence exists for plant-based meat or milk in Vietnam.** We found no choice experiment, auction or in-store price test. The only Vietnamese "tested" item is a sensory panel of 51 students rating an energy bar made from brewery by-products (RD-32), with no price element. The best revealed evidence is the cafe oat-milk upcharge. The best tested evidence from nearby is a Thai trial of plant milks in iced coffee (macadamia and pistachio won; powdery texture lost) and a Chinese choice experiment on blended meatballs. [VN-direct and VN-adjacent, Low to Medium, RD-32; CON-35; CON-22; CON-23, tested and revealed]
13. **Blends are the lowest-risk way in.** 95% of Vietnamese respondents are at least slightly interested in trying a meat and plant-based blend, and 55% are very interested (six-country average 49% very interested). Across the region, people sceptical of plant-based meat ranked blends first among protein options. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; ECO-23, stated]
14. **On shelves and in search, Vietnamese buyers use the words *chay* and *sữa hạt*. *Thuần chay* (vegan) is rising. *Thịt thực vật* and *đạm thực vật* (plant protein) have faded.** *Chay* appears in 87 of 186 audited product names. No study has tested which label Vietnamese shoppers trust. [VN-direct, Low, CON-37; FORM-01, revealed and inferred]
15. **The survey base is thin and skewed.** It consists of one large regional online panel (GFI 2023), one commercial panel with a broad category definition (Rakuten 2021 and 2025), one solid academic urban survey (Delley et al. 2024) and about ten small convenience or student samples. Two papers from the same Hanoi University of Science and Technology (HUST) group report an identical "84% aware but not used" figure that does not match their own sample counts. [VN-direct, High (as a statement about the literature), register in data_consumer_surveys.csv, stated]

GFI question: "What is your familiarity with plant-based meat?" Fieldwork 2023, online panel. [VN-direct for the Vietnam row, VN-adjacent for the others; Medium; CON-02; stated]

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vietnam (1,049) | 10% | 48% | 22% | 17% | 3% | 90% | 42% | 48% |
| Thailand (932) | 7% | 34% | 27% | 23% | 9% | 93% | 59% | 54% |
| Philippines (960) | 9% | 48% | 26% | 13% | 4% | 91% | 43% | 40% |
| Malaysia (1,051) | 11% | 46% | 24% | 13% | 6% | 89% | 43% | 44% |
| Indonesia (966) | 12% | 49% | 24% | 10% | 5% | 88% | 39% | 38% |
| Singapore (1,013) | 3% | 42% | 37% | 15% | 3% | 97% | 55% | 33% |
| Six-country average (5,971) | 9% | 44% | 27% | 15% | 5% | 91% | 47% | 43% |

Reading the table:
- Vietnam has an average awareness rate but a below-average trial rate: 42% have tried, against 59% in Thailand. Almost half of Vietnamese respondents (48%) have heard of plant-based meat but never tried it. That is why GFI finds its largest "Curious" group in Vietnam (43%, second only to Indonesia at 44%). [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; CON-03, stated]
- Among Vietnamese who have tried plant-based meat, about half still eat it at least monthly. That repeat proxy is above the average, and Singapore's is the lowest. GFI also reports that Vietnamese respondents are "the most interested in increasing their future consumption". [VN-direct, Medium, CON-03, stated]
- Caution, which is our inference: modern analogues are almost absent from Vietnamese shops (FORM-01), so "plant-based meat" is probably read by many Vietnamese respondents as traditional mock meat (*chả chay*, *giò chay*, vegetarian sausage and ham). The 20% who say they eat it monthly are therefore probably mostly eating *đồ chay*, not modern extruded analogues. The Vietnamese questionnaire wording is not public, so this cannot be checked. [VN-direct, Low, CON-02; FORM-01, inferred]

Rakuten Insight measures a broader category ("plant-based alternatives to animal food products": plant milk, dairy substitutes, meat alternatives, egg substitutes, condiments), so its trial rates are much higher. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-06; CON-07, stated]

| Rakuten Insight | Vietnam 2021 (n=4,018) | Vietnam 2025 (n=1,942) | Thailand 2025 | Indonesia 2025 | Philippines 2025 | Malaysia 2025 | Singapore 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ever tried any plant-based alternative | 86% | 92% (86% in 2024) | 71% | 81% | 70% | 67% | 61% |
| Of those who tried: plant milk | 87% | 89% | 92% | not read | not read | not read | not read |
| Of those who tried: plant-based meat alternatives | 49% | 47% | 48% | not read | not read | not read | not read |
| Consume several times a week | 44% | 43% | 35% | 44% | not read | not read | 14% |

Vietnam has had the highest trial rate of any Asian market in both waves. The trial is almost all plant milk, and the share of Vietnamese triers who have tried plant-based meat alternatives stayed flat at 47% to 49% from 2021 to 2025. The 43% who consume "several times a week" include people drinking soy and nut milk; this is not evidence of analogue demand. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-06; CON-07, stated]

### 3.2 Drivers and barriers from other sources

- **Rakuten 2025, Vietnam.** Reasons for choosing plant-based: healthier 55%, trying a food trend 36%, following a vegetarian or vegan diet 35%, animal welfare 33%, taste better 31%, environment 30%, allergies 15%. Reasons for not eating: difficult to find 33%, no need 22%, costs more 21%, taste 18%, unsure of ingredients 15%, less healthy 3%. The 2021 Vietnam wave had the same top reason (healthier, 61%) and the same top barrier (difficult to find, 39%). Across the two waves the availability and ingredient barriers eased a little, and taste fell from 29% to 18%. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-06; CON-07, stated]
- **Food safety.** In the urban survey by Delley et al., Vietnamese rated "has a very high level of food safety" at 5.35 of 6 and "good for my health" at 5.40. "Affordable" scored 4.96 and "in line with my culture" 4.39. Swiss scores were far lower for safety (4.59) and price (3.54). Focus groups in the same project found that Vietnamese green consumers cut red meat for health and food-safety reasons, including fears of hormones. A 2019 Hanoi study reports that 95% of people worry strongly about food safety, especially drug residues in meat. That figure comes to us secondhand, through the Faunalytics literature review. In Singapore, people who worried about drug residues in meat were willing to pay more for alternative proteins. Our transfer assumption is that Vietnam behaves similarly or more strongly. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-08; CON-10; Low, CON-39 via CON-05; VN-adjacent, Medium, CON-21; stated]
- **Associations with plant-based meat (six-country average; no Vietnam split).** "Healthy" 65%, "easy to digest" 44%, "tastes good" 38%, "high in protein" 33%, "low in cholesterol" 31%, "hygienic" 29%. Negative associations are lower: "overly processed" 19%, "bad chemicals or additives" 16%. The negatives are concentrated among people who will not buy anyway. [VN-adjacent, Medium, CON-02; ECO-23, stated]
- **Family and social influence.** Vietnamese are more swayed by family and by the choices of those around them than Swiss consumers are ("hindering familial influence"). Meat is "non-negotiable" at special-occasion meals. In the south, a focus on building social relationships lowers plant-based food choice. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-08; CON-10; Low, CON-11, stated]
- **Religion.** Buddhist values and periodic vegetarian eating (on the 1st and 15th days of the lunar month, and during the 7th lunar month) sustain a large *chay* occasion. One study of 378 people found that religious values and health benefits raise intention to eat periodically vegetarian. Google searches for "chay" peak every August, which is when the 7th lunar month usually falls. Among Hanoi students, religious belief did not predict intention. [VN-direct, Low, CON-16; CON-13; Medium, CON-37, stated and revealed]
- **Environment.** Environment is weak as a driver. It ranks sixth of seven Rakuten reasons, and in one Hanoi student sample it had a small negative effect (beta -0.092). [VN-direct, Low to Medium, CON-07; CON-13, stated]
- **Cost as a motive.** Some Vietnamese eat less meat to save money. About 3.4% of Vietnamese and Indonesian social media posts mention cutting meat because of prices, and students who go vegetarian for economic reasons seek little nutrition information. Charity eateries in the south sell plant-based meals at USD 0.30 to 0.50. The reference price for a *chay* meal is therefore very low. [VN-direct, Low, CON-05; CON-15; CON-11, revealed and stated]

### 3.3 Where Vietnam differs from the regional average

Vietnam differs from the regional average in six ways:
- lower intention to cut meat;
- lower stated price sensitivity;
- higher availability, variety and cooking-knowledge barriers;
- higher concern about additives;
- more interest in blends;
- less interest in exact meat taste.

We also infer a stronger food-safety motive. All six differences come from stated data. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; CON-07; CON-08, stated]

## 4. Willingness to pay

The full data are in `data_consumer_wtp.csv`.

**Stated (Vietnam).** GFI asked: "Imagine you were doing your grocery shopping and the following products were available that would suit the dish you were wanting to cook. Which would you purchase?" The answer options were plant-based meat, meat or neither, with the relative price then moved up or down. GFI itself says the answers show relative interest rather than willingness to pay. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; ECO-23, stated]

| Price of plant-based meat relative to meat | Vietnam | Thailand | Philippines | Malaysia | Indonesia | Singapore | Average |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20% cheaper | 88% | 82% | 87% | 72% | 84% | 67% | 80% |
| Same price | 74% | 65% | 73% | 54% | 66% | 41% | 62% |
| 20% dearer | 64% | 50% | 56% | 42% | 44% | 26% | 47% |
| 50% dearer | 47% | 38% | 44% | 33% | 30% | 18% | 35% |
| 100% dearer | 26% | 21% | 30% | 20% | 23% | 10% | 22% |

Vietnam's line sits highest at every point except +100%. Two cautions apply. First, stated purchase intention in cheap online surveys usually overstates behaviour; we found no Vietnamese calibration of how far. Second, these are shares of people choosing, not a demand curve. For design purposes, a product must reach roughly price parity with the meat it displaces to reach most interested buyers. At +50%, interest falls below half. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02, stated and inferred]

**Revealed (Vietnam).**
- Café oat milk: in about 100 speciality cafés, drinks with oat milk cost VND 10,000 to 15,000 more per cup than drinks with cow milk (August 2023). This is the clearest evidence that Vietnamese urban consumers pay a premium for a plant-based product. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-35, revealed]
- Shelf price ladder for plant milk (September 2026, prices per litre): Fami soy milk VND 22,900; Oatside VND 59,900; Vinamilk high-protein nut milk VND 80,600 to 93,800 (5 g protein per 100 ml); Oatly VND 98,000 to 139,900; 137 Degrees VND 139,900 to 158,900. Cow milk cost VND 33,000 to 45,000 per litre in 2023. Mass soy milk undercuts dairy, while the premium imports sell at two to four times the dairy price in a niche channel. [VN-direct, High for prices, FORM-01; CON-35, revealed]
- Imported plant-based meat on shelf is priced far beyond the stated tolerance (headline finding 3). The fact that Beyond, Omni, Next Meats and Heura are absent in 2026 is consistent with buyers refusing that premium, but we have no sales data to prove it. [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01; ECO-15, revealed and inferred]

**Tested.**
- Vietnam: none found for plant-based meat, blends or plant milk. The only tested item is RD-32: 51 HUST students rated a spent-yeast and DDGS energy bar at 4.45 for overall acceptability on a 1 to 7 hedonic scale. The test had no price element. [VN-direct, Low, RD-32, tested]
- Nearby (VN-adjacent): 100 Thai consumers tasted ten plant milks in iced sweetened coffee. Macadamia and pistachio milks had the highest liking and purchase intent. Nutty, beany and full-bodied notes drove liking, while powdery texture and a dark brown flavour drove rejection. Our transfer assumption is that Vietnamese sweet iced milk coffee (*cà phê sữa đá*) is a comparable use. [VN-adjacent, Medium, CON-22, tested]
- General: a Chinese choice experiment (n=1,245) on frozen meatballs found that environmental information raised willingness to pay for beef and soy blended meatballs by about 31% and for all-soy meatballs by about 126% relative to a control group. Positive willingness to pay was limited to consumers low in food-technology neophobia (fear of foods made with new technology). [general, Medium, CON-23, tested]
- GFI APAC announced an Asia-focused sensory study of blended meat with A*STAR and partners. We could not locate its results. [VN-adjacent, Low, CON-25, tested]

Sizing, all as ceilings rather than estimates (our derivation): the urban population was 39.4 million in 2025 (MAC-19). Applying the Vietnam GFI rates to all urban residents gives at most about 1.2 million weekly and 7.9 million monthly plant-based meat eaters. Three things make the real number smaller: the panel is online adults in two cities; stated frequency overstates purchase; and much of it is probably traditional mock meat. Channel structure matters as much as head counts. Modern trade takes about 45% of urban FMCG spending and about 25% of rural spending. Total FMCG spending was flat in 2024, with shoppers trading down, and one in five urban households bought FMCG on TikTok Shop. [VN-direct, Low, MAC-19; CON-02; Medium, CON-34, inferred and revealed]

## 7. Plant milk: the only modern plant-based category with scale, and why

**Size.** Only branded soy milk has a traceable number. Vinasoy's soy milk segment earned about VND 4,800 billion in 2025 (VND 3,596 billion in the first nine months plus about VND 1,200 billion in Q4). Dividing by its 90.6% share of branded soy milk (a 2024 estimate from Bao Viet Securities) gives a branded soy milk market of about VND 5,300 billion, or about USD 204 million at 26,000 VND per USD, in 2025. This is our derivation, and it assumes the 2024 share held in 2025 and that the segment is mostly soy. [VN-direct, Low, CON-26; CON-27; CON-28, revealed and inferred]

We found no traceable market size for nut, seed and oat milks. VIRAC's 2020 claim that nut milk was 12% of liquid milk in 2018, growing 18% a year from 2014 to 2019, has no published method. Press repeats it and attributes it to the statistics office. [VN-direct, Low, CON-31; CON-32, revealed]

Vinasoy's revenue alone is more than any published estimate of the whole Vietnamese plant-based meat market; those estimates are rejected below. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-26; ECO-17; ECO-18, inferred]

**Why plant milk succeeded and plant meat did not.** This comparison is our inference, drawn from the revealed evidence in the table.

| Factor | Plant milk | Plant-based meat | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price against the animal product | Fami about VND 22,900 per litre, below cow milk at VND 33,000 to 45,000 | Imported analogue 12 to 18 times chicken or eggs per 100 g of protein | [VN-direct, High, FORM-01; CON-35, revealed] |
| Maker | Local incumbents with plants and national distribution (Vinasoy capacity 390 million litres a year; Vinamilk; TH; Nutifood) | Imports and micro-startups | [VN-direct, Medium, CON-27; FORM-01, revealed] |
| Familiarity | Soy milk long established (Vinasoy held 82.7% of it in 2014); flavours such as red bean, black bean and walnut; people make it at home | Burger and sausage formats; the familiar version is cheap *đồ chay* | [VN-direct, Medium, ECO-42; CON-37, revealed] |
| Role in the diet | A healthy addition, a children's drink, a coffee ingredient | Sold as a meat replacement to people who do not want to cut meat | [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; CON-35, stated and revealed] |
| Channel | Traditional and modern trade, cafes, e-commerce | Restaurants and gourmet stores only | [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01; ECO-15, revealed] |
| What buyers ask for | Health, taste, convenience | Availability, variety, cooking fit, fewer additives, price | [VN-direct, Medium, CON-07; CON-02, stated] |

Plant milk's success was not built on protein. Most plant milks carry 0.7 to 2.5 g of protein per 100 ml, and some "plant" milks are blended with cow milk (TH true NUT walnut, several Vinamilk lines). The rise of a 5 g per 100 ml high-protein nut milk, and of searches for it, is the first sign that protein content itself now sells in this aisle. [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01; ECO-45; CON-37, revealed]

## 8. Naming and framing

- ***Chay*** is the incumbent frame. It appears in 87 of 186 audited product names, and the generic search term "chay" outranks every modern term by a wide margin, with a peak each August. It carries Buddhist and traditional associations, and in the market it is attached to cheap, low-protein foods (median 4.7 g protein per 100 g in the audit). [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01; CON-37, revealed]
- ***Thuần chay*** (vegan) is the modern term on the rise: its search index grew about tenfold from 2019 to 2024. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-37, revealed]
- ***Thịt thực vật*** (plant meat) and ***đạm thực vật*** (plant protein) were media terms in 2021 to 2022. Search interest has since faded, and people mostly search for what *thịt thực vật* is, a sign of low familiarity. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-37, revealed]
- ***Thực vật*** (plant) is used by incumbents for modern dairy alternatives (Vinamilk's *sữa chua thực vật*, plant yoghurt) and as a protein claim ("100% Protein từ thực vật" on bibigo mandu). ***Sữa hạt*** has become the generic name for plant milks, including soy blends. [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01, revealed]
- English "plant based" is searched rarely, but its interest spiked in 2026 for reasons we could not identify. [VN-direct, Low, CON-37, revealed]
- **Trust.** No study has tested which of these labels Vietnamese shoppers trust or reject. GFI's Vietnamese wording is not public. The social listening study searched "+chay", "thuần thực vật" and "dựa trên thực vật" in Vietnam but found too few posts to rank barriers. [VN-direct, Low, CON-05, revealed]
- **Our inference** (Low) is that *chay* works for religious and periodic occasions. A health and protein claim (for example *giàu đạm thực vật*, rich in plant protein) with a food-safety signal is the more promising frame for flexitarian meat eaters. "Meat replacement" framing conflicts with a population that intends to eat more meat. This needs a label test before anyone relies on it.

## 9. Implications for frontier actors

**Founders and product developers**
- Make products for the Vietnamese meals people already eat, not Western formats. Vietnamese respondents asked for variety and cooking know-how more than any other country, and for a product that "tastes like meat" less than any other. Targets include *chả lụa* (pork roll), meatballs and sausages, fillings for *chả giò* (spring rolls), and braising (*kho*), stir-fry and broth pieces. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02, inferred]
- Price against the meat being replaced, per serving: at parity or below for the mass market. Stated interest halves by +50%. Blended products (meat plus plant protein) can reach parity sooner, and 95% of Vietnamese respondents are open to them. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02, inferred]
- Raise protein visibly. Audited *chay* foods have a median of 4.7 g protein per 100 g. A credible protein upgrade (10 g per 100 g or more for meat-like foods; 5 g per 100 ml or more for milks) gives a reason to buy that incumbents do not offer. Vinamilk's high-protein nut milk shows the market has started to respond. [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01; CON-37, inferred]
- Keep ingredient lists short, avoid additive-heavy recipes, and signal food safety (traceability, certification). Fewer additives is a top Vietnam-specific ask, and meat-safety fears are a real motive. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; CON-08, inferred]
- Plant milk remains the proven category. The opportunities are barista milks tuned for *cà phê sữa đá* (nutty and full-bodied, not powdery), high-protein milks, and local nut, seed and bean bases. [VN-direct and VN-adjacent, Medium, CON-35; CON-22, inferred]

**R&D bodies and universities**
- Build tested-evidence capacity in Vietnam: sensory panels in Vietnamese dishes, choice experiments and label tests. Stop running small student surveys based on the theory of planned behaviour, which add little. [VN-direct, High for the gap, register, inferred]
- Work on texture and flavour in Vietnamese cooking conditions (broth, braising, frying) and on protein upgrades to *chay* staples at *chay* cost. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; FORM-01, inferred]

**Investors and development finance**
- The revealed demand is in plant milk and in incumbent *chay* lines, not in branded plant-based meat for consumers. The investable routes are ingredients, co-manufacturing and protein upgrades sold to incumbents (Vinasoy, Vinamilk, VISSAN, CJ, SG Food), plus blended products made with meat processors. Treat consumer brands of imported-style analogues as unproven. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-26; FORM-01; ECO-23, inferred]

**Funders and philanthropies**
- Fund the missing evidence: a Vietnam choice experiment and in-store price test, a label-trust test, a count of how many people eat vegetarian periodically, and open e-commerce sales data. Each can be done for tens of thousands of USD (see open_questions.csv). [VN-direct, Medium, inferred]
- Health and food-safety messages fit Vietnamese motives. Climate messages do not lead. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-07; CON-08; CON-13, inferred]

**Policy makers**
- Clear labelling rules would help shoppers. Hybrid "plant" milks that contain cow milk sit in the plant-milk aisle. *Chay* and *thực vật* claims are unregulated in meaning. Protein claims on *chay* foods are rarely numeric. The expected new Food Safety Law (late 2026) is the place to set these rules. [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01; ECO-45, inferred]
- Public messaging on food safety and healthy protein is aligned with consumer motives. Messaging about meat reduction is not: only 15% of urban online respondents intend to cut meat. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02, inferred]

**Food manufacturers**
- Blending plant protein into existing processed-meat and *chay* lines is the lowest-risk move. It uses existing brands and channels, can lower cost, and meets stated openness to blends. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; ECO-23, inferred]

**What not to do**
- Do not read the regional "5% to 63%" or the 74% at parity as a market forecast. Both are hypothetical and stated, from online urban panels. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02, stated]
- Do not target students as early adopters of premium products. They have low incomes and, regionally, the lowest awareness. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-13; CON-02, inferred]
- Do not launch premium imported analogues into gourmet-only channels expecting mass uptake. That route has been tried since 2019 and has not produced a visible market. [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01; ECO-15; ECO-16, revealed]
- Do not position products as meat replacements or lead with environmental claims. [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02; CON-07, inferred]

## 10. What would change this view

- A tested study (choice experiment or in-store trial) showing that Vietnamese shoppers repeatedly buy a modern analogue or blend at a price at least 20% above the meat it replaces.
- E-commerce or retail panel data (Metric.vn, NielsenIQ, Kantar) showing sustained growth in modern plant-based meat since 2024.
- The Vietnamese GFI questionnaire showing that "plant-based meat" was defined as modern analogues and excluded traditional mock meat. That would raise the value of the 20% monthly figure.
- A local producer reaching price parity with pork or chicken per serving at mass scale.
- A food-safety shock in meat (residues, disease) followed by a measurable switch to plant proteins.
- Euromonitor or company data showing that plant milk growth reverses after 2025, which would weaken the plant-milk analogy.
