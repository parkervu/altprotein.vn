# Wave 6 research brief: the demand side (Part VI, edition 1.2)

## The study you are contributing to

AltProtein Vietnam has published "Alternative protein in Vietnam: a supply-side scoping study", edition 1.1 (September 2026). It is an evidence-tagged package (57 pages, 92 data tables, 1,275 sources). Its headline: Vietnam's case for alternative protein is a supply case (about 99% of soy protein in feed is imported; about USD 10 billion of feed-ingredient imports in 2025). It covers plant-based, fungal/mycoprotein, fermentation (biomass and precision) and cultivated meat and seafood, for food and feed. Insect protein appears only as an incumbent benchmark, never as a recommendation (do not recommend insect products or companies).

Edition 1.2 adds **Part VI: the demand side**. The main readers remain the actors who move the frontier of alternative protein: founders and startups, R&D bodies and universities, investors and development finance, funders and philanthropies, policy makers, food and feed manufacturers, international organisations. Part VI must answer, for them: **what demand exists or could exist in and for Vietnam that would pull novel protein products and ingredients, what those products must be (price, protein, taste, format, label, channel) to win it, who buys first, and how large it could be.** It is not a consumer marketing plan. Demand is treated as a design input for what to make.

Four demand layers are in scope: end consumers (households), foodservice and institutions, ingredient buyers (B2B food makers), and export demand. Four product frames: traditional *chay* as the incumbent baseline (studied, not promoted), plant-based and fungal, fermentation-derived proteins, cultivated meat and seafood.

## What the supply study already established (do not re-research; cite as context)

- Retail audit (September 2026, 186 SKUs, 11 modern and premium stores in Nha Trang and Ho Chi Minh City): median label protein of 43 ready-to-eat or ready-to-cook foods is 4.7 g per 100 g; 53% under 5 g; only 16% reach 10 g. Every locally made product with 10 g or more protein uses imported textured soy, soy protein or wheat gluten, mostly Chinese. Modern plant-based meat is nearly absent; the only imported analogues seen cost VND 542,000 to 622,000 per 100 g of protein versus about VND 34,000 to 46,000 for eggs and chicken breast, VND 66,000 for tofu, VND 112,000 for hydrated gluten or TVP analogues, VND 369,000 for filled chay wrappers.
- Incumbents: Vinasoy (about 80% of soy milk, more than VND 4,100 billion revenue 2024); industrial *đồ chay* made by Vissan, CJ Foods Vietnam (Cau Tre, bibigo), SG Food, Cholimex, An Nhien, Au Lac, Thoai An, LC Foods; plant milks from Vinamilk, TH, Nutifood; startups Emmay (mushroom/mycelium), Kashew (cashew cheese), Ong Tempeh.
- GFI APAC: no plant-based meat launches in Vietnam in Mintel GNPD 2022 to Q2 2023. Imported brands (Beyond, OmniFoods at Starbucks 2023, Next Meats, Heura) came via restaurants and gourmet shops.
- Published Vietnamese plant-based market sizes ("VND 1,000 billion", "USD 500 million") have no traceable method: do not use them as findings.
- Rules: new protein foods are sold by self-declaration; no novel-food approval route; a new Food Safety Law is expected at the October to November 2026 National Assembly session. Precision-fermented proteins probably count as GMO products (Decree 43/2026).
- 2050 balance model: meat food use rises; OECD-FAO puts Vietnamese pig, poultry and beef food use at 61.9 kg per person in 2035. Scenario S-ALT assumes 1% (2030), 5% (2040), 10% (2050) of meat demand replaced by plant-based and fermented foods.
- Existing source IDs you may cite by ID if you use the same document: ECO-23 (GFI APAC 2023 State of the Industry), ECO-24 (GFI APAC 10 charts, the regional consumer survey), RD-32 (Nguyen M.Q. et al. 2024, consumer insights, alternative protein from brewing by-products, HUST), MAC-31 (USDA FAS Food Processing Ingredients Annual 2026), COST-39 to COST-41 (Bach Hoa Xanh meat and egg prices), FORM-01 (the retail audit). If you use one of these, reference it by that ID; do not add a duplicate.

## Evidence rules (strict)

1. **Never fabricate.** Every number needs a source you actually retrieved (web page, PDF, database, paper). If you only saw a figure in a search snippet, say "snippet only" and mark Low. If you cannot find something, say "we found no ..." and log it as an open question. A clean negative is a finding.
2. **Evidence label and confidence** on every substantive claim, in the form `[VN-direct, High, SRC-ID]`:
   - `VN-direct` (about Vietnam), `VN-adjacent` (comparable Southeast Asian country or context; state the transfer assumption), `general` (global; no claim it applies to Vietnam).
   - `High` (primary source read), `Medium` (reputable secondary, or primary read in part), `Low` (single claim, press, marketing, snippet, or our derivation from weak inputs).
3. **Demand evidence type** (new in Part VI; add to every demand claim): `stated` (what people say in surveys, focus groups, intentions, attitudes, stated willingness to pay), `revealed` (what people or firms actually do: sales, purchases, prices, trade data, menus, shelf presence, consumption statistics), `tested` (controlled tasting, choice experiments, auctions, field or market trials), `inferred` (our inference from indirect evidence). Example: `[VN-direct, Medium, CON-04, stated]`. Stated-preference evidence overstates behaviour; say so when it matters and look for revealed evidence to check it.
4. Numbers: always units and the year the number refers to; ranges with "to" (4 to 8). USD unless stated; VND with thousands separators; 26,000 VND per USD if you convert (say so). Never average disagreeing numbers: give both, state which you prefer and why, and log a disagreement.
5. For surveys, always record: who ran it, who paid, year of fieldwork, sample size, sample frame (online panel, urban only, age range), exact question wording if available. Online urban panels are not the Vietnamese population: say so.
6. Writing: plain English, British spelling, short sentences, **no em dashes or en dashes anywhere** (use commas, colons or separate sentences). Place and company names unaccented in English text (Ho Chi Minh City, Vinasoy); Vietnamese terms in italics with diacritics and an English gloss on first use (*đồ chay*, traditional vegetarian food). Provinces use post-1 July 2025 names where relevant.
7. Prefer 2023 to 2026 data; today is 24 September 2026. Say when the latest available is older.
8. Search in both English and Vietnamese (e.g. "ăn chay", "thực phẩm chay", "thịt thực vật", "đạm thực vật", "thịt nuôi cấy", "khảo sát người tiêu dùng"). Vietnamese press (VnExpress, Tuoi Tre, Thanh Nien, VietnamPlus, Vietnam News, Cafef, Vietnam Investment Review), market research firms (Decision Lab, Q&Me, Kantar Worldpanel Vietnam, NielsenIQ, Rakuten Insight, Cimigo, YouGov, Statista snippets), government (NSO/GSO VHLSS, National Institute of Nutrition general nutrition survey), FAO, OECD-FAO, USDA FAS GAIN reports, GFI APAC, Faunalytics, academic databases.
9. Academic literature: you can load the Scite and OpenAlex tools with ToolSearch (query "+Scite search" or "select:mcp__Scite__search_literature,mcp__OpenAlex__search_works") and use them. Check for retractions.
