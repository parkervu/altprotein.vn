# Business buyers of protein ingredients in Vietnam's food industry: who buys, how much, at what price and specification, and what a new protein ingredient must offer

Agent: BUYERS (wave 6). Source prefix: BUY. Date: 24 September 2026.

> **Method note.** This stream covers ingredient buyers (B2B food makers). Feed buyers are in the supply study. The session's web-search quota was used up before this stream started, so we worked by direct retrieval: the UN Comtrade public API (Vietnam-reported and partner-reported data, 13 HS lines, 2019 to 2025), company filings and product pages, trade-fair documents, commercial shipment summaries, site-level news archives (CafeF, VnExpress, Tuoi Tre) and OpenAlex. Coverage is therefore stronger for listed firms and firms that publish ingredient lists, and weaker for private firms (Uniben, Asia Foods, Duc Viet, Le Gia, IDP, FrieslandCampina Vietnam). Tags follow the wave 6 brief: `[evidence label, confidence, source, demand evidence type]`. We convert at 26,000 VND per USD where needed.

## Headline findings

1. **Revealed B2B demand for protein ingredients is real but modest in value.** Vietnam imported about USD 285 million of protein ingredients across 13 HS lines in 2023 (plant proteins USD 87 million, dairy proteins USD 119 million, gelatin USD 62 million, egg proteins USD 16 million), up from USD 190 million in 2019. Partner-reported data put 2025 at USD 286 million FOB. These lines also carry feed-grade whey, supplement premixes and capsule gelatin, so food-industry use is lower than the total. [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-01; BUY-02, revealed]
2. **The plant-protein trade is a Chinese commodity trade whose tonnage nearly tripled.** China reported shipping 53,976 t of HS 3504, 2106.10 and 1109 to Vietnam in 2025, against 18,375 t in 2019, at unit values of USD 0.89 to 1.31/kg. Wheat gluten from all origins rose from 11,327 t (2019) to 21,991 t (2025). China's HS 3504 unit value (USD 0.89 to 1.03/kg in 2024 to 2025) is too low for food-grade isolates, so part of this tonnage is probably feed-grade protein. [VN-direct, High, BUY-02, revealed; food and feed split: VN-direct, Low, inferred]
3. **The price to beat for a domestic plant protein is low, and animal proteins cost 3 to 5 times more per kg of protein.** Chinese TVP and soy concentrate entered at USD 1.24/kg FOB in 2025 (about USD 1.9 per kg of protein at 65% protein); sampled soy isolate shipments were USD 1.95 to 2.20/kg; wheat gluten USD 1.31/kg. Egg white powder from India was USD 5.90/kg, whey protein concentrate 80 from the United States USD 8.13/kg and from Germany USD 14.05/kg, casein from New Zealand USD 7.59/kg. [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-02; BUY-07, revealed]
4. **Vietnamese meat processors already sell low-inclusion "hybrid" products.** In an online label audit of 118 Vissan products, 19 of 61 unique meat formulations (31%) list soy protein and 22 (36%) list at least one added protein (soy, wheat, milk protein or egg white). Soy sits mostly in value lines (canned "3 Bong Mai" range, pate, *giò lụa* (steamed pork roll) with head meat or skin, DHA sausages). Premium lines are sold on meat content instead: Masan's Ponnie sausage was positioned on "88% meat". [VN-direct, High, BUY-05; BUY-13, revealed]
5. **The clearest large-buyer pull for a non-soy protein isolate comes from dairy.** Vinamilk's high-protein nut milk delivers 9 g of protein per 180 ml "mainly from pea", and is sold as soy-free, lactose-free, non-GMO, gluten-free and glyphosate-free. Vinamilk names its high-protein and plant milks among the drivers of H1 2026 revenue of VND 35,034 billion. [VN-direct, High, BUY-15; BUY-16, revealed]
6. **Industrial *đồ chay* (traditional vegetarian food) makers are the most import-dependent and most switchable buyers.** The supply study's retail audit found that every protein-dense local chay product uses imported TVP, soy concentrate, soy isolate or gluten. These owner-managed firms can trial a new ingredient in weeks, but each buys small volumes. [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01, revealed; switching: VN-direct, Low, inferred]
7. **Instant noodles are a volume market, not yet a protein market.** Vietnam ate 8,231 million servings in 2025, fourth in the world and about 81 per person. Acecook uses soy protein only in seasoning and garnish sachets, but already buys pea starch (82 to 84 g/kg of De Nhat noodle blocks) and mung bean starch, which are the co-products of pulse protein fractionation, and it calcium-fortifies Hao Hao. We found no protein-fortified noodle. [VN-direct, High, BUY-03; BUY-22, revealed]
8. **Buyers are adding capacity and tightening quality assurance.** Food processing reached USD 88 billion in 2025 (up 11%). New plants include Acecook Vinh Long (USD 200 million), CPV Food in Dong Nai (former Binh Phuoc, USD 250 million), JBS in Hai Phong (USD 100 million) and Cholimex in Tay Ninh (former Long An, VND 850 billion). Food-safety crises, including about 130 t of African swine fever infected pork at Ha Long Canfoco, whose H1 2026 revenue fell 32%, raise the documentation and traceability bar for any new supplier. [VN-direct, Medium, MAC-31; BUY-20; BUY-21; BUY-27; BUY-30; BUY-31, revealed]
9. **We found no Vietnamese food company piloting mycoprotein, precision-fermented dairy protein or a novel-protein hybrid meat.** Corporate alternative-protein moves remain the seafood exporters' cultivated-meat stakes already known from the supply study (Vinh Hoan, Minh Phu). Masan MEATLife calls itself a "consumer protein platform" but we found no plant-based product; Vissan's 2025 annual report text does not mention plant-based or chay lines. A clean negative. [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-04; BUY-10, revealed]
10. **B2B prices are negotiated, not listed, and the organised channel is thin.** Distributors show soy protein and gluten as "price on request" with regional price policies. Large processors import major ingredients directly and buy minor ones through distributors. Fi Vietnam (May 2026, Ho Chi Minh City) listed about 250 exhibitors, mostly Chinese; pea protein and US dairy protein suppliers were present, but no major soy isolate brand. [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-33; BUY-36; BUY-37; MAC-31, revealed]
11. **Fungal biomass cannot beat soy on cost per kg of protein, but can be priced against egg and dairy proteins.** At 45% protein on a dry basis (the supply study's balance-model assumption for mycoprotein-type biomass), it would need to cost about USD 1/kg to match soy isolate per kg of protein, but could reach about USD 3.3/kg against egg white and USD 3.9 to 4.6/kg against casein or whey concentrate, if it delivers binding, texture or flavour. [VN-direct, Low, BUY-02, inferred]
12. **Precision-fermented dairy proteins face the most demanding buyers.** Infant-formula and medical-nutrition makers pay whey-concentrate prices (USD 8 to 14/kg), but market "non-GMO" and increasingly run clinical studies (Nutifood with Military Hospital 175, June 2026). The first customers are specialty nutrition lines, not bulk whey replacement, and regulatory status is the gate. [VN-direct, Low, BUY-02; BUY-16; BUY-19, inferred]
13. **Plausible first B2B customers:** domestic TVP to chay makers (An Nhien, Au Lac, Thoai An, LC Foods) and frozen co-packers (Cholimex, CJ Cau Tre, Vissan chay lines); a rice or mung bean protein isolate to Vinamilk, Vinasoy and Nutifood; fungal biomass to Vissan and Masan MEATLife value or hybrid lines and to Ajinomoto Vietnam as a flavour ingredient; precision-fermented dairy protein to Nutifood and Vinamilk specialty nutrition. [VN-direct, Low, data_buyer_map.csv, inferred]

## 1. Demand revealed by trade data

### 1.1 What the customs lines contain

No HS code maps cleanly to one protein ingredient. Soy protein isolate appears under both HS 3504 and HS 2106.10 in Vietnamese declarations: commercial shipment samples declare Chinese soy isolate under 2106.10.00 (2025) and pea protein under 3504.00.00 [VN-direct, Low, BUY-07; BUY-09, revealed]. HS 2106.10 also carries protein-based supplement premixes, and HS 3504 carries peptones, hydrolysates and collagen peptides. Whey (0404.10) mixes feed-grade sweet whey powder with food-grade demineralised whey. Gelatin (3503) serves food, capsules and technical uses. Pea protein has no separate code (as the supply study found).

Vietnam's own quantities are unreliable: UN Comtrade flags every Vietnam-reported net weight in these 13 lines, 2019 to 2023, as estimated, and Vietnam had not reported 2024 or 2025 when we queried on 24 September 2026. Values (CIF) are the better Vietnam-side measure. Partner-reported (mirror) exports give reported tonnages and a series to 2025, at FOB values [VN-direct, High, BUY-01; BUY-02, revealed].

Three patterns matter for ingredient makers:

- **Plant proteins are the fastest-growing tonnage.** China-reported plant-protein tonnage to Vietnam (HS 3504, 2106.10, 1109) rose from 18,375 t (2019) to 28,323 t (2023), 51,130 t (2024) and 53,976 t (2025), worth USD 59.7 million in 2025 [VN-direct, High, BUY-02, revealed]. Most of the 2024 jump came from HS 3504 (10,028 t to 25,267 t) at about USD 1/kg, which fits feed-grade proteins better than food isolates (not verified); gluten added about 5,500 t [VN-direct, Low, BUY-02, inferred].
- **Wheat gluten doubled.** Partner-reported gluten imports went from 11,327 t (2019) to 21,991 t (2025), 88% from China in 2025. Gluten is the base of *mì căn* (wheat gluten mock meat) and appears on Vissan sausage labels; some may also go into aquafeed pellets as a binder (not verified) [VN-direct, High, BUY-02; BUY-05, revealed].
- **Dairy proteins split into a cheap feed-heavy bulk and a small, expensive food tier.** Whey (0404.10) moved about 64,000 t in 2025 at USD 1.23/kg, a unit value consistent with sweet whey powder, much of it for piglet feed (inference). The food tier is whey protein concentrate (1,634 t, USD 9.91/kg), casein and caseinates (about 1,400 t), and milk protein concentrates within 0404.90. USDA reports that Vietnam's dairy imports reached USD 1.5 billion in 2025 and that local processors rely on imported milk powders, whey, lactose, cream and cheese [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-02; MAC-31, revealed].

### 1.3 What the trade data cannot tell us

The split between food and feed, and between buyer segments, is not visible at HS six-digit level. The cheapest fix is a declaration-level dataset (eight-digit code, goods description, importer) for 2024 and 2025 (open question 1). Until then, treat "plant protein imports" as an upper bound for food demand.

## 2. Who the buyers are

### 2.1 Meat processors

**Vissan** is the best-documented buyer. Its 2025 annual report gives total revenue of VND 2,972 billion (89% of plan), pre-tax profit of VND 105 billion and processed food output of 18,547 t; the 2026 plan is VND 3,270 billion and 20,350 t [VN-direct, High, BUY-04, revealed]. Both fell from 2024 (VND 3,182 billion revenue and more than 20,000 t of processed food) [VN-direct, High, BUY-06, revealed]. It runs a 5,000 t/yr frozen food plant in Ho Chi Minh City and a 3,000 t/yr plant in the north [VN-direct, High, BUY-04, revealed]. On its online shop, 84 of 118 processed SKUs publish ingredient lists [VN-direct, High, BUY-05, revealed]:

Added proteins named on labels are *protein đậu nành* or *đạm đậu nành* (soy protein), *gluten* or *đạm lúa mì* (wheat protein), *protein sữa* (milk protein) and *bột lòng trắng trứng* (egg white powder), usually alongside modified starch, wheat fibre, phosphates and carrageenan. *Chả chiên* (fried pork loaf), for example, lists soy protein, wheat protein and egg white powder together. The pattern is functional: added proteins bind water and fat in fine pastes and cut meat cost in value lines. Vissan's canned chay range uses "heo lát chay" and "bò lát chay" (vegetarian pork and beef slices) at 55%, whose composition the label does not disclose. Label presence says nothing about inclusion level; illustratively, if 30 to 50% of Vissan's 18,547 t carried soy protein at 1 to 3%, it would buy about 55 to 280 t a year [VN-direct, Low, BUY-04; BUY-05, inferred]. A single large processor is therefore a tens-to-hundreds-of-tonnes customer, not a thousands-of-tonnes one.

**Masan MEATLife** (MEATDeli, Ponnie, 3F Viet, and the sausage and canned-meat maker Masan Jinju consolidated since late 2022) had 2025 revenue of VND 9,230 billion, with processed meat up 21.9% and "innovation products" at 31% of category revenue [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-10; BUY-11; BUY-14, revealed]. It says it is moving from a traditional meat company to a *nền tảng đạm tiêu dùng* (consumer protein platform) and claims about 61% of the protein category in WinCommerce's 4,592 stores [VN-direct, Low, BUY-10, stated]. We did not verify its ingredient lists. Its premium sausage Ponnie was marketed on "88% meat" (2022) [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-13, stated].

**C.P. Vietnam** (revenue about VND 85,000 billion in 2025, supply study) runs CPV Food in Dong Nai (former Binh Phuoc), a USD 250 million processed-chicken complex exporting to Japan, Singapore and Hong Kong [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-31; IND-43, revealed]. Its published R&D and net-zero messaging concerns feed, farm efficiency and packaging; we found no plant-based product or statement in Vietnam [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-31, revealed].

**Ha Long Canfoco** (Hai Phong) is shrinking after about 130 t of African swine fever infected pork was found in its supply (incident from September 2025, disclosed early 2026): H1 2026 net revenue was VND 204 billion (down 32%) and Q2 2026 its largest ever quarterly loss (VND 12 billion) [VN-direct, High, BUY-20, revealed]. **Ba Huan** is an egg producer in financial distress (tax arrears of VND 30.4 billion, September 2026), not a protein-ingredient buyer [VN-direct, High, BUY-23, revealed]. **JBS** is building export-processing plants (USD 100 million) to process imported meat for re-export, which signals meat-processing FDI but not alternative-protein demand [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-30; BUY-46, stated]. We could not document Duc Viet or Le Gia in this wave (open question 9).

### 2.2 Instant noodles

Vietnam consumed 8,557 million servings in 2021 and 8,231 million in 2025 (WINA), fourth after China and Hong Kong, Indonesia and India; that is about 81 servings per person on USDA's population of 101.9 million [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-03; MAC-31, revealed]. Hao Hao, Kokomi, 3 Mien, Omachi and Gau Do rank among the top food brands [VN-direct, Medium, MAC-31, revealed]. Acecook's new Vinh Long plant has capacity of 81,000 t of products and 27,600 t of seasoning and oil sachets a year, 1.2 billion packs in phase one [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-21, revealed].

On Acecook's ingredient lists, soy protein appears in seasoning or garnish sachets (De Nhat "thịt bằm" (minced pork) flavour, Modern, Pho De Nhat); the De Nhat noodle block contains 82 to 84 g/kg of pea starch; Mien Phu Huong uses mung bean starch; and Hao Hao is fortified with calcium (333 mg per pack) while delivering 6.2 to 6.9 g of protein [VN-direct, High, BUY-22, revealed]. Masan Consumer (Omachi, Kokomi) had 2025 revenue of VND 30,557 billion and H1 2026 VND 15,637 billion, with Omachi gaining 1.3 points of value share [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-11; BUY-12, revealed]. We found no protein-fortification statement from any noodle maker [VN-direct, Low, BUY-22, revealed]. Two points follow. Fortification is operationally normal (calcium), so protein fortification is a formulation and price question, not a novelty. And noodle makers already buy the starch that a pulse-protein plant co-produces.

### 2.3 Dairy, plant milk, infant and medical nutrition

**Vinamilk** reported H1 2026 revenue of VND 35,034 billion (up 17.7%), with high-protein and plant milks named as contributors [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-15, revealed]. Its *sữa hạt cao đạm* (high-protein nut milk) lists water, *đạm đậu Hà Lan* (pea protein), vegetable oil and six nut or bean components (peanut, almond, walnut extract and black, mung and white bean extracts), and claims 9 g protein per 180 ml, "không đậu nành" (no soy), no lactose, non-GMO, no gluten and a glyphosate-free commitment for all its plant milks [VN-direct, High, BUY-16, revealed]. The page explains that products need at least 5 g protein per 100 ml to be called *cao đạm* (high protein) and targets "người ngại đậu nành" (people wary of soy) [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-16, stated]. This is the only large Vietnamese buyer we found using a non-soy protein isolate.

**Vinasoy** (about 80% of soy milk, more than VND 4,100 billion revenue in 2024, supply study ECO-41) uses whole soybeans; it launched Veyo Smarty kids' nut milk built on "đạm thực vật" (plant protein) and says it is a member of the Plant Based Foods Association [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-17, stated]. **Nutifood** runs six plants in Vietnam and one in Sweden, a research institute (NNRIS), and in June 2026 signed an MOU with Military Hospital 175 to run clinical studies of Varna Gentle Care (cancer patients) and Varna Life Sua Hat, a nut-milk product for people with diabetes [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-18; BUY-19, stated]. Plant-based medical nutrition therefore already exists as a buyer category.

For infant formula and nutrition powders, the relevant imported proteins are whey protein concentrate (USD 16.2 million, 1,634 t in 2025), demineralised whey within HS 0404.10, casein and caseinates, and milk protein concentrates [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-02, revealed]. USDA lists whey proteins and colostrum among "best prospects" for US exporters, and the US Dairy Export Council ran a pavilion at Fi Vietnam 2026 [VN-direct, Medium, MAC-31; BUY-33, revealed]. We did not research formula makers individually (Vitadairy, Nutricare, IDP, FrieslandCampina Vietnam, TH), which is a gap.

### 2.4 Bakery and snacks

Orion Vina had 2023 revenue of KRW 475.5 billion (about VND 8,760 billion) and more than 67% of the Chocopie category; Bibica (VND 1,511 billion) and Hai Ha (VND 915 billion) are far smaller [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-29, revealed]. Bakery protein demand is mainly egg and dairy: egg powders (egg white, yolk, whole egg) imported mostly from India totalled USD 16.9 million FOB in 2025 [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-02, revealed]. We found no protein-claim bakery product from these firms (not audited in detail).

### 2.5 Seasoning and soup bases

The seasoning market was VND 35,707 billion in 2022, forecast VND 40,812 billion in 2026 (Euromonitor, cited in press) [VN-direct, Low, BUY-24, revealed]. Masan's seasonings earned VND 9,700 billion in 2021 and Cholimex Food VND 2,513 billion [VN-direct, Low, BUY-28, revealed]; Ajinomoto Vietnam earned about VND 7,600 to 8,700 billion a year in 2017 to 2019 and holds about 60% of MSG [VN-direct, Low, BUY-25, revealed]. Protein enters this segment as meat and bone extracts (Aji-ngon), yeast extract and hydrolysed proteins, priced as flavour rather than protein; Ajinomoto also sells a mushroom-based *hạt nêm* (seasoning granules) for chay cooking, frozen gyoza and a medical food [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-26, revealed].

### 2.6 Chay makers

Chay makers are the most concentrated cluster of import-dependent plant-protein buyers: An Nhien (imported Serbian soy protein concentrate on its label; Serbia shipped 259 t of HS 2106.10 to Vietnam in 2025 at USD 0.85/kg), Au Lac (exporter), Thoai An (gluten 35% in *chả lụa chay*, vegetarian pork roll), LC Foods (TVP granules 4 to 7 mm), SG Food, Thanh Dung and the chay lines of Vissan, CJ Cau Tre and Cholimex [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01; BUY-02, revealed]. Cholimex's new Tay Ninh (former Long An) plant adds 10,000 t/yr of EU-coded frozen food capacity [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-27, revealed]. A 2021 statement by the founder of VMEAT, a Vietnamese plant-based meat startup, captures buyer logic: more than 90% of its raw materials were imported because domestic materials had "two problems, quality and price", with domestic soybeans more than twice the price of US and Canadian imports [VN-direct, Low, BUY-38, stated].

### 2.7 Sports nutrition

The importers most often matched to "soy protein isolate" shipments are sports-supplement traders (Vietnam BOCCI, Nutri Sport) bringing in finished whey and isolate tubs, plus Brenntag Vietnam [VN-direct, Low, BUY-08, revealed]. Sports nutrition is an import market for finished goods, not an ingredient market, and it inflates the unit values of HS 2106.10 and 3504 (US HS 2106.10 averaged USD 19.47/kg in 2024) [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-02, inferred].

A new ingredient must decide which rung it is competing on.

### 3.2 Domestic list prices

We found no published Vietnamese B2B prices. VMC Group's Da Nang branch and TDGIMEX list Chinese vital wheat gluten (Xinrui brand, 25 kg bags) as *liên hệ* (contact for price), and VMC notes that price policy varies by region [VN-direct, High, BUY-36; BUY-37, revealed]. The supply study's retail prices for dried TVP (VND 327,000 to 500,000 per kg) are 8 to 12 times the Chinese border unit value, so distributor and retail margins, not ingredient cost, set consumer prices (supply study, FORM-01).

### 3.3 Specifications that buyers reveal

- **Meat pastes and sausages (*giò*, *chả*, *xúc xích*, pate):** gel strength, water and fat binding, emulsion stability through retort sterilisation, bland flavour, light colour; used with starch, wheat fibre, phosphates and carrageenan [VN-direct, High, BUY-05, revealed]. Thai work on light pork sausages found 2% soy isolate raised moisture and cooking yield and cut purge loss (transfer assumption: similar emulsified products and processing) [VN-adjacent, Medium, BUY-39, tested]. Soy and milk proteins treated with transglutaminase improved sausage texture at reduced phosphate [general, Medium, BUY-43, tested]. Soy isolate gel behaviour depends on its protein composition and processing history, so two isolates at the same price can perform differently in the same paste [general, Medium, BUY-42, tested]. We found no published Vietnamese study on soy protein in *giò lụa* in an OpenAlex search (September 2026), a gap for R&D bodies [VN-direct, Low, our literature search, inferred].
- **Plant milks and nutrition drinks:** solubility, low viscosity and stability through UHT, neutral flavour, complete amino acid profile, and documentation for soy-free, lactose-free, gluten-free, non-GMO and glyphosate-free claims [VN-direct, High, BUY-16, revealed]. Mung bean protein isolates of 85 to 88% protein have been made from Thai-grown beans, Malaysian work has benchmarked mung bean isolate against soy isolate for textured protein, and reviews report that functionality varies with extraction and modification (transfer assumption: similar beans and processes in Vietnam) [VN-adjacent, Medium, BUY-47; BUY-40; BUY-41, tested].
- **Chay analogues:** granule size (4 to 7 mm), slices, rehydration ratio, chewiness, non-GMO claims and Halal certificates for exporters (An Nhien, Au Lac), 20 to 25 kg bags (supply study, FORM-01, ECO-26).
- **Instant noodles:** particle size and rehydration of garnish, heat and oil stability, cost per pack (inferred from product formats) [VN-direct, Low, BUY-22, inferred].
- **All segments:** certificates of analysis and traceable origin. Authorities in Hung Yen alone handled 473 food-safety cases from mid-December 2025 and fined a nut-milk maker whose product failed its own self-declared specification; police urged firms to avoid raw materials of unclear origin [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-45, revealed]. USDA notes that 2025 scandals at long-established canned-meat and nutrition-drink processors pushed buyers towards "safe, high-quality food ingredients" [VN-direct, Medium, MAC-31, stated].

Minimum order quantities and shelf-life requirements were not published by any buyer we could read (open question 4).

## 4. The organised channel for ingredient demand

- **Direct import versus distributors.** Large processors import major ingredients (tree nuts, wheat flour, milk powder) directly; minor ingredients such as additives and flavours come through importers and distributors, and in-person contact is expected [VN-direct, Medium, MAC-31, stated]. Plant proteins sit between: large buyers can import containers (16 t pea protein shipments in 2025, BUY-09), while SMEs buy 20 to 25 kg bags from distributors.
- **Distributors.** VMC Group (claims 19 branches, supply study), TDGIMEX, Brenntag Vietnam (food and nutrition is a focus industry; named among soy isolate importers) and Barentz Vietnam (Fi Vietnam exhibitor) [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-08; BUY-33; BUY-35; BUY-36; BUY-37, revealed].
- **Trade fairs.** Fi Vietnam 2026 ran 13 to 15 May at SECC, Ho Chi Minh City; the organiser expected 300+ exhibitors from 40+ countries and 9,000+ attendees and lists the next edition for 17 to 19 May 2028 [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-32, stated]. The 9 May 2026 exhibitor list shows about 250 exhibitors, a large majority Chinese. Protein-relevant names include Yantai Shuangta Food (known as a pea starch and pea protein producer; identification ours), a US dairy booth shared with the US Dairy Export Council liaison office (Actus Nutrition, Alamfoods, Darigold, Proliant Dairy Ingredients, Saputo, Tedford Tellico), the EU Dairy Programme (Bord Bia), Indian egg-powder makers (Eggway, Ovo Farm, Ovobel, SKM Egg Products), gelatin and collagen makers (Anigel, Athos Collagen) and Morinaga Milk Vietnam. We saw no major soy isolate brand [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-33, revealed]. ProPak Vietnam (processing and packaging) runs 10 to 12 March 2027, with ProPak Hanoi on 13 to 15 October 2026 [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-34, stated]. We could not load the Vietnam Foodexpo site.
- **Associations.** The Vietnam Association of Food Science and Technology (VAFoST) runs seminars and projects; its home page shows no alternative-protein activity [VN-direct, Low, BUY-44, revealed]. The HCMC Food and Foodstuff Association site refused access.
- **How ingredients get specified.** No buyer publishes qualification steps or times. The evidence we have is indirect: Nutifood's hospital MOU shows a clinical-evidence route for medical nutrition; Vinamilk's claims show documentation duties that pass to suppliers; enforcement of self-declared specifications raises the value of certificates of analysis [VN-direct, Low, BUY-16; BUY-19; BUY-45, inferred]. Qualification time is the most important unknown for a founder (open question 4).

## Implications for frontier actors

- **Founders.** Sell first to chay makers and co-packers, with a domestic TVP that matches Chinese landed cost; that is the only segment where import substitution, fast trials and switching intent coincide. For an isolate, design for the dairy buyer: soy-free, non-GMO, soluble and UHT-stable, priced between Chinese and Western pea, with a plan to sell the starch co-product to noodle makers. Pitch fungal biomass on function and flavour against egg and dairy proteins, never on protein cost against soy. Expect prices to be negotiated through distributors and trials to be won with certificates, not claims.
- **R&D bodies and universities.** Publish functional tests of local proteins in Vietnamese formats (*giò lụa*, *xúc xích*, UHT nut milk, noodle garnish); we found no published Vietnamese study of soy protein in *giò lụa*. Offer pilot-scale samples in 20 to 25 kg lots, which is how SME buyers test.
- **Investors and development finance.** Plant-protein imports are worth USD 87 to 109 million a year at the border (2023 to 2025), an unknown share of it feed, and prices are capped by Chinese supply; returns depend on volume, co-products and distribution, not premiums. Dairy and nutrition buyers pay 3 to 5 times more per kg of protein but qualify suppliers slowly.
- **Funders and philanthropies.** The cheapest high-value public good is a declaration-level customs dataset and a standing panel of 10 to 15 R&D buyers; both would resolve the food and feed split and the qualification-time question for every founder.
- **Policy makers.** National eight-digit subheadings for soy isolate, pea protein and TVP would make demand visible. Low MFN duties (3 to 10%) give domestic makers little protection, so competitiveness has to come from cost, quality and logistics. Clear treatment of precision-fermented proteins under the new Food Safety Law matters because leading buyers market "non-GMO".
- **Food manufacturers.** Joint development with a local protein maker can secure soy-free and traceable supply at lower landed cost than Western pea. Hybrid lines already exist in value ranges; the open question is whether a clean-label "meat plus mushroom" or "meat plus pulse" story can travel up to premium lines that now sell on meat content.
- **What not to do.** Do not read HS 3504 or 2106.10 totals as food-isolate demand; do not position an alternative protein against premium meat lines that market "88% meat"; do not build a consumer brand to reach B2B buyers; do not expect list prices.

## What would change this view

- Declaration-level customs data showing that food takes most (or very little) of the Chinese plant-protein tonnage.
- A large processor (Masan MEATLife, Vissan, C.P., CJ) launching hybrid or plant-based lines, or Vinamilk dropping pea protein.
- A shift in Chinese export prices (supply shocks, trade remedies) or in preferential tariffs for Chinese proteins.
- A ruling that precision-fermented proteins are GMO food ingredients requiring labelling, or a novel-food route in the new Food Safety Law.
- Evidence that ingredient qualification at large buyers takes months rather than years (or the reverse).
- Protein-fortified instant noodles from Acecook or Masan Consumer.
