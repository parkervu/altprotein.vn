# Does export origin give domestic textured protein a customer? Rules of origin and recipe costing for Vietnamese wrapped foods

Line: W9-L4 Does export origin give domestic textured protein a customer? (wave 9). Source prefix: ORG. Date: 25 September 2026.

## Verdict

No. Under every agreement we read, a dumpling or other stuffed pasta (HS 1902.20) made with imported Chinese textured soy keeps its origin: the EU and UK rules cap non-originating flour, starch, rice, meat and fish, not soy protein, and CPTPP and RCEP ask only for a change of chapter. Imported textured soy counts only in chapter 21 *chay* (vegetarian) food preparations bound for the EU, or for the UK under its bilateral agreement, and even there it may make up 10% of product weight or, on our reading of the text, 10% of the ex-works price, which at observed prices allows about a third of the weight or more; soy isolate and wheat gluten pass without limit, and UK importers can claim CPTPP instead (1.6% in 2026). Where the rule binds, switching to domestic textured soy costs at most USD 0.06 per kg of product against USD 0.38 to 0.53 of duty saved, but reformulation or the value tolerance gets the same result, so domestic textured protein (play T1) gains at most a sliver: seasoned dry textured soy exported as such, of which the EU imported USD 0.01 million from Vietnam in 2025. Yield class: **clean negative** for T1, with a **correction** to wave 8's proposed origin constraint (the binding EU recipe item is wheat flour, not soy, and the UK constraint can be avoided) and a small **added constraint** (Vietnam cannot ship meat-filled wrapped foods to the EU, so plant fillings are the EU substitute for pork).

## Headline findings

1. **The EU origin text is now verified and matches the UK text word for word.** EVFTA Protocol 1, Article 5 and Annex II, read from the Publications Office copy of the agreement, contains the same chapter 11, 19, 20 and 21 rules and the same tolerance article as the UK-Vietnam Origin Reference Document. Weight caps apply only to non-originating materials (Annex I, Note 4.2) [VN-direct, High, ORG-01; XBA-30, revealed].
2. **In a dumpling, imported textured soy does not matter.** The chapter 19 rule is a change of heading with caps on non-originating materials of headings 1006 and 1101 to 1108 (rice, flours, starches), chapters 2, 3 and 16 (meat, fish), chapter 4 and sugar. Textured soy (2106.10), soy isolate (3504) and wheat gluten (1109) are outside every cap [VN-direct, High, ORG-01, revealed].
3. **The binding EU recipe item is the wrapper.** Non-originating flour, starch or rice may not exceed 20% of product weight, the general tolerance cannot lift that cap, and flour milled in Vietnam from imported wheat is non-originating because chapter 11 requires wholly obtained cereals. Vietnamese rice flour and cassava starch are originating [VN-direct, High, ORG-01, revealed].
4. **In chay food preparations (2106.90), imported textured soy is allowed up to 10% of product weight or ex-works price.** On the value reading, Chinese textured soy at USD 1.27 to 1.34 per kg CIF may make up about 32 to 46% of the weight of a product selling at USD 4.27 to 5.87 per kg (our calculation). On the weight reading, 10% dry textured soy equals about 5 to 7 g of protein per 100 g [VN-direct, High for the rule and Medium for the value reading, ORG-01; TIC-12, revealed (rule) and inferred].
5. **CPTPP and RCEP do not bind at all.** Both need only a change of chapter for 1902; CPTPP needs a change of subheading (or 30 to 40% regional value content) for 2106.90, and treats flour milled in Vietnam from imported wheat as originating; RCEP needs a change of heading or 40% regional value content for 2106.90 [VN-direct, High, ORG-04; XBA-30; ORG-06, revealed].
6. **For the UK the constraint is avoidable.** Vietnamese goods can claim UK-CPTPP: stuffed pasta 0% (as under the bilateral agreement), prawn dumplings 0% (1% under the bilateral agreement), meat dumplings 0% (GBP 5.60 per 100 kg bilateral) and chay preparations 1.6% in 2026 (0% bilateral, 8% MFN) [VN-direct, High, ORG-03, revealed].
7. **The EU duty at stake on a vegetable or plant-protein dumpling is 9.5 to 12.9% of value** (USD 0.42 to 0.68 per kg at USD 4.27 to 5.87 per kg): MFN 8.3% plus EUR 6.10 (cooked) or EUR 17.10 (uncooked) per 100 kg against 0% since August 2020 (our calculation). Chay preparations pay 9% plus an agricultural component [VN-direct, High, ORG-02; ORG-15; XBA-28; XBA-29, revealed].
8. **Meat and vegetable versions differ sharply for the EU.** Meat-filled stuffed pasta from Vietnam still pays EUR 6.787 per 100 kg in 2026 (MFN EUR 54.30), fish-filled 1% (MFN 8.5%), vegetable 0%; and Vietnam is not on the EU list for entry of meat products (Implementing Regulation 2021/404 Annex XV as adopted), while it is listed for fishery products. EU-bound Vietnamese wrapped foods are therefore seafood or plant-filled [VN-direct, High for tariffs and Medium for the listing, ORG-02; ORG-10; ORG-11, revealed].
9. **Japan's edge on Vietnamese dumplings is larger than any edge the package reports: 17.5 points.** Japan charges 21.3% (WTO) on stuffed pasta other than prawn-led lines and gives China and Thailand no concession; Vietnam pays 3.8% under CPTPP in FY2026, with no recipe condition beyond a change of chapter. Canada charges 11% MFN and nothing under CPTPP [VN-direct, High, ORG-05; ORG-07, revealed].
10. **The recipe switch is cheap but unnecessary.** Replacing 10 to 20% dry Chinese textured soy with domestic supply changes cost by minus USD 0.14 to plus 0.06 per kg of product (wave 7 costs; our calculation). Where the EU weight limit binds, the duty saved is at least seven times the worst-case cost, but cutting imported textured soy to 10% and adding isolate or gluten meets the rule at similar cost [VN-direct, Low, TIC-12; TIC-13; BUY-02; ORG-02, inferred].
11. **Vietnamese wrapped foods abroad get their protein from prawns, not soy.** A Marks and Spencer prawn gyoza made in Vietnam (sold in the UK, Belgium, France and the Netherlands) is 37% tiger prawn at 8.8 g protein per 100 g; Migros dim sum are 40 to 50% shrimp; a Mr Chen's vegetable gyoza uses a rice flour, corn starch and tapioca wrapper and has 3.6 g. None lists textured soy [VN-direct, Low, ORG-12; XBA-26, revealed].
12. **Certified plants could not be counted.** The BRCGS Directory and the IFS database sit behind logins. Company pages show BRC or BRCGS claims at Cholimex Food (with EU establishment codes for frozen foods), GN Foods and SG Food; CJ's Kizuna plant states FSSC 22000 [VN-direct, Low to Medium, ORG-13; ORG-14; AIB-20; XBA-20, revealed].

Four details matter for recipes [VN-direct, High, ORG-01, revealed (rule)]:

- **Caps count only non-originating materials.** Vietnamese farmed shrimp and pork, rice and cassava are wholly obtained, so a shrimp dumpling with Vietnamese prawns and a rice or cassava wrapper meets every cap.
- **The tolerance cannot lift a cap.** Article 5(4) stops the 10% general tolerance from exceeding the 20% flour cap.
- **Milling counts.** Annex I Note 3.5 says non-originating chapter 10 cereals are not limited by the 20% cap, because the cap names flours; flour bought from a Vietnamese mill that grinds imported wheat is itself non-originating (chapter 11 rule) and counts.
- **The tolerance text offers weight or value.** Article 5(3)(a) reads "10 per cent of the weight of the product or ex-works price" for chapters 2 and 4 to 24. The value of a material is its customs value at import (Article 1(o)). We read this as the exporter's choice; an official view would settle it (open question).

Classification decides the rule. A spring roll with a pasta-type wrapper is stuffed pasta (chapter 19); one classed as prepared vegetables (2004 or 2005) needs only a change of heading with at most 20% sugar, so neither flour nor textured soy can break it; one with more than 20% meat classed in 1602 needs Vietnamese-raised meat [VN-direct, High, ORG-01, revealed (rule)]. We did not find a binding classification for chay spring rolls (package MA-08 already flags this).

**For T1 itself** the rules set a design condition, not a demand: domestic textured soy extruded from imported defatted soy flour (1208) or soybean meal (2304) is originating under all four agreements; extruded from imported soy concentrate (2106.10, the same subheading) it is not [VN-direct, High, ORG-01; ORG-04; ORG-06, revealed (rule)]. Wave 7 L5 costed a flour-based line (TIC-12; TIC-13), which meets the condition.

Three readings [VN-direct, Medium, ORG-02; ORG-05; ORG-10, inferred]:

- **The EU is the only market where the recipe can cost Vietnam its preference.** In the UK, Japan and Canada, CPTPP gives the same or nearly the same rate with a change of chapter. In the US there is no preference to lose.

The practical EU substitute for a pork filling is plant protein, which strengthens TPP-08's product choice without helping T1.

What the numbers say [VN-direct, Low, TIC-12; TIC-13; BUY-02; ORG-01; ORG-02, inferred]:

- **Where the rule binds, the duty dwarfs the switch cost.** In an EU-bound chay preparation with 15 to 20% dry textured soy, the duty saved (USD 0.38 to 0.53 per kg plus an agricultural component) is at least seven times the worst-case switch cost (USD 0.06).
- **But the rule rarely binds, and cheaper fixes exist.** A maker can cut imported textured soy to 10% of weight and add soy isolate or wheat gluten, which are other headings; or document that the imported soy is under 10% of the ex-works price. Neither needs a domestic extruder.
- **The only case where domestic textured soy is necessary is seasoned dry textured soy exported as textured soy.** The market is negligible: Vietnam exported USD 0.52 million of 2106.10 in 2023 (EXP-39), and the protein-rich dry pieces found in Europe (An Nhien) are made from isolate (XBA-26), which is already originating.
- **The recipe item worth costing for the EU is the wrapper, not the filling.** Vietnamese cassava starch sold at VND 12,500 to 13,200 per kg (USD 0.48 to 0.51) in March 2026 (FS-12) and is originating; we did not cost a rice-flour or starch wrapper against wheat.

EU imports of 2106.90 from Vietnam doubled to USD 66.7 million (37,278 t) in 2025, at USD 1.79 per kg (XBA-28; our calculation), but the line is a catch-all and its chay share is unknown [VN-direct, Medium, XBA-28, revealed].

## 7. Answer: does export origin give T1 a customer?

**No, and further desk work on this question would be noise.** The recipe condition on Vietnam's tariff edge is real but falls on wheat flour and starch in EU-bound chapter 19 foods, and on meat fillings, not on textured soy. The single place where imported textured soy can cost a Vietnamese exporter its preference (EU-bound chay preparations above 10% dry textured soy by weight, if the value reading is refused) has cheaper fixes and no observed volume. T1's case remains import substitution for domestic chay makers, as Part VI says.

What should change:

- **ch27 (27.3).** Add an origin paragraph based on the verified EU text; narrow wave 8's proposed Barrier 4 to the flour and starch cap and the meat listing; add Japan's 17.5-point and Canada's 11-point edges on stuffed pasta; note that meat-filled wrapped foods cannot go to the EU from Vietnam.
- **TPP-08.** Replace the textured soy clause wave 8 proposed for *avoid* with the EU flour cap; add rice-flour or cassava-starch wrappers for EU buyers; note that plant fillings are the EU substitute for pork; ship to the UK, Japan and Canada under CPTPP.
- **TPP-01.** Add the design condition (extrude from soy flour or meal, not concentrate) and state that origin rules give domestic textured soy no export premium.
- **T1 demand check.** Add the clean negative; market pull stays at 5 and the kill test is unchanged.
- **Manufacturer brief, export angles.** Add the wrapper check and the CPTPP route; say that switching to domestic textured soy does not change the origin of a dumpling.
- **OQ-244.** Close, with a residual question on the value basis of the tolerance.

## Next-wave candidates

1. **Preference utilisation of Vietnamese stuffed pasta in the EU.** A *deeper* version of this line, and one desk research could probably close (DG Trade utilisation data or a Comext extraction by tariff regime). A high utilisation rate would show that exporters already meet the flour cap and would lower the priority of wrapper advice in TPP-08; a low rate would make the wrapper the first fix for EU buyers. It does not bear on T1.
2. **Official view on the tolerance basis (weight or value).** *Deeper*, not closable by desk: it needs a MOIT answer or an EU binding origin decision. It changes recipe guidance for EU-bound chay preparations only; no T1 conclusion depends on it, so it is low priority.
3. **Wrapper composition and cost for EU-bound lines.** A *different kind* of question (technical economics with exporters): flour share by weight in current dumplings, spring rolls and bao, and the cost of rice-flour or cassava-starch wrappers. Closable only with three exporter calls. A cheap compliant wrapper would make the EU route in TPP-08 practical; it would also be a small new use for cassava starch, not for textured protein.
4. **Japanese buyers of Vietnamese wrapped foods.** A *different kind* of question: with a 17.5-point edge on stuffed pasta, who in Japan buys Vietnamese dumplings and would they take a plant-protein SKU? Needs importer contact (JETRO), not desk work. A yes would add Japan beside the UK and EU in TPP-08.

Further desk work on origin rules for T1 would be **noise**: the rules are read for the EU, UK, CPTPP and RCEP, and none creates a customer for domestic textured soy.

## Limits

- **Interpretation.** Our reading that the 10% tolerance may be measured by value is based on the treaty wording, not on a ruling. The weight limit is the conservative case, and we report both.
- **Classification.** Which heading a given chay product or spring roll falls in (1902, 1602, 2004, 2005, 2106) decides the rule; we found no binding classification for chay spring rolls.
- **Agreements not read.** The Japan-Vietnam EPA, ASEAN-Japan, Korea-Vietnam and ASEAN-Korea texts, and Korean and Australian preferential rates, were not reached. Japan's schedule shows no Japan-Vietnam EPA concession on the two lines that matter, so CPTPP is the operative route there.
- **Animal-health lists.** The EU meat-products list was read as adopted in March 2021; later amendments were not checked. UK, Japanese and Korean meat rules were not checked.
- **Costs.** T1 costs are wave 7 estimates (Low). Product values are stuffed-pasta unit values used as proxies for chay preparations. The EU agricultural component on 2106.90 was not calculated. The value tolerance uses a CIF price for Chinese textured soy derived from its FOB value plus 3 to 8% freight.
- **Products.** Open Food Facts records are crowd-sourced and their origin fields may describe an ingredient; only printed percentages were used.
- **Certification.** No count of BRCGS or IFS sites was possible; company claims were not verified against certificates.
- **Currency.** EUR and GBP duties are converted at ECB rates for 24 September 2026; the result shifts by a few cents per kg with the exchange rate.
