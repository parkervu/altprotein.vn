# What is on public plates outside the big cities and in factory canteens

Line: W9-L2 WHAT IS ON PUBLIC PLATES OUTSIDE THE BIG CITIES AND IN FACTORY CANTEENS (wave 9). Source prefix: UPL. Date: 25 September 2026.

## Verdict

Upland boarding plates are meat plates. In 13 ethnic-minority boarding schools in five provinces that publish their menus (22 school-weeks, 353 meals, January to September 2026), 240 of 242 lunches and dinners carried a meat, fish or egg dish, pork led 74% of them, no lunch was animal-free, and the four kitchens that publish cost sheets bought 107 to 215 g of raw pork per pupil a day, including a Decree 339 border-commune lunch that spent 91 to 97% of its non-rice money on pork. The thin plate is breakfast (86% carry little or no animal food), not lunch, and the only kitchen-weighed boarding diets we found met protein and zinc needs but not calcium or vitamin A; so wave 8's zinc-fortified soy dish base (TPP-11) has no observed gap to fill in the kitchens it names, and the upland school part of ch21.2 should change. Factory caterers' published menus lead with pork (33% of protein dishes), fish (26%) and chicken (13%), put tofu in 9% (plant-only 3%) and name no textured soy, which lowers the canteen route's animals per tonne from about 1,090 to about 745 and leaves wave 7's welfare conclusion intact. Yield class: **changed a conclusion** (TPP-11 and the upland school framing in ch21.2) and **added a route** (self-run upland school kitchens on Decree 66, 188 and 339 money, which already replace about a third of their pork with tofu on two lunches in five), with a weak update to funder units; the benchmark of about 19,000 t of protein by 2035 does not move.

## Headline findings

1. **Lunch and dinner almost always carry meat.** Of 242 lunches and dinners coded, 240 (99.2%) carried a meat, fish or egg dish and 217 (89.7%) carried meat; pork led 179 (74.0%), chicken 31 (12.8%), fish 14 (5.8%) and egg 13 (5.4%). All 124 lunches carried animal food and 119 (96.0%) carried meat. The two exceptions were a take-home bread and milk supper on Fridays at one school. [VN-direct, High, UPL-01 to UPL-17, revealed]
2. **Breakfast is the thin meal.** Of 111 breakfasts, 38 (34.2%) had no animal food (bread, *xôi đỗ* (sticky rice with beans), plain instant noodles) and 57 (51.4%) had only a small amount (pork floss, sausage slices, one egg, a milk carton): 85.6% carry little or no animal food. Across all 353 meals, 27.5% carry little or no animal food, almost all of them breakfasts. [VN-direct, High, UPL-01 to UPL-17, revealed]
3. **Kitchens buy a lot of pork.** Published cost sheets give 165 g of raw pork per pupil at a pork lunch and 107 g plus 79 g of tofu at a tofu lunch (Muong Nhe, 759 boarders), 110 to 215 g of pork per pupil a day at two Leng Su Sin schools and 104 g a meal at Sen Thuong (our calculation from kg bought and pupils stated). A Decree 339 lunch at Muong Nhe bought 107 to 179 g of pork per pupil for VND 19,871 to 27,071, with pork taking 97.3% of the non-rice spend on pork days. [VN-direct, High, UPL-01; UPL-04; UPL-07; UPL-09; UPL-10, revealed]
4. **Tofu stretches pork; nothing else plant-based appears.** Tofu is in 55 of 242 lunches and dinners (22.7%), always beside or inside a pork dish except one egg-and-tofu lunch. At Muong Nhe the tofu lunch uses 35% less pork and costs 27% less than the pork lunch for about 8% less protein (our calculation). No textured soy, gluten or *chay* (vegetarian) mock meat appears in 353 meals. [VN-direct, High, UPL-01; UPL-01 to UPL-17, revealed and inferred]
5. **Lunch zinc is met where menus are published.** With rice and at lean-pork values, observed boarding and Decree 339 lunches carry 6.7 to 8.2 mg of zinc and a meatless egg-and-tofu lunch 4.9 mg, above the lunch share of 1.7 to 3.4 mg that wave 8 derived from Decision 3958 for a diet with some animal food; VND 8,000 charity lunches carry 2.4 to 3.2 mg, enough at age 6 to 7 and at the edge for boys aged 10 to 11 (our calculation). Kitchen-weighed diets at two boarding schools in Yen Bai (now Lao Cai) in 2019 supplied 74.4 g of protein (38% animal) and zinc at 131 to 157% of need, but calcium at 36.5%, vitamin A at 41 to 45% and vitamin B2 at 48 to 52%; a Thai Nguyen study reports protein and zinc met. [VN-direct, Low for our calculation; High, UPL-18; Low, UPL-19 (abstract); APR-04; revealed and inferred]
6. **The money rules are new and larger than Part VI records.** Semi-boarders get VND 936,000 a month and 15 kg of rice (Decree 66/2025); full boarders in the new boarding schools VND 1,170,000 and 15 kg, midday boarders VND 450,000 and 8 kg (Decree 188/2026, signed 27 May 2026); about 417,708 border-commune pupils get VND 450,000 and 8 kg for lunch (Decree 339/2025; more than VND 2,000 billion and 30,000 t of rice a year at draft stage). At the observed spending pattern, Decree 339 money alone would buy about 9,100 to 10,900 t of pork a year (our calculation). [VN-direct, High and Medium, UPL-20; UPL-21; UPL-22; APR-09; UPL-04, revealed and inferred]
7. **Plates can be cut, and charity plates are thin.** A charity-funded lunch at VND 8,000 gives 34 g of pork with 82 g of tofu, or 1.7 duck eggs; a Lao Cai principal was arrested in 2024 after rations at a boarding primary school fell below the regulated norm; a feed company's top-up of about VND 10,000 a meal added a third dish and breakfasts at three schools. [VN-direct, High, UPL-08; Medium, UPL-24; UPL-25, revealed]
8. **Canteen protein dishes are pork, fish and chicken; tofu is a minority.** In 172 protein dishes on five caterers' published menus (2023 to 2025), pork leads 33.1%, fish 25.6%, chicken 13.4%, egg 9.9%, tofu 8.7% (plant-only 2.9%) and beef 5.8%; minced or processed meat is 17.4%. Of meat-led dishes, 62.6% are pork, 26.4% poultry and 11.0% beef. One caterer serves *chay* dishes two days a month; none names textured soy or mock meat. [VN-direct, Medium, UPL-27 to UPL-33, stated]
9. **Canteen prices and portions.** Tiers run from VND 17,000 to 50,000: one protein dish at VND 20,000 and two at 25,000 (Hoang Kim); at VND 20,000 to 25,000 "pork, tofu and basa", at 25,000 to 30,000 chicken and sea fish, at 35,000 to 40,000 beef and mackerel, with a main dish of 90 to 120 g (Haseca). Hai Phong's 2026 average lunch is about VND 27,000 (west) to 31,000 (east), with some firms at VND 17,000. [VN-direct, Medium, UPL-27; UPL-29; UPL-32; UPL-34, stated and revealed]
10. **Animals per canteen meal stay low.** At the observed caterer meat mix a canteen meal replaced spares about 0.015 animals (0.010 to 0.029 across caterers) against 0.020 at the national mix and 0.134 in ACE's estimate for Sinergia; a tonne of protein through canteens spares about 745 animals (51 pigs, 691 birds, 4 cattle) rather than about 1,090 (our calculation, wave 7 L3 method). Upland school main meals, 84% pork among meat-led meals, give 0.009. [VN-adjacent, Low, UPL-27 to UPL-32; FUF-03; AFN-26, inferred]

## 1. Method and sample

**How we reached the menus.** Wave 7 L1 read urban primary schools only. Here we searched in the desktop browser (Google, then DuckDuckGo's HTML service, which returns real addresses) for *thực đơn* (menu), *bán trú* (semi-boarding), *PTDTBT* (*phổ thông dân tộc bán trú*, semi-boarding school for ethnic minority pupils), *dân tộc nội trú* (full boarding school) and *Nghị định 339*, and used school sites' own search and listing pages. Dien Bien, Quang Ngai and Cao Bang school sites were fetched directly; Lai Chau and Son La files were read in the browser, with PDF text extracted there. Scanned PDFs that we could download were rendered and read as images. We coded every 2026 upland boarding menu we could read, one to five weeks per school (September 2026 where published, otherwise the latest readable 2026 week); schools and weeks were not chosen by content. Older menus (2024) and a garbled text layer were skipped.

**Gaps.** Lao Cai school sites (gdlaocai.edu.vn) failed after one page loaded; a Tuyen Quang school site failed in the browser; Dak Lak school sites and one Son La site refused our fetch tool and were not retried in the browser; and scanned PDFs on browser-only sites (Khao Mang in Lao Cai, Lang Mo in Lai Chau) could not be read because the browser pane was hidden and screenshots failed. No school from Tuyen Quang (including former Ha Giang), Lao Cai, Gia Lai or Dak Lak is in the sample. Lao Cai enters through the Yen Bai diet study and one press case.

**The sample** (`data_upland_school_weeks.csv`): 13 schools, 22 school-weeks, 353 meals (124 lunches, 118 dinners, 111 breakfasts), January to September 2026.

**Coding** (`data_upland_school_meals.csv`). Each meal is coded by its protein sources in the order printed (pork, chicken, duck, beef, fish, seafood, egg, milk, tofu, legume, peanut), its lead animal source, whether it has meat, and an animal-food level: *dish* (at least one meat, fish or egg dish), *small* (animal food only as a garnish or small item: pork floss, sausage in noodles, one egg in noodles, a milk carton, a meat bun or rice cake) or *none*. "Little or no animal food" is *small* plus *none*. Minced or processed meat dishes (loaves, sausages, spring rolls, meatballs, minced pork, stuffed tofu) are flagged because they are the blend slot wave 7 L1 identified.

**Robustness.** Taking one week per school gives 0.7% of lunches and dinners and 85.1% of breakfasts with little or no animal food. Semi-boarding schools (10) and full boarding schools (3) differ only in breakfast: full boarders' breakfasts always carry some animal food (noodles with egg and sausage, sticky rice with pork floss, pho with minced pork). Per school, pork leads 43 to 100% of lunches and dinners (mean 72%) and tofu appears in 0 to 70%. Four schools repeat a fixed weekly cycle, so more weeks from them would add rows but not information.

**Typical plates.** A Muong Nhe week: breakfast of sticky rice with pork floss or minced pork, or bread with mung-bean filling; lunch of stir-fried pork or pork with tofu in tomato sauce, with pumpkin or cabbage soup; dinner of pork with a vegetable, local chicken with ginger once a week, duck-egg omelette on Sunday night [UPL-01 to UPL-03]. Lai Chau schools add pork loaf (*giò*), fish cake and duck eggs [UPL-11 to UPL-13]; the Cao Bang and Na Hy boarding schools serve two protein dishes a meal, including Chinese sausage, spring rolls and minced pork [UPL-15; UPL-17]; Mang Den in the Central Highlands braises pork with egg, pork loaf, fish or shrimp and serves no tofu at all [UPL-16].

**Against the city.** Wave 7 L1 found pork leading 38% of urban primary lunch days and plant protein on 15.1% of days, almost always tofu beside meat [PMR-10 to PMR-31]. Upland boarding kitchens are more pork-heavy (74% of main meals) and use tofu more often (22.7%), but the same way: inside or beside a pork dish.

**Press and studies that point the other way.** Before the 2025 increase, a principal in Bac Ha (Lao Cai) diverted VND 765 million of Decree 116 support and rations fell below the regulated norm [UPL-24]; a school in Son La had two dishes a meal until a feed company (De Heus) added about VND 10,000 a meal, and pupils at two schools skipped breakfast [UPL-25]; a border school in Nghe An spends VND 17,000 to 18,000 a meal on at least two main dishes [UPL-23]. The published menus describe schools that publish, and plans rather than plates; the cost sheets are purchases, but from four schools.

**Pork takes the budget.** On Decree 339 pork days at Muong Nhe, pork is 97.3% of the lunch spend (rice is supplied in kind); on tofu days pork and tofu are 91.5% [UPL-04]. The Muong Nhe boarders' spend of about VND 54,000 to 55,000 a day is above what Decree 66's VND 936,000 a month gives over 20 to 23 days (VND 40,700 to 46,800); the sheet does not say what funds the difference.

**Tofu is the kitchen's extender.** On a Muong Nhe pork lunch the kitchen buys 125 kg of pork (VND 18.5 million, about 23.8 kg of protein at lean-pork values); on a tofu lunch 81 kg of pork and 60 kg of tofu (VND 13.5 million, about 21.9 kg of protein): 35% less pork, 27% less money, 8% less protein (our calculation). This is revealed pork-stretching under a fixed per-pupil budget, done with tofu at VND 25,000 to 28,000 a kg.

**The money rules** (`data_meal_money_and_prices.csv`). Decree 66/2025 (in force May 2025): VND 936,000 a month and 15 kg of rice per semi-boarder for up to nine months [UPL-20]. Decree 188/2026 (27 May 2026): VND 1,170,000 and 15 kg per full boarder in the new boarding schools, VND 450,000 and 8 kg per midday boarder, plus VND 100,000 a pupil a month for running costs [UPL-21]. Decree 339/2025: VND 450,000 and 8 kg a month for lunch in land-border communes [APR-09], for about 417,708 pupils (58% primary), more than VND 2,000 billion and 30,000 t of rice a year at draft stage [UPL-22]. Kitchens are run by schools; supplier notices ask for a business registration, a food-safety certificate, a quality commitment and school supply experience [UPL-26]. Charity and company money also flows: Nuoi Em lunches at VND 8,000 [UPL-08]; a Hope Foundation and De Heus top-up of about VND 10,000 a meal and 86,000 breakfasts in 2024 to 2025 [UPL-25].

**Illustrative size** (our calculation, Low). 417,708 pupils x VND 450,000 x 9 months = VND 1,692 billion a year. If 80 to 95% buys pork at VND 148,000 a kg, that is about 9,100 to 10,900 t of pork a year, or 1,700 to 2,100 t of protein at lean-pork values (an upper bound). The whole institutional displacement Part VI assumes for 2035 on the benchmark path is about 0.65 kt of meat protein.

## 6. Computations for the three questions

**(a) TPP-11 and the zinc gap.** Wave 8 L4's TPP-11 is a zinc-fortified soy dish base whose first buyers include Decree 339 border-commune kitchens, justified by lunches with little animal food. The kitchens it names spend their Decree 339 money on pork (107 to 179 g a pupil, 91 to 97% of the non-rice spend), and no upland lunch in 124 was animal-free. A fortified soy base sold to them would do what tofu already does: stretch pork. It would lower zinc a little (on the Muong Nhe tofu day, 6.7 against 7.5 mg) and would not fill a gap. Where plates are thin (breakfasts; VND 8,000 charity lunches; schools where rations are cut), the documented shortfalls in weighed diets are calcium, vitamin A and B2 more than zinc. TPP-11 as specified has no observed buyer need.

**(b) Animals per meal for the canteen route** (`data_animals_per_meal.csv`; wave 7 L3 method: one 20 g protein dish replaced at 0.8 net displacement, 0.15 kg protein per kg carcass, FAOSTAT carcass weights [FUF-03]; meat-led dishes only).

Per tonne of protein delivered through canteens, the observed mix spares about 745 animals (51 pigs, 691 birds, 4 cattle and buffalo) against about 1,090 at the national mix; applied to D-BENCH's 649 t of R4 meat protein displaced in 2035, that is about 604,000 animals instead of 886,000, and all displacing routes about 2.4 million instead of 2.7 million (93% birds) (our calculation). **(c) TPP-04.** The canteen ingredient has two observed slots: the tofu slot (8.7% of protein dishes, mostly tofu with pork), where it competes with tofu as wave 7 L1 and L4 found, and the minced or processed meat slot (17.4%), where a blend displaces pork. Portions (90 to 120 g) support the 20 g protein target. A later buyer not in TPP-04 is the upland school kitchen, which already uses tofu to cut pork by a third on two lunches in five under fixed per-pupil budgets and buys through commune supplier notices that require a registration and a food-safety certificate.

## 7. What should change in ch21.2, ch25.3, ch28.4, TPP-04 and TPP-11

- **ch21.2.** Keep the under-five stunting statement; add that upland boarding school plates are meat-heavy and that weighed boarding diets met protein and zinc, with calcium, vitamin A, B2 and breakfast as the shortfalls; do not carry wave 8's "the gap is zinc" into the school-age text.
- **ch25.3.** Add a paragraph on upland boarding and border-commune meals (money rules, pork-led plates, tofu as the extender, no textured soy, supplier documents, governance risk) and add the canteen dish mix and 2026 prices to the factory-canteen paragraph.

## Next-wave candidates

1. **Served against planned meals in upland boarding schools.** *Different kind* (inspection records or a field check, not menus). Desk cannot close it; DOET inspection summaries might. If many kitchens serve well below their published plans, the thin-plate case returns and a cheap fortified item regains a buyer need; if plans hold, TPP-11 stays dropped.
2. **Breakfast nutrition in boarding schools.** *Different kind* (weighed intake). Not desk-closable. If breakfasts leave boarders short of protein, calcium or vitamin A, a fortified bread filling or sticky-rice topping is the plant product with a real public-meal slot; this would add a candidate, not revive TPP-11.
3. **National counts and budgets for Decree 66 and Decree 188.** *Deeper* version of this line; partly desk-closable (MOET or Ministry of Finance budget annexes). It sizes a public kitchen channel that buys mostly pork; a large count would make the upland kitchen route worth a TPP-04 trial arm.
4. **Would upland kitchens take a textured-soy pork extender?** *Different kind* (three calls). If kitchens and their commune suppliers would buy it at the tofu price per gram of protein, upland kitchens become a second school route that displaces pork (few animals, some import substitution).
5. **Served canteen menus with grams from three caterers.** *Deeper*; needs requests. It would narrow the 0.010 to 0.029 animals-per-meal range and the fish share. Given wave 7 L3's conclusion does not depend on it, this is low priority.

**Where to stop.** More upland menus from the same platforms would be noise: 13 schools in five provinces show the same pattern and several repeat a fixed weekly cycle. More caterers' sample menus would also be noise; the remaining uncertainty is about served plates and breakfasts, which need records or calls.

## Limits

- Selection: only schools that publish menus are in the sample, and the four cost sheets come from one border cluster in Dien Bien. Menus are plans; cost sheets are purchases, not intake. Lao Cai, Tuyen Quang (including former Ha Giang), Gia Lai and Dak Lak schools were not reached.
- Coding is from dish names; small quantities (pork floss, sausage slices) are judged as garnish without weights. Some menus are weekly templates.
- Nutrient figures use lean-pork values for fattier cuts (upper bounds), exclude vegetables, value duck eggs as hen eggs and, for charity lunches, assume 110 g of rice. The Yen Bai study is from 2019, before Decree 66; the Thai Nguyen study was read as an abstract only.
- Decree 66 and Decree 188 amounts come from government and press summaries; the decree texts were not read. The Decree 339 pupil count is a draft-stage estimate (September 2025).
- Canteen menus are caterers' marketing samples (2023 to 2025), four of five southern; one sample-menu author (Phu My) contributes 84 of 172 dishes. Non-caterer menu content (equipment sellers, an international-school caterer) was excluded. No served canteen menu with grams was found.
- Animals-per-meal figures inherit wave 7 L3's factors and count meat only; fish, which leads a quarter of canteen dishes, is not counted.
- Browser screenshots failed (the pane was hidden), so scanned PDFs on sites we could open only in the browser were not read; container fetches of several provincial platforms were refused.
