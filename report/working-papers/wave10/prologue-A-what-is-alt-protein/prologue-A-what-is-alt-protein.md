# Researcher A: what alternative protein is (prologue, edition 1.4)

Forty facts are in `facts.csv` (PA-01 to PA-40) and 22 new sources in `sources.csv` (PRA-01 to PRA-22). Package claims reuse package source IDs and quote the package text. "Glossary GL-xxx" marks a package term ID, not a source ID.

## 1. Protein and protein quality

Protein supplies nitrogen and the amino acids the body uses to build and repair tissue. The European Food Safety Authority sets the adult reference intake at 0.83 g of protein per kg of body weight a day [PRA-01]. Nine amino acids are essential: the body cannot make them, so food must supply them [PRA-02; glossary GL-003].

Two scores measure protein quality. PDCAAS is the older one and is capped at 1.0. DIAAS, which FAO recommended in 2013 to replace it, scores the digestibility of each essential amino acid and is not capped [PRA-02]. Measured in pigs, soy isolate scores about 97, pea concentrate 70, brown rice protein 42 and pork products 113 to 137 [SCI-40; NQR-07]. Human values can be lower [SCI-42]. "Crude protein" is nitrogen times 6.25, so it also counts the nucleic acids that are plentiful in microbial biomass [glossary GL-002].

## 2. What "alternative protein" and "novel food" mean

The package defines alternative protein as protein for food or feed from plants, microorganisms, fungi, algae, insects or cultivated cells rather than conventional livestock and fish [glossary GL-001]. The word "feed" matters (section 5).

"Novel food" is a legal term. In the EU it is food not eaten to a significant degree before 15 May 1997 [EXP-46]. In Singapore it is food without 20 years of history as food; selling an unapproved one has been an offence since 28 November 2025 [EXP-34]. The United States has no such category: most new ingredients enter as GRAS (generally recognised as safe by qualified experts), a route with no FDA pre-market approval and voluntary notification, which FDA proposed to tighten in August 2026 [EXP-45]. Vietnam has no novel-food category [REG-01; REG-02].

## 3. The families

**Plant-based.** This is the oldest and most mature family. Tofu, tempeh, seitan (wheat gluten) and Vietnamese chay food are traditional. Industry refines plants into concentrates (about 60 to 80% protein) and isolates (about 90%) [glossary GL-007; GL-008]. Extrusion, which cooks and shapes protein under heat and pressure, gives textured vegetable protein (TVP). High-moisture extrusion gives fibrous whole cuts [glossary GL-044; GL-045]. Tofu holds 10.9 g of protein per 100 g in the Vietnamese food table, against 19 to 20 g for lean pork and chicken [DIE-12; APR-04]. Tempeh holds about 17 to 25% [PRA-19]. Dried TVP on Vietnamese shelves has a median of 64 g per 100 g [FORM-01]. Modern meat analogues arrived in 2016 with the Beyond Burger and the Impossible Burger [PRA-12; PRA-13]. Since the 2021 peak their market has shrunk. The US share of meat sales halved to 0.7% in 2025, and Beyond Meat's revenue fell from USD 465 million to 276 million [GLB-01; GLB-46].

**Fermentation, traditional.** Moulds and bacteria have transformed food for centuries. Koji mould makes soy sauce, miso and Vietnamese *tương*; it cannot make aflatoxin, but wild starters still need checks [SCI-19].

**Fermentation, biomass.** Here the microbe itself is the product. Quorn mycoprotein, a fungus grown in fermenters on glucose, ammonia and salts, went on sale in the UK in 1985 [PRA-03; PRA-04]. It is about 12% protein wet and 30 to 45% dry, and it needs a heat step to cut its RNA [PRA-05; SCI-21; SCI-20]. Yeast and bacterial single-cell protein (SCP) is mostly a feed story. ICI's Pruteen plant ran from 1979 to 1987 and sold at about twice the price of soy [COST-43]. China now leads on scale. Angel Yeast makes 11,000 t a year of yeast protein [HSC-14]. A Clostridium protein made from steel-mill gas was certified for feed in 2021 and reached 10,200 t in 2023 [FTG-22; FTG-08]. Gas fermentation grows bacteria on gases instead of sugar. Solar Foods' Solein, made from hydrogen and CO2, was approved in Singapore in 2022 but comes from a plant of only 160 to 230 t a year [COST-53; RGN-04]. The 20,000 t a year FeedKind methane-protein plant in China halted in 2026 [FTG-06].

**Fermentation, precision.** Here engineered microbes make one specific molecule. It is an old technology in medicine and cheese: recombinant insulin was approved in 1982 and fermentation-made chymosin (rennet) in 1990 [PRA-10; PRA-07]. In food it has so far reached market as small, high-value ingredients. FDA raised no questions on Impossible Foods' heme protein in 2018, used at up to 0.8% of a burger [PRA-11]. It did the same for Perfect Day's whey protein in 2020 [PRA-14]. Singapore approved Remilk's whey protein in 2023 [RGN-04].

**Cultivated meat and seafood.** Animal cells are multiplied in a growth medium inside bioreactors, sometimes on edible scaffolds [PRA-16]. The first burger was tasted in London in August 2013 [PRA-15; PRA-22]. Singapore gave the first approval in November 2020, and sales began that December [RGN-04; NOV-10].

**Other families.** Spirulina and chlorella are 50 to 70% protein by dry weight but are sold mainly as supplements [PRA-18]. Duckweed holds about 20 to 44% protein. The EU cleared a Lemna protein concentrate in 2023 but not whole Wolffia powder, because of manganese [SCI-34; SCI-35; SCI-28; SCI-30]. Insect meal is sold as feed, including from a 10,000 t a year plant in Vietnam; this study treats it as a benchmark only [ECO-01; SCI-27].

**Hybrids.** Hybrids mix animal and alternative protein. Soy is already the usual extender in processed meat. The only cultivated chicken sold in Asian retail contained 3% cultivated cells [NOV-10; glossary GL-203].

## 4. Cost and scale in 2026, one line each

- Plant-based: cheap to make (package estimate USD 790 to 2,070 per t of dry TVP in Vietnam) and mature; demand, not technology, limits it [COST-01; GLB-01].
- Biomass fermentation: a mycoprotein model gives USD 29.56 per kg of protein, able to match beef but not chicken; capacity is largest in China [COST-43; HSC-13].
- Gas fermentation: largest food plant 160 to 230 t a year; flagship feed plants have closed or halted [COST-53; FTG-06].
- Precision fermentation: yields are 5 to 15 times below the level experts say bulk protein needs, and no peer-reviewed food cost model was found [SCI-01; SCI-05].
- Cultivated: models range from USD 37 to 51 per kg [COST-47] down to USD 5.66 per kg by 2030 [PRA-17]; the largest run is 20,000 litres [FTB-24].

## 5. Food and feed

Alternative protein also means feed. About 77% of the world's soy is fed to livestock. Some 87% of soybeans are crushed, 99% of the resulting meal goes to feed, and only about 6% of soy is eaten directly [PRA-21; PRA-20]. Fishmeal is capped at about 4 to 4.5 Mt a year, most of it used by aquaculture [FM-06; FM-35]. In August 2026 protein cost about USD 880 per t in soybean meal and USD 3,846 in fishmeal [FM-01]. In Vietnam, feed is already the most advanced alternative-protein market, and 98.4% of soybeans are imported [ECO-01; FM-12; MAC-04].

## Disagreements and caveats

- **Quorn.** UK approval is dated 1984 [PRA-03] or 1981 [PRA-04]; the 1985 launch is agreed. It is sold in 17 countries [PRA-06, 2019] or 20 [PRA-03, 2023]. Several authors have Quorn links.
- **Chymosin share.** Fermentation-made chymosin is given as over 90% of rennet [PRA-09], or about 70% (US) and 90% (UK) of cheese [PRA-08]. The bases and years differ, so do not average them.
- **Fishmeal used by aquaculture.** The share is 69% in 2016 [FM-06] or about 87% citing FAO 2024 [FM-35].
- **Solein plant size.** It is 160 t a year according to the company and 230 t according to the press [COST-53; FTG-04].
- **Cultivated-meat cost.** Humbird's USD 37 to 51 per kg [COST-47] and CE Delft's USD 5.66 per kg [PRA-17] rest on different assumptions about growth-factor prices, cell density and reactor cost. The CE Delft figure was commissioned by advocates and we read only the press summary.
- **Tofu protein.** The food table gives 10.9 g per 100 g; shop labels have a median of 4.98 g [DIE-12; FORM-01].
- **Duckweed protein.** It is 20 to 30% in laboratory Wolffia [SCI-34] and 44.3% in Thai samples [SCI-35].
- **Protein quality.** DIAAS values come mostly from pigs. Human values can be lower, and PDCAAS flatters plant proteins [SCI-42; NQR-15].
- **Advocacy data.** Plant-based sales, investment and cultivated-industry counts come from GFI (advocacy).

## What we could not verify

- WHO TRS 935 (2007), the full text of Finnigan et al. 2019 and Flamm 1991 on chymosin returned 403, a captcha or a paywall; other sources are used.
- The launch date of Perfect Day's first retail product (we cite the 2020 FDA letter), a year for the chymosin shares, and the start year of the Chongqing FeedKind plant.
- Spirulina DIAAS: none found; the 70 to 90% digestibility figures are not DIAAS.
- A peer-reviewed cost model for precision-fermented food protein and a verified extrusion capital cost (the package also records both as absent), and the CE Delft report itself.
