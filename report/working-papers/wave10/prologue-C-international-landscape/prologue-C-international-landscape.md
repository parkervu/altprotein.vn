# Researcher C: the international landscape, September 2026

Prologue research, wave 10. Files: `facts.csv` (51 facts), `sources.csv` (32 new sources), `data_investment_by_year.csv`. Package material was reused first and package IDs are cited where they carry the claim. We used 23 web searches. GFI's charts were read from its open reports; GFI is an advocacy organisation.

## 1. Investment

Private investment in alternative protein rose to a peak in 2021 and has fallen every year since, except for a small plant-based rebound in 2025. Alternative protein companies raised USD 881 million in 2025: USD 450 million for plant-based, USD 357 million for fermentation and USD 74 million for cultivated meat and seafood [CAP-51; PRC-01]. That is the first year below USD 1 billion since 2018 [HSC-10]. The first half of 2026 brought USD 359 million, of which USD 252 million went to fermentation [PRC-01]. The cumulative total since 2017 is "over USD 19.5 billion": about USD 10.7 billion plant-based, 5.4 billion fermentation and 3.4 billion cultivated [PRC-01].

The year-by-year table uses GFI's latest values: fermentation and cultivated from the April 2026 reports [FTB-17; FTB-24], plant-based from the June 2025 report because the 2026 one sits behind a form [PRC-02], and 2024 and 2025 plant-based from GFI's web page [GLB-06]. Summed, they reproduce GFI's cumulative totals within rounding. All three pillars peaked in 2021: about USD 3.8 billion plant-based, 1.8 billion fermentation and 1.5 billion cultivated. Cultivated investment is now about 95% below its peak (our calculation). Inside fermentation the money has moved to precision fermentation, whose share of fermentation funding rose from 33% in 2023 to 66% in 2025 [FTB-17]. Asia-Pacific is a small part of private funding: USD 12 million for fermentation and USD 4.7 million for cultivated companies in 2025 [FTB-17; FTB-24].

## 2. Market arc

The modern wave has clear markers. The Impossible Burger first went on sale in New York on 27 July 2016 [PRC-09]. Beyond Meat listed on Nasdaq on 2 May 2019 at USD 25 a share [PRC-08]. Its revenue peaked at USD 464.7 million in 2021 [GLB-46] and fell to USD 275.5 million in 2025 [PRC-06]. In October 2025 it swapped USD 1.11 billion of convertible notes for USD 209 million of new notes and about 316 million shares [PRC-07]; that accounting gain, not trading, explains its 2025 net income [PRC-06]. US plant-based meat retail sales fell from USD 1.4 billion (2021) to USD 1.0 billion (2025) [GLB-01; GLB-02]. The boom's forecasts overshot badly: Barclays expected USD 140 billion by 2029 [GLB-44], against USD 6.6 billion of global plant-based meat and seafood retail sales in 2025 [GLB-05].

The fade hit cultivated meat hardest. Several firms closed, and Believer Meats filed for bankruptcy weeks after US approval of a USD 154 million plant [HSC-08; FTB-24]; only three companies had sustained cultivated sales in 2025 [FTB-24]. Meati, a mycoprotein maker, went bankrupt in 2025 despite commercial output [FTB-17].

The exceptions are familiar foods. Plant milk held 13% of US milk sales in 2025 [GLB-01], Oatly grew revenue 4.7% to USD 862.5 million in 2025 with its first full year of positive adjusted EBITDA, though still a net loss [PRC-10], and German tofu, tempeh and seitan volume rose 16.7% in 2024 [GLB-09]. Global plant-based retail sales were about USD 28.9 billion in 2025, up 3% [GLB-06].

## 3. Regulation

Approval routes exist, but approvals are few and sales fewer. Singapore has approved 18 novel foods, six of them cultivated, most recently Aleph Farms' beef dated 22 July 2026 [RGN-04]. The United States has cleared five cultivated products [RGN-03]. Worldwide, seven companies are cleared for human food, in Singapore, the US, Australia and New Zealand, and Israel [FTB-24; RGN-18].

Europe has applications but no cultivated authorisation. Aleph Farms applied in Switzerland in July 2023 [PRC-15], Gourmey made the first EU application in July 2024 [PRC-11] and Mosa Meat followed in January 2025 [PRC-12]; none had been authorised by September 2026 on the evidence found [FTB-45; FTB-24; HSC-12]. The EU did authorise its first mycelium protein, Fermotein, in June 2026 [FTB-17; HSC-12]. In the UK, Meatly's cultivated chicken went on sale in pet food on 7 February 2025 [PRC-14], and the Food Standards Agency runs a GBP 1.6 million sandbox with eight companies [PRC-13]. In the US, precision fermentation mostly enters through GRAS: the FDA sent 10 "no questions" letters for fermentation-made ingredients in 2025, three to Chinese firms [FTB-17]. China approved a mycoprotein and its first ingredient from a genetically modified microorganism in 2025 [HSC-11; FTB-17].

## 4. Restrictions

Italy banned cultivated food and feed, and meat names for plant-protein products, by Law 172 of 1 December 2023 [PRC-18]. Hungary's parliament followed in November 2025 [PRC-19; FTB-24]. In the US, seven states had bans or moratoria by the end of 2025: Florida and Alabama (2024 bans); Mississippi, Montana and Nebraska (2025 bans); Indiana (moratorium to June 2027) and Texas (two-year ban to September 2027) [HSC-35; FTB-24]. South Dakota added a temporary ban from 1 July 2026 [PRC-16; PRC-17]. On names, the EU settled on Regulation (EU) 2026/1739: 31 meat terms such as "steak" and "chicken" are reserved for animal products from August 2029, but "burger" and "sausage" remain allowed [PRC-21; PRC-20]. Some MEPs are already trying to add "burger" and "sausage" [PRC-21; PRC-22].

## 5. Public funding beyond Asia

Amounts are as found and should not be added. Denmark: a DKK 675 million plant-based fund for 2023 to 2030, made permanent [VIS-01; VIS-04]. The Netherlands: EUR 60 million from the National Growth Fund for cellular agriculture, granted conditionally in April 2022 [PRC-24; PRC-23]. The UK: a USD 30 million Bezos Centre at Imperial College (philanthropic) [PRC-27] and GBP 15 million of UKRI money for a national alternative protein innovation centre [PRC-25; PRC-26]. The United States: the 2022 bioeconomy executive order behind the National Biotechnology and Biomanufacturing Initiative was revoked in March 2025 [PRC-28]. Israel: about NIS 325 million of cumulative alternative protein support by the end of 2025 [FTB-24]. China's 2025 No. 1 Central Document asks to "cultivate and develop biological agriculture and open up new types of food resources" within a "diversified food supply system" [PRC-29].

## 6. Feed side

Feed is where microbial protein has real approvals. China certified a bacterial protein made from steel-mill gas as its first new feed ingredient in August 2021 [PRC-30]. Methane-fed protein is fragile at scale: Calysseo's 20,000 tonne plant in Chongqing halted in 2026, while Unibio plans 50,000 tonnes in Saudi Arabia for 2028 [FTG-06; FTG-07]. String Bio in India reports expert-panel GRAS for US shrimp feed [PRC-31]. Insect meal, a benchmark only, has seen several European failures [AIS-21]. Fishmeal is increasingly made from by-products (34% in 2024) [AQF-04], and supply tightened in 2026 [HSC-29].

## 7. Asia snapshot

Asia holds over a third of global plant-based dairy sales [GLB-49] but little private investment. Singapore still approves products but dropped alternative protein from its food-security targets [RGN-08; RGN-09]. Thailand named alternative proteins in its 2026 to 2035 biotechnology framework [RGN-35]. Korea has a Food Tech Industry Promotion Act and the region's largest cultivated start-up base, but no approval yet; Japan has draft guidance only [RGN-21; GLB-49; RGN-33].

## Disagreements and caveats

- **2021 total.** About USD 6.9 billion in GFI's current series [HSC-10], about 7.1 billion from rounded pillars, and 5.0 billion as first published [PRC-04]; the later series adds listed companies.
- **2024 pillars (USD million).** Plant-based 342 [GLB-06], 309 [PRC-02] or 324 [AIS-01]; fermentation 632 [FTB-17], 651 [PRC-05] or 631 [HSC-10]; cultivated 144 or 139 [FTB-24], or 146 [HSC-10]. Latest GFI values used; not averaged.
- **Cumulative header.** "2017 to Q1 2026" in GFI's table, H1 2026 in its text [PRC-01].
- **Plant-based dairy.** USD 22.7 billion [GLB-49] plus USD 6.6 billion of meat exceeds the USD 28.9 billion headline [GLB-06]; category scopes differ.
- **SDIC.** Over CNY 4 billion (USD 555 million) [FTB-17] against about USD 1 billion [RGN-43].
- **South Dakota end date.** 30 June 2031 [PRC-16] or 30 June 2030 [PRC-17].
- **EU naming dates.** Trilogue on 5 March 2026 [PRC-20] or in February [PRC-22]; entry into force 18 August [PRC-21] or 19 August 2026 [PRC-22].
- **Believer Meats.** GFI gives October and November for the USDA grant [FTB-24]; the plant shut in December 2025 [FTB-45].
- **Japan.** Draft guidelines in December 2025 [FTB-24] and draft checkpoints on 28 May 2026 [RGN-33].
- **GFI's global public funding tally** (at least USD 2.5 billion since 2021) is an advocacy aggregate [PRC-05]; do not add it to the national amounts.
