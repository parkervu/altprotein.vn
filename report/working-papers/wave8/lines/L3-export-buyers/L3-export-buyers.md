# Export buyers as actors: what foreign buyers have committed to, and how they source

Line: W8-L3 Export buyers as actors (wave 8). Source prefix: XBA. Date: 25 September 2026.

## Verdict

European retailers' protein targets are real but they do not pull on Asian imports: six Dutch chains aim for 60% plant protein by 2030 and Rewe for 60% by 2035, yet shares have barely moved since 2023, the largest UK and French buyers (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Carrefour) set no split targets, and retailers meet their goals with pulses, price cuts and hybrids made in Europe. Their tier 1 food suppliers are European; Asian goods reach them through European importers and brand owners, and the plant-based items of Vietnamese origin we found on Western shelves are vegetable-led wrapped foods with about 5 g of protein per 100 g. The real export base is one Part VI does not show: Vietnam exported USD 128.2 million of stuffed pasta (dumplings and wontons) in 2023, about 250 times its textured-protein exports, and it out-supplies China and Thailand in the UK, US and Korea, while its duty-free textured protein barely sells in the EU, Japan or Korea. Two constraints a plan would hit late: the zero tariff depends on the recipe (UK-Vietnam origin rules cap imported flour in dumplings at 20% of weight and block chay food preparations with more than 10% imported textured soy), and the largest foreign owner of Vietnamese dumpling capacity, CJ, is building a Hungarian plant to supply Europe from late 2026. Yield class: **added a constraint** (rules of origin, certification, near-shoring) and **added a route** (plant-based extension SKUs through buyers already taking Vietnamese wrapped foods), with **clean negatives** on retailer targets as an import pull and on Japanese and Korean demand for Vietnamese textured soy; ch27's destination ranking survives, but its first buyer, first product and first test change.

## Headline findings

1. **Retailer protein targets exist but progress is flat.** All six Dutch chains target 60% plant protein by 2030 and set 50% for 2025, and their shares 'changed little since 2023'; Lidl Nederland moved from 42.7% to 43.1% (2023 to 2024) and needs about 2.8 points a year (our calculation); Ahold Delhaize's European brands reached 45% in 2025 against 50% by 2030 [general, High, XBA-02; XBA-14; XBA-01, revealed].
2. **Where targets bite, they are met at home.** Albert Heijn launched 15 hybrid meat and dairy products with field bean protein at price parity in 2025 with "mixed" results; Lidl sells a 60/40 beef and pea mince below the price of beef mince and prices about 30 plant basics at or below animal equivalents; Lidl's plant-based suppliers are European makers (Vivera, Hilcona, Biovegan) [general, High, XBA-01; XBA-02; XBA-07, revealed].
3. **The largest UK and French buyers have no split targets.** Sainsbury's plant share of protein sales was 14% in 2022/23 and 13% in each year to 2025/26; Tesco's was 9% (2024/25) with only a sales-growth goal; Carrefour's plant-based alternatives sales grew from EUR 621 million (2024) to EUR 664 million (2025), with EUR 1 billion targeted by 2030. Lidl GB is the exception, with 25% of protein tonnage plant-based by 2030 [general, High, XBA-08; XBA-12; XBA-11; XBA-05, revealed].
4. **Tier 1 food suppliers are European.** Lidl's food supplier list for 2025 deliveries has 751 entries and none in Asia; Asian foods come through European importers and traders on the list (for example Thai Union Trading Europe; Kreyenhop and Kluge, not verified as an Asian-food importer). Lidl GB's 390 national suppliers include none in Asia. Sainsbury's top five food-supplying countries are the UK, Spain, France, Italy and the Netherlands [general, High, XBA-07; XBA-06; XBA-10, revealed].
5. **The entry ticket is IFS or BRCGS.** Lidl requires IFS or BRC with annual audits; 74.8% of Carrefour-brand plants were IFS or BRC certified in 2025 and 7.3% audited by Carrefour. FSSC 22000, held by CJ's Vietnamese plant and claimed by Au Lac, is not what these buyers name. No buyer published minimum orders [general, High, XBA-04; XBA-11; XBA-20; EXP-38, revealed].
6. **Vietnam already sells wrapped foods at scale.** Stuffed-pasta exports were USD 128.2 million (30,013 t) in 2023. In 2025, partner-reported imports from Vietnam were USD 48.1 million (US), 32.4 million (EU27), 15.5 million (Japan), 14.8 million (UK), 13.3 million (Korea) and 10.6 million (Australia) [VN-direct, High, XBA-29; XBA-28, revealed].
7. **The tariff edge is unused for protein ingredients.** EU27 imports of textured protein (2106.10) from Vietnam were USD 0.01 million (3 t) in 2025 against USD 42.6 million (16,381 t) from China; Japan and Korea took under USD 0.35 million a year from Vietnam against USD 11.5 to 32.0 million from China [VN-direct, High, XBA-28, revealed].
8. **Japan's finished and ingredient markets are closing to newcomers.** Otsuka Foods ended production of its three listed Zero Meat products between January and July 2025; Fuji Oil, No. 1 in Japanese soy protein ingredients, has added a textured soy plant in Chiba and has no Vietnamese unit [general, High, XBA-17; XBA-18; XBA-19, revealed].
9. **Vietnam-origin plant-based items on Western shelves are vegetable-led.** Sainsbury's Plant Pioneers battered fishless fillets (jackfruit, 5.4 g protein per 100 g), Waitrose's Menyū vegetable steamed buns (5.2 g) and Costco's Royal Asia vegetable spring rolls carry Vietnam origin in Open Food Facts; an Australian dumpling brand (Mr Chen's) is labelled "Made in Vietnam" at Coles [VN-direct, Low (crowd-sourced) and High (retailer label), XBA-26; XBA-25, revealed].
10. **The main foreign owner of Vietnamese wrapped-food capacity is near-shoring.** CJ built its Kizuna plant (Can Giuoc, Tay Ninh) in 2022 as its first "Global to Global" base to export dumplings and rolls to Japan, the EU and Australia, but will make bibigo dumplings for Europe in Hungary from the second half of 2026; bibigo vegetable dumplings at Coles are made in Australia [VN-direct, Medium, XBA-20; XBA-23; XBA-25, stated and revealed].
11. **The zero tariff depends on the recipe.** Under the UK-Vietnam agreement, chapter 21 foods must change heading, so a chay food preparation with more than 10% imported textured soy (also heading 2106) loses preference; stuffed pasta may contain at most 20% non-originating flour, starch or rice, and flour milled from imported wheat is non-originating. UK-CPTPP rules are looser. The EU text is expected to match (not verified) [VN-direct, High for the UK and Medium for the EU, XBA-30; EXP-23; EXP-24, revealed (rule)].
12. **The diaspora channel already carries high-protein dry chay.** An Nhien's dry soy pieces (48 to 61 g protein per 100 g, based on imported soy isolate) are recorded in France, Germany and Spain, and Au Lac analogues (40 to 42 g) in Germany, Norway and Colombia [VN-direct, Low, XBA-26; XBA-27; EXP-38, revealed].

## 1. Method and reach

We read ch27, Appendix AF, TPP-08 and TPP-09, destination_markets.csv, market_access.csv and the export open questions (OQ-243 to OQ-256), then the wave 7 incumbent, investor and policy actor files and the L2 (tariffs) and L4 (commitments and private label) lines. Wave 7 covered Vietnam-facing buyers only; no foreign buyer had been profiled. We reused package sources (EXP-05, EXP-19, EXP-22 to EXP-24, EXP-33, EXP-37 to EXP-41) and wave 7 sources (AIB-17, AFN-18, CPC-09, CPC-15).

Web search was not available. We opened company reports and pages by known address and followed their links; downloaded Lidl's two supplier lists, Sainsbury's databook, the Ahold Delhaize and Carrefour reports and Questionmark's European and Dutch supermarket benchmarks with their background data; used Coles' own search and product pages for Australian country-of-origin labels; used the Open Food Facts product and search services; pulled mirror trade data from the UN Comtrade public API; and read rules of origin from the UK Trade Tariff API. We did not use general search engines.

Blocked or unreadable: Tesco (script-rendered pages), Albert Heijn, Rewe, Aldi UK and Woolworths (HTTP 403), Sainsbury's product search (403), Open Supply Hub (authentication required, so the Sainsbury's and Marks and Spencer tier 1 food lists could not be read), the BRCGS Directory (script application with login), ImportYeti (bot check), Mr Chen's and Kreyenhop and Kluge (connection failures), Pulmuone (HTTP 417), EUR-Lex (empty page) and Coles after four product pages (bot check). We did not try to get round any of these.

Outputs: `data_export_buyer_register.csv` (20 actors), `data_retailer_protein_split.csv` (25 rows), `data_supplier_list_check.csv` (10), `data_vietnam_origin_products_abroad.csv` (13), `data_wrapped_food_trade_mirror.csv` (230) and `data_origin_rules.csv` (8).

What the targets mean for Vietnam [general, Medium, XBA-01; XBA-02; XBA-14, inferred]:

- **They are met with what is cheapest and closest.** Lidl Nederland credits price parity (sales of discounted substitutes up 22.6% in three months) and a 60/40 beef and pea mince; Albert Heijn used hybrids with field bean protein; Sainsbury's pushes beans. None of these levers needs an imported analogue.
- **They measure protein from all foods.** The Dutch Protein Tracker counts bread, cereals and pulses; a retailer can move its split without buying a single meat analogue.
- **They are behind schedule.** Lidl Nederland dropped its 50% plant-promotion goal as "too ambitious" and cut its plant dairy target from 15% to 10% [XBA-02]. A behind-schedule buyer pushes price and hybrids, not new imported lines.

This is a clean negative for the idea that retailer commitments create a pull for Asian-made plant-based meat. It fits Part VI's finding that European growth is price-led and strongest in plain formats (DM-18 to DM-22).

## 4. How these buyers source

**Supplier lists.** Lidl publishes a food supplier list for 2025 deliveries: 751 entries, 374 in Germany and almost all others in Europe, with one each in the UAE, Egypt and Turkey and none in Asia [general, High, XBA-07, revealed]. It is a list of contract holders, not factories: Asian foods arrive through European importers on the list, such as Kreyenhop and Kluge (Oyten), which we take to be an importer of Asian foods (its website did not load, so this is not verified), and Thai Union Trading Europe (Utrecht). Lidl GB's list of 390 national suppliers (1 April 2026) is 95% British (our calculation: 370 of 390) [XBA-06]. Sainsbury's reports 603 own-brand food suppliers at 1,103 sites, with the UK, Spain, France, Italy and the Netherlands as its top five food countries by site count, and publishes its tier 1 food list on Open Supply Hub; so does Marks and Spencer. We could not read either list because Open Supply Hub requires a login [XBA-10; XBA-16]. We found no food factory list for Tesco, Aldi, Rewe, Carrefour or Albert Heijn (`data_supplier_list_check.csv`).

**What this means.** A Vietnamese co-packer does not become a European discounter's supplier directly. It supplies a European importer or brand owner, which holds the contract, the specification and the liability [general, Medium, XBA-06; XBA-07, inferred]. Part VI's TPP-08 names the retailer as first buyer; on this evidence the first buyer is the intermediary.

**Requirements.** Lidl Nederland's suppliers must be certified to IFS or BRC, with annual independent audits [XBA-04]. Carrefour-brand plants are IFS or BRC certified (74.8% in 2025) or audited by Carrefour (7.3%) [XBA-11]. Sainsbury's requires all own-brand tier 1 sites to be on Sedex, with SMETA audits by risk [XBA-10]. UK retailers also require deforestation-free soy, including soy in feed (EXP-33). CJ's Kizuna plant holds FSSC 22000 and HACCP, which CJ said would let it "list immediately" in EU and Australian retail [XBA-20]; Au Lac claims FSSC 22000 [EXP-38]. Neither Lidl nor Carrefour names FSSC 22000. We found no published minimum order quantities.

## 7. Japanese and Korean makers

- **Fuji Oil** is Japan's No. 1 maker of soy protein ingredients, with textured and granulated soy for "ham and sausages", "frozen foods and prepared foods" and "food service and school lunches". It has invested in a textured soy plant in Chiba and makes soy ingredients in China; it has no Vietnamese unit [general, High, XBA-18; XBA-19, revealed]. It is the incumbent a Vietnamese exporter would displace, not a buyer.
- **Otsuka Foods** ended production of Zero Meat demi-glace hamburg (January 2025), sausage (June 2025) and ham (July 2025) [general, High, XBA-17, revealed]. A leading Japanese finished analogue brand has exited.
- **Marukome** still sells its Daizu Labo soy-meat range; its pages do not state where it is made [general, Low, XBA-31, revealed].
- **CJ CheilJedang** is the one Asian maker with a Vietnamese export plant. Kizuna (Can Giuoc, Tay Ninh; opened February 2022) makes dumplings, processed rice, kimchi and sauces for export to China, Japan, Southeast Asia, the EU and Australia, uses ASEAN and CPTPP tariff benefits and was the first site with CJ's automated spring-roll forming; CJ planned KRW 100 billion of investment by 2025 and three times the 2022 export volume [VN-direct, Medium, XBA-20, stated]. PlanTable, its plant-based brand made with its own textured soy, reached 30 export countries including Germany and the UK in 2022 and nine items in 2023 [XBA-21; XBA-24]; we found no newsroom item on it after November 2023 [Low]. In November 2024 CJ announced a Hungarian plant to make bibigo dumplings for Europe from the second half of 2026, citing European dumpling demand growing more than 30% a year [VN-direct, Medium, XBA-23, stated].
- **Daesang** lists Miwon Vietnam as an affiliate; we found no plant-based sourcing from Vietnam [general, Low, XBA-32]. Pulmuone and Nissin pages could not be read.

For Japan and Korea, the revealed picture is a closing market for finished analogues and ingredient demand served by domestic makers and China. The 6.6 point Japanese tariff edge over China (EXP-41) has not produced trade (section 6).

## 8. Rules of origin: the recipe condition on the tariff edge

Part VI states that Vietnamese textured protein, food preparations and stuffed pasta enter the EU and UK at 0%. That is true only for goods that meet the origin rules. OQ-244 asked whether products made from imported soy protein or gluten qualify. The UK Trade Tariff answers it for the UK (`data_origin_rules.csv`) [VN-direct, High, XBA-30, revealed (rule)]:

| Agreement | Line | Rule | Effect on Vietnamese recipes |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK-Vietnam | Chapter 21 (2106.10, 2106.90) | Materials of any heading except the product's; limits on dairy and sugar | A chay food preparation with more than 10% by weight of imported textured soy or concentrate (heading 2106) is non-originating. Isolate (3504), gluten (1109) and domestic textured soy are fine |
| UK-Vietnam | 1902 (stuffed pasta) | Change of heading; non-originating materials of 1006 and 1101 to 1108 at most 20% of weight; chapters 2, 3 and 16 at most 20% | Wheat wrappers above 20% of weight break origin; rice-paper and rice-flour wrappers from Vietnamese rice pass |
| UK-Vietnam | 1101 (wheat flour) | All chapter 10 and 11 materials wholly obtained | Flour milled in Vietnam from imported wheat is non-originating |
| UK-Vietnam | Tolerance | 10% of weight for chapters 2 and 4 to 24; may not exceed Annex II caps | Sets the 10% allowance |
| UK-CPTPP | 1902 | Change from any other chapter | Imported wheat flour allowed |
| UK-CPTPP | 2106.90 | Change of subheading, or 30% to 40% regional value content | Imported textured soy allowed |

The UK-Vietnam agreement rolled over the EU agreement, so the EU rules are expected to match, but we could not read the EU text [VN-direct, Medium, EXP-23, inferred]. If they match, an EU-bound chay food preparation built on imported Chinese textured soy pays 12.8% or 9% plus an agricultural component, and a wheat-wrapper dumpling over the flour cap pays 8.3% plus EUR 6.10 per 100 kg (MA-01 to MA-08), which is roughly the edge the package credits to Vietnam. Two consequences follow [VN-direct, Medium, XBA-30, inferred]:

- **Export recipes must be designed for origin:** rice-paper or rice-flour wrappers, and fillings based on soy isolate, gluten or domestic textured soy.
- **Domestic textured soy gains an export role.** Chay makers who swap imported Chinese textured soy for domestic product would make their EU-bound food preparations originating. This links TPP-08 to T1, the domestic textured protein play, which Part VI treats as purely import substitution.

## 9. What this means for the export route ranking, TPP-08, TPP-09 and ch27

**Proposed export route ranking** [VN-direct, Medium, inferred from sections 3 to 8]:

1. **Plant-based extension SKUs through buyers already taking Vietnamese wrapped foods** (importers and brand owners in the UK, US, EU, Korea, Japan and Australia). Evidence: USD 128 million of stuffed-pasta exports; Vietnam-origin vegetable items on UK and North American shelves. Conditions: IFS or BRCGS certification, origin-compliant recipes, the US surcharge, CJ's near-shoring.
2. **Diaspora and Asian-grocery chay (TPP-09)** in the US, Australia and Europe. Evidence: listings in the US (EXP-37) and records in France, Germany, Spain and Norway, including high-protein dry pieces.
3. **Direct EU and UK retailer private label (TPP-08 as written).** Real only through an importer or brand owner; retailer targets do not pull on imports.
4. **Japanese and Korean ingredient buyers for textured soy.** Clean negative on revealed trade; keep only for a specified fungal or fermented protein with a named buyer.
5. **Plant-based seafood.** Still the weakest; one UK private-label jackfruit fillet exists, while German sales fell 22% in 2025 (EXP-05).

**TPP-08.** Keep the destination and formats. Change first buyers to importers and brand owners; add IFS or BRCGS and origin-compliant recipes; replace the ten-buyer survey with quote requests to three importers and two brand owners. Add "chay food preparations with more than 10% imported textured soy, and wheat-wrapper dumplings above 20% imported flour, for the EU" to *avoid*.

**TPP-09.** Unchanged in direction. Add that protein-rich dry chay already travels; the gap is protein in frozen formats.

**ch27 advice.** Startups: the first export product is a vegetable or plant-protein version of a wrapped food a buyer already takes from Vietnam. Manufacturers: IFS Food or BRCGS is the entry ticket; FSSC 22000 alone may not be. Investors: price CJ's move to Hungary for EU supply. Policy makers: an origin-rule note for chay and dumpling exporters, and a check of the EU text, would protect the tariff edge Part VI values at 6 to 13 points.

**Demand model.** R7 (exports) contributes 2.5 kt of the 19.05 kt benchmark in 2035 at a protein share of 0.25, which assumes about 60% ingredients. Ingredient exports have no revealed base, the wrapped plant-based items found abroad carry 4 to 5.4 g of protein per 100 g, and today's high-protein dry chay uses imported isolate, which R7 does not count. At a share of 0.05 to 0.12, R7 would deliver 0.5 to 1.2 kt and the benchmark total 17.1 to 17.8 kt (our calculation: 19.05 minus 2.5 plus 0.5 to 1.2). We suggest a sensitivity, and reporting the 16.55 kt delivered in Vietnam beside the headline, not a new headline [VN-direct, Low, XBA-28; XBA-26, inferred].

## Next-wave candidates

1. **Who exports Vietnam's wrapped foods, and how much is vegetarian?** A *deeper* version of this line. Not closable with our tools: it needs eight-digit customs declarations by exporter or bill-of-lading data (ImportYeti was blocked). A vegetarian share above about a tenth would support the R7 benchmark and make the extension-SKU route the first export recommendation; a negligible share would leave it as a hypothesis.
2. **Importer quote test.** A *different kind* of question (primary contact, not desk). Three importers and two brand owners asked to quote a vegetable and a plant-protein SKU. A quote at or near current wrapped-food prices would turn TPP-08 into a costed route; refusals on protein content or price would downgrade it below TPP-09.
3. **EU origin text and certified-site counts.** *Deeper*, and closable in an hour with a browser and free logins (EUR-Lex or Access2Markets; BRCGS Directory; IFS database; Open Supply Hub). Would confirm or remove the EU recipe constraint and show how many Vietnamese plants can already supply a discounter.
4. **Origin-compliant recipe costing.** A *different kind* of question (technical economics): the cost of rice-paper or domestic-flour wrappers and domestic textured soy against imported inputs. If the duty saving (8% to 13%) exceeds the recipe cost, domestic textured soy (T1) gains an export customer, which would change T1's demand case.

Further desk work on retailer targets or on Japanese and Korean corporate pages would be **noise**: the targets are published and flat, and the trade data already show where ingredient demand goes.

## Limits

- **Blocked sources.** Tesco, Albert Heijn, Rewe, Aldi UK, Woolworths and parts of Coles could not be read; Sainsbury's and Marks and Spencer tier 1 food lists sit behind an Open Supply Hub login. Tesco's progress on its 300% goal is unknown.
- **Crowd-sourced product data.** Open Food Facts records are user-entered; their "origins" field may describe an ingredient, and store fields are incomplete. We tag them Low; only the Coles labels are retailer-verified.
- **Mirror trade.** UN Comtrade six-digit data cannot separate vegetarian from meat or seafood fillings. Partner-reported and Vietnam-reported figures differ (see disagreements); 2025 partner data may be revised.
- **Rules of origin.** The UK rules are read from the official tariff service; the EU rules are inferred from the rollover and need checking. Classification of a given chay product (2106.90, 1902.20, 1602 or 1905) decides which rule applies.
- **Summaries.** The Aldi page was read through a summarising tool (Medium). Questionmark's benchmarks are a third-party compilation of retailer disclosures; where we could, we used the retailer's own figure.
- **Coverage.** We did not reach Pulmuone, Nissin, Jumbo's or Albert Heijn's own pages, Australian or US Asian importers beyond Weee!, Costco and Coles, or any buyer's minimum-order terms.
