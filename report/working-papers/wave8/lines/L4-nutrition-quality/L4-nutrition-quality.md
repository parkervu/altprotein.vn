# The nutrition route: what a plant or blended protein must meet for children and public meals

Line: W8-L4 THE NUTRITION ROUTE: WHAT A PLANT OR BLENDED PROTEIN MUST MEET FOR CHILDREN AND PUBLIC MEALS (wave 8). Source prefix: NQR. Date: 25 September 2026.

## Verdict

For primary-school children, protein is not the gap that Part VI's ch21.2 implies: published Hanoi lunches already carry about 31.8 g of protein against a 2026 recommendation of 32 to 49 g for the whole day, and even a rice-and-tofu lunch clears the lunch share after a conservative protein-quality (DIAAS) adjustment; the binding nutrients are zinc and iron, and Vietnam's own school guidance doubles a child's zinc requirement (5.6 to 11.2 mg a day at age 6 to 7) when a lunch has little animal protein. Plant proteins score lower on quality (tofu 64 to 98, textured soy 65 to 97, wheat gluten 33 to 66, against 100 or more for pork, chicken, egg and milk) but are far cheaper: textured soy as an ingredient gives 25 g of quality-adjusted protein for VND 950 to 4,100 against VND 8,575 to 10,988 for eggs; a 20 to 30% soy extender in minced pork keeps the dish at a DIAAS of about 97 or more. Unfortified soy cannot supply a lunch's zinc in a portion a child will eat, and no Vietnamese mandatory fortification reaches it (Decree 09/2016 covers salt, wheat flour and oil only), so a zinc-fortified soy dish base is the one plant product that could beat eggs on cost per unit of both protein and zinc, on paper and at ingredient level. Trials in Vietnam and Thailand show that about 5 mg of zinc added to a school food raises zinc status, while school-age protein, milk or egg feeding barely moves height; stunting is largely set before age two, so a plant product reaches UNICEF's stunting agenda only through complementary-food standards, where it faces the young-child protein-quality pattern, registration as a food for children under 36 months and trial evidence that favours milk or fortified blends. Yield class: **changed a conclusion** (ch21.2 and ch21.8: the upland school gap is zinc and iron, not cheap protein; TPP-04's 20 g per dish is an adult figure) and **added a constraint and a candidate** (zinc, iron and sodium specifications, a fortification gap and a registration rule; a new fortified school dish-base profile); the benchmark headline of about 19,000 t of protein by 2035 does not move.

## Headline findings

1. **Protein quantity is not the school-age gap.** The National Institute of Nutrition (NIN) online lookup gives 32 g of protein a day at age 6 to 7, 38 g at 8 to 9 and 49 g for boys aged 10 to 11; a lunch is meant to give 30 to 40% of the day (9.6 to 19.6 g across these ages). Published Hanoi lunches carry 31.8 g (21.2 to 38.6 g), 17.5 g of it animal. The 2026 recommendation already scores the Vietnamese diet at a PDCAAS of 0.89 and dropped the animal-protein ratio. [VN-direct, Medium, NQR-01; APR-04; PMR-26; DIE-04; revealed]
2. **Zinc is the binding nutrient, and the rule says so.** Decision 3958/QĐ-BYT Appendix 5 sets zinc needs by diet: 11.2 mg a day at age 6 to 7 when the diet has "ít hoặc không có chất đạm động vật hoặc cá" (little or no animal protein or fish), 5.6 mg with moderate animal protein and 3.3 mg with plenty. Every band implies the same absorbed need (about 1.7 mg a day, our calculation), so a meatless lunch doubles the zinc a caterer must serve. Zinc deficiency affected 53.3% of children aged 6 to 59 months in 2020, and stunting is 32% among ethnic minority children (2023). [VN-direct, High, APR-04; APR-42; APR-27; revealed]
3. **Protein quality (DIAAS, older-child pattern that applies from age 3).** Pork 113 to 137, beef 99 to 135, chicken 123 (in vitro), eggs above 100, milk 123 to 143; soy milk 91 to 99; tofu 64 to 98 (sources disagree); extruded or textured soy 65 to 97; soy-based meat analogue 110 and soy sausage 86 (in vitro); wheat-based sausage 33 and raw wheat 54 to 66 (the gluten proxy); mung bean 86 in pigs but about 69 on human digestibility; white rice 60; mycoprotein PDCAAS 0.996. We found no DIAAS measured in school-age children and no Vietnamese DIAAS study. [general, Medium, NQR-05 to NQR-11; NQR-13; NQR-14; SCI-41; SCI-42]
4. **A 20 to 30% soy extender in minced pork keeps protein quality.** Because DIAAS is additive in mixed meals, a dish with 20, 25 or 30% of its protein from textured soy scores at least 101.8, 99.5 or 97.2 on the older-child pattern (at least 90 on the young-child pattern), with zinc per gram of protein 12 to 19% below all-pork and still above egg or chicken (our calculation). The hybrid-extender route raises no nutrition objection for schools. [general, Medium, NQR-07; NQR-08; NQR-05; APR-04; inferred]
5. **Plant zinc is low and poorly absorbed.** Per 100 g of protein, lean pork carries 13.2 mg of zinc, milk 10.3, chicken 4.4 to 9.1, tofu 7.3, egg 6.9, defatted soy flour (the textured-soy base) 5.0 and mung bean 4.7. Soybean phytate gives a phytate-to-zinc molar ratio of about 34 (9 to 43), above the 15 at which the guidance rates absorption poor, and most soymilk phytate ends up in tofu curd. Plant foods carry no vitamin B12. [VN-direct and general, Medium, APR-04; DIE-12; NQR-17; NQR-18; NQR-19; inferred]
6. **What real and hypothetical lunches deliver** (our calculation, rice included, vegetables excluded). An observed pork day carries 4.6 to 5.5 mg of zinc; an observed tofu day 2.7 to 2.8 mg, enough because fish and a little pork keep it in the moderate band. A lunch of rice and unfortified textured soy carries 2.6 mg but falls in the poor band, where a 6 to 7 year old needs 3.4 to 4.5 mg per lunch: short by 0.7 to 1.9 mg. Adding 3 mg of zinc to the soy closes the gap. [VN-direct, Low, PMR-23; PMR-24; APR-04; DIE-12; inferred]
7. **No mandatory fortification reaches a soy product.** Decree 09/2016/NĐ-CP (in force) requires iodised salt for eating and processing, iron and zinc in wheat flour used in processing, and vitamin A in soy, palm, rapeseed or peanut oil except oil for industrial processing. A 2025 amendment draft concerned iodised salt in fish sauce; we found no amending decree. A fortified dish base sold to caterers can self-declare under Decree 15/2018, but a food for children up to 36 months must register. [VN-direct, Medium, NQR-02; NQR-03; NQR-04; revealed]
8. **Sodium disqualifies today's analogues for children.** The 2026 limits are under 1,800 mg a day at age 6 to 7 and under 2,000 mg at 8 to 11, so a lunch should carry no more than 540 to 800 mg. Frozen meat analogues in the retail audit carry a median 597 mg per 100 g; a portion giving 20 g of protein carries about 690 mg (540 to 850), the whole lunch allowance. Tofu carries 12 mg per 100 g. [VN-direct, Medium, NQR-01; FORM-01; inferred]
9. **Cost of 25 g of DIAAS-adjusted protein** (our calculation, capped at 100): textured soy ingredient VND 954 to 4,115; tofu 5,000 to 18,632 (household to kitchen platform); eggs 8,575 to 10,988; chicken 9,750 to 12,026; minced pork with 25% soy 13,237 to 13,743; pork 17,341 to 17,725; UHT milk 22,975 to 29,750; soy milk 27,689 to 30,123; branded analogue 28,000 to 32,558. [VN-direct, Low, COST-39; COST-40; COST-41; CPC-12; DIE-05; DIE-23; PMR-C09; inferred]
10. **Cost of a lunch's zinc share at age 6 to 7** (0.50 to 0.67 mg absorbed): chicken thigh VND 4,900 to 6,500 (62 to 82 g), minced pork with 25% soy 4,800 to 6,600, lean pork 5,300 to 7,100 (40 to 54 g), eggs 5,700 to 9,700 (2.1 to 3.2 eggs), UHT milk 9,000 to 12,000 (252 to 336 ml); tofu would need 210 to 560 g and unfortified textured soy 68 to 182 g dry, neither a real portion. A 40 g serving of zinc-fortified textured soy costs VND 725 to 2,097 before the fortificant, whose price we could not retrieve; the whole fortified biscuit in the Vietnamese school trial cost under USD 0.08 (about VND 2,080). [VN-direct, Low, APR-04; COST-39 to COST-41; CPC-12; NQR-22; inferred]
11. **Trials: fortification moves micronutrient status; protein source barely moves height.** In rural Vietnam, biscuits with 5.6 mg zinc and 6 mg iron five days a week for four months cut the risk of anaemia and of zinc and iodine deficiency by more than 40% (510 children aged 6 to 8). Zinc-fortified rice and seasoning in Thai school lunches raised zinc status. Daily milk and egg for two years in rural Chinese schools did not change height; the one soy school-meal trial (Bangladesh, two schools) added 0.5 cm in 8 months with no effect on serum zinc. In infants, meat did no better than fortified cereal in four countries and only modestly better in China, and Cambodian fish-based foods did no better than corn-soy blends. We found no trial of textured soy, plant-meat or blended products in children anywhere. [VN-direct and VN-adjacent, High, NQR-22 to NQR-30; tested]
12. **Border-commune lunches can afford protein; they need zinc.** Decree 339/2025 gives VND 450,000 a month (VND 20,455 to 22,500 a school day) for non-rice food, rice being supplied in kind. If half buys protein foods, that is 26 to 33 g of egg protein or 14 to 16 g of pork protein a day (our calculation). [VN-direct, Medium, APR-09; COST-41; CPC-12; inferred]

## 1. What a school lunch must supply

Decision 3958/QĐ-BYT (school-meal guidance, December 2025) sets lunch at 30 to 40% of daily energy and gives iron, zinc, calcium and vitamin A needs by age in Appendices 3 to 6; it cites the 2016 recommended intakes [APR-04]. The NIN online lookup, whose age groups were updated in March 2026 after Decision 116/QĐ-VDD (12 February 2026, the RNI cited by Decision 1982 [APR-16]), gives protein and sodium [NQR-01]. Its zinc and iron values are identical to Decision 3958, so either the 2026 revision kept them or the lookup mixes versions; we could not obtain Decision 116 itself. NIN's summary of the 2026 revision says the protein need now uses a PDCAAS of 0.89 for the Vietnamese diet, drops the animal-protein ratio and adds upper limits for iron and zinc [DIE-04, re-read 25 September 2026; article dated 3 August 2026].

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 to 7 | 32 (9.6 to 12.8) | 11.2; 5.6; 3.3 | 1.7 to 2.2; 3.4 to 4.5 | 7.1 to 7.2 (2.1 to 2.9) | under 1,800 (540 to 720) |
| 8 to 9, boys | 38 (11.4 to 15.2) | 12.0; 6.0; 3.3 | 1.8 to 2.4; 3.6 to 4.8 | 8.9 (2.7 to 3.6) | under 2,000 (600 to 800) |
| 10 to 11, boys | 49 (14.7 to 19.6) | 17.2; 8.6; 5.2 | 2.6 to 3.4; 5.2 to 6.9 | 11.3 (3.4 to 4.5) | under 2,000 (600 to 800) |

[VN-direct, Medium for protein and sodium (NQR-01, version not labelled), High for zinc and iron (APR-04); revealed; lunch shares our calculation. Full table in `data_school_lunch_requirements.csv`.]

**Reading.** The guidance's bands do the work a protein-quality rule would do. Zinc absorption is rated 50% with plenty of animal protein, 30% with a moderate amount (phytate-to-zinc molar ratio 5 to 15) and 15% with little or none; iron absorption 10% with 30 to 90 g of meat or fish a day and 15% above 90 g [APR-04]. Multiplying each band by its absorption gives the same absorbed need, about 1.65 to 1.68 mg of zinc a day at age 6 to 7 and 2.6 mg for boys aged 10 to 11 (our calculation). A lunch's share is 0.50 to 0.67 mg absorbed at 6 to 7. A plant product that removes the animal food from a lunch does not change the child's need; it changes how much zinc the kitchen must serve to meet it.

**Protein against the need.** Published lunches carry 31.8 g of protein [PMR-26], two to three times the lunch share. In reference-protein terms (need x 0.89) the lunch share is 8.5 to 11.4 g at age 6 to 7 and 13.1 to 17.4 g for boys aged 10 to 11 (our calculation). Decision 3958's own energy-share rule (protein 13 to 20% of energy) would put the lunch at 15.3 to 31.4 g for a 6 to 7 year old boy, which is why software-built menus land high (see `disagreements.csv`).

Three points matter for Vietnam.

- **Processing and method move the numbers.** Soy products range from DIAAS 64 (tofu) to 91 (soymilk) in the one quantitative review, textured soy from 65 to 97, and PDCAAS-like scores flatter plant proteins against DIAAS [NQR-05; NQR-15]. **Bioavailability.** Whole oilseeds carry about 1.5% phytate on a dry basis [NQR-17], and soybean phytate ranged from 0.42 to 1.92% as phosphorus supply varied [NQR-18]. With soybean zinc of 3.8 mg per 100 g, the phytate-to-zinc molar ratio is about 34 (9 to 43), above the 15 at which the guidance rates zinc absorption poor (our calculation) [APR-04; DIE-12]. Most soymilk phytate is taken into the tofu curd [NQR-19], so tofu inherits it. Soy's high iron is also largely unavailable: the phytate-to-iron molar ratio of soybean is about 9.9, and Hurrell recommends below 1, preferably below 0.5, for an iron-fortified food, or complete enzymatic removal of phytate; iron absorption from porridges of whole-grain cereal and legume with about 1 g phytic acid per 100 g can be as low as 2 to 3% [NQR-16]. Phytate-to-mineral ratios set zinc and iron needs by diet type, which is why EFSA sets adult zinc requirements at four phytate intakes [NQR-20]. Polished rice is low in phytate, which makes it a good fortification vehicle [NQR-23]. [general, Medium; inferred for the ratios]

The finding is precise: tofu in today's plant slot is safe for zinc because it sits beside fish or meat; an all-plant day, which a caterer could build with a cheap textured-soy dish, is not, unless the soy is fortified. Vegetables would add some zinc; they are excluded because the menus do not give grams.

**Vitamin B12.** Plant foods have none (FCT 2007); pork, egg, milk and chicken do [DIE-12]. The 2026 lookup lists no B12 value for these ages [NQR-01]. At two plant days a week B12 is not a school-lunch issue; a product that replaces animal food every day would need it.

**Sodium.** The 2026 limits are under 1,800 mg a day at age 6 to 7 and under 2,000 mg at 8 to 11 [NQR-01]; Decision 3958 says under 4 g of salt a day [APR-04]. Frozen analogues in the AltProtein Vietnam retail audit carry a median 596.5 mg of sodium per 100 g (470 to 740, n = 8) at 17.4 g of protein; seasoned dry textured soy up to 818 mg; tofu 12 mg [FORM-01]. A 20 g-protein portion of a frozen analogue therefore carries about 686 mg (540 to 851), the whole lunch allowance for a 6 to 7 year old (our calculation). [VN-direct, Medium; revealed and inferred]

## 4. Fortification rules

`data_fortification_rules.csv` extracts the clauses.

- **Decree 09/2016/NĐ-CP** (28 January 2016, in force) makes iodine, iron, zinc and vitamin A the mandatory fortificants (Article 5) and names three vehicles (Article 6): "Muối dùng để ăn trực tiếp, dùng trong chế biến thực phẩm phải được tăng cường I-ốt" (salt for eating and processing must be iodised), "Bột mỳ dùng trong chế biến thực phẩm phải được tăng cường sắt và kẽm" (wheat flour used in processing must carry iron and zinc) and vitamin A in soy, palm, rapeseed or peanut oil "trừ dầu thực vật dùng trong chế biến thực phẩm theo phương pháp công nghiệp" (except oil for industrial processing). Levels sit in national technical regulations (Article 7). [VN-direct, Medium, NQR-02; revealed]
- **What reaches a plant product:** only iodised salt, and iron and zinc through any wheat flour in the recipe. Tofu, textured soy and soy milk get nothing. Vital wheat gluten is not flour; whether a gluten maker's flour must be fortified is a question for MOH (our reading).
- **Amendment:** a Deputy Prime Minister's meeting on 18 September 2025 accepted that traditional fish-sauce makers should be encouraged, not required, to use iodised salt, and asked MOH to submit the draft by 23 September 2025 [NQR-03]. We found no issued amending decree on 25 September 2026 (Low-confidence negative).
- **Voluntary fortification:** Decree 09 regulates only the mandatory vehicles as read. Under Decree 15/2018, processed packaged foods self-declare (Article 4), but foods for special diets and nutrition products for children up to 36 months must register their declaration (Article 6) [NQR-04]. A zinc-fortified dish base for caterers can self-declare if it makes no special-diet claim; a complementary food cannot.
- **The school guidance invites it:** "Ưu tiên sử dụng các thực phẩm được tăng cường vi chất dinh dưỡng theo quy định hiện hành" (prefer foods fortified with micronutrients under current rules) and "sử dụng thực phẩm có bổ sung sắt, kẽm, canxi" (use foods with added iron, zinc, calcium) [APR-04]. [VN-direct, High; revealed]

**Border communes.** Decree 339/2025 gives pupils VND 450,000 a month and 8 kg of rice [APR-09], VND 20,455 to 22,500 a school day for non-rice food (our calculation). If half buys protein foods (our assumption), eggs give 25.6 to 32.8 g of protein a day and pork 14.4 to 16.2 g. Protein is affordable even here with eggs; the lunch still needs zinc and iron, and an egg carries only about 0.4 mg of zinc (55 g egg, 86% edible, 0.9 mg per 100 g; our calculation).

**Can a fortified soy dish beat eggs on cost per unit of quality?** On paper and at ingredient level, yes: about 2 to 12 times cheaper per 25 g of quality-adjusted protein, and about 3 to 13 times cheaper per lunch zinc share before the fortificant (still cheaper if the fortificant cost as much as the whole trial biscuit) (our calculation). Four things narrow the gap: the textured-soy cost is an ex-factory estimate before oil, seasoning and labour (eggs also need cooking); eggs bring B12, vitamin A and no approval risk; iron in soy needs an expensive form or phytase; and no child has been asked to eat a fortified textured-soy dish. Unfortified tofu and textured soy cannot beat eggs on zinc at all.

## 6. Evidence from trials

`data_trials.csv` lists eleven entries.

- **Vietnam, school age.** NIN's trial with 510 children aged 6 to 8 gave biscuits with iron 6 mg and zinc 5.6 mg five days a week for four months: haemoglobin +1.87 g/L, plasma zinc +0.61 µmol/L, and more than 40% lower risk of anaemia and of zinc and iodine deficiency [NQR-22]. A school-milk trial with 454 children aged 7 to 8 in three schools reported better height-for-age and 10% less stunting over six months; the abstract does not say how groups were allocated [NQR-25]. [VN-direct, High and Medium; tested]
- **Thailand, school lunches.** Zinc-fortified extruded rice (about 8 mg extra zinc a day) cut zinc deficiency to 29% against 39% in controls [NQR-23]; a seasoning powder with 5 mg zinc and 5 mg iron lowered the odds of zinc deficiency to 0.63 but did not move anaemia [NQR-24]. [VN-adjacent, High; tested]
- **Protein at school age.** Daily milk (200 g) and an egg for two years in rural south-west China did not change height; boys gained 0.6 to 0.7 kg [NQR-26]. A soybean school meal in two Bangladeshi schools added 0.5 cm of height over eight months and raised haemoglobin but not serum zinc or ferritin [NQR-27]. [VN-adjacent, Medium and Low; tested]
- **Infants, where stunting forms.** Meat was no better than a micronutrient-fortified cereal for growth in Guatemala, Pakistan, DR Congo and Zambia [NQR-28]; in rural China, meat added 0.26 cm over a year, and more than half the 18-month deficit was already present at 6 months [NQR-29]; in Cambodia local fish-based products did no better than corn-soy blends, and the authors conclude fortification may be necessary [NQR-30]. A review of two large supplementation trials found protein-quality scores did not predict recovery and milk protein outperformed vegetable proteins [NQR-31]. [general and VN-adjacent, High and Medium; tested]
- **Clean negative.** We found no trial of textured soy, plant-meat analogues or blended meat-and-plant foods in children in Asia or elsewhere (four searches; not a systematic review). [general, Low]

**What the trials say for Part VI.** In school-age children the measurable benefit of a food intervention is micronutrient status, not height, and it comes from fortification rather than the protein source. For stunting, the window is the first two years, and the comparators there are fortified cereal blends and milk.

## 7. The specification NIN or UNICEF would need

`data_product_spec.csv` sets this out. No NIN or UNICEF document we read names plant or blended products for schools or stunting; the specification below is what the rules and trials imply, not a stated requirement.

| Parameter | School lunch dish, ages 6 to 11 | Complementary food, 6 to 23 months |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | 10 to 15 g per child serving (rice adds about 8.7 g) | per the standard UNICEF supports (not retrieved) |
| Protein quality | DIAAS at least 75 on the young-child pattern, or a blend with meat, egg or milk | DIAAS at least 75 on the young-child pattern; milk or animal protein favoured by current trials |
| Zinc | 3 to 5 mg added per serving (zinc oxide or sulphate) if the dish replaces meat or fish | 4 to 5 mg per 40 to 50 g daily dose [NQR-21] |
| Iron | 2 to 3 mg in a phytate-resistant form, or phytase-treated soy | per standard |
| Sodium | at most 300 mg per serving (half the lunch allowance at age 6 to 7) | low |
| Vitamin B12 | only if served daily | add |
| Registration | self-declaration; no children or health claims | registration as a product for children up to 36 months [NQR-04] |
| Evidence | a 4 to 5 month school trial of serum zinc and haemoglobin with plate waste, on NIN's 2009 design [NQR-22] | a growth and micronutrient trial against fortified cereal or milk |

[VN-adjacent, Medium; inferred]

**Answer to the brief.** A plant or blended product can meet NIN's school rules as written if it carries zinc (and ideally iron) and little sodium; protein quality is only binding for plant-only lunches for older boys. A 20 to 30% soy blend in minced dishes needs nothing extra. For UNICEF's stunting work a school product is the wrong instrument; the relevant instrument is the complementary-food standard, where a soy product must clear the young-child pattern, register, and compete with fortified cereal and milk products that trials already support.

## Next-wave candidates

1. **Zinc-fortified soy or blended school dish trial** (different kind: tested evidence, not desk). Four to five months in 6 to 10 schools served by one caterer, measuring serum zinc, haemoglobin, served portions and plate waste, on the design NIN used in 2009. Desk cannot close it. A positive result would let NIN name a fortified plant dish in menu software and make TPP-11 a lead brief for UNICEF and provinces; a null or poor acceptance would confine plant products in schools to blends in minced dishes.
2. **Phytate, zinc and DIAAS of Vietnamese tofu and textured soy** (deeper; laboratory, not desk). Ten samples for phytate and minerals cost little; DIAAS needs an overseas pig or in vitro laboratory. If tofu's ratio is below 15 (unlikely on our numbers), unfortified tofu days would count as moderate and the fortification case weakens.
3. **The complementary-food standard UNICEF will support in 2027 to 2031** (different kind: rule and document; partly desk, mostly a call to UNICEF Viet Nam and MOH). Whether it names an animal-source requirement, a DIAAS threshold or the Codex formulated-complementary-food guideline decides whether any soy product can enter stunting programmes.
4. **Decision 116/QĐ-VDD full text** (deeper; desk possible if NIN posts it). It would confirm the zinc and iron values by age and give the new upper limits, which cap fortification levels.
5. **What border-commune kitchens serve under Decree 339** (deeper; partly desk through school sites in Dien Bien, Son La, Lao Cai, Tuyen Quang and Cao Bang, plus calls). Wave 7 L1 reached no upland school. If upland lunches are rice, vegetables and little animal food, the zinc gap is real and large, and TPP-11 has a funded buyer.

**Where to stop.** More desk searching for DIAAS values would be noise: the ranges above are wide because methods differ, and only new measurements on Vietnamese products would narrow them. More trial searching would also be noise: the absence of any trial on the product types Part VI proposes is the finding.

## Limits

- DIAAS values come from pigs and in vitro systems, mostly for Western products; the tofu figures disagree by method and pattern, and the one paper likely to hold measured tofu and seitan DIAAS (NQR-32) could not be read (HAL access denied). The plant-burger paper (NQR-12) was paywalled and PubMed returned a captcha; its findings come from a citing paper.
- Blend and lunch protein-quality figures are lower bounds from additivity, not measured.
- Zinc absorption per food uses the guidance's diet-level bands (50%, 30%, 15%) as food-level factors, a simplification. Phytate figures for soy are for soybeans, not measured Vietnamese tofu or textured soy.
- Protein and sodium needs come from NIN's online lookup, which does not label its RNI version; the zinc and iron values match Decision 3958, which cites the 2016 RNI. Decision 116/QĐ-VDD was not obtained. Protein needs for girls aged 8 and over were not retrieved.
- Costs use retail prices of 24 September 2026 and kitchen-platform prices from wave 7 L4; the textured-soy cost is an ex-factory estimate for the ingredient only; the fortificant price was not found. The USDA FoodData Central API refused our requests (rate limit), so mycoprotein and textured-soy zinc were not checked against a second database.
- Decrees 09/2016 and 15/2018 and Notice 503 were read through a fetch tool that summarises pages (Medium). The absence of an amending decree is a single search (Low).
- Trials were found by four OpenAlex and Scite searches, not a systematic review; several were read at abstract level only.
