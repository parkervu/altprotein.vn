# Feed buyers as actors: the demand check for microbial feed protein (T2)

Line: W8-L2 Feed buyers as actors: the demand check for microbial feed protein (play T2) (wave 8). Source prefix: FBA. Date: 25 September 2026.

## Verdict

Vietnamese feed mills already buy microbial feed ingredients. In 2025 they imported about 9,200 t of inactive yeast and other dead single-cell organisms, 2.4 times the 2019 tonnage, and 95% of it cost about USD 1.1 per kg. That is revealed demand, and the package did not have it. The export standards that Part VI treated as T2's pull are neutral. ASC, BAP and GLOBALG.A.P. neither require nor reward a yeast, fungal or bacterial ingredient at 1 to 5% inclusion. The largest global aquafeed maker also reached only 0.6% novel ingredients against a 5 to 10% target for 2025. T2's market pull should stay at 4, but for a different reason. The price test gets much harder: a Vietnamese product must match imported bulk yeast, or show in head-to-head trials a gain worth about 1.2 to 1.8% of shrimp-feed cost. Yield class: **added a constraint** (the revealed price and the head-to-head test), with a **correction** to ch27's reading of the export pull. The score itself does not change.

## Headline findings

1. **Vietnam imports about 9,200 t of inactive yeast a year, and the volume is growing.** Partner-reported exports to Vietnam under HS 2102.20 were 3,828 t (USD 8.3 million) in 2019, 9,336 t (USD 13.0 million) in 2024 and 9,193 t (USD 13.5 million) in 2025. The 2025 figure is a lower bound because Cambodia had not yet reported. China shipped 4,236 t in 2025, up from 1,899 t in 2019; Brazil shipped 2,712 t and Serbia 940 t [VN-direct, Medium, FBA-17, revealed]. HS6 data cannot split feed from food. Unit values of about USD 1 per kg suggest mostly feed-grade product, but that is our inference.
2. **Buyers pay about USD 1.1 per kg for most of it.** In 2025, 8,729 t (95%) came in at below USD 2 per kg, averaging USD 1.10 per kg FOB. Another 464 t of specialty products averaged USD 8.55 per kg, and more than half of that value came from Japan [VN-direct, Medium, FBA-17, revealed]. That is the revealed willingness to pay T2 must meet. It sits at the bottom of the package's theoretical functional band of USD 1,000 to 4,250 per t for pangasius.
3. **Vietnam already makes and exports the same product.** Partner countries recorded 8,166 t of HS 2102.20 from Vietnam in 2025 at USD 1.39 per kg, mostly to the United States and Japan [VN-direct, Medium, FBA-19, revealed]. Domestic yeast plants include AB Mauri La Nga (6,000 t a year) and Lesaffre's Saf-Viet [VN-direct, Medium, IND-15; IND-17, revealed]. "Made on Vietnamese carbohydrate" is not new.
4. **The standards are neutral for T2.** ASC Feed Standard v1.2 has been mandatory since 2 February 2026. Under it, feed stuffs need due diligence only on the ingredient maker. Any single ingredient under 1% of a mill's yearly aquafeed intake counts as eligible automatically. The standard sets no requirement and gives no credit for microbial protein [general, High, FBA-01; EXP-30, revealed]. BAP's August 2026 amendment counts ingredients from "algae and other aquatic microorganisms" towards its 75% marine-ingredient target, but it does not say whether yeast or bacteria grown on sugar qualify [general, High, FBA-05, revealed]. GLOBALG.A.P. and the UK Soy Manifesto say nothing about novel ingredients [general, Medium, FBA-06; EXP-33, revealed].
5. **ASC certification is spreading fast in Vietnam.** On 25 September 2026 the ASC register listed 15 certified Vietnamese feed mills, the most of any country (15 of 114 worldwide), and 6 more in initial audit [VN-direct, High, FBA-04, revealed]. From 1 May 2028 all ASC farm audits use the Farm Standard, and indicator 1.4.3 requires compound feed from an ASC-certified mill [general, Medium, FBA-02; FBA-03, revealed]. The certification pull is for traceable soy and marine inputs, not for microbial protein.
6. **The biggest buyer missed its novel-ingredient target by a factor of 3 to 7 (Skretting) and 8 to 17 (Nutreco group).** Nutreco set a target of 5 to 10% novel ingredients by volume for 2025. It reached 0.6% (Skretting 1.5%, Trouw Nutrition 0.3%) and now judges novel ingredients on footprint, resilience and customer value rather than volume. Its target of 100% deforestation-free soy and palm reached 40% [VN-adjacent, High, FBA-07, revealed]. Skretting counts yeast single-cell protein, bacterial single-cell protein, mycoprotein and functional ingredients as "novel", so T2 products sit inside that 1.5% [general, Medium, FBA-08, stated].
7. **No named Vietnamese mill has published a purchase of microbial protein.** Grobest has evaluated bacterial single-cell protein and fermented corn protein, then in 2024 moved its research to canola protein concentrate (up to 80% fishmeal replaced in a Taiwan trial) [VN-direct, Medium, FBA-13, revealed]. The 2025 reports of C.P. (CPF), De Heus and Tongwei contain no microbial-protein target or trial [VN-adjacent, Medium, FBA-11; FBA-12; FBA-14, revealed]. The only novel-ingredient offtakes in Vietnam are for insect meal (Vinh Hoan; Skretting), which we cite as a benchmark only [VN-direct, High, EXP-50; VCO-28, revealed]. This answers wave 7's AIS-Q14 with a clean negative.
8. **Pangasius exporters increasingly make their own ASC-certified feed.** Feed One, a 75%-owned Vinh Hoan subsidiary, has been certified since July 2024. Nam Viet Aquaculture Feed and Sao Mai Super Feed were certified in 2026, and Godaco is in audit [VN-direct, High, FBA-15; FBA-04, revealed]. T2's pangasius buyers are these integrated groups.
9. **Vietnamese mills pay less for fishmeal than the World Bank benchmark suggests, and the price is rising.** Partner-reported fishmeal exports to Vietnam averaged USD 1,163 per t FOB in 2025, 32% below the World Bank's Peru 65% CIF benchmark of USD 1,706. In January to July 2026 the average was USD 1,622 per t, up about 39%; India's price rose from USD 1,228 to 1,728 per t (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, FBA-20; FM-01, revealed]. The benchmark reached USD 2,500 per t in August 2026 [general, High, FM-01, revealed].
10. **The buyer's test is head-to-head, per unit of performance.** Take T2's indicative cost of USD 2,227 per t at 2% inclusion. It must beat commodity yeast by a gain worth 1.2 to 1.8% of shrimp-feed cost, or 2.9 to 5.9% of pangasius-feed cost. At the high end of the cost stack (USD 6,612 per t) the hurdles are 5.7 to 8.7% and 14 to 29% (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, FBA-17; FM-04; COST-43, inferred]. Even a tolled plant's variable cost (USD 883 to 1,905 per t) spans the bulk yeast price.
11. **EU deforestation rules do not reach Vietnamese seafood through its feed.** The EUDR covers soy, palm, cattle, wood, cocoa, coffee and rubber. Fish and crustaceans are not covered commodities, so the regulation reaches Vietnamese shrimp and pangasius exports only through retailer and certification rules (our reading) [general, Medium, EXP-27, revealed].

## 1. What we tested and how

Part VI left T2's market pull at 4 on an inferred export pull. Its reasoning was that the ASC feed standard and UK retailers' soy commitments reward traceable non-soy protein, "but no buyer pays a premium" (ch27.5; `play_demand_check.csv`). Wave 7's actor check covered food incumbents, funders, investors and policy bodies, but not feed buyers. Its investor group asked whether any anchor buyer exists for a non-insect microbial feed ingredient (AIS-Q14).

We did four things:
- Built a register of 19 feed buyers and standard setters (`data_feed_buyer_register.csv`). Where reachable, we read their 2024 to 2026 sustainability reports and standards in full text.
- Read the current texts and dates of ASC (feed and farm), BAP, GLOBALG.A.P., the UK Soy Manifesto and the EUDR (`data_feed_standards_timeline.csv`).
- Measured revealed demand: HS 2102 trade from the UN Comtrade public API (Vietnam-reported and mirror), fishmeal prices paid in 2024 to 2026, and the ASC feed-mill register queried through the ASC site's own search (`data_inactive_yeast_trade.csv`, `data_fishmeal_prices_paid.csv`, `data_asc_feed_mills_vietnam.csv`).
- Derived what a buyer needs to see, with a price-per-performance calculation (`data_t2_price_per_performance.csv`).

We reused the package's feed sources (FM, AQF, FTG, REG, EXP, VCO, IND, COST) and did not repeat wave 7's tariff work (FTR-32: Chinese HS 2102 enters at 0% under ACFTA and RCEP).

## 3. What the standards require, and when

| Standard | In force | Marine ingredients | Soy | Microbial or novel ingredients | GHG |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ASC Feed v1.2 | Mandatory since 2 Feb 2026; medium-risk ingredients allowed as non-eligible to 2 Feb 2029 | Majority sustainability level rises one level every three years | Commitment with cut-off 31 Dec 2020; eligible soy volume at least equal to soy in ASC feed sold | Feed stuffs: ingredient-maker due diligence only; under-1% ingredients eligible; no credit | Report kg CO2e per t yearly; no target |
| ASC Farm v1.0.1A2 | Effective 5 Aug 2026; all audits from 1 May 2028 | Via mill | Via mill | GMO disclosure above 0.9% | Farm-level criterion |
| BAP Feed Mill 3.3 | 22 Dec 2025; amendment 5 Aug 2026 | 75% certified or improvement-project, suspended to 1 Jul 2027 where none is made nationally | 50% certified; 100% deforestation-free plan due 1 Jan 2026 | Algae and other aquatic microorganisms count towards the 75% | Not in clauses read |
| GLOBALG.A.P. CFM 3.1 | 1 Jan 2024 | No IUU or endangered species | FEFAC guidelines | None | None |
| UK Soy Manifesto | Deadline end 2025 | None | Deforestation-free, cut-off 2020, including feed | None | None |
| EUDR | 30 Dec 2026 (large and medium), 30 Jun 2027 (small) | None | Soy on the EU market | None | None |

Sources: FBA-01; FBA-02; FBA-03; FBA-05; FBA-06; EXP-27; EXP-30; EXP-33 [general, High to Medium, revealed].

**Reading.** Three points matter for T2. First, ASC makes compliance cheap for a small-inclusion ingredient but gives it nothing extra. A T2 product at 2% of shrimp feed will usually be under 1% of a mixed mill's aquafeed intake (our inference). Second, the GHG rule is disclosure only. A low-carbon ingredient at 1 to 5% moves a mill's reported figure very little and earns no price. Third, the deadline that will bite is ASC's farm transition on 1 May 2028. After it, mills supplying ASC farms must hold ASC certificates and will ask every ingredient maker for due-diligence data. A Vietnamese T2 supplier should expect that request, not a premium.

The package's ch27.5 cites the ASC Feed Standard v1.1 text (EXP-29). v1.1 never took effect; v1.2 did (EXP-30). v1.2 keeps the 2020 cut-off and the GHG rules and adds the eligible-volume rule for soy and palm (3.2.2, 5.1.10) and the medium-risk exception to 2029 [general, High, FBA-01, revealed].

## 5. What named buyers do, not what they say

- **Nutreco and Skretting** give the clearest revealed evidence. They set a volume target, reported against it, missed it several times over, and dropped volume as the measure. Their screening criteria are food safety, nutritional value, scalability, cost competitiveness and environmental and social impact. A Vietnamese supplier would face those tests (FBA-07; FBA-08).
- **Grobest** is the only Vietnamese shrimp-feed leader that says it has tested bacterial single-cell protein. It did not report the result, and it moved to canola protein concentrate, a cheaper plant input (FBA-13).
- **CPF, De Heus and Tongwei** publish soy and marine sourcing targets but no microbial-protein content. CPF sells its innovation as probiotics. De Heus names dependency on Chinese feed additives as a strategic risk, a stated opening for a domestic supplier (FBA-11; FBA-12; FBA-14).
- **Integrated pangasius groups** now own ASC-certified feed mills. Vinh Hoan's Feed One is the obvious anchor-buyer candidate because the parent has already signed a multi-year novel-ingredient offtake (insect, benchmark only) (FBA-15; EXP-50).
- **Export buyers** set rules for soy, marine inputs and GHG disclosure. They pay no premium and set no novel-ingredient target that we found (FBA-01; FBA-05; EXP-33).

| Requirement | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|

| Shrimp (feed USD 1,270 to 1,920 per t) | Low (2,227) | 2% | 22.5 | 1.2 to 1.8 |
| Shrimp | High (6,612) | 2% | 110.2 | 5.7 to 8.7 |
| Pangasius (feed USD 385 to 770 per t) | Low (2,227) | 2% | 22.5 | 2.9 to 5.9 |

Sources: FBA-17; FM-04; COST-43; COST-44 [VN-direct, Low, inferred]. Rows for 1% and 5% inclusion and the specialty tier are in `data_t2_price_per_performance.csv`. Shrimp is the only plausible first market for a purpose-grown product. Pangasius works only for a by-product yeast priced near USD 1.1 per kg. The specialty tier (USD 8.55 per kg) leaves headroom, but it is only about 460 t a year and is held by branded yeast fractions with their own trial dossiers (FBA-21).

## 7. Should T2's market pull move?

C1 defines market pull as "named buyers with a present need and a willingness to pay". A 5 means several named buyers are already buying the imported or incumbent equivalent (`play_criteria.csv`).

- **For moving up:** the incumbent equivalent is being bought, in growing volume (2.4 times since 2019), and ASC certification is spreading.
- **For moving down:** the revealed willingness to pay is low (USD 1.1 per kg). The standards are neutral. The best-documented buyer programme reached 1.5%. No named Vietnamese mill has published a microbial purchase.
- **Position: stay at 4.** Trade data do not name the importers, so the rubric's 5 is not met. Revealed purchases of the incumbent equivalent rule out a 3. What changes is the evidence and the kill test. The evidence moves from "export pull through standards, inferred" to "domestic purchases of imported feed yeast, revealed". The kill test moves from "ASC or UK retailers drop soy traceability" to a price-per-performance test against commodity yeast.
- **A supply-side point for the ch11 owner.** Bulk imports at USD 1.1 per kg arrive duty-free from China, and domestic yeast plants already export at USD 1.39. That fits C6 = 2 ("commodity with cheaper imports", unless function is proprietary) better than 3. With C6 = 2, T2's balanced score falls from 3.45 to 3.35, and T5 becomes sole third. T2 stays in the top four under every preset (our calculation from `plays.csv` and `play_weight_presets.csv`). We mark this weak, because C6 is outside the demand check.

## Next-wave candidates

1. **Name the importers of HS 2102.20.10, the national line for yeast used in animal feeding.** This is a *different kind* of question: transaction data rather than documents. Desk tools cannot close it; it needs a declaration-level customs extract (DMV-02) or a trade-data vendor. If three or more ASC mills each import 500 t or more, T2's market pull meets the rubric's 5. If most of the tonnage goes to food or pet food, the revealed pool shrinks and the score should drop to 3.
2. **Three structured conversations with mill nutritionists** (Grobest, Skretting, De Heus's Vinh Long marine R&D centre, Sheng Long). Ask which yeast products they use, at what dose and price, what gain they measure, and what head-to-head evidence they would need. This is a *different kind* of question (primary interviews). It is not desk work. It would turn the price-per-performance hurdle from inferred into stated or tested, and could reverse T2's order between shrimp and pangasius.
3. **By-product yeast and bacterial biomass as the T2 route.** The revealed price is set by by-product yeasts. The candidate domestic streams are spent brewer's yeast and amino-acid fermentation biomass (Vedan, Ajinomoto; OQ-017, OQ-018). This is a *different kind* of question (a supply-side route). Company reports and trade press could close it partly. If domestic by-product biomass exists at scale and is not already sold into feed, it adds a route that meets USD 1.1 per kg, which purpose-grown biomass cannot.
4. **BAP's reading of "other aquatic microorganisms".** A *deeper* version of this line, closable with one email to the Global Seafood Alliance. It matters more for microalgae (T6, T3) than for T2.
5. **More feed-company sustainability reports: stop here.** We read the reports of the five largest reachable groups. Reading Haid's annual report or GreenFeed's pages would add citations, not change the verdict. We judge further desk work on company documents to be noise.

## Limits

- HS 2102.20 at six digits mixes food, feed and pet-food yeast. We infer feed use from unit values.
- Partner-reported values are FOB and exclude freight. 2025 annual data lack Cambodia (yeast) and Peru, Ecuador, Senegal and Mauritius (fishmeal). The 2026 monthly data cover only 6 to 10 reporters.
- Vietnam-reported tonnages for HS 2102 are Comtrade estimates and were not used.
- The fisheries and livestock product registers could not be reached. Registered microbial feed products are therefore not counted.
- Haid's website and Cargill's global aqua pages could not be read. Tongwei's report was searched in Chinese by keyword only. Vinh Hoan's 2025 annual statements are image scans; we used the interim statements.
- ASC Farm Standard indicators and GLOBALG.A.P. requirements were read through page summaries, not the full PDFs.
- The T2 cost stack (app-i) is an indicative estimate for a 10,000 t plant. Feed prices are derived from percentage changes (FM-04). Both are Low confidence, so the price-per-performance hurdles are Low confidence too.
- No interviews. Stated needs come from published reports, and no buyer was asked what it would pay.
