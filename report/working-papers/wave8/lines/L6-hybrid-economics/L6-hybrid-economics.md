# When do blends pay? Processor extender economics over the pork price cycle

Line: W8-L6 When do blends pay? Processor extender economics over the pork price cycle (wave 8). Source prefix: HXE. Date: 25 September 2026.

## Verdict

Extending pork products with soy pays at every hog price since 2019 by a wide margin: hydrated soy costs a processor VND 10,000 to 15,000 per kg against VND 83,000 to 141,000 for lean trimmings, so the break-even extender price is 5 to 12 times what Chinese soy costs landed. Replacing 20% of the lean in a *chả lụa* (steamed pork roll) saves VND 9,100 to 16,600 per kg (USD 350 to 640 per tonne, 14 to 15% of raw-material cost); the pork cycle changes the size of the saving, not its sign, and the "hedge" is small. The lean-meat benchmark in TPP-03, ch24.4 and the T9 kill test therefore never binds: a fungal or fermented extender competes with hydrated soy (about USD 2.5 to 2.7 per kg of protein) and with the chicken, skin, starch and water that value lines already use, so it must win on sensory tolerance or on the label, not on price against pork. Extension does not happen everywhere it pays, which shows that product identity, texture limits in fine gels and trust set its reach, not cost. Yield class: **changed a conclusion** (the processor-route price target and kill test, and the "tolerates price" and "hedge" arguments) plus **added a constraint** (format and sensory ceilings); the R3 volumes in ch28 stand.

## Headline findings

1. **The break-even extender price is 5 to 12 times the price of soy.** Against lean trimmings at 1.8 times the live hog price, a 50% protein textured soy stops paying only above USD 8.9 per kg dry at the 2019 hog price and USD 15.0 at the 2020 price; soy isolate only above USD 16 to 27. Chinese textured soy lands at USD 1.29 to 1.44 and isolate at about USD 2.0 to 2.4. Even with the lowest lean price basis (1.5 times live) and the 2019 trough, the headroom is 5.4 times (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, DIE-25; AIB-15; TIC-12; BUY-07; FTR-32, inferred]. Pork has never come near a price at which extension stops paying; that would need a live hog below about VND 7,000 per kg.
2. **A 20% extension of a *chả lụa* saves VND 9,100 to 16,600 per kg, and of a sausage VND 7,900 to 14,400 per kg.** That is USD 350 to 640 and USD 300 to 550 per tonne of output at the 2019 and 2020 hog prices, and VND 12,000 and 10,400 per kg at the 2019 to 2024 median. At 10% and 30% replacement the savings halve or rise by half. Raw-material cost falls by 14 to 16% at 20% (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, HXE-01; BUY-05; DIE-25; TIC-12, inferred].
3. **The price cycle scales the saving but does not decide it, and the hedge is small.** The saving at the 2020 hog price is 1.8 times the saving at the 2019 price. A 20% extension cuts the recipe's exposure to the 2019 to 2020 pork shock by about a sixth, from VND 44,800 to 37,300 per kg (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, DIE-25, inferred]. "Hedge against pork price swings" (brief-manufacturers, ch21) overstates this; the saving itself is the reason to extend.
4. **Vietnamese processors already use soy, and cut cost in value lines mainly with animal by-products and starch.** Seven commercial Vietnamese emulsion sausages tested in a 2018 study all contained soy protein [VN-direct, Medium, HXE-02, revealed]. At Vissan, soy appears in the DHA "nutrition" pork sausage, which declares more lean than the plain one (58% against 55%) and costs 5% more per kg. The cheapest *giò lụa* (Huong Que, VND 124,200 per kg, below retail lean pork at VND 132,000) lists chicken, pork fat, water and modified starch, not soy [VN-direct, High, BUY-05; COST-39, revealed]. Soy with milk protein binds the lines built on head meat or skin.
5. **A new extender has two possible jobs, with prices a factor of three apart.** As an extender replacing 10 to 30% of the lean, it must match hydrated soy: about USD 2.5 to 2.7 per kg of protein, or about USD 0.7 to 1.2 per kg dry for a 45% protein fungal biomass. As a binder at 1 to 3% of the recipe, it can be priced against egg white and milk protein (USD 3.3 to 4.6 per kg dry, ch26.2). A fungal extender priced at egg white parity has to replace 1.2 to 1.4 times as much lean as soy to give the processor the same saving, for example 24 to 29% against soy's 20% (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, BUY-02; TIC-12, inferred]. Part VI prices TPP-03 against lean and TPP-06 against egg and dairy; neither states the soy benchmark for the extension job.
6. **Sensory ceilings are lower in fine gels than in coarse mince.** Coarse products tolerate about 25% (25% hydrated soy concentrate improved beef patties; 25% mycoprotein replacing lean pork was preferred in a Chinese red sausage) [general, Medium, HXE-05, tested; VN-adjacent, Medium, HXE-06, tested]. In fine emulsions, soy lowered acceptance of Taiwanese emulsified pork meatballs, whole mushroom kept quality only at 10%, and a US study put practical lean replacement below 30% [VN-adjacent, Medium, HXE-08; HXE-07, tested; general, Medium, HXE-04, tested]. In the one Vietnamese consumer test of *chả lụa* recipes, graininess and heterogeneity cut liking, which rules out textured granules in *giò* and *chả* [VN-direct, Medium, HXE-01, tested]. No study supports 50% replacement in a pork product.
7. **We found no Vietnamese study of whether consumers detect or object to plant protein in *giò*, *chả* or sausages.** Consumers in a 2018 Vietnamese test were as satisfied with soy-containing commercial sausages as with alternatives [VN-direct, Medium, HXE-02, tested]. Elsewhere, informed tasting lowers demand for blends (GLB-30), and hybrids above 30% substitution often failed in the UK market [general, Medium, HXE-09; HXE-12, tested]. A 2024 Vietnamese paper repeats a claim that more than 90% of market *giò lụa* contained borax or chemical additives, a sign that what goes into *giò* is a trust issue [VN-direct, Low, HXE-03, stated].
8. **For Vissan, extension could be worth about as much as its whole 2025 profit, which it has not captured as far as labels show.** A 20% extension of 30 to 50% of its 18,547 t of processed food would save VND 58 to 112 billion a year at median hog prices (VND 73 to 142 billion at Q1 2026 prices) and need 224 to 434 t of textured soy, or 249 to 482 t of fungal biomass. Vissan's 2025 pre-tax profit was VND 105.1 billion and its cost of goods sold was 77.2% of revenue (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, BUY-04; BUY-05; TIC-12, inferred; VN-direct, High, BUY-04, revealed].
9. **Nationally, the prize is large in money and small in protein.** Extending 20% of the lean in all processed meat (the Part VI 5% processed-share assumption) would need about 19,000 t of textured soy a year, 1.5 times Vietnam's 2025 textured-protein imports from low-cost origins, save about USD 190 million a year, and displace about 9,100 t of meat protein, 0.9% of the total. At the D-BENCH 2035 adoption share (6%) it is about 1,150 t of soy, USD 11 million and 0.05% of meat protein (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, DMO-0010; DMA-059; TIC-12, inferred].
10. **R3 will mostly deliver imported soy, not domestic or novel protein.** Chinese textured protein and isolate enter at 0% duty (FTR-32), so the cheapest extender is imported unless play T1 exists. Counting only the D-BENCH domestic share (25% by 2035) cuts R3's "domestic or novel" delivered protein in 2035 from 0.79 kt to about 0.20 kt. Meat displaced does not change (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, FTR-32; DMA-016; demand_outputs.csv, inferred].
11. **Clean negatives.** No 2025 annual hog price is published in the sources we could reach; no Vietnamese business-to-business lean trimming price; Vissan's 2025 annual report gives no processed-food breakdown by product; TCVN 8157:2017 and 8158:2017 (cooked comminuted and coarse meat) exist but we could not open their text [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-04; HXE-14, revealed].

## 1. The question and how we answered it

Part VI calls blends "the most likely entry route" for fungal, fermented and plant proteins (ch24.4) and proposes TPP-03, a 20 to 30% extender for processed meat priced "below the cost of the lean meat replaced". It notes that pork prices swing and that 31% of Vissan's meat formulations list soy protein, but it has no processor cost model. We asked four questions. What does a processor save at different hog prices? At what extender price does extension stop paying? How much replacement do products tolerate? How big is the prize?

We built a transparent raw-material model (`tools/hybrid_model.py`, which writes the data files). It swaps lean pork 1:1 for an extender hydrated to 18% protein, the minimum the US school-meal rule sets for hydrated alternate protein products [general, High, HXE-10, revealed]. This keeps the finished product's protein almost unchanged. Processing, packaging and any compensating flavour or colour are left out. All prices are converted at VND 26,000 per USD. We did no web searches. Evidence comes from the package's data, wave 7 lines L2 and L5, Vissan's annual report and online shop, Scite and OpenAlex, and one US federal regulation.

## 3. Two representative products

**Chả lụa or giò lụa.** The only published Vietnamese recipe set we found varies lean meat (70 to 90%), lard (10 to 30%) and starch (0 to 10%) with fixed fish sauce, sugar, MSG, polyphosphate, salt and 10% ice. The best-liked recipe (C1: 70 lean, 25 lard, 5 starch) scored 6.79 of 9 for texture with 68 consumers aged 18 to 26; the 90% lean recipe scored lowest (5.38) [VN-direct, Medium, HXE-01, tested]. We use C1: lean is 61.5% of the batch, and at an assumed 95% steaming yield the product holds about 12.3 g of protein per 100 g from the lean alone. A traditional near-all-lean *giò lụa* measured 19.1 to 19.4 g per 100 g [VN-direct, Medium, HXE-03, tested], and a Hanoi group mixes lean with "approximately 15% pork fat" [VN-direct, Low, HXE-13, tested].

**Sterilised pork sausage.** Vissan's *xúc xích tiệt trùng heo* declares 55% lean pork, fat, corn starch, sugar and salt; the DHA version declares 58% lean plus soy protein [VN-direct, High, BUY-05, revealed]. We assume 15% fat, 20% water, 7% starch and 3% seasonings, and a 98% yield (about 10.7 g of protein per 100 g).

Full recipes and prices are in `data_hybrid_formulations.csv`. Fat, starch and seasoning prices do not affect the savings, only the percentages.

## 6. The pork cycle and the "hedge"

Part VI says an extender "fits a cost motive processors already have: pork prices can rise 70% in a year" (ch24.4) and tells manufacturers it is a way to "hedge pork price swings" (brief-manufacturers; ch21). Both are true in direction and small in size.

- The saving per tonne at the 2020 price is 1.8 times the saving at the 2019 price. Q1 2026 prices give savings 27% above the 2019 to 2024 median.
- A 2019 to 2020 swing raises the raw-material cost of *chả lụa* by VND 44,800 per kg (68%). At 20% extension the rise is VND 37,300 (66%); at 30%, VND 33,600 (64%). Extension trims about a sixth of the exposure (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, DIE-25, inferred].
- Because extension pays even at the 2019 trough, a processor that could extend would do so all the time, not only when pork is dear. Timing pilots to pork peaks is unnecessary; high prices raise attention, not the case.

## 7. What processors already do

Vissan lists soy protein in 19 of 61 meat formulations (BUY-05).

## 8. Two jobs, two prices

| Job | Inclusion | Price the ingredient must meet | For a 45% protein fungal biomass | Volume per large processor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ceiling (never binding): lean pork | | USD 17 to 29 per kg of protein (2019 to 2020) | USD 8 to 13.5 per kg dry | |
| Binder (gel, water and fat binding) | 1 to 3% of the recipe | egg white and milk protein: USD 7 to 10 per kg of protein | USD 3.3 to 4.6 per kg dry (ch26.2) | tens of tonnes (ch26.3: 55 to 280 t of soy protein at Vissan) |
| Extender (replacing 10 to 30% of lean) | 10 to 30% of the lean | hydrated soy: USD 2.5 to 2.7 per kg of protein (Chinese), 1.5 to 3.1 (domestic T1) | USD 0.7 to 1.2 per kg dry | hundreds of tonnes (section 10) |

## 9. Sensory and quality limits

`data_hybrid_sensory_limits.csv` lists the studies. The pattern:

| Format | Evidence | Level without loss | Tags |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coarse mince (patties, meatballs, fillings) | 25% hydrated soy concentrate improved beef patties; 25% mycoprotein replacing lean pork was preferred in Harbin red sausage; 30% pea texturate "can still attract consumers" but half would not buy; chicken mince with 30 to 50% plant protein beat meat in Singapore | about 25%; 30% at the edge | [general, Medium, HXE-05; HXE-09, tested]; [VN-adjacent, Medium, HXE-06; NOV-37; NOV-40, tested] |
| Fine emulsions and gels (*giò*, *chả*, frankfurters, kung-wan) | soy lowered acceptance of Taiwanese emulsified pork meatballs, whey did best; frankfurters took 30% textured soy without texture or off-flavour change but lost juiciness at any level; whole mushroom kept quality only at 10% in emulsion sausage | 10 to 20% (our reading) | [VN-adjacent, Medium, HXE-08; HXE-07, tested]; [general, Medium, HXE-04, tested] |
| Vietnamese *chả lụa* | liking rose with chewy, firm, elastic texture and fell with graininess, heterogeneity and air holes | not tested for extenders | [VN-direct, Medium, HXE-01, tested] |

Four implications (our inference) [VN-direct, Low, inferred]:
- *Giò lụa*, the format TPP-03 and OQ-197 name first, is the hardest place to extend: its value is a springy, homogeneous gel. A textured granule will read as grainy; an extender there must be a fine isolate, concentrate or paste, and 10 to 20% is the defensible range until tested.
- Coarse formats (*xíu mại* meatballs, dumpling and spring-roll fillings, nuggets) are where 20 to 30% is supported.
- "Up to 50%" in TPP-03 has no support for pork products.
- Whole mushrooms are not fungal biomass: at 30% and above they dilute protein and raise cooking loss (HXE-07). A *đạm nấm* (mushroom protein) label needs protein-dense biomass.

## 10. Consumer detection and complaint

We found no Vietnamese study that measured whether consumers detect or object to soy or other plant protein in *giò*, *chả* or sausages (OpenAlex and Scite searches in English and Vietnamese, 25 September 2026). That is a clean negative for the academic record; we could not search the press. What exists:
- Tested acceptance of soy-containing commercial sausages equal to alternatives (HXE-02) [VN-direct, Medium, tested].
- Informed tasting lowers demand for a 30% mushroom blend (GLB-30) and hybrids above 30% substitution have often failed in the UK; negative UK reviews blamed sensory quality, not hybridity [general, Medium, HXE-09; HXE-12, tested and stated].
- A trust problem around *giò*: a 2024 paper cites a claim that more than 90% of market pork *giò lụa* contained borax or chemical additives (not verified by us) [VN-direct, Low, HXE-03, stated]; the package records *thịt giả* (fake meat) crime stories (NOV-72) and premium sausages sold on "88% meat" (BUY-13).

Our inference [VN-direct, Low, inferred]: a declared plant extender in a traditional *giò* risks being read as *độn* (padding). Nutrition-positioned sausages and coarse fillings carry less of that risk.

## 12. Does extension already happen wherever it pays?

Not quite, and the difference is the finding. Extension pays everywhere: in every product, at every hog price since 2019, with any of the soy extenders on the market. If cost decided, every value SKU would be extended to its sensory limit. Instead 64% of Vissan's formulations list no added protein, the cheapest *giò lụa* uses chicken and starch rather than soy, and soy shows up where it binds lower-grade meat or supports a nutrition claim. So extension already happens wherever product identity, texture and trust allow, and cost is not the constraint. The labels cannot show inclusion rates, so we cannot tell how far soy use in the 19 listed formulations goes beyond binding (OQ-203).

This changes the question for a new extender. It is not "is it cheaper than pork?" (everything is) but "does it let the processor go further than soy, or sell something soy cannot?" Possible answers are a higher undetected replacement level in coarse formats, a non-soy or *đạm nấm* label that value-line buyers prefer, or functions soy lacks in fine gels. None has been tested in Vietnam.

## Next-wave candidates

1. **Processor purchasing and R&D interviews (2 to 3 firms).** Inclusion rates of soy in existing lines, the price paid for lean trimmings, and whether a soy-free or *đạm nấm* extender has any value to them. *Different kind* (primary interviews, not desk). Desk research cannot close it. If processors already run 15 to 20% soy extension in value lines, R3 is partly baseline and a novel extender's room shrinks; if they cap soy for label reasons, a non-soy label becomes the product's reason to exist (TPP-03, T9).
2. **Equal-saving blind test: fungal against soy, not against all-meat.** *Deeper* version of OQ-197 with a sharper design. Needs a sensory lab and samples; not desk-closable. A fungal extender scoring at or above soy at 1.3 times soy's replacement level would justify egg-white-level pricing; failing it confines fungal biomass to the binder job (TPP-06).
3. **Label and standard rules for meat content** (quantitative declaration such as "Nạc heo (55%)", TCVN 8157:2017 and 8158:2017, any minimum meat or protein content for *giò* and *xúc xích*). *Different kind* (rules). Desk-closable on thuvienphapluat.vn with the standards' text; overlaps W8-L1. A minimum meat content would cap R3 directly.
4. **Vietnamese press and complaint record on *giò độn* (padded pork rolls).** *Different kind* (revealed consumer reaction). Needs a press search, which this session could not do. Evidence of complaints about declared fillers would lower R3 adoption and push extenders to coarse and nutrition formats.

Further desk work on hog price series or on the international sensory literature would be **noise**: no plausible value moves the break-even within a factor of five, and the sensory pattern is consistent.

## Limits

- The lean trimming price is modelled (1.5 to 2.2 times live), not observed. The conclusions survive the whole range; exact savings do not.
- FAOSTAT's "meat of pig with the bone" producer price is read as live weight, as the package does; its level matches live spot prices, but FAOSTAT does not say so in the extract.
- Soy isolate prices rest on three shipment records (BUY-07, Low). Fungal biomass prices are Part VI's break-evens, not quotes; no fungal biomass is on sale in Vietnam.
- The model is raw materials only. It ignores yield changes (soy usually raises cooking yield, whole mushroom lowers it), compensating flavour and colour, reformulation and qualification costs, and any revenue loss from detection.
- Recipes: one Vietnamese recipe study (young urban panel, n = 68) and one set of declared label values; other shares are assumptions.
- Sensory studies are mostly abstracts; several are outside Vietnam; none tests an extender in *giò lụa*.
- National sizing inherits the Part VI 5% processed-share assumption (Low).
- No web search was available; the press record on fillers and on 2025 hog prices is a gap.
