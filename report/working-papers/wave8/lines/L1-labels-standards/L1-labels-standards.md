# Label, claim and product-standard rules as demand gates

Line: W8-L1 Label, claim and product-standard rules as demand gates (wave 8). Source prefix: LBL. Date: 25 September 2026.

Tags: `[evidence label, confidence, source, demand evidence type]`. Government rules and standards are tagged *revealed*: they show what a regulator or an institutional buyer requires. "Our calculation" marks our arithmetic and "our reading" marks legal interpretation. This is a research summary, not legal advice.

## Verdict

Vietnamese rules already settle most of the label questions that Part VI treats as open. A voluntary national standard copied from Codex sets the protein claim conditions (a "high protein" claim at 10 g per 100 g), no national standard sets a minimum meat content for *giò*, *chả*, *xúc xích* or pâté, and the goods-label decree already forces a printed percentage for any ingredient named in the product name. A national standard also already defines vegetarian and vegan claims, but it counts egg, milk and honey as vegetarian, so DMV-13 as written would write egg into *chay*. The GMO label applies only above 5% of ingredients, so the "GMO collision" in chapter 24 is much narrower than stated. Two of the four label moves in section 29.4 are largely already law and need enforcement, one needs recasting and one shrinks to a single question, so the yield class is **changed a conclusion**. The line also found two instrument changes the package has missed: Decree 37/2026 replaced the goods-label decree, and Circular 30/2026 replaced the nutrition-label circular.

## Headline findings

1. **A 10 to 12 g per 100 g chay food qualifies for a "high protein" claim, and 5 g qualifies for "source of protein".** TCVN 14429:2025 (from Codex CAC/GL 23) sets "source" at 10% of the reference value per 100 g, or 5% per 100 kcal, or 10% per serving, and "high" (*cao*) at twice that. The protein reference value is 50 g, so the thresholds are 5 g and 10 g per 100 g, or 2.5 g and 5 g per 100 kcal (our calculation). The standard is voluntary. [VN-direct, High, LBL-03; LBL-04; LBL-02, revealed]
2. **The word alone does not guarantee density.** Through the per 100 kcal route, watery foods can claim "high protein" at 4 to 5 g per 100 g: Vissan's *Heo hầm chay* (chay stewed pork, 4.5 g and 59 kcal per 100 g) reaches 7.6 g per 100 kcal (our calculation). Of 43 audited foods, 20 (47%) already meet "source" and 7 (16%) meet "high" per 100 g, and none carries a claim. Counting the energy route, 27 of 38 and 13 of 38 do (our calculation). [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01; LBL-03, revealed]
3. **Protein has been on every non-exempt pre-packaged label since 1 January 2026.** Circular 29/2023/TT-BYT made energy, protein, carbohydrate, fat and sodium mandatory. Circular 30/2026/TT-BYT replaced it on 10 July 2026 with the same core rules. Neither sets claim conditions. Foods made by small food businesses are exempt, which covers most wet-market chay (our reading). [VN-direct, High, LBL-01; LBL-02, revealed]
4. **Claims trigger no registration and no advertising approval.** Chay foods, including supplemented foods, are self-declared under Decree 15/2018. Advertising content is confirmed only for health supplements, medical, special-diet and infant products. The 2025 Advertising Law amendment (in force 1 January 2026) and Decree 342/2025 (in force 15 February 2026) add truthfulness and endorser duties, not approvals. [VN-direct, High, REG-02; LBL-18; LBL-19; FTR-20, revealed]
5. **A 20 to 30% extender keeps the meat product name.** TCVN 7049:2020 (heat-treated processed meat), TCVN 7050:2020 (not heat treated) and TCVN 7048:2020 (canned meat) set no minimum meat or protein content and neither limit nor mention plant protein. For canned meat the maker self-declares the meat share. [VN-direct, High, LBL-11; LBL-12; LBL-13, revealed]
6. **Percentage labelling of blends is already law whenever the meat is named.** The goods-label rule says an ingredient used in the product name "must be quantified". Vissan prints the named meat's share on 28 of 29 unique meat-named formulations, and sells meat-named products at 27 to 65% named lean meat (21 processed-meat formulations; our count). [VN-direct, High, LBL-06; LBL-09; BUY-05, revealed]
7. **The national vegetarian standard is not Buddhist chay.** TCVN 13625:2023 (ISO 23662:2021) lets "vegetarian" products contain egg, milk and honey. It is silent on alliums (onion, garlic and related plants). "Vegan" (*thuần chay*) excludes every animal input. Using TCVN 13625 unchanged as the legal meaning of *chay*, as DMV-13 proposes, would make egg lawful in *chay*. [VN-direct, High, LBL-15; CHY-52; CHY-57, revealed]
8. **False chay claims are already unlawful, but nobody checks for meat.** Labels must be truthful and names must not mislead about composition. The largest recorded chay inspection wave (September 2020, after a botulism case) checked hygiene and origin papers: in Hanoi, 93 facilities were inspected and 14 fined a total of VND 55 million, and no meat content was reported. We found no government report of meat or animal DNA in chay products. [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-06; LBL-23; LBL-24; LBL-26, revealed]
9. **No rule reserves *thịt*, *sữa* or *phô mai*.** The dairy vocabulary standard has no Codex-style restriction on dairy terms. The new dairy regulation QCVN 28:2026/BCT (in force 1 September 2026) covers dairy only. A national standard is itself titled *Sữa đậu nành* (soy milk), with a floor of 2.0 g protein per 100 ml. Vissan sells *Heo hầm chay* and *Ragu bò chay* (meat words plus *chay*). [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-06; FTR-07; LBL-16; LBL-17; BUY-05, revealed]
10. **The only state use of a novel-meat name is *thịt nhân tạo*.** No legal text or standard uses *thịt nuôi cấy* (cultivated meat), *thịt thực vật* (plant meat) or *protein thay thế* (alternative protein). A 2025 Bac Ninh biotechnology scheme lists "making *thịt nhân tạo* (artificial meat) from stem cells" as a goal. [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-20; LBL-21, revealed]
11. **The GMO label bites only above 5% of ingredients.** It also does not apply where neither the modified gene nor its product can be detected. A Vietnamese lactoferrin formula contains 80 mg per 100 g (0.08%, our calculation), so a precision-fermented lactoferrin at that dose would need no mandatory GMO label. The open question narrows to high-inclusion uses and to whether "non-GMO" claims stay lawful. [VN-direct, Medium, REG-02; LBL-10; LBL-25, revealed]
12. **The package cites a replaced label decree.** A customs letter of 12 February 2026 states that Decree 37/2026/ND-CP (23 January 2026) replaced Decree 43/2017 and Decree 111/2021. Exact-phrase searches show that its text keeps the name rule, the percentage rule and the GMO wording. We could not read its label chapter. [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-07; LBL-08; LBL-09, revealed]

## A. Nutrition labelling and protein claims (task 1)

### A.1 The instruments

| Instrument | Binding? | What it says | What it does not say | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Circular 30/2026/TT-BYT (in force 10 Jul 2026; replaced Circular 29/2023/TT-BYT) | Mandatory | Pre-packaged foods declare energy, protein (*chất đạm*), carbohydrate, fat and sodium per 100 g, per 100 ml, per serving or per pack. Showing the % of the reference value is optional. Protein reference value 50 g. Stock labelled before 1 Jan 2026 may be sold to expiry. Tolerances are set by Art 49(1) of Decree 37/2026 | No claim conditions and no front-of-pack warnings. Exempts single-ingredient foods and "food made by small food businesses" | LBL-01; LBL-02 |
| TCVN 14429:2025 (CAC/GL 23-1997, revised 2013) | Voluntary national standard | Protein "source" (*nguồn cung cấp*): 10% of NRV per 100 g (solids), or 5% per 100 kcal, or 10% per serving. "High" (*cao*): twice source. Comparative claims need a 25% difference. Every claim needs a nutrition panel. Health claims must be accepted by the national authority | No per 100 ml protein condition and no list of synonyms (such as *giàu*, rich) in the extracts read | LBL-03 |
| TCVN 7088:2025 (CXG 2-1985, revised 2021) | Voluntary | NRV-R protein 50 g for the general population over 36 months | Not a claim rule | LBL-04 |
| Circular 43/2014/TT-BYT Art 8 | Mandatory | Claims for added vitamins, minerals, amino acids and similar only at 10% of the RNI or more. Health claims need 10% of the RNI plus specific scientific evidence | Protein is not named | LBL-05 |

[VN-direct, High, LBL-01 to LBL-05, revealed]

### A.2 What the thresholds mean for the target profiles

| Claim | Per 100 g (solids) | Per 100 kcal | Per serving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source of protein | 5 g | 2.5 g | 5 g |
| High protein (*cao đạm*) | 10 g | 5 g | 10 g |

Inputs: NRV 50 g × 10% = 5 g; × 5% = 2.5 g; "high" = twice "source" (our calculation from LBL-03 and LBL-04).

- **TPP-02 (10 to 12 g per 100 g) clears "high" per 100 g** with a margin of 0 to 2 g. The tolerance for declared values sits in Decree 37/2026 Art 49(1), which we could not read, so the safe design margin is unknown (our reading). [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-02; LBL-03, inferred]
- **The claim itself is easier to reach than the target.** The per 100 kcal route rewards low-energy foods. Plain tofu at 3.8 g and 48 kcal per 100 g, and *Heo hầm chay* at 4.5 g and 59 kcal, both pass "high" on energy (our calculation). A shopper cannot tell from the word *cao đạm* whether a chay roll has 5 g or 12 g. TPP-02 should print the number ("10 g đạm/100 g") on the front, not only the word. [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01; LBL-03, inferred]
- **The market already designs to a claim threshold.** The only protein claim in the audit is Vinamilk's *Sữa Hạt Cao Đạm* (high-protein nut milk) at 5.0 g per 100 ml. At 46.7 kcal per 100 ml (no-sugar variant) that is 10.7 g per 100 kcal, twice the "high" level on energy (our calculation). TPP-05's target of 5 g or more per 100 ml matches existing practice. [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01; LBL-03, revealed]
- ***Giàu đạm* is not a defined term.** The standard uses *cao* (high). A maker who writes *giàu đạm* is judged only against the truthfulness rules. Aligning it with the "high" threshold is prudent (our reading). [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-03; LBL-06, inferred]
- **Health claims have no route for ordinary foods.** A line such as "builds muscle" needs acceptance by the national authority (TCVN 14429). The only binding rule, Circular 43/2014 Art 8(2), covers added micronutrients and needs scientific evidence. TPP-02 should stay with content claims (our reading). [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-03; LBL-05, inferred]

All three product standards are voluntary TCVN. The binding rules are the food-safety limits and the goods-label decree.

### B.2 The binding label rules

Decree 43/2017/ND-CP, read in this line [VN-direct, High, LBL-06, revealed]:

- Art 9: labels must be "*trung thực, rõ ràng, chính xác, phản ánh đúng bản chất của hàng hóa*" (truthful, clear, accurate and reflecting the true nature of the goods).
- Art 11: "*Tên hàng hóa không được làm hiểu sai lệch về bản chất, công dụng và thành phần của hàng hóa*" (the name must not mislead about the nature, use or composition of the goods).
- Art 13(4): "*Trường hợp tên của thành phần được sử dụng làm tên hay một phần của tên hàng hóa thì thành phần đó bắt buộc phải ghi định lượng*" (when an ingredient's name is used as, or as part of, the product name, that ingredient must be quantified).
- Art 18(1): any extra content must be truthful and accurate.

Decree 37/2026/ND-CP replaced Decree 43/2017 and 111/2021 from 23 January 2026 [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-08, revealed]. Exact-phrase searches of the legal database return Decree 37/2026 for the Art 11 phrase, the Art 13(4) phrase, "*bắt buộc phải ghi định lượng*" and the GMO wording, so the rules carry over [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-09, revealed]. We could not read the new article numbers.

### B.3 What processors already do

From the package's Vissan label audit (BUY-05), re-cut in `data_vissan_quid_check.csv` (our count):

- 29 unique non-chay formulations name a meat or liver (*heo*, *bò*, *gà*, *thịt*, *gan*). Of these, 28 print the named ingredient's percentage. The exception is one liver pâté, where no percentage appears in the excerpt read.
- Across 21 processed-meat formulations the named lean share is 27 to 65%. The lowest are *Patê thịt heo* (27% lean pork, plus chicken and fat), *Patê thịt bò* (32%) and *Heo 2 lát* and *Bò 2 lát* (34%). Most of the low-share products also list soy or milk protein.
- Products with generic names (*giò lụa*, *xúc xích Bavaria*, hotdog) list pork and chicken without a percentage, as the rule allows.

[VN-direct, High, BUY-05; LBL-06, revealed]

**Answer to the task.** A 20 to 30% plant or fungal extender can be sold under the meat product name. No standard sets a minimum. A 55% lean sausage with 20 to 30% of its lean replaced keeps 38.5 to 44% lean (our calculation: 55% × 0.7 and 55% × 0.8). That is above the 27 to 34% lean in meat-named products already on sale. If the name names the meat (*giò lụa heo*, *xúc xích bò*), the meat share must be printed. If the name names the extender (*giò lụa nấm*), the extender share must be printed. The risk is not the name. It is a "misleading composition" complaint if the extender is hidden in a premium line sold on meat content; the ingredient list prevents this. [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-06; LBL-11; BUY-05, inferred]

## C. Chay, vegetarian and vegan (task 3)

### C.1 The definition that exists

TCVN 13625:2023 (ISO 23662:2021), published by MOST under Decision 586/QĐ-BKHCN [VN-direct, High, LBL-15, revealed]:

- **Vegetarian** covers ovo-lacto, ovo and lacto vegetarians. Eggs, milk (for the lacto types), honey, bee products and wool products are allowed. Meat, fish, shellfish and all other animal-derived inputs (additives, flavourings, enzymes, carriers, processing aids) are excluded.
- **Vegan** excludes every animal-derived input.
- Plants must take GMP measures against unintended presence, including cleaning of shared lines.
- Terms such as "*sản phẩm chay*", "*sản phẩm thuần chay*" and "*phù hợp cho người ăn chay*" must meet the criteria and stand in the same field of vision as the trade name. One of our two reads of the standard lists *sản phẩm chay* among these terms and the other does not; CHY-57 lists it, so we treat it as covered.
- There is nothing on alliums, Buddhist practice, GMOs, precision fermentation or animal cells.

The Vietnamese Buddhist sense of *chay* differs. The Sangha newspaper's panel says the five pungent plants bind precept holders, and strict buyers avoid egg (CHY-52; ch22.4). So "chay" under TCVN 13625 is broader than the chay that pagoda kitchens buy. The 5% of audited chay-named products that list egg or dairy would pass as "vegetarian" under the standard. [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-15; CHY-52; FORM-01, inferred]

### C.2 Legal force and enforcement

- **No binding definition.** We found no law, decree or circular that defines *thực phẩm chay*. The only national administrative mention is one line in a 2021 MARD meeting notice ("*quản lý thực phẩm chay*", managing chay food). [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-21; LBL-22, revealed]
- **False claims are still unlawful.** A "chay" label on a product containing meat misstates its nature and composition under the goods-label rules. Selling a product that does not match its declaration is fined under Decree 115/2018 Art 22 (read in part). [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-06; LBL-27, revealed]
- **Enforcement checks origin, not meat.** After the Pate Minh Chay botulism case in September 2020, provinces ran coordinated chay inspections. Hanoi inspected 93 of 126 chay facilities and fined 14 for VND 55 million in total. Can Tho recalled 13 products. The checks covered hygiene, origin papers, storage and legal documents, and no meat or animal content was reported. The 2022 and 2025 seizures cited in ch22.4 (CHY-59; CHY-60) were also about untraceable or unlabelled goods. [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-23; CHY-59; CHY-60, revealed]
- **No measured prevalence.** Government press search found no case of meat or animal DNA in chay products, and OpenAlex and Scite found no Vietnamese survey beyond the Can Tho kit paper (CHY-61). This is a clean negative for published evidence, not proof of absence. [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-24; LBL-26; CHY-61, revealed]

## D. Names: *thịt*, *sữa*, *phô mai* and the new-meat words (task 4)

| Question | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Is *sữa* reserved for dairy? | No. TCVN 11216:2015 defines milk as a mammary secretion but, unlike Codex STAN 206, has no clause restricting dairy terms. QCVN 28:2026/BCT (Circular 09/2026/TT-BCT, in force 1 Sep 2026) regulates dairy composition and makes the principal label state the dairy type, and says nothing about plant drinks. The naming regime is permissive. It lets makers use meat and dairy words with a qualifier, and it has no reserved names to fight. The naming risk Part VI found (ch23.8, ch24.5) is in media and state vocabulary, not in law.

## E. GMO labelling and precision-fermented proteins (task 5)

| Rule | Text | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Decree 15/2018 Art 10 | GM information on the label when "at least one GM ingredient is more than 5% of total ingredients" | REG-02 |
| Joint Circular 45/2015/TTLT-BNNPTNT-BKHCN Art 1 | Same 5% trigger. Does not apply to "GM food ... in which the gene or the product of the modified gene cannot be detected", to fresh or unpackaged GM food, or to emergencies. Still listed in force | LBL-10 |
| Decree 111/2021 Appendix I item 5 (now Decree 37/2026) | Write "*biến đổi gen*" beside the GM ingredient's name, with its content | REG-26; LBL-09 |
| Decree 43/2026 (biosafety) | Changed definitions (a product of a GMO includes "protein from the modified gene"), not the labelling threshold | REG-18 (package) |

[VN-direct, High, REG-02; LBL-10; REG-26, revealed]

What this means (our reading) [VN-direct, Medium, LBL-10; LBL-25; REG-02, inferred]:

- **Low-inclusion uses are outside the mandatory label.** VitaDairy's ColosBaby Lactoferrin contains 80 mg of lactoferrin per 100 g, or 0.08% (our calculation: 0.08 g / 100 g). That is about 60 times below the 5% trigger (5 / 0.08 = 62.5). A precision-fermented lactoferrin at this dose needs no GMO label.
- **High-inclusion uses are inside it.** A drink or snack whose main protein is precision-fermented whey or egg protein would pass 5%. It would carry "*biến đổi gen*" unless the recombinant protein counts as "not detectable".
- **The detectability exemption is the real question.** A recombinant protein identical to the native protein cannot be told apart by a lab, yet it is by definition the product of the modified gene. No text or letter resolves this.
- **The brand clash is voluntary.** VitaDairy and Vinasoy market "non-GMO" (NOV-62; NOV-63). A buyer using a low-dose precision-fermented ingredient would face no mandatory label. It would still face the question of whether its "non-GMO" claim stays truthful under the goods-label rules.

## F. Declarations, advertising and whether claims trigger registration (task 6)

- **Decree 15/2018 still governs.** Pre-packaged processed foods, supplemented foods and additives are self-declared. Registration is limited to health supplements, medical and special-diet foods, products for children to 36 months and some additive cases (Art 4 to 7). Decree 46/2026, the would-be successor, remains suspended (package G.3.3). [VN-direct, High, REG-02; REG-06, revealed]
- **Claims do not change the route.** No article links a nutrient claim to registration. Vinasoy self-declares *Fami Canxi Ít Đường* as a "*thực phẩm bổ sung*" (supplemented food) with a calcium content. [VN-direct, High, REG-02; FTR-20, revealed]
- **Advertising.** Decree 15/2018 Art 26 requires advertising content confirmation only for the registered categories. Law 75/2025/QH15 (in force 1 January 2026) bans untruthful ads and unproven comparisons, requires a declared product, and makes endorsers check product documents. Decree 342/2025/ND-CP (in force 15 February 2026) requires only the product name and responsible firm in ordinary food ads. [VN-direct, Medium, REG-02; LBL-18; LBL-19, revealed]
- **The 2026 draft law keeps registration narrow** (medical, special-diet, infant and supplement products) and adds no claim provisions in the summaries read by the package. [VN-direct, Medium, REG2-05; REG2-06, revealed]

## Next-wave candidates

1. **Agency interpretation of detectability** (DMV-16, OQ-201). Would MAE and VFA treat a recombinant protein identical to a native one as a detectable product of the modified gene, and may a product that contains one below 5% still claim "non-GMO"? This is a *deeper* version of this line. Desk research cannot close it, because no letter exists; a joint written question can. A "not detectable" answer would remove the GMO label from all but novel-sequence proteins and reopen formula and dairy buyers (ch24.2). A "detectable" answer confirms the label for high-inclusion uses only.
2. **Compliance and enforcement in the chay aisle.** Do packaged chay foods now print protein as Circular 30/2026 requires, and does any province test chay for meat DNA? This is a *different kind* of question: what happens, not what the rule says. A 50-SKU label audit and a request to VFA and NIFC could close it cheaply; desk work cannot. Widespread non-compliance or zero testing would raise DMV-07's priority and change how TPP-02 earns trust.
3. **Decree 37/2026 chapter IV and Art 49 tolerances.** This is *deeper* and desk-closable with the official gazette text. It matters only if the wording changed; the phrase searches suggest it did not. Expected yield is low beyond the tolerance figure for TPP-02's design margin.
4. **The new Food Safety Law and its decree** (National Assembly session from October 2026). Do they add claim conditions, a chay definition or reserved names? This is *deeper* and closable by reading the text once it is posted. A reserved-name clause would reverse the naming finding and make DMV-15 urgent.
5. We think further desk work on Vietnamese label and standard texts would be noise. The remaining uncertainty is in agency interpretation and in enforcement, not in the texts.

## Limits

- The fetch tool returns extracts, not full verbatim texts. TCVN pages on Thư Viện Pháp Luật show summaries or partial texts (full standards are sold). Two clauses were seen only in extracts: TCVN 14429's per 100 ml protein condition (not shown) and TCVN 13625's list of label terms (two reads differ; logged).
- The label chapter of Decree 37/2026 did not render: the page stopped at Art 32. The carry-over of the name, quantitative-ingredient and GMO rules rests on a customs letter and exact-phrase index matches. Article numbers for the new rules are unknown.
- The fetch tool hit a session limit for about 90 minutes. vfa.gov.vn could not be reached (robots file timed out). tcvn.gov.vn offered no standards search, and its home page carried an unrelated hidden link, so we did not rely on it. Provincial portals were not searched for enforcement cases.
- Audit counts come from FORM-01 (Nha Trang and Ho Chi Minh City, September 2026): 43 foods, 38 with an energy value. Serving-size routes were not tested.
- The Vissan table uses ingredient excerpts from BUY-05, which may be truncated. "28 of 29" counts formulations with a percentage anywhere for the named meat.
- Penalties (Decree 115/2018) were read only in part.
- This is not legal advice. Where an interpretation matters, a written opinion from VFA, MAE or MOST is the cheapest way to settle it.
