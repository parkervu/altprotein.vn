Wave 3 research notes for Part V (Futures 2030 to 2050) of the AltProtein Vietnam supply-side study, edition 1.1. Question tree: F7 (weak signals and wildcards) and F8 (scenarios, as signposts). Research date 24 September 2026; all sources accessed 2026-09-24.

## Headline findings

1. **Vietnam's alternative-protein research intensity is about 30% of Thailand's and 40% of Singapore's.** Core-basket works per 10,000 national publications, 2015 to 2025: Vietnam 4.1, Thailand 13.4, Singapore 10.3, Philippines 5.6, Malaysia 5.4. This confirms edition 1.0's OpenAIRE estimate with a second database. [VN-direct] Medium. [trend], 2015 to 2025. (HSC-01; BIB-01)
2. **Frontier fields are growing much faster than science as a whole, led by the ones with approvals behind them.** Precision-fermentation papers: 9 (2020), 188 (2025), 234 (2026 to September). AI with food protein: 4 (2020), 127 (2026 so far). Cultivated meat: 508 (2025). All OpenAlex articles and reviews grew about 0.9% a year from 2015 to 2025. Gas-fermentation protein (about 60 papers a year) and molecular farming of food proteins (under 5 a year) remain small. [general] Medium. [trend], 2015 to 2026. (HSC-01)
3. **Vietnam is absent from several frontier fields.** Indexed articles and reviews, 2015 to 2025: 0 on mycoprotein, 0 on molecular farming, 1 on cultivated meat (a legal analysis), 2 on precision fermentation, 2 on gas-derived protein, and 1 on duckweed protein. Thailand has 39 on duckweed protein, even though Vietnam holds a national duckweed collection. [VN-direct] Medium. [trend]. (HSC-01; RD-44)
4. **This closes open question OQ-124, and shows raw counts overstate Vietnam's output about twofold.** HSC screened all 91 raw Vietnamese core-set records. Only 40 (44%) are genuine supply-side work with a Vietnamese author. 12% have no Vietnamese author at all, mostly because two OpenAlex institution records (RIA1 and "Department of Animal Health") absorb foreign authors. [VN-direct] Medium. (HSC-01)
5. **Vietnamese output is speeding up, and the work is moving home.** Screened works: 7 (2015 to 2019), 9 (2020 to 2022), 13 (2023 to 2025), and 11 in 2026 up to September. Before 2025, most papers were aquafeed trials with Australian partners (about 60% internationally co-authored). The 2025 to 2026 wave is mostly domestic food-technology work (about 70% without foreign co-authors) from IUH, HUIT, HCMUTE, Phenikaa and VAST, including the first Vietnamese livestock stem-cell characterisation relevant to cultivated meat. [VN-direct] Medium (Low for the co-authorship shares). [signal], 2025 to 2026. (HSC-01)
6. **Top institutions and partners.** Raw counts for the adjacent set, 2015 to 2026: VAST 37, VNU-HCM 36 (including HCMUT 21), HUST 20, Duy Tan 17, Can Tho 17, RIA1 17 (inflated by mis-mapping), IUH 15, VNU Hanoi 14, Nong Lam 14, Nha Trang 12. Raw international co-authorship is 52 to 62%, against 50.4% for all Vietnamese science. Company co-authors appear on 2 to 3% of papers, against 3.6% overall. Australia is the leading partner country. No Singapore co-authorship appears in either set. [VN-direct] Medium. (HSC-01)
7. **The anchoveta wildcard is partly happening in 2026.** Peru's 2026 first-season quota was 1.9 Mt, 36% below 2025 and the lowest in a decade. Only about 25% was landed before the season closed on 21 August 2026, with 65% juveniles in the surveyed stock. World fishmeal output fell 26% year on year in January to April 2026. NOAA (10 September 2026) gives a greater than 90% chance of a very strong El Niño in winter 2026-27, and 75% for a record event above +2.5 °C. [general] Medium (High for NOAA). [signal; projection with published probabilities], 2026 to 2027. (HSC-28; HSC-29; HSC-30)
8. **Private capital is at a trough.** Alternative-protein funding was USD 881 M in 2025, down 20% and below USD 1 billion for the first time in 7 years. Cultivated meat raised USD 74 M (down 49%) and fermentation USD 357 M (down 43.5%). Meatable, CellRev and Upstream Foods closed in 2025, and Believer Meats shut its plant in December 2025 despite FDA clearance. [general] Medium. [trend], 2024 to 2026. (HSC-10; HSC-08; FTB-24)
9. **China's fermentation base is the benchmark Vietnam must meet.** Angel Yeast opened an 11,000 t a year yeast-protein plant (November 2025). Cabio and Nourish have about 170,000 t of finished-product capacity, and Fushine is moving to a 20,000 t mycoprotein plant. A plant that costs USD 100 M to build in the US costs about USD 15 M in China. The NHC approved mycoprotein and its first ingredient from a genetically modified microorganism in 2025. [VN-adjacent] Medium. [signal], 2025 to 2026. (HSC-11; HSC-13; HSC-14)
10. **Singapore keeps approving products but has stepped back politically.** It dropped "30 by 30" on 6 November 2025. It now targets 30% local protein by 2035 and calls alternative proteins "not part of the national food security strategy in the short term". It still approved cultivated duck (17 April 2026) and the world's first cultivated beef (4 August 2026). [VN-adjacent] Medium. [signal]. (HSC-36; HSC-12)
11. **Backlash is real.** Seven US states now ban or pause cultivated meat, and 21 have labelling laws (as of April 2026). [general] High. [signal]. (HSC-35)
12. **The only probabilistic forecast of cultivated-meat volume is sober. Consultancy forecasts look like hype.** A Rethink Priorities panel gave a 54% chance that world output stays below 100,000 t a year through 2051, and 6% for more than 100,000 t at under USD 10 per kg by 2031. By contrast, Kearney (2019) projected 35% of "meat" from cultured sources by 2040, McKinsey (2021) 1.5 Mt by 2030, and BCG with Blue Horizon (2021) an 11% alternative-protein share by 2035. Only 9 of 84 resolved timeline predictions made before 2021 were right. [general] Medium. [projection with published probabilities]. (HSC-18; HSC-19; HSC-21 to HSC-23)
13. **A Vietnamese feed company has already made yeast protein.** Thai Duong Feed JSC ran a 2016 to 2019 national project on yeast protein (more than 46% protein) made from cereals and molasses to replace soybean meal. It produced 120 t of liquid product and installed a line costing VND 167 billion. The project links to a 2019 US patent application, the only Vietnam-linked alternative-protein patent we found. The line's current status is unknown. [VN-direct] Medium (Low for the patent). [signal]. (HSC-06; HSC-07)
14. **ASF cuts both ways as a wildcard.** Vietnam has licensed three live attenuated ASF vaccines. But recombinant genotype I/II strains, circulating since 2023, are not covered, and reversion and shedding remain concerns. Vaccine escape would cut feed demand. A fully protective vaccine would raise demand for feed protein. [VN-direct] Medium. [signal], 2026 to 2035. (HSC-31)
15. **H5N1 is quiet for now.** There were 12 human cases worldwide from August 2025 to June 2026 (8 in Cambodia), none in Vietnam, and no person-to-person spread. The CDC rates the risk as low. [VN-adjacent] High. (HSC-26; HSC-27)
16. **Vietnam's intellectual-property route runs through process know-how, not patents.** Patent reports are Western-centred and show no Vietnamese filings. Vietnam's realistic route to 2040 is process know-how on local feedstocks, strains and species. It can also use the mid-2030s expiry of first-generation precision-fermentation patents. [VN-direct] Low. [estimate], 2035. (HSC-02 to HSC-06; FTB-21)
17. **Two critical uncertainties emerge for the 2050 scenarios.** The first is whether feed-protein import stress becomes chronic or stays benign (anchoveta, maize and soy under climate change, shipping). The second is whether Asia's fermentation base becomes an open regional market or falls under Chinese price dominance. The 26 wildcards are in `wildcards.csv`. [VN-direct] Low. [estimate], 2035 to 2050. (HSC-01 to HSC-37)

## Detailed findings

### 1. OpenAlex bibliometrics (closes edition 1.0 open question OQ-124)

**Method.** OpenAlex works API through the OpenAlex MCP connector, run on 24 September 2026 with no personal identifier in any request. Search in titles and abstracts (stemmed phrases), types "article" and "review" only (this drops errata, peer-review records, paratext and preprints), retracted works excluded. Global and country totals use 2015 to 2025 (full years); 2026 counts run to 24 September 2026 and are partial. Country counts are works with at least one author affiliated in that country (so a co-authored work counts for every country on it). Intensity is works per 10,000 of the country's own 2015 to 2025 article and review output (Vietnam 176,197; Thailand 235,346; Malaysia 449,947; Singapore 247,069; Philippines 102,161; Indonesia 2,823,259, inflated by national-journal indexing). Every query string, and the canonical OQL, is stored in `biblio_trends_frontier.csv` and `vn_research_profile.csv` so the counts can be rerun (HSC-01). All counts are keyword lower bounds with false positives; compare by order of magnitude.

**1c. Vietnam's research profile: institutions, co-authorship and partners (OQ-124)**

Two transparent Vietnam sets were defined (full strings in `vn_research_profile.csv`):
- **Core set**: the core-basket terms above with a Vietnamese affiliation, 2015 to 24 September 2026: 91 raw works.
- **Adjacent set**: the core terms plus spirulina, *Arthrospira*, *Pichia pastoris* or *Komagataella*, duckweed or *Lemna* or *Wolffia*, protein isolate, protein concentrate, yeast protein and *Aspergillus oryzae*: 301 raw works.

**Manual screen of the core set.** HSC read the titles and first five authors of all 91 raw works. Only **40 (44%)** are supply-side alternative-protein or feed-protein work with a genuine Vietnamese author; 4 are consumer or legal studies, 5 are insect studies (benchmark only), 4 have a doubtful Vietnamese link and 38 are off-topic or foreign-only records wrongly tagged as Vietnamese. Eleven of the 91 (12%) have no Vietnamese author at all: two mis-mapped OpenAlex institution records (Research Institute for Aquaculture No.1, ID I4210134375, absorbs Indian authors; "Department of Animal Health", ID I4210119377, absorbs Danish, Brazilian and other veterinary departments) cause most of these. [VN-direct] Medium. HSC-01.

> **Correction to edition 1.0.** Appendix K.12 listed the *Leucaena* shrimp-feed trial (10.21077/ijf.2023.70.1.129063-09) as a probable wrong Vietnam tag. OpenAlex shows both authors at Tra Vinh University, so it is a genuine Vietnamese study published in an Indian journal. Conversely, the only "Vietnamese" extrusion experiment (10.1093/ijfood/vvae080), whose affiliation edition 1.0 could not verify, lists three authors at National Pingtung University of Science and Technology (Taiwan) and no Vietnamese affiliation: it should be dropped from Vietnam's count. The 256-citation plant-based meat review (10.1007/s00217-021-03810-1) is India-led with one Ton Duc Thang University co-author. [VN-direct] Medium. HSC-01.

**Screened trend.** Genuine supply-side works by period: 7 (2015 to 2019), 9 (2020 to 2022), 13 (2023 to 2025) and **11 in 2026 to September alone**; 18 of the 40 appeared in 2025 to 2026. [VN-direct] Medium; [trend] 2015 to 2026. HSC-01.

**A shift in who does the work (signal).** Before 2025 the Vietnamese work was mostly aquafeed replacement trials run with foreign partners (about 60% internationally co-authored, mainly Australian networks at Curtin, Sunshine Coast, Murdoch and Deakin, with Can Tho University, Nha Trang University and RIA1). In 2025 to 2026 the new papers are mostly food-technology work from applied universities in Ho Chi Minh City, about 70% without a foreign co-author: jackfruit meat analogue and high-moisture meat analogue (Industrial University of Ho Chi Minh City, IUH), gac-seed protein concentrate and spirulina protein seasoning (HCMC University of Industry and Trade, HUIT), hot-melt extruded proteins (HCMC University of Technology and Engineering), single-cell protein from yeasts isolated from fermented foods (Phenikaa University, 2026), bovine adipose-derived stem cells (VAST, 2025, the first livestock-cell characterisation relevant to cultivated meat we found), and a red-seaweed fishmeal substitute (RIA1 with Can Tho University, 2026). Edition 1.0 listed IUH and Phenikaa among institutions with no relevant output; that is no longer true. [VN-direct] Medium (screen of titles and author lists; Low for the co-authorship shares); [signal] 2025 to 2026. HSC-01.

**Top institutions.** Raw adjacent-set counts (unscreened, institution lineage, 2015 to September 2026): VAST (all units) 37; VNU Ho Chi Minh City (all units) 36, of which HCMUT 21 and HCMC University of Science 11; HUST 20; Duy Tan University 17; Can Tho University 17; RIA1 17 (inflated by mis-mapping); IUH 15; VNU Hanoi (all units) 14; Nong Lam University 14; Nha Trang University 12; Dalat University 11. On the screened core set: RIA1 6, Nha Trang 5, Can Tho 4, Duy Tan 3, HUIT 3, IUH 2, Tra Vinh 2, VNU Hanoi 2. HUST's rice distillers'-grain protein papers do not use the core terms, so it ranks low on the screened core set and high on the adjacent set. Duy Tan's count is driven by reviews from a few internationally recruited researchers, which is a weak signal of domestic laboratory capacity. [VN-direct] Medium. HSC-01.

**Co-authorship and partners.** Raw international co-authorship: 61.5% of the core set and 52.2% of the adjacent set, against 50.4% for all Vietnamese articles and reviews in 2015 to 2025. Company co-authors: 2.2% (core) and 3.0% (adjacent), against 3.6% for all Vietnamese output: industry is less present in this field than in Vietnamese science generally. Leading partner countries: Australia (16.5% of core, 9.3% of adjacent), then Thailand, the United States, China, India, Japan and Germany. **No Singapore co-authorship appears in the top 20 partners of either set**, despite Singapore's regional lead in cultivated-meat research (81 papers). Funders: NAFOSTED is the most acknowledged (5 core, 22 adjacent works), then the Ministry of Education and Training, VAST, VNU-HCM and Vingroup; many Vietnamese papers carry no funder metadata. [VN-direct] Medium. HSC-01.

**What this means for 2040.** At about 20% a year, Vietnam's core-basket output would rise from 17 works (2025) to roughly 100 a year by the mid-2030s (our arithmetic, 17 x 1.2^10 = 105, horizon 2035), about Thailand's 2025 level. That is a mechanical extrapolation, not a forecast; it needs pilot infrastructure and funding that the bibliometrics cannot show. [VN-direct] Low; [estimate] 2035. HSC-01.

### 2. Patent signals

- **The public patent reports are Western-centred and say nothing about Vietnam.** GFI Europe counted more than 5,000 alternative-protein patents published by European organisations in 2015 to 2024, up 960% in a decade, dominated by plant-protein texturisation and functionality (Switzerland 1,232, Netherlands 884, France 751, Germany 596) (HSC-02, HSC-03) [general] Medium; [trend] 2015 to 2024. An IP firm's analysis found filings peaked in 2022 and fell in 2023 (US plant-based filings down 37%), while Japan and Korea raised cultivated-meat filings and five companies held 46.9% of 2023 cultivated filings (HSC-04) [general] Medium; [signal] 2023. A law-firm note says US assignees file about 30% of the sector's patents in mainland China and 50% in Europe (HSC-05) [general] Low.
- **No Vietnamese national filing could be searched.** The Scite patent index is US-weighted and IP Viet Nam's database was not reachable with our tools. The one Vietnam-linked filing found is a US application for high-protein yeast biomass from starch-containing cereals (US 2020/0109428 A1, filed 4 October 2019 by an individual applicant; application status, no grant found) (HSC-06) [VN-direct] Low; [signal].
- **That filing leads to a domestic plant not in edition 1.0.** A national science-information notice describes a 2016 to 2019 project at Thai Duong Feed JSC that selected yeast strains, made a dry yeast-protein feed product with more than 46% protein (PKM-SAN) from cereals and molasses "to replace soybean and other imported protein sources", produced 120 t of liquid product, set pig-feed inclusion rates (8.3% for piglets), and installed a line costing VND 167 billion (report code 16724/2019 at NASATI) (HSC-07) [VN-direct] Medium; [signal]. The published installed capacity ("15,000 tonnes liquid per day and night") is implausible against 120 t produced and is treated as unverified. Its 2026 operating status is unknown.
- **Implication for 2035 to 2050.** First-generation precision-fermentation patents lapse from about 2033 to 2036 (FTB-21). With no Vietnamese patent position and a Western and Chinese filing base, Vietnam's realistic IP route to 2040 is process know-how on local feedstocks and strains (cassava residues, *tương* koji strains, tropical fish cells), not composition-of-matter claims. [VN-direct] Low; [estimate] 2035.

In 2026 the largest cultivated run is 20,000 L (FTB-24) and no product sells at commercial scale anywhere (HSC-12). ### 7. What the scan means for the 2050 scenarios (F8)

The scan points to two critical uncertainties that are genuinely open to 2050 and that other streams can quantify:
1. **Feed-protein import stress: benign or chronic?** Anchoveta, soy and maize supply under climate change (WC-01, WC-05), shipping and conflict risk (WC-06, WC-07), against a possible soy glut (WC-09). The 2026-27 El Niño is the first live test.
2. **Who owns the fermentation base in Asia: open regional market or Chinese price dominance?** Chinese capacity and cost (WC-08; HSC-13, HSC-14), Singapore's retreat from food-security framing (HSC-36), and whether ASEAN recognition emerges (WC-25).

Cross-cutting shocks that can switch a path rather than define one: ASF and H5N1 (WC-02 to WC-04), a novel-food scandal (WC-13), a biosecurity clampdown (WC-21). Links to edition 1.0's 2035 scenarios: chronic import stress plus open regional markets favours Scenario 2 (feed-first) growing into Scenario 3 (regional hub); benign import stress plus Chinese price dominance points to Scenario 1 (import price-taker). [VN-direct] Low; [estimate] 2035 to 2050. This is a suggestion for the scenario writers, not a finding.

## Implications by audience

- **Investors.** The 2025 funding trough (USD 881 M) and Chinese capacity at one-sixth of US build cost mean a Vietnamese fermentation bet in the 2020s must be a low-capex feed play with an offtaker, ideally on second-hand tanks. The 2026-27 El Niño is a near-term test of the fishmeal thesis: watch the Peruvian quotas before committing. Treat consultancy forecasts of cultivated-meat volume as hype; the only probabilistic forecast gives even odds that output stays below 100,000 t a year to 2051.
- **Policy makers.** Research intensity is about 30% of Thailand's, with zero indexed work on mycoprotein and molecular farming and one duckweed-protein paper despite a national germplasm collection. A small directed call (duckweed protein, cassava-residue mycoprotein, DIAAS data) would move Vietnam from absent to visible within five years, because global fields such as gas protein and molecular farming are themselves small. Fix affiliation metadata: OpenAlex mis-maps RIA1 and DAH records.
- **Startups.** New domestic food-tech groups (IUH, HUIT, HCMUTE, Phenikaa) are publishing in 2025 to 2026 without foreign partners; they are the nearest academic partners for plant-based and fermented food products. A Vietnamese feed company already ran a yeast-protein project (2016 to 2019); learn from it before building from scratch.
- **Feed and food manufacturers.** Price the 2026-27 El Niño into 2027 aquafeed contracts; the Peruvian first season landed only a quarter of a record-low quota. Chinese yeast-protein capacity (Angel Yeast 11,000 t) offers an alternative supply as well as competition.
- **Research bodies.** Australian aquafeed networks are Vietnam's main research partners; no Singapore co-authorship appears at all, which is a gap for cultivated and fermented food work. The rerunnable queries in `vn_research_profile.csv` make an annual bibliometric tracker cheap.
- **International bodies.** Fundable public goods with leverage: duckweed protein screening from the national collection; ASF genomic surveillance linked to feed-demand planning; an El Niño early-warning link from fishmeal markets to Vietnamese aquafeed.
