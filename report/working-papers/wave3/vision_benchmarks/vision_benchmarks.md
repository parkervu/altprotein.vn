## Headline findings

1. **No country we checked has a quantified national target for fermentation-derived or cultivated protein volumes.** Quantified protein targets are of three kinds: feed-protein self-sufficiency (EU 35% by 2035; Japan 28% by FY2030; China 10% soybean meal in feed by 2030), diet ratios (Netherlands 60:40 to 50:50 animal to plant; Flanders 60:40 plant to animal, both by 2030) and sector value (Canada CAD 25 bn of sales by 2035; Thailand future-food exports THB 350 bn by 2027). [general] Medium. [projection: official targets], 2027 to 2035. (VIS-15, VIS-12, GEO-11, VIS-05, VIS-06, VIS-08, VIS-30)
2. **Feed self-sufficiency targets are usually missed, then cut.** Japan's pure-domestic feed self-sufficiency was 28% in FY2000 and 27% in FY2024 (24% estimated for FY2025); its FY2030 target fell from 34% (2020 plan) to 28% (2025 plan). China reached 13.4% soybean meal in feed in 2025 against a target of below 13%. Singapore dropped "30 by 30" after reaching about 26% for protein and 8% for fibre. [VN-adjacent] High. [trend], 2000 to 2025. (VIS-11, VIS-12, GEO-11, VIS-25)
3. **The EU set its first numeric feed-protein target in July 2026.** EU-grown oilseeds and protein crops are to supply 35% of the protein from those crops used as feed by 2035, up from 25.8% in 2025, backed by a harmonised protein balance sheet and an EU-wide benchmark. The 2018 EU protein report had no target, and the high-protein self-sufficiency figure was still about 26% seven years later. [general] High. [projection: official target], 2035. (VIS-15, VIS-17)
4. **Formulation moves faster than land.** China cut the soybean-meal share of feed by 0.14 to 0.45 percentage points a year between 2017 and 2025 (USDA and official series); Norway cut the marine share of salmon feed from 90% (1990) to about 30% (2013), 2.6 points a year, under price pressure. Land-based self-sufficiency in Japan moved by less than 0.5 points a year in either direction. [general] Medium. [trend], 1990 to 2025. (GEO-10, GEO-11, VIS-19, VIS-12; vis_calc.py)
5. **Novel feed ingredients stay tiny even in the most advanced aquafeed market.** In 2020 insect meal, single-cell protein, fermented products and microalgae made up 0.4% (8,126 t) of Norwegian salmon-feed ingredients, and only 8.3% of all ingredients were Norwegian. The composition "resembled that of 2016". [general] High. [trend], 2016 to 2020. (VIS-18, VIS-20)
6. **QNT's alternative-protein scenario lies above every observed analogue.** S-ALT puts microbial protein at about 1.0% of Vietnam's high-protein feed protein in 2030, 4.4% in 2040 and 8.4% in 2050; no country has reached 1% at national scale. By contrast S-EFF's lever (soybean-meal inclusion down 1% a year) sits at the low end of China's achieved pace (0.9 to 2.9% a year, relative). [VN-direct] Low. [estimate], 2030 to 2050. (QNT outputs; vis_calc.py)
7. **Our credible ambitious range for Vietnam (not a forecast):** soybean-meal import need of about 7 to 9 Mt in 2050 against 10.4 Mt on trend (the S-EFF to S-ALT span); microbial feed protein at about 1% of high-protein feed protein by 2030, 2 to 4% by 2040 and 4 to 8% by 2050; and, once a baseline exists, a domestic-origin share of feed protein rising 0.5 to 1 point a year (the EU's target pace). Holding soybean-meal imports at the 2025 level (7.2 Mt) through 2050 is the stretch case. [VN-direct] Low. [estimate], 2030 to 2050. (QNT; AQF; VIS-15; vis_calc.py)
8. **Plans without a statistic cannot be judged.** The Dutch protein strategy published no official quantitative targets or calculations; Canada's CAD 25 bn roadmap has no published progress report; Denmark's action plan has no KPIs. Japan (annual KPI checks), the EU (balance sheet and benchmark) and China (feed-ratio statistic) are tracked, even when they miss. For Vietnam the first milestone is therefore a published protein and feed balance sheet. [general] Medium. [trend]. (VIS-05, VIS-08, VIS-10, VIS-01, VIS-13, VIS-15)
9. **Peer protein budgets are modest and steady.** Denmark's Plant-based Food Grant is DKK 675 M for 2023 to 2030 (about USD 12 M a year) and was made permanent in 2024; Germany has spent about EUR 6 M a year on protein crops since 2016; Canada's supercluster receives about USD 22 M a year federal. Vietnam's feed scheme (Decision 1625) is about USD 6 M a year. A dedicated Vietnamese protein line of USD 5 to 15 M a year would sit within this peer range. [general] Medium (figures); [VN-direct] Low (range). [estimate], 2027 to 2035. (VIS-01, VIS-04, VIS-24, VIS-06, NTS-23; vis_calc.py)
10. **Denmark will tax livestock emissions from 2030,** at DKK 300 per t CO2e in 2030 and DKK 750 in 2035 before a 60% deduction (DKK 120 and 300 effective), paired with a permanent plant-based fund and a DKK 40 bn land fund. It is the only agricultural carbon price we found; Vietnam's carbon market excludes agriculture (ECF headline 6). [general] Medium. [projection: official target], 2030, 2035. (VIS-03, VIS-04)
11. **Security framing has displaced climate framing.** The EU's July 2026 plan is titled for "resilience, strategic autonomy and sustainability"; Japan and Korea state their targets as food security; the 2024 Dutch government recast animal protein as "indispensable" for security; Singapore left alternative protein out of its food-security targets for cost and acceptance reasons. A Vietnam vision led by feed security and industry (the study's framing) is the more durable choice. [general] Medium. [signal], 2024 to 2026. (VIS-15, VIS-13, VIS-29, VIS-05, VIS-25)
12. **Asian peers use a statute plus five-year plans.** Korea's Food Tech Industry Promotion Act produced a first five-year basic plan in July 2026 (policy funds KRW 51 bn in 2024 rising to KRW 100 bn by 2027); Japan's revised Basic Act drives a five-year plan with annual verification. Neither sets an alternative-protein volume. [VN-adjacent] Medium. [signal], 2026 to 2030. (VIS-14, VIS-28, VIS-13)
13. **Good long-range food foresight pairs three or four exploratory scenarios with a normative vision and dated milestones.** FAO used 3 scenarios to 2050 (2018) and 4 scenarios, 18 drivers and 4 "triggers" (2022); the JRC's food 2050 exercise used 4 scenarios on 2 axes and a stress test that found 29 policy challenges, 8 of them common to all scenarios. Challenges common to all scenarios are the natural no-regret list. [general] High. (VIS-32, VIS-33, VIS-35, VIS-36)
14. **Backcasting pays off only when it is embedded.** Reviews stress follow-up agendas and adaptive monitoring as the weak link. The Dutch Novel Protein Foods backcast of the 1990s seeded the Profetas research programme, yet the 2020 Dutch strategy still lacked measurable targets. A European plant-protein backcast to 2030 found that the actions needed for its milestones had to start "immediately". [general] Medium. (VIS-37 to VIS-42, VIS-05)
15. **Vietnam's own foresight capacity is thin.** Foresight has been studied at NISTPASS since the late 1990s with APEC Center for Technology Foresight support, including a 2002 food-processing pilot; the Delphi for the STI strategy 2021 to 2030 had only 15 experts per field and could not rank priorities. Its authors advise small, high-quality scenario workshops because statistics are thin. Vietnam 2035 (World Bank and MPI, 2016) is the precedent for a jointly authored national vision. [VN-direct] High. (VIS-45, VIS-46)
16. **Nobody has a protein plan to 2050.** Protein horizons stop at 2030 to 2035 (EU, Canada, Singapore 2035; Japan FY2030; China, Israel, the Netherlands 2030). Japan's MIDORI strategy (2050) and Denmark's land deal (2045) reach further but carry no protein indicator. A Vietnam 2050 protein vision would be unusually long-range; it should anchor on Vietnam's 2050 input plans (power, hydrogen, climate caps; NTS headline 2) and be reviewed at each five-year plan. [general] Medium. [trend], 2026. (VIS-15, VIS-08, VIS-25, VIS-13, GEO-11, VIS-23, VIS-49, VIS-03; NTS)

**Readings.**
- **Land-based self-sufficiency is sticky.** Japan spent two plan cycles on feed rice and forage and ended where it began (27 to 28%). Its feed-rice area halved in one year (98,666 ha in FY2024 to 46,004 ha in FY2025) when food rice ran short, showing that feed crops lose to food crops under stress (VIS-12). Vietnam's crop strategy sets no maize, soybean or cassava target and its land plan releases rice land without saying for what (NTS headline 6). [VN-adjacent] High; [trend].
- **Formulation and by-products are the fast lever.** China's official series fell 3.6 points in eight years; Norway's marine share fell 60 points in 23 years once fishmeal became dear. Both moved through feed formulation, not new land. This matches QNT's tornado result that inclusion trends and FCR move Vietnam's 2050 soybean-meal need more than diets (QNT headline 5). [general] Medium; [trend].
- **Novel proteins are a small, slow wedge.** No feed-protein plan we read counts on novel proteins for a numeric share. The EU plan names precision and biomass fermentation and algae but sets no share, and commissioned a fermentation study for late 2026 (VIS-15). Norway's novel share was 0.4% after years of research funding. [general] High; [trend].
- **Vietnam's position is closest to Korea's and Japan's, not the EU's.** It imports almost all its soybean meal and much of its maize, and has little land for feed crops (AQF headline 13: domestic soybean is "not a feed-protein lever"). Korea's compound-feed output (about 21.5 Mt) is the same size as Vietnam's livestock feed (about 21.5 Mt in 2024; NTS). Neither Korea nor Japan has moved feed-protein self-sufficiency. [VN-adjacent] Medium; [estimate] for the comparison.

**Methods that worked (our synthesis, [general] Medium).**
1. **Three or four exploratory scenarios, built on two named axes and a short driver list** (FAO 2022: 18 drivers; JRC: 9 drivers, 2 axes). Four is the modal number for 2050 food exercises. Edition 1.0 already has three 2035 states (ch13); Part V can reuse two critical uncertainties as axes to give four 2050 scenarios.
2. **A separate normative vision, stated as a feasibility question** (Riera et al. types A and B: "demonstrate the feasibility of a specific scenario or frame the conditions for its feasibility"). This keeps the vision from being read as a forecast (VIS-36).
3. **Backcast milestones every five years, tied to the planning calendar** (Japan five-year plans with annual verification; Finland each electoral term; Korea five-year basic plans under a statute). For Vietnam the natural anchors are the five-year socio-economic plans (2026 to 2030, 2031 to 2035 and so on) and the successor livestock and fisheries strategies due before 2030 (NTS signposts).
4. **Stress-test the robust moves against every scenario and keep those common to all** (JRC: 8 of 29 challenges were common). This is the method for F10.
5. **Name triggers and signposts, and assign a statistic to each milestone** (FAO triggers; EU balance sheet; Japan KPIs). The Dutch and Canadian cases show that a vision without a statistic loses force within one political cycle (VIS-05, VIS-10).
6. **Embed and follow up.** Participatory backcasting has impact when it seeds a research programme, a budget line or a regular review (Quist and Vergragt; Remans et al.). The weak link in every review is monitoring for adaptive management (VIS-39).
7. **Fit the method to thin data.** Vietnam's own foresight team recommended small, high-quality scenario workshops rather than large Delphi rounds because statistics are unreliable and expert pools small (VIS-45). The IASVN "100-year planning" commentary proposes three layers: fixed long-term foundations, multiple 20 to 30-year scenarios, and 5 to 10-year implementation and adjustment (VIS-47) [VN-direct] Low.

**Readings.**
- **The ambitious part of a Vietnam vision should be formulation, by-products and aquafeed functional ingredients, not a large self-sufficiency ratio.** This is what the analogues show can move (China, Norway), and it matches QNT (efficiency does twice the work of alternative protein) and AQF (alternatives matter most for omega-3). [VN-direct] Low; [estimate].
- **A domestic-origin target needs a statistic first.** Vietnam has no published feed-protein balance; the EU is only now issuing guidance on a harmonised balance sheet. Publishing one by 2027 to 2028 is the cheapest, most robust first milestone. [VN-direct] Medium; [estimate].
- **The S-ALT 2050 share (8.4%) should be presented as a stretch, not a target.** No national feed system has reached even 1% novel protein. A vision can aim for it, but only with signposts showing cost parity with fishmeal first (ECF: fishmeal parity is reachable at the low-cost end; soybean parity is not). [VN-direct] Low; [estimate].
- **No consumption ratio.** The Dutch and Flemish diet-ratio goals are unmeasured, and diet is outside this study's scope. [general] Medium.

### Q5. What the benchmarks imply for robust moves (F10)

- **No-regret (pay off in every scenario):** a protein and feed balance statistic; a feed-formulation and FCR efficiency programme (the lever with a real-world pace); a novel-food and feed-ingredient route with published timelines; shared pilot-plant access. Every successful benchmark has the first two; the Dutch and Canadian cases lacked a statistic. [general] Medium.
- **Options (cheap to hold):** a small permanent fund on the Danish model; siting studies for cassava-glucose and gas-fermentation clusters; participation in the EU-style balance-sheet method so Vietnamese data are comparable. [general] Medium.
- **Bets (need signposts):** novel feed-protein capacity beyond about 1% of feed protein before fishmeal parity; any livestock carbon price (Denmark is the only precedent, from 2030). [general] Medium.

## Implications by audience

- **Investors.** Outside China, peer governments fund protein at roughly USD 5 to 25 M a year and set targets for feed security, not for novel-protein volumes. Do not underwrite a Vietnamese plant on the expectation of a national novel-protein quota; underwrite on fishmeal substitution and functional aquafeed ingredients, where the analogues show movement.
- **Policy makers.** The strongest benchmark package is: a statistic (balance sheet), a numeric feed-security target with a realistic pace (0.5 to 1 point a year), a five-year plan cycle with annual verification, and a modest permanent fund. Avoid consumption ratios and sector-value headlines without baselines.
- **Startups and founders.** Even Norway's advanced salmon-feed market took only 0.4% novel ingredients in 2020. Plan for small initial shares in premium feeds (shrimp, marine fish, omega-3) and for export approvals elsewhere.
- **F&B and feed manufacturers.** Formulation is the lever that moves national protein imports fastest in the analogues (China, Norway). A company-level soybean-meal inclusion trend of minus 1 to 2% a year is in line with China's achieved pace.
- **Research bodies.** Vietnam's foresight base is small (15 experts per field in the last national Delphi). A small standing foresight cell on food and feed, running compact scenario workshops and updating a public balance sheet, would match what worked elsewhere.
- **International bodies.** Fundable regional public goods: a harmonised feed-protein balance method for ASEAN (following the EU guidance), and support to embed a Vietnam protein vision in the 2031 to 2035 plan cycle.

## Method and limits

- Rates of change are simple averages between two published points; they ignore year-to-year noise and definitional changes (flagged in the disagreements log).
- The Vietnam ranges re-read QNT and AQF outputs; they add no new model. They are estimates for a normative vision, not forecasts.
