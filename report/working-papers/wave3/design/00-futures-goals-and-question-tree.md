# Futures round (wave 3): goals and question tree

Project: "Alternative protein in Vietnam: a supply-side scoping study" (AltProtein Vietnam). Edition 1.0 (September 2026) covers the present and the outlook to 2035. This round adds **Part V: Futures 2030 to 2050** to the same package (edition 1.1).

## User decisions for this round
- Packaging: a new Part V inside the same package (chapters, appendices, data, charts), with the executive summaries and briefs updated.
- Stance: **evidence-anchored foresight plus one bold normative vision**. Horizon scanning and exploratory scenarios with signposts, plus one "Vietnam 2050" vision with backcasting milestones. Every speculative claim is labelled as such.
- Topics: all four: frontier production technology; drivers and shocks; economy and policy futures; quantitative projections (a transparent protein and feed balance model for 2030, 2040, 2050).
- Demand boundary: demand enters **only as a macro input** (population, income, diet, published FAO/OECD/IFPRI-type outlooks) to the supply and feed balance. No consumer research, no market-size forecasts for Vietnamese alt-protein products.
- Unchanged from edition 1.0: supply side; food and feed; insects only as incumbent/benchmark, never a recommended play; neutral framing led by food and feed security, industry and trade, with animal welfare and environment as stated motivations; English plus Vietnamese summary.

## Futures question tree (becomes Part V)

- **F1. Drivers to 2050.** Which structural forces will reshape Vietnam's protein supply between 2030 and 2050: climate (Mekong salinity, subsidence, sea-level rise, heat stress, extreme events; climate effects on soy, maize and fishmeal in exporting countries), trade and geopolitics, animal disease and pandemics, energy transition (renewables, hydrogen, nuclear), demography and income, AI and automation? Which are near-certain trends and which are critical uncertainties?
- **F2. Frontier technology.** For each frontier family (gas and electro-fermentation / power-to-protein; AI-designed proteins and strains; biofoundries and autonomous labs; cell-free protein synthesis; molecular farming in crops; next-generation precision fermentation; next-generation cultivated meat and seafood; advanced biomass fermentation; hybrid products), what is the readiness today, the credible cost and scale trajectory to 2035, 2040, 2050, and when (under which conditions) does Vietnam become a sensible place to do it?
- **F3. Inputs that change.** How will the cost and availability of Vietnam's key production inputs change: renewable electricity, green hydrogen, captured CO2, cassava and sugar under climate change, nitrogen, water, land; carbon prices?
- **F4. Protein and feed balance.** What might Vietnam's protein demand (macro input only), feed demand and feed-protein import need look like in 2030, 2040 and 2050 under scenarios; how much of that could alternative proteins supply, from which feedstocks, with what land, energy and emissions implications?
- **F5. Economy and policy futures.** Cost learning curves; capital flows; carbon pricing and trade measures (CBAM-type, EUDR); national and regional bioeconomy strategies (Vietnam, Thailand, China, India, Korea, Singapore, ASEAN); jobs and skills; just transition for livestock and aquaculture workers.
- **F6. Regional race to 2050.** How will competitors' positions evolve and where could Vietnam specialise?
- **F7. Weak signals and wildcards.** What early signals (patents, publications, startups, pilot plants, policy drafts) and low-probability high-impact events could change the picture?
- **F8. Scenarios.** Which two or three critical uncertainties define distinct 2050 futures; what does each look like; what signposts show which is unfolding; how do they connect to the 2035 scenarios in chapter 13?
- **F9. Vision.** What would an ambitious but plausible 2050 vision for Vietnamese protein supply look like, and what milestones backcast from it for 2030, 2035, 2040 and 2045?
- **F10. Robust moves now.** Which actions pay off across all scenarios (no-regret), which keep options open, and which are bets that need signposts?

## Futures evidence rules (in addition to the edition 1.0 rules)
- Keep the evidence label (VN-direct, VN-adjacent, general) and confidence (High, Medium, Low) on every finding.
- Add a **foresight type** to every forward-looking statement:
  - `trend`: an observed, measured change expected to continue (state the data and period).
  - `projection`: a number from a published model or official target (name the model, scenario and year).
  - `estimate`: our own calculation from stated assumptions.
  - `signal`: an emerging development with some evidence (pilot, paper, patent, policy draft) that may or may not scale.
  - `wildcard`: a low-probability, high-impact event.
  - `vision`: a normative statement of what could be aimed for (only in the vision chapter).
- State the horizon year (2030, 2035, 2040, 2045, 2050) for every forward-looking number.
- Never present a scenario, estimate or vision as a forecast. No probabilities unless a published source gives them.
- Distinguish official targets (what the government aims for) from projections (what models expect) from our estimates.
