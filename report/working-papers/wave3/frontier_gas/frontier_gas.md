# FTG: gas and electro-fermentation, power-to-protein (2026 to 2050)

Stream: frontier_gas. Source ID prefix: FTG. Question tree: F2 (frontier technology), F3 (inputs that change). All web sources accessed 2026-09-24. Status: complete. WebSearch calls used: 14 of 15.

Companion files in this folder: `frontier_gas_tech.csv` (10 routes), `vn_energy_inputs_2050.csv` (50 rows), `co2_point_sources_vn.csv` (18 rows), `ftg_estimates.py` (all our arithmetic, rerunnable).

**Terms.** SCP = single-cell protein (microbial biomass sold for its protein). HOB = hydrogen-oxidising bacteria (grow on H2, O2 and CO2). CAP = *Clostridium autoethanogenum* protein, an acetogen biomass made from steel-mill or ferroalloy off-gas. Power-to-protein = electricity makes H2 by electrolysis (or formate, acetate, methanol), and microbes turn it plus CO2 into protein. e-protein = protein from power-to-protein. TEA = techno-economic analysis. LCA = life-cycle assessment. LCOE, LCOH = levelised cost of electricity, of hydrogen. TRL = technology readiness level (1 to 9). FOAK = first-of-a-kind plant. WACC = weighted average cost of capital. CDW = cell dry weight. "Our estimate" = our own calculation from stated inputs, not a forecast.

Evidence tags: [VN-direct] / [VN-adjacent] / [general]; confidence High / Medium / Low; foresight type [trend] / [projection] / [estimate] / [signal] / [wildcard]; horizon year. Non-FTG source IDs (COST, FS, IND, GT, REG, RGN) refer to the edition 1.0 source register.

## Headline findings

1. **Power-to-protein works but is not yet an industry.** The largest hydrogen-oxidising-bacteria plant makes 160 to 230 t a year (Solar Foods Factory 01, Finland). The first commercial plant (3,200 t a year) is planned for late 2028, with the investment decision still pending in September 2026 and conditional on binding offtake. [general] High. [signal], 2028. (FTG-03, FTG-04, FTG-05)
2. **Asia's largest dedicated gas-fermentation feed-protein plant has stopped.** Calysseo's 20,000 t a year methane-to-protein plant in Chongqing (China) halted production in 2026; its partner Adisseo declined to renew a loan citing "a severe shortage of working capital". 2025 revenue was USD 0.71 M against a net loss of USD 15.5 M. [VN-adjacent] Medium. [signal], 2026. (FTG-06)
3. **Failures come from capital intensity, not biology.** NovoNutrients (CO2 plus H2) closed and Arkeon (archaea on CO2 plus H2) became insolvent in 2025; both blamed financing time and capital needs. The survivors have state or strategic backing (Solar Foods with Business Finland and GEA; Unibio with a Saudi state-linked investor planning 50,000 t a year from 2028; Shougang LanzaTech inside a steel group). [general] Medium. [trend], 2024 to 2026. (FTG-06, FTG-07, FTG-10, FTG-11)
4. **China's steel-gas protein (CAP) is real, small and a by-product.** Output was 10,200 t of protein in 2023 (4,400 t in H1 2024), made at 0.1 to 0.15 t per t of ethanol, with a negative gross margin in H1 2024. A 2022 plan to add 120,000 t of protein capacity by 2026 was not met. [VN-adjacent] Medium. [trend], 2021 to 2024. (FTG-08, FTG-09, FTG-22)
5. **Power-to-protein is an electricity business.** A 2030-design plant uses about 69 MWh of electricity per t of protein on baseload power (73 to 83 MWh of generation with solar and wind), 0.69 t of H2 and 2.95 t of CO2; electrolysers take 54 to 56% of the power. With 2050 technology the figure falls to about 61 MWh (64 to 70 generated). [general] High. [projection], 2030 and 2050, Fasihi et al. 2025 model. (FTG-01)
6. **Published cost path at the world's best sites:** EUR 5.5 to 6.1 per kg protein (2028), 4.0 to 4.5 (2030), 3.1 to 3.5 (2035), 2.1 to 2.3 (2050), about USD 6.6 to 7.3, 4.8 to 5.4, 3.7 to 4.2 and 2.5 to 2.8. The authors judge it competitive with soy and pea isolates for food, but say it "may not become a competitive feed at scale". [general] High. [projection], 2028 to 2050, 7% WACC. (FTG-01)
7. **In Vietnam the same technology would cost more.** Our indicative estimate for hydrogen-route protein made in Vietnam is USD 6.9 to 12.6 per kg (2030), 4.7 to 8.6 (2040) and 3.3 to 6.5 (2050), against fishmeal protein at USD 2.6 to 3.8 and soybean-meal protein at about 0.88 (2025 to 2026 prices). Only the low end reaches the fishmeal band, around 2050; soybean meal is out of reach in every year. [VN-direct inputs] Low. [estimate], 2030 to 2050. (FTG-01, FTG-19; `ftg_estimates.py`)
8. **Hydrogen price is the switch.** Each USD 1 per kg of H2 adds about USD 690 per t of protein. With 2030 plant costs, no positive H2 price reaches 2025 fishmeal-protein parity. With 2050 plant costs, parity with August 2026 fishmeal protein needs delivered green H2 at about USD 0.7 to 2.9 per kg; a realised price at 80% of that parity needs about USD 1.8 or less even in the best case. [VN-direct inputs] Low. [estimate], 2030 and 2050. (FTG-01; `ftg_estimates.py`)
9. **On today's grid it would be a high-carbon protein.** Electricity alone at Vietnam's 2023 grid factor (0.6592 t CO2 per MWh) gives about 46 t CO2 per t of protein. At the average grid intensity implied by the revised power plan it falls to about 24 to 28 t (2030) and about 1.1 to 1.4 t (2050, if the plan's 27 Mt power-sector cap is met). Dedicated renewables are a precondition, not an option. [VN-direct] Medium (arithmetic), Low (plan delivery). [estimate], 2030 and 2050. (FTG-01, FTG-15, COST-08)
10. **The physical scale fits Vietnam's 2050 energy plans.** One million tonnes of protein a year would need 64 to 83 TWh (4 to 6% of planned 2050 supply), 43 to 56 GW of solar (about 15 to 19% of the 2050 solar plan) or 14 to 22 GW of offshore wind, 390 to 740 km2 of solar land, 0.69 Mt of H2 (3.5 to 6.9% of the 2050 hydrogen target) and 2.95 Mt of CO2. Replacing all the protein in 2025 soybean-meal imports (2.62 Mt) would need 168 to 218 TWh and 113 to 146 GW of solar. [VN-direct] Low to Medium. [estimate], 2030 to 2050. (FTG-01, FTG-15, FTG-16, FTG-19)
11. **Vietnam's plans set aside the energy, on paper.** The revised power plan (Decision 768/QD-TTg, April 2025) targets 293 to 296 GW of solar and 114 to 139 GW of offshore wind for domestic demand by 2050, plus about 15 GW (2035) and 240 GW (2050) of offshore wind "to produce new energy (hydrogen, green ammonia)". The hydrogen strategy (Decision 165/QD-TTg, February 2024) targets 100 to 500 kt of clean H2 a year by 2030 and 10 to 20 Mt by 2050. [VN-direct] High. [projection: official targets], 2030 to 2050. (FTG-15, FTG-16)
12. **Delivery lags the plans.** Vietnam's first green hydrogen plant (24,000 t H2 and 182,500 t ammonia a year, Vinh Long, former Tra Vinh) broke ground in March 2023 and had only perimeter works by May 2026. The new offshore wind decree (Decree 272/2026, July 2026) covers grid supply only and has no track for hydrogen or "new energy" wind. [VN-direct] Medium. [signal], 2026. (FTG-27, FTG-28)
13. **Vietnam's power is cheaper than Europe's but not best-in-class.** Solar LCOE is projected at USD 29 to 85 per MWh in 2030 and 18 to 64 in 2050 (capacity factor 17%), against a generation cost of EUR 15 to 19 (2030) and 8 to 10 (2050) per MWh at the best global sites. A Vietnamese WACC 2 to 4 points above the 7% used in the global model adds roughly 15 to 35% to the capital-driven costs. [VN-direct] Medium. [projection], 2030 and 2050, BloombergNEF 2023 and Fasihi et al. 2025. (FTG-19, FTG-01)
14. **CO2 is not the bottleneck; free CO2 is scarce.** 100 kt of protein needs about 0.30 Mt of CO2 a year, more than the three operating ethanol plants release (about 0.21 Mt, our estimate). One Mt of protein needs about 13% of Hoa Phat's 2025 Scope 1 emissions (22.5 Mt). But Hoa Phat already burns its steel-mill gases for 80 to 90% power self-sufficiency, so off-gas has a power value. [VN-direct] Low to Medium. [estimate], current. (FTG-20; FS-09)
15. **Methane protein is the cheapest gas route on paper but not in Vietnam.** One TEA puts methanotroph protein at USD 1.10 to 2.57 per kg, but at Vietnam's 2026 LNG price basis (USD 11.98 per MMBtu plus 1.87 transport, set by MOIT) the gas alone costs USD 0.72 to 1.25 per kg of protein at USD 8 to 14 per MMBtu, about the whole price of soybean-meal protein, and it is fossil carbon. [VN-direct] Low. [estimate], 2026. (FTG-25, FTG-31)
16. **Vietnam has almost no research base in this field.** Of 494 publications on gas or CO2-based microbial protein in 2016 to 2026, Vietnamese institutions appear on 2 (a 2016 methanotroph isolation and a co-authored Australian fish trial); China has 105, the US 64, Denmark 47. Global output roughly tripled between 2016 and 2025. [VN-direct] High (count). [trend], 2016 to 2026. (FTG-14, FTG-35)
17. **Globally, microbial protein could cover 10 to 19% of crop-based feed protein demand by 2050** depending on socio-economic pathway (SSP1, SSP2, SSP5) and production route, cutting global cropland by about 6%. This is a model potential, not a forecast. [general] Medium. [projection], 2050. (FTG-32)
18. **When the Vietnam window opens (our judgement):** for bulk feed from power-to-protein, not before about 2040 to 2045, and only if five conditions align: delivered green H2 at or below about USD 1.5 to 2 per kg, firmed renewable power at or below about USD 30 to 45 per MWh, capital cost near the 2050 learning path with WACC at or below about 8%, a sustained fishmeal-protein price above about USD 3,000 per t (or a carbon price on competing protein), and a feed-list entry for bacterial biomass. Earlier windows are narrower: formulating imported approved e-protein food ingredients (from about 2028 if a novel-food route exists), or a CAP-type co-product if a steel-gas ethanol or aviation-fuel project is built. [VN-direct] Low. [estimate], 2028 to 2045. (all)

As functions of electricity and H2 price (our derivation from Fasihi component costs, USD per t protein):
- Integrated plant: cost = electricity use (65 to 83 MWh) x power price + non-energy cost (USD 3,040 to 3,800 in 2030; 1,440 to 1,800 in 2050, at 7% WACC). Every USD 10 per MWh adds USD 650 to 830 per t protein.
- Buying H2: cost = 0.69 t H2 x H2 price + about 25 to 29 MWh of other electricity + core-plant and other costs. Each USD 1 per kg of H2 adds USD 694 per t protein; at USD 2 per kg the H2 line alone (USD 1,388) exceeds the whole price of soybean-meal protein.
- Learning: Fasihi's capex falls 57% from 2030 to 2050 in the reference case (10% learning rate) and 77% in the advanced case (15%). The electrolyser high-cost case adds only 3 to 6% in 2030; WACC plus or minus 2 points moves cost 12 to 15% (FTG-01). So finance and power price matter more than electrolyser price.
- Reality check: Solar Foods' Factory 02 plan (EUR 191 M on balance sheet for 3,200 t a year, plus EUR 127 M expected off balance sheet) implies about EUR 60,000 per annual t of product, several times the TEA capex for a first plant (our derivation, Low). Real first plants are small and expensive. [signal], 2026 to 2028.

### 4. Vietnam's inputs to 2050 (sub-question 4)

**Renewable electricity.** Decision 768/QD-TTg (15 April 2025) sets non-hydro renewable shares of 28 to 36% (2030) and 74 to 75% (2050); solar 46.5 to 73.4 GW (2030) and 293.1 to 295.6 GW (2050); onshore wind 84.7 to 91.4 GW, offshore wind for domestic demand 113.5 to 139.1 GW, batteries about 96 GW and nuclear 10.5 to 14.0 GW in 2050; supply 1,360 to 1,511 TWh (2050); power-sector emissions 197 to 199 Mt (2030) and about 27 Mt (2050) (FTG-15). [VN-direct] High. [projection: target].
- Our derived average grid intensity: about 0.35 to 0.40 t CO2 per MWh in 2030 (upper bound) and about 0.02 in 2050, against 0.6592 in 2023 (COST-08). [estimate] Low.
- BloombergNEF (2023): solar LCOE USD 53 to 105 (2023), 29 to 85 (2030), 18 to 64 (2050) per MWh; offshore wind 121 to 218 (2023) and 53 to 99 (2050); capacity factors 17% (solar), 43 to 51% (offshore) (FTG-19). [VN-direct] Medium. [projection].
- Irradiance: 1,868 to 2,002 kWh/m2/yr in the south (Tay Ninh; Lam Dong, former Binh Thuan), 1,500 to 1,700 in the north (FTG-18).

**Green hydrogen.** Decision 165/QD-TTg (7 February 2024) targets 100 to 500 kt a year (2030) and 10 to 20 Mt (2050) of H2 from renewables or with carbon capture; it pilots green H2 in fertiliser, refining, steel and cement, and sets no cost target (FTG-16). Four Mekong projects of about 30,000 t each (Vinh Long, former Tra Vinh and former Ben Tre; Ca Mau, former Bac Lieu; Dong Thap, former Tien Giang) were "in development" in March 2025 (FTG-17). The Tra Vinh plant has stalled (FTG-27). IEA reports a 240 MW Vietnamese electrolyser project at FID (FTG-29; see Disagreements). Published Vietnamese LCOH: USD 2.81 to 2.85 per kg (south) and 3.76 to 3.88 (north) for PV plus PEM at a 6% discount rate, which we treat as optimistic; secondary citations of USD 3.05 (2030) and 1.59 (2050) (FTG-18). Installed electrolysers cost USD 600 to 1,200 per kW in China vs 2,000 to 2,600 elsewhere (FTG-29), which favours importing Chinese electrolysers.

**Offshore wind for "new energy".** About 15 GW by 2035 and 240 GW by 2050 are earmarked for hydrogen and green ammonia (FTG-15). At 43 to 51% capacity factor that is 904 to 1,081 TWh a year in 2050, close to the whole domestic supply (our estimate). Decree 272/2026 (4 July 2026) creates the offshore wind framework for grid supply only (FTG-28). [VN-direct] Medium. [signal].

**CO2 point sources** (full list in `co2_point_sources_vn.csv`):
- Steel: Hoa Phat group Scope 1 22.5 Mt CO2e in 2025; Dung Quat (Quang Ngai) holds 75% of group crude-steel capacity (about 17 Mt, our derivation); off-gases already fuel in-house power (FTG-20). Formosa Ha Tinh (Ha Tinh) not quantified.
- Fertiliser: Phu My (Ho Chi Minh City, former Ba Ria-Vung Tau), Ca Mau (Ca Mau), Ha Bac (Bac Ninh, former Bac Giang), Ninh Binh (Ninh Binh) named as CO2 sources, no volumes (FTG-21). Coal-based Ha Bac and Ninh Binh may have surplus pure CO2 (our inference, unverified).
- Ethanol: three plants, about 207,000 t of near-pure fermentation CO2 a year (our estimate from FS-09): Dung Quat (Quang Ngai) about 82,000 t; Dai Tan (Da Nang, former Quang Nam) about 75,000 t; Tung Lam (Dong Nai) about 50,000 t. Planned An Khe (Gia Lai) adds about 50,000 t from 2028 if built.
- Refineries (Dung Quat, Nghi Son) and future high-CO2 gas fields (Ca Voi Xanh; Kim Long-Ac Quy-Ca Voi; Block 15; Su Tu Bien) would add concentrated streams (FTG-21).
- Biogas from cassava-starch wastewater is widespread but unquantified (FS-04, FS-06).

**CCUS.** Theoretical storage about 12 Gt CO2 (over 10 Gt in saline aquifers, 1.15 Gt in oil and gas fields); one CO2-EOR pilot (Rang Dong, 2011); no national CCUS roadmap with dates found this round (FTG-21). [VN-direct] Medium.

**Nitrogen.** Hydrogen-route protein needs about 0.24 t NH3 per t protein (FTG-01). One Mt of protein would need about 0.24 Mt NH3 a year. The Tra Vinh project design includes 182,500 t of green ammonia a year (FTG-27). Vietnam's grey-ammonia plants are gas or coal based (FTG-21).

**Gas.** MOIT's 2026 price ceiling for LNG power assumes LNG at USD 11.98 per MMBtu plus 1.87 transport (FTG-31). Domestic pipeline gas prices for industry were not found.

Comparisons [estimate, Low]:
- **Against the 2050 power plan:** 1 Mt of protein needs 4.2 to 6.1% of planned 2050 electricity supply and 15 to 19% of the 2050 solar plan, or 6 to 9% of the output of the 240 GW of "new energy" offshore wind.
- **Against the hydrogen strategy:** 100 kt of protein would absorb 14 to 69% of the entire 2030 H2 target; 1 Mt would absorb 3.5 to 6.9% of the 2050 target.
- **Against feed imports:** soybean meal imports were 5.703 Mt in 2025 (edition 1.0 FI-013), about 2.62 Mt of protein at 46%. Replacing all of it would need 168 to 218 TWh (11 to 16% of 2050 supply), 113 to 146 GW of solar (38 to 50% of the 2050 solar plan), 1,030 to 1,950 km2 of solar land, 1.8 Mt H2 and 7.7 Mt CO2. Physically feasible within the plans; economically not, because the cost floor stays 3 to 7 times above soybean-meal protein (section 3).
- **Indicative cost in Vietnam** (USD per t protein; electricity use x Vietnamese supply price from BNEF LCOE with a 40 to 54% balancing uplift, plus Fasihi non-energy costs x 1.15 to 1.35 for WACC 9 to 11%): 6,900 to 12,600 (2030); 4,700 to 8,600 (2040); 3,300 to 6,500 (2050). On grid power at USD 75 per MWh in 2030: 8,700 to 10,300. Best global sites (Fasihi): 4,800 to 5,400 (2030); 2,520 to 2,760 (2050).

**Overall.** For bulk feed, the Vietnam window for power-to-protein opens no earlier than about 2040 to 2045 and only if most conditions align. It is a 2050 option to keep open, not a 2030 build. Three narrower windows open earlier:
1. **Food ingredients (from about 2028).** If Vietnam adopts a novel-food route that relies on Singapore or US approvals, Vietnamese food makers could import and formulate e-protein ingredients. No domestic production is implied.
2. **Co-product protein at an industrial site (2030s).** A CAP-type unit makes sense only inside a larger gas-to-ethanol or aviation-fuel project at a steel mill (Dung Quat, Ha Tinh), where the protein is a by-product.
3. **Pilots on free CO2 (now to 2030).** A 100 to 1,000 t a year research pilot on ethanol-plant CO2 with purchased or grid power would build skills and data, not supply.

## Implications by audience

- **Investors (VC, impact, DFIs, strategics).** Do not fund a Vietnamese gas-fermentation feed plant before 2035 on current evidence: the world's only 20 kt Asian plant has stopped and first-of-a-kind capex runs well above TEA figures. Keep an option: track Solar Foods (2028) and Unibio Saudi (2028) as the two global cost signals. The Vietnamese angle worth backing now is green H2 and offshore wind, which are the upstream of any 2040s power-to-protein. DFIs could make a Vietnamese WACC of 8% or less possible; that alone is worth 15 to 35% of the capital-driven cost.
- **Policy makers (MOIT, MAE, MOST, MOF, provinces).** Three no-regret moves: (1) give offshore wind "for new energy" a legal track and name food and feed protein among its uses alongside ammonia; (2) add a monitored listing route for novel microbial feed on the Chinese model (certificate, monitoring period, catalogue) so that imported or domestic bacterial proteins can be tested; (3) require new ethanol, steel and fertiliser projects to report CO2 volumes and purity, which is the missing input for any CO2-to-protein plan. Do not count power-to-protein in 2030 or 2035 feed-security plans.
- **Startups and founders.** In Vietnam, build on sugar-based fermentation now (edition 1.0 plays) and treat gas fermentation as a 2035-plus pivot. If you want exposure, license or distribute an approved foreign ingredient, or run a small pilot on ethanol-plant CO2 with a university. Pick thermotolerant strains: cooling is 8% of power in the global model and more in the tropics.
- **F&B and feed manufacturers.** No domestic gas-fermentation protein will be available at scale before the 2040s. Imported methane or CAP protein from China or Saudi Arabia may appear from 2028; demand species-specific trial data (edition 1.0 found methanotroph meal with lower digestibility in one shrimp screen).
- **Research bodies.** Vietnam has 2 publications against China's 105. A modest programme (hydrogen-oxidiser and methanotroph strains that grow at 40 to 45 °C, gas-liquid mass transfer, tropical techno-economics with Vietnamese inputs, and a CO2 and biogas inventory) would give Vietnam a seat at the table by 2035. Partnering with Chinese institutes (TIB, CAAS Feed Research Institute) is the fastest route.
- **International bodies.** Fundable public goods: a Vietnamese point-source CO2 atlas; an open tropical power-to-protein cost model using the Fasihi source data; support for a proportionate novel-feed listing route; and linking green-H2 finance (JETP-type) to food and feed uses, not only ammonia for export.

### W1. Fasihi et al. 2025, Nature Communications (FTG-01), full text read via Scite (pages 1 to 8 of about 12)
- Model: hourly, cost-optimised, semi-flexible Power-to-SCP plant on hybrid PV and wind with batteries, H2 storage, low-temperature DAC for CO2, on-site green ammonia. Based on Solar Foods (Xanthobacter sp. SoF1) techno-economic data. Global 0.45 degree grid, 2028 to 2050. WACC 7% everywhere, plant life 25 years. EUR/USD 1.2 long-term rate used for literature conversion.
- Capex of core SCP plant: EUR 14,567 per t protein per year (first plant, 16.42 kt SCP/yr, 2028); EUR 8,649 (full-scale 164.2 kt SCP/yr = 101.4 kt protein/yr, 2030). Reference learning (10% learning rate, 150 Mt SCP/yr by 2070): EUR 6,175 (2035), 3,702 (2050), 3,041 (2070). Advanced (15% LR): 4,371 (2035), 1,985 (2050). These capex are 2 to 5 times higher than most earlier TEAs.
- Cost of protein at best sites: EUR 5,500 to 6,100 per t protein (2028); 4,000 to 4,500 (2030); 3,100 to 3,500 (2035); 2,100 to 2,300 (2050), and 2,300 to 2,500 "widely available across all continents" in 2050. Finland 2028 above EUR 7,300.
- Electricity: 69.3 MWh per t protein baseload (2030); 73 to 83 MWh generation per t protein semi-flexible in most of the world (2030); 70 to 73 MWh at 7 selected sites. 2050 baseload 60.9 MWh per t protein; generation 64 to 70 at the low end. Split (2030): electrolysers 54 to 56%, SCP core plant 19 to 20%, electric steam 11 to 13%, chiller 8%, DAC and its heat 3 to 4%.
- Mass balance (Table 1, this study): H2 0.451 kg per kg cell dry weight (CDW); O2 2.09; CO2 1.92 kg/kg CDW (2.953 kg CO2 per kg protein); NH3 0.156 kg/kg CDW; core-plant electricity 12.47 MWh/t CDW (9.93 in 2050) and heat 5.96 MWh/t CDW (5.26 in 2050). Dry matter 65% protein. Our derivation: 0.451 / 0.65 = about 0.69 kg H2 per kg protein.
- Cost shares 2030 (7 sites): electricity supply 37 to 49% (EUR 1,598 to 2,708 per t protein); SCP core plant non-energy 24 to 32%; H2 supply non-energy 9 to 15%; CO2 supply non-energy 8 to 10%. H2 supply all-in EUR 2,416 to 4,656 per t H2 (2030). DAC CO2 all-in EUR 166 to 188 per t CO2 (2030). Point-source CO2 in the literature: EUR 0 to 42 per t CO2.
- Electricity supply cost to plant: EUR 20 to 24 per MWh at best sites 2030; 12 to 14 in 2050. PV-dominated sites: supply cost 22.6 to 26 vs generation 14.7 to 17.8 EUR/MWh (balancing adds up to 54%). "Asia" is PV-dominated in the model.
- Sizing: 100 kt protein per year needs 1,777 to 4,382 MW PV-wind nameplate and 808 to 1,245 MW electrolyser (HHV basis 616 to 949 MW) at 7 sites; baseload equivalent 878 MW power. Global: 1 Mt protein (2035) needs 33.6 GW PV + 4.1 GW wind + 8.0 GW electrolyser + 3.4 Mt CO2/yr DAC.
- Land (power plant area coverage): 330 to 1,000 m2 per t protein per yr in PV-dominated regions (2030); 1,200 to 3,000 where wind dominates; about 400 m2 at low-cost sites in 2050. Direct land impact of PV about 50% of gross coverage.
- Sensitivities (2030): WACC +/- 2 points = +/- EUR 400 to 1,100 per t protein (12 to 15%); WACC 5% cuts cost about 15%. Electrolyser high-cost case +3 to 6%. DAC high-cost case +5 to 10%. PV capex +/-10% = up to EUR 200 per t protein (4%).
- Competitiveness: food (soy isolate EUR 1.8 to 8.1, pea isolate 3.5 to 9.3 per kg protein) possibly from 2028 at best sites; feed (soybean EUR 0.66 to 1.75 per kg protein): "e-protein may not become a competitive feed at scale" under current conditions. Needs regulation that prices land, deforestation or GHG.
- Productivity assumption: 1.0 g CDW/L/h to 2030 (Solar Foods improved from 0.8 in 2019 to 1.0 in 2024), rising to 1.4 by 2050; literature maximum 2.6.

### W2. Leger et al. 2021, PNAS (FTG-02), abstract plus Scite excerpts
- PV to H2 (electrolysis efficiency 65 to 75%), formate (30 to 50%) or methanol (50 to 60%), DAC CO2, bacterial SCP. PV farm land efficiency 4.1 to 5.6% (30th to 70th percentile of 628 farms).
- Per unit land, SCP gives over 10 times the protein yield of any staple crop. One design hectare supplies about 15 t protein per year (over 500 people).
- Non-electron-donor energy: 21 to 31 MJ per kg dry weight for feed, 23 to 43 MJ for food. Stoichiometry: 1.76 kg CO2, 0.112 kg NH3 per kg dry biomass.
- Cost: H2 SCP feed about USD 2.6 per kg dry biomass, methanol SCP about USD 2.8; about USD 4 to 5 per kg protein, above fishmeal (about USD 2.5 per kg protein) and soybean meal (about USD 1 per kg protein) at the time. Most cost is the electron donor.
- Water: plants and animals use about 100 and 10,000 times more water than SCP (cited claim).

### W16. IEA Global Hydrogen Review 2025, executive summary (FTG-29)
- Low-emissions H2 about 1 Mt in 2025 (under 1% of production); 4.2 Mt/yr by 2030 from projects with FID or operating; all announced projects 37 Mt/yr (down from 49 a year earlier). FID reached by 9% of the 2030 pipeline. Electrolysis installed 2 GW (2024), plus 1 GW to July 2025; China 65% of installed capacity. Installed electrolyser cost USD 600 to 1,200 per kW in China vs USD 2,000 to 2,600 elsewhere.
- Southeast Asia: H2 demand 4 Mt/yr (2024); low-emissions production could reach 480 kt/yr by 2030 with only 6% at FID. "Viet Nam" hosts one of few projects of this scale outside China to reach FID: a 240 MW electrolyser under construction.
- Disagreement: IEA (2025) describes a Vietnamese 240 MW electrolyser project as at FID and under construction; Vietnamese press (2026, FTG-27) reports the Tra Vinh plant had only site perimeter works by May 2026. It is not certain they are the same project. Position: treat Vietnamese green H2 supply before 2030 as unproven.
