# FTB: The bio-design frontier to 2050 (AI, synthetic biology, precision fermentation, cultivated, molecular farming, cell-free, biomass fermentation)

Wave 3 research notes, agent FTB, for Part V (Futures 2030 to 2050) of the AltProtein Vietnam supply-side study, edition 1.1. Question tree: F2 (frontier technology) and F7 (weak signals and wildcards). Research date 24 September 2026; all sources accessed 2026-09-24.

**How to read.** Every finding carries an evidence label ([VN-direct], [VN-adjacent], [general]), a confidence (High, Medium, Low) and, where forward-looking, a foresight type ([trend], [projection], [estimate], [signal], [wildcard], [vision]) with a horizon year. Source IDs use the prefix FTB. Company and advocacy figures are labelled as claims. No probabilities are given because no source gives them. Nothing here is a forecast. Insects are out of scope for this stream.

**Builds on edition 1.0** (not repeated): ch10 technology fit, app-j science (titres, cell lines, duckweed, TEAs), ch05 rules (GMO Decree 43/2026), ch06 knowledge and talent, data/approvals.csv.

**Files in this folder:** `frontier_bio.md` (this file), `frontier_bio_tech.csv` (14 technology rows with 2030 to 2050 trajectories and Vietnam windows), `ai_biodesign_evidence.csv` (21 rows, demonstrated vs claimed), `approvals_trend.csv` (24 new items plus 20 aggregated count rows derived from edition 1.0).

## Headline findings

1. **AI speeds up the R&D loop, not the factory.** Peer-reviewed studies show Bayesian optimisation cut the experiments needed to optimise a cell-culture medium by 38% (2022) and cut experiments for *Pichia* protein production and immune-cell media by 3 to 30 times versus design-of-experiments (2025). None shows a lower production cost at scale. [general] High. Trend, horizon 2030. FTB-02, FTB-04.
2. **The clearest AI result relevant to Vietnam is an aquafeed enzyme.** An autonomous biofoundry raised a phytase's activity at neutral pH 26-fold in four rounds over four weeks, building fewer than 500 variants (2025). Vietnam already expresses an aquafeed phytase. [general] Medium. Signal, horizon 2030. FTB-06.
3. **Independent review: AI adoption in cultivated meat is "far below the ceiling".** Rethink Priorities (June 2026) found no good estimate of time saved, no published company use of protein-engineering tools such as AlphaFold, and named open data as "the binding constraint". [general] High. FTB-01.
4. **AI plus alternative-protein research is surging, but not in Vietnam.** OpenAlex counts rose from 35 papers (2024) to 120 (2025) and 172 (2026 to September). Leaders: US 69, China 54, India 48. Vietnam: 2, both reviews. [general; VN-direct for Vietnam] Medium. Trend 2022 to 2026. FTB-10.
5. **Compute is no longer Vietnam's constraint; wet-lab automation, data and people are.** FPT's Vietnam AI Factory ranked 38th on the June 2025 TOP500 (46.65 petaflops, NVIDIA H200). Viettel began operating an NVIDIA DGX B200 system with shared research access in February 2026. The AI Law (No. 134/2025/QH15, in force 1 March 2026) enables a national AI computing centre and a National AI Development Fund. Vietnam has no biofoundry and no Global Biofoundry Alliance member. [VN-direct] Medium. Signal, horizon 2030. FTB-11, FTB-13 to FTB-16.
6. **Vietnam's AI and biotechnology policies are converging but not yet connected.** The National AI Strategy (Decision 1671/QĐ-TTg, 28 August 2026) sets 2030 targets of about 1,100 EFLOPS (FP8) of AI compute and 10,000 advanced AI experts, and names AI in breeding, livestock and aquaculture. A biotechnology strategy for 2026 to 2030 (vision 2045) is being drafted with "bioinformatics and AI" as a priority sector. Directive 13/CT-TTg (April 2026) asks MAE to set up biotechnology clusters by December 2026. None of the texts we read mentions biofoundries, fermentation or alternative protein. [VN-direct] Medium. Official targets and signal, horizon 2026 to 2030. FTB-48 to FTB-50.
7. **Vietnam's protein-design base is small but growing fast.** Papers on AlphaFold, protein language models, protein design or enzyme engineering, 2019 to September 2026: Singapore 208, Thailand 130, Malaysia 113, Indonesia 76, Vietnam 46, Philippines 25. Vietnam's count rose from 2 (2020) to 19 (2026 to date), led by VNU Ho Chi Minh City and FPT University. [VN-direct] Medium. Trend 2020 to 2026. FTB-10.
8. **Capital has retreated, and what remains is moving to precision fermentation.** Fermentation funding fell from USD 632 million (2024) to USD 357 million (2025), but precision fermentation's share rose from 33% (2023) to 66% (2025). Cultivated meat and seafood raised USD 73.9 million in 2025, down from about USD 1 billion in 2021. [general] Medium. Trend 2021 to 2025. FTB-17, FTB-24, FTB-01.
9. **US approvals of precision-fermented animal proteins accelerated in 2025**, with FDA "no questions" letters for beta-lactoglobulin, lactoferrin, ovalbumin and whey proteins (Vivici, TurtleTree, Onego Bio, Verley), including one for a Chinese beta-lactoglobulin maker (Shanghai Changing Biotechnology). Meanwhile, a proposed rule would end self-affirmed GRAS. [general] Medium. Signal, horizon 2026 to 2028. FTB-18, FTB-19.
10. **Commodity cost parity for precision fermentation is not evidenced.** The only published targets are "envisaged" figures of USD 10 per kg by 2025 and USD 1 per kg by 2035. We found no new peer-reviewed food-protein TEA for 2024 to 2026. The economics work today for high-value proteins: conventional lactoferrin sells at USD 750 to 1,500 per kg. [general] Medium. Estimate, horizon 2035. FTB-23, FTB-41.
11. **First-generation precision-fermentation patents lapse in the mid-2030s.** Core families behind heme meat analogues and animal-free dairy were filed from 2013 to 2016, so the earliest members expire around 2033 to 2036. Continuations extend some protection. [general] Low. Estimate, horizon 2035. FTB-21.
12. **For cultivated meat, approval is not the binding constraint.** Believer Meats received an FDA letter in July 2025 and shut in December 2025, after its plant cost USD 154 million. Specialised companies fell from 155 to 142. The largest production run is 20,000 litres (Vow, Australia). [general] Medium. Trend 2024 to 2026. FTB-24, FTB-45.
13. **Cultivated meat's scale and footprint are both conditional.** Supplying 1% of global meat would need about 300 million litres of new mammalian culture capacity, about 30 times the world's 2021 capacity. With pharmaceutical-grade media, emissions are 4 to 25 times those of beef. [general] High. Projection (model-based). FTB-26.
14. **Hybrid products come first.** A December 2025 review judges that hybrids "may reach foodservice price points first within the decade" and that whole cuts will stay premium "through the early 2030s". [general] Medium. Projection (expert judgement), horizon 2030 to 2035. FTB-44.
15. **Molecular farming suits low-dose proteins, not bulk protein.** USDA APHIS cleared Moolec's soybean with pork proteins and its pea with bovine myoglobin in 2024. We found no completed FDA food review. By our estimate a hectare yields roughly 50 to 300 kg of the target protein. [general] Low to Medium. Estimate, horizon 2035. FTB-28 to FTB-31.
16. **Cell-free protein synthesis is a design tool, not a food factory.** The best documented costs are about USD 2.8 per mg, 5 to 8 orders of magnitude above food-protein prices. Bulk food use before 2050 is a wildcard. [general] Medium. Wildcard, horizon 2050. FTB-35, FTB-36.
17. **Distressed Western fermentation capacity is moving to Asia.** Fermentation equipment from the failed US mycoprotein firm Meati, valued at USD 15 to 20 million and including 300,000 litres of pilot and production capacity, sold for USD 75,000 in 2026 and is being shipped to India. This opens a cheap-capacity window for feed-protein ventures. [general] Medium. Signal, horizon 2027 to 2030. FTB-38.
18. **Neighbours have specific bio-design instruments that Vietnam lacks.** Thailand has a ten-year engineering and synthetic biology strategy (2026) and a public pilot plant with a GMP food and feed facility. Korea formalised standards for cell-cultured foods (June 2026) and enacted a Food Tech Industry Promotion Act. India's BioE3 policy funds a fermentation biofoundry for "smart proteins". [VN-adjacent] Medium. Signal. FTB-17, FTB-20, FTB-42, FTB-43.
19. **Vietnam windows (our estimates):**
    - Now: AI-assisted design of feed enzymes and yeast media.
    - 2027 to 2030: feed single-cell protein, possibly on second-hand tanks.
    - 2030 to 2035: contract precision fermentation of high-value proteins, and mycoprotein for food once a new-food route exists.
    - 2035 or later: contained molecular farming.
    - 2040s: cultivated seafood cells from Vietnamese species.
    - Not before 2050: cell-free synthesis for food.

    [VN-direct] Low. Estimate. See section 8.

## Detailed findings by sub-question

### 1. AI for protein, strain and media design (sub-question 1)

#### What is demonstrated (peer-reviewed, measured)
| Application | Result | Study | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bayesian optimisation (BO) of a 14-component serum-free medium, C2C12 mouse muscle cells | 181% more cells than DMEM control at about the same cost (+1.6%), found in 38% fewer experiments (81 vs 132) than an efficient design-of-experiments (DOE) method; a good-but-suboptimal medium found within 30 experiments (77% less effort) | Cosenza et al. 2022, doi:10.1002/bit.28132 | FTB-02 [general] High (full-text excerpts read) |
| Multi-objective BO, cost vs growth, C2C12 | Several media with more than 100% more growth than control; one medium with 23% more growth at 62.5% of control cost. Authors note they did not benchmark against DOE in this study | Cosenza et al. 2023, doi:10.1002/elsc.202300005 | FTB-03 [general] High |
| BO iterative design for media (human PBMCs) and for recombinant protein production in *Komagataella phaffii* (Pichia) | Improved outcomes with 3 to 30 times fewer experiments than estimated for standard DOE; about 3-fold with few factors, 10 to 30-fold with 9 factors including categorical ones; transfer learning cut a new campaign by at least 20% | Narayanan et al. 2025, doi:10.1038/s41467-025-61113-5 | FTB-04 [general] High |
| Fully automated design-build-test-learn (DBTL) loop (BioAutomata, iBioFAB biofoundry), lycopene pathway | Evaluated less than 1% of possible variants and outperformed random screening by 77% | HamediRad et al. 2019, doi:10.1038/s41467-019-13189-z | FTB-05 [general] High (abstract) |
| Autonomous enzyme engineering (protein language model + ML + biofoundry, no human judgement in loop) | Phytase (*Yersinia mollaretii*) variant with 26-fold higher activity at neutral pH; a methyltransferase with 90-fold better substrate preference; each in 4 rounds over 4 weeks with fewer than 500 variants built | Singh et al. 2025, doi:10.1038/s41467-025-61209-y (numbers from the 2025 preprint abstract, doi:10.1101/2025.02.12.637932) | FTB-06 [general] Medium (abstract only) |
| Protein language model generation (ProGen) | Artificial lysozymes active with catalytic efficiency similar to natural ones, some at low sequence identity to any natural protein | Madani et al. 2023, doi:10.1038/s41587-022-01618-2 | FTB-07 [general] Medium (abstract not read in this session; known result) |
| Generative multimodal protein model (ESM3) | A bright fluorescent protein at 58% sequence identity to known fluorescent proteins | Hayes et al. 2025 (EvolutionaryScale), doi:10.1126/science.ads0018 | FTB-08 [general] High (abstract) |
| LLM-agent "AI-native" biofoundry (preprint) | DNA polymerase: hit rate above 66% in 3 autonomous rounds; 37% lower sequencing error than a commercial reference | Zhang et al. 2026, doi:10.64898/2026.02.01.703093 | FTB-09 [general] Low (preprint; not peer reviewed) |

#### What is claimed, not demonstrated
- Rethink Priorities (Moulange and McAuliffe, 9 June 2026) reviewed AI in cultivated meat to April 2026. Conclusion, verbatim: "AI could provide a large relative uplift for cultivated meat, but actual adoption is far below the ceiling, constrained by data scarcity, company secrecy, funding shortfalls, and a small workforce." They "lack good estimates" of time saved, and found no published cultivated-meat company applications of protein-engineering tools such as AlphaFold or AlphaProteo. FTB-01 [general] High (report read).
- Company claims recorded by FTB-01 (all unverified claims): Clever Carnivore medium USD 0.07 per litre, with AI "contributing"; Meatly medium GBP 0.22 per litre with a 1.5p target; Multus MediOP ML media platform; Gourmey and DeepLife digital twin; Magic Valley and Pythag Tech bioprocess AI; Hoxton Farms BrightQuant cell-density model; Vow 20,000 L bioreactor for under USD 1 million. FTB-01 [general] Low.
- "Open-access data is the binding constraint" (FTB-01). This is the single most important caveat for any AI thesis in this field.

#### Bibliometric signal
- OpenAlex query (AI or ML or BO or protein language model, AND cultivated meat or precision fermentation or alternative protein or meat analogue or mycoprotein or single-cell protein; title and abstract; retracted excluded): 375 works 2016 to September 2026, rising from 11 (2022) and 23 (2023) to 35 (2024), 120 (2025) and 172 (2026 to date). Top countries: US 69, China 54, India 48, UK 25, Canada 19, Singapore 11, Korea 7, Thailand 7, Malaysia 5. Vietnam: 2 records, both broad reviews, no primary study. FTB-10 [VN-direct for the Vietnam count; general otherwise] Medium (keyword query, lower bound, includes reviews). Foresight type: trend (2022 to 2026).

#### Reading
- Demonstrated gains are real but narrow: 38% to 97% fewer experiments (about 1.6 to 30 times fewer) for media and protein-production optimisation, and weeks instead of months for single-enzyme campaigns. None of these is a cost-of-goods reduction at production scale. [general] Medium.
- The largest documented effect for Vietnam's feed-first path is enzyme engineering (phytase is an aquafeed enzyme Vietnam already works on; see RD-35 in edition 1.0). [VN-adjacent] Medium.
- AI does not remove the bottlenecks edition 1.0 found binding in Vietnam: regulation, shared pilot infrastructure, cheap feedstock conversion, and scale-up capital. FTB-01 says the same for cultivated meat globally.

### 2. Biofoundries, cloud labs and Vietnam's AI and compute base (sub-question 2)

#### Biofoundries
- The Global Biofoundry Alliance (GBA) members page lists, among members visible to us (list read only to "LARA", alphabetical, page partly image-based): A*STAR SPARROW (Singapore), HKUST(GZ) Biofoundry (China), K-Biofoundry (Korea), Australian Genome Foundry and CSIRO BioFoundry (Australia), plus US, UK, German, Danish, Estonian and South African members. No Vietnamese member. FTB-11 [VN-adjacent] Medium (partial list; the full list could not be rendered by fetch or browser).
- K-Biofoundry (KRIBB, Daejeon; National Bio Foundry Project) describes itself as an "open platform" for industry, academia and startups through collaborative R&D, strategic partnerships and incubation, offering DNA and strain engineering, high-throughput screening, process optimisation and scale-up, and data services. No fee schedule or foreign-user rule is published on the page. FTB-12 [VN-adjacent] Medium.
- iBioFAB (Illinois) ran both the BioAutomata and the 2025 autonomous enzyme-engineering campaigns (FTB-05, FTB-06): the acceleration evidence in section 1 comes from biofoundries, not from AI software alone. [general] High.
- Remote use by Vietnamese teams: technically possible through collaboration (the K-Biofoundry model) or commercial cloud labs; the practical barriers are shipping strains and genetic material across borders (MAE genetic-resources and biosafety rules under Decree 43/2026 apply to GMOs), IP terms, and cost. We could not retrieve a published cloud-lab price list (Emerald Cloud Lab pricing page returned 404). [VN-direct] Low; gap logged.

#### Vietnam's AI and compute base (2025 to 2026)
- **AI Law.** Law on Artificial Intelligence No. 134/2025/QH15, passed 10 December 2025 (429 of 434 deputies), in force 1 March 2026. Three risk tiers; transition to 1 March 2027 for most existing systems and 1 September 2027 for health, education and finance. It enables state investment in a national AI computing centre, a National AI Development Fund, a "controlled open data system", AI vouchers for SMEs (the National Technology Innovation Fund to allocate 30 to 40% of its resources to support including vouchers), and a controlled sandbox. FTB-13, FTB-14 [VN-direct] Medium (law firm summary and state-aligned press; the law text itself was not read).
- **Compute.** FPT's AI Factory in Vietnam ranked 38th on the June 2025 TOP500 list (46.65 petaflops, NVIDIA H200), and its Japan AI Factory 36th (49.85 petaflops): the first Vietnamese-owned systems on the list. FTB-15 [VN-direct] Medium (press, consistent with company releases). Viettel began operating an NVIDIA DGX B200 system at its Hoa Lac centre (Hanoi) on 6 February 2026, stated as "up to 1.5 exaFLOPs FP8", mainly for Vietnamese language models, with shared access for research institutes and enterprises. FTB-16 [VN-direct] Medium (company page; the FP8 figure is a vendor peak, not a TOP500 result).
- **What is missing.** We found no Vietnamese biofoundry, no automated DBTL facility, no Vietnamese protein-design group publishing on food proteins, and no programme linking the new compute to life sciences (FTB-10; FTB-13 notes the AI Law summary is silent on biotechnology). [VN-direct] Medium.

#### Vietnam's 2025 to 2026 policy instruments touching bio-design
- **National AI Strategy to 2030, vision 2045 (Decision 1671/QĐ-TTg, signed 28 August 2026).** Official targets for 2030 include:
  - about 560 EFLOPS (BF16) or about 1,100 EFLOPS (FP8) of AI compute;
  - about 500 MW of AI data-centre power;
  - at least 10,000 advanced AI experts;
  - at least 5 shared national AI platforms and 1 national AI computing coordination platform;
  - "top 3 in Southeast Asia" for AI R&D;
  - an AI economy of about 6% of GDP.

  Pillar 6 covers "Chuyển đổi hoạt động sản xuất nông nghiệp bằng AI trong lai tạo giống, trồng trọt, chăn nuôi, thủy sản" (AI-driven transformation of breeding, cultivation, livestock and aquaculture). The summary we read does not mention AI for protein, drug or materials discovery. FTB-48 [VN-direct] Medium (legal database summary of the decision; full text not read). Foresight type: projection (official target), horizon 2030.
- **A national biotechnology strategy for 2026 to 2030 (vision 2045) is being drafted.** A national workshop on 29 November 2025, co-organised by MOST and VNU Ho Chi Minh City, named four priority sectors, one of them "bioinformatics and artificial intelligence" (tin sinh học và trí tuệ nhân tạo). No mention of synthetic biology, biofoundries, fermentation or alternative protein. FTB-49 [VN-direct] Medium. Foresight type: signal, horizon 2026 to 2027.
- **Directive 13/CT-TTg (21 April 2026) on developing biotechnology as "ngành kinh tế - kỹ thuật quan trọng" (an important economic-technical sector).** Two tasks:
  - MOST was to complete special mechanisms and pilot models by May 2026;
  - MAE is to lead biotechnology clusters linking "nghiên cứu - sản xuất - chế biến - thương mại hóa" (research, production, processing and commercialisation) by December 2026.

  Named domains are breeding, vaccines, biologics, cell technology, environment and biosecurity. There is no explicit mention of fermentation, synthetic biology, AI or alternative protein. FTB-50 [VN-direct] Medium (government newspaper summary).
- **Resolution 189/NQ-CP (16 November 2023)** implements Politburo Resolution 36-NQ/TW. It includes a scheme to incubate firms producing biotechnology products at industrial scale, with no mention of synthetic biology or biofoundries. FTB-51 [VN-direct] Medium.
- **Reading.** Vietnam now has an AI strategy with hard compute and talent targets, and a biotechnology strategy in drafting that names bioinformatics and AI. Neither text we read connects the two through biofoundries, fermentation or protein design. The draft biotechnology strategy and MAE's December 2026 cluster plan are the two near-term openings to add a bio-design and biomanufacturing line. [VN-direct] Medium. Foresight type: signal, horizon 2026 to 2027.

#### Reading
- Compute is no longer the binding constraint for Vietnamese bio-design: GPU capacity on H200 and B200 class systems exists in-country since 2025 to 2026, and protein models such as ESM run on far smaller hardware. The binding constraints are wet-lab automation (no biofoundry), domain data, and people who combine protein science with machine learning. [VN-direct] Medium; [estimate] for 2030.
- A cheap path is "design in Vietnam, build and test abroad": use in-country compute and open models for design, and partner biofoundries in Korea, Singapore or China for build and test, until a domestic automated lab is justified. [estimate] Horizon 2030. Confidence Low (no Vietnamese precedent found).

#### Vietnam's protein-design research base (bibliometrics)
- OpenAlex (title and abstract mentions of AlphaFold, protein language model, protein design, ESM-2, RFdiffusion, enzyme engineering or directed evolution; 2019 to September 2026; retracted excluded): Singapore 208 works, Thailand 130, Malaysia 113, Indonesia 76, **Vietnam 46**, Philippines 25. Vietnam's count rose from 2 (2020) to 6 (2024), 16 (2025) and 19 (2026 to date). Leading Vietnamese affiliations: VNU Ho Chi Minh City 6, FPT University 5, VNU Hanoi 4, Hue University 4, Thai Nguyen University 4, Can Tho University 3; Taipei Medical University is the most frequent foreign co-affiliation (6). ### 3. Precision fermentation to 2050 (sub-question 3)

#### Where it stands in 2026 (new since edition 1.0)
- **Capital is shrinking but shifting to precision fermentation.** Fermentation companies (biomass plus precision) raised USD 357 million in 2025, down from USD 632 million in 2024; USD 5.2 billion since 2016. Precision fermentation took 66% of 2025 fermentation funding, up from 33% in 2023. Europe (USD 179 million) out-raised North America (USD 154 million) for the first time. 163 specialised fermentation companies in 2025. FTB-17 [general] Medium (GFI, advocacy). Foresight type: trend (2023 to 2025).
- **US approvals accelerated in 2025.** FDA "no questions" letters for precision-fermented animal proteins in 2025 include Vivici beta-lactoglobulin (March 2025), TurtleTree lactoferrin (March 2025), Onego Bio ovalbumin (September 2025) and Verley whey proteins, GRN 1241 (September 2025). A January 2025 regulatory consultancy note also lists Shanghai Changing Biotechnology beta-lactoglobulin and Geltor chicken collagen among proteins with letters. FTB-18, FTB-19 [general] Medium (trade press and advisory note; dates conflict, see Disagreements). An earlier letter to Imagindairy was reported in January 2024 (FTB-19, headline only).
- **The US self-affirmed GRAS route is closing.** A proposed rule sent for White House review (reported January 2025) "would make FDA notification mandatory for all GRAS substances", effectively ending self-affirmed GRAS; a Senate bill (Better Food Disclosure Act of 2025) would do the same. FTB-18 [general] Medium. Foresight type: signal (2026 to 2028). Implication: longer US timelines, but a public list that other regulators, including Vietnam, can rely on.
- **A Chinese company now holds a US GRAS letter for a precision-fermented dairy protein** (Shanghai Changing Biotechnology, beta-lactoglobulin). FTB-18 [general] Medium. Foresight type: signal. It shows Chinese precision fermentation reaching export-grade regulatory quality, which matters for Vietnam's import-price-taker scenario (ch13 Scenario 1).
- **Asia-Pacific policy signals:** Korea enacted a Food Tech Industry Promotion Act (reported December 2025 and March 2026 in the same GFI report; date to be confirmed); India's BioE3 policy funds 21 bio-enabler infrastructure projects and a fermentation biofoundry for "smart proteins"; China's State Development and Investment Corporation announced over CNY 4 billion (about USD 555 million) for biomanufacturing infrastructure, and Jiangnan University validated an engineered *Fusarium* strain at 5,000 L. FTB-17, FTB-20 [VN-adjacent] Medium.
- **IP disputes are now common**: Onego Bio and VTT v. The EVERY Company (egg-protein strains), Fonterra v. Perfect Day (dairy proteins), Impossible Foods v. Motif FoodWorks (heme), The Better Meat Co v. Meati (mycoprotein). FTB-17 [general] Medium. Foresight type: trend.
- **First-generation patents expire in the mid-2030s.** Core families behind heme meat analogues (Impossible Foods; priority filings 2013 to 2015, for example "Ground meat replicas", family 54241231, and "Secretion of heme-containing polypeptides", filed September 2014) and animal-free dairy (Perfect Day; family 54105986, still issuing continuations in 2025) date from 2013 to 2016. With a 20-year term from filing, the earliest members lapse about 2033 to 2036, though continuations and later process patents extend protection. FTB-21 [general] Low (our estimate from filing dates in a patent database; no freedom-to-operate analysis). Foresight type: estimate, horizon 2035.
- **Industrial hosts are opening up.** Novonesis published the safety lineage of a mycotoxin-free industrial *Aspergillus oryzae* line "for alternative and novel protein production at industrial scale" (Lehmbeck et al. 2025, doi:10.1007/s00253-025-13482-6). Together with OPENPichia (edition 1.0), this lowers the host barrier; the product-specific strain and titre remain proprietary. FTB-22 [general] Medium (abstract).
- **Continuous processing signal.** Pow.Bio with Bühler reported continuous fermentation at 3,000 L with "over threefold productivity gains and a 50 percent reduction in costs" (company claim relayed by GFI). FTB-17 [general] Low. Foresight type: signal.

#### Cost parity: what the literature does and does not say
- The only peer-reviewed cost targets remain the "envisaged" USD 10 per kg by 2025 and USD 1 per kg by 2035 (Nielsen, Meyer, Arnau and co-authors, 2025; edition 1.0 SCI-02). These are targets, not model outputs. We found no new peer-reviewed TEA for a precision-fermented food protein in 2024 to 2026 (a 2026 lactoferrin TEA preprint was withdrawn). FTB-23 [general] Medium.
- The best economic niche remains high-value proteins. Conventional bovine lactoferrin retails at USD 750 to 1,500 per kg because about 10,000 litres of milk yield 1 kg (trade press). FTB-41 [general] Low. This is why lactoferrin, not bulk whey, is where precision fermentation first earns margins.
- **Our reading:** commodity parity (whey or egg white at USD 5 to 15 per kg of protein) is not evidenced for 2030. A plausible path is: high-value, low-dose proteins (lactoferrin, heme, sweet proteins, enzymes) economic now; functional dairy and egg proteins for premium blends 2030 to 2035 as titres, continuous processing and chromatography-free downstream improve; bulk commodity use only if the 2035 target of about USD 1 to 2 per kg is approached, which no published data yet support. [general] Low. Foresight type: estimate, horizons 2030, 2035, 2040.

#### Vietnam window
- Edition 1.0 found: no food-grade contract fermentation in Vietnam, precision-fermented proteins probably "products of a GMO" under Decree 43/2026, and no Vietnamese food-protein expression work. Nothing in 2025 to 2026 changes that.
- GFI APAC and Hawkwood's site-selection study ranked "Thailand, Australia, and Vietnam" as "particularly strong locations for food biomanufacturing" on feedstock access and operating cost (regulatory readiness scored Vietnam lowest; edition 1.0 RGN-01). FTB-17 [VN-direct] Medium.
- **Window opens when** (our estimate): a closed-production GMO certificate has been issued to at least one food or feed fermentation plant with a published timeline; a Vietnamese or regional contract manufacturer offers food-grade capacity above 10 m3; and imported precision-fermented ingredients are cleared by the Vietnam Food Administration by reliance on US, Singapore or Canadian decisions. Plausible horizon 2030 to 2035 for contract manufacturing of high-value proteins; 2040 or later for commodity dairy or egg proteins, and only with low-cost sugar and power. [VN-direct] Low. Foresight type: estimate.

### 4. Cultivated meat and seafood to 2050 (sub-question 4)

#### Investment, companies, closures
- Cultivated meat and seafood investment fell from about USD 1 billion in 2021 (FTB-01) to USD 139 million in 2024 and USD 73.9 million in 2025; USD 3.4 billion cumulative 2016 to 2025. Specialised companies fell from 155 (2024) to 142 (2025). FTB-24 [general] Medium (GFI). Foresight type: trend (2021 to 2025).
- **Closures and consolidation:** Believer Meats filed for bankruptcy in 2025 after its US plant (cost USD 154 million, 11% over budget) had received USDA inspection; Gourmey and Vital Meat merged as PARIMA; Fork and Good acquired Orbillion Bio; Nexture Bio acquired Matrix F.T. FTB-24 [general] Medium. Note: Believer received an FDA "no questions" letter in July 2025 (edition 1.0 APR-023) and still failed, which shows approval is not the binding constraint.
- **Umami Bioworks (Singapore)** merged with Shiok Meats in 2024 to 2025; registered two cultivated white-fish ingredients on the EU Feed Materials Register for pet food (2 September 2025) with a US pet-food partner; said its Singapore human-food dossier was in the "final stage"; programmes in Japanese eel (pilot scale) and bluefin tuna (early), the latter with Umios (Maruha Nichiro). FTB-25, FTB-24 [VN-adjacent] Low to Medium (company statements relayed by trade press). No Vietnamese partner mentioned.

#### Technology records and scale
- **Media cost:** several companies announced media at USD 0.20 per litre or below in 2025, which GFI calls an over 99% decrease, partly validated by a GFI-commissioned analysis; protein-free media (Believer, PARIMA, Meatly) and food-grade cyclodextrins replacing albumin. FTB-24 [general] Low to Medium (company claims plus advocacy validation). Company claims of USD 0.07 per litre (Clever Carnivore) and GBP 0.22 per litre (Meatly) are in FTB-01 [general] Low. These are far below the costs behind Humbird's USD 37 to 51 per kg (edition 1.0), but no peer-reviewed TEA yet uses them.
- **Scale:** Vow (Sydney) produces at 20,000 L, 35,000 L total, "up to low hundreds of tons" of cultivated quail a year; most companies run below 1,000 L; Joes Future Food (China) ran 2,000 L trial production of pork. FTB-24 [general] Medium. Foresight type: trend.
- **Scale realism:** supplying 1% of global meat (about 3 Mt) with a Humbird-type fed-batch process would need about 440 facilities and about 300 million L of new mammalian cell culture capacity, roughly 30 times the world's 2021 mammalian culture capacity (11.75 million L). Risner et al. 2024, doi:10.1021/acsfoodscitech.4c00281. FTB-26 [general] High (full-text excerpt). Foresight type: projection (model-based), horizon unspecified.
- **Environment is conditional.** In the same LCA, global warming potential ranged from 12 to 1,508 kg CO2e per kg; scenarios with pharmaceutical-grade purified media were 4 to 25 times the median for retail beef (about 60 kg CO2e per kg). FTB-26 [general] High. Implication: the environmental case depends on food-grade media and low-carbon power, both still to be shown at scale.
- **Published cost range:** a 2025 review summarises the fall from USD 2.3 million per kg (2013 burger) to about USD 63 per kg in recent estimates. Gu et al. 2025, doi:10.1111/1541-4337.70221. FTB-27 [general] Medium (abstract). Pasitka et al. 2024 (edition 1.0) modelled USD 13.67 per kg for a 50:50 hybrid.
- **Fish cells:** spontaneously immortalised mackerel myoblasts (doubling every 24.3 h after 37 to 43 generations) and immortalised olive flounder myoblasts are reported (Saad et al. 2023; Krishnan et al. 2023, via FTB-27). No striped catfish (*tra*) or shrimp food line yet (edition 1.0). [general] Medium.

#### Approvals (see approvals_trend.csv)
- Approved markets remain Singapore, the United States and Australia and New Zealand; seven companies cleared to sell as of GFI's 2026 report; 2025 launches: Vow (Australia), Mission Barns (first US retail), Wildtype salmon (US restaurants), Meatly (UK pet food). FTB-24 [general] Medium.

#### Parity and 2040 to 2050 picture
- A December 2025 review of global readiness judges that "hybrid products may reach foodservice price points first within the decade, while fully cultivated whole cuts are expected to remain premium offerings through the early 2030s"; it also reports China's 5 kg pilot production (2023) and a Korean application fee of KRW 45 million (about USD 35,000 to 40,000) that pushes Korean startups to launch abroad. Hwang et al. 2025, doi:10.3390/foods14244222. FTB-44 [general] Medium (review judgement, not a model). Foresight type: projection (expert judgement), horizon 2030 to 2035.
- Believer Meats "shuttered in December 2025" (FoodNavigator, February 2026); EU and Japan reviews were still under way and no EU authorisation existed as of February 2026. FTB-45 [general] Medium.
- No peer-reviewed source gives a parity date for cultivated meat with commodity pork or chicken. The literature supports hybrids (10 to 50% cells in plant matrix) as the first volume product; Aleph's Singapore steak is 10 to 20% cells (edition 1.0 APR-018). [general] Medium.
- **Plausible 2040 to 2050 production system (our estimate):** continuous or perfusion culture in 20 to 200 m3 food-grade vessels, protein-free or near protein-free media from hydrolysates and recombinant growth factors made by precision fermentation or molecular farming, cells used mainly as fat and flavour inputs at 5 to 30% in hybrid products, with fish and fat cells earlier than whole muscle. [general] Low. Foresight type: estimate, horizon 2040 to 2050.
- **Vietnam window (estimate):** research only to 2035. A role could open in the 2040s for cultivated seafood fat or flavour cells from Vietnamese species (*tra*, shrimp, eel, grouper) if (1) a food-grade fish cell line exists (Vietnamese or licensed), (2) media below about USD 0.5 per litre is demonstrated in peer-reviewed or audited data, and (3) Vietnam has a novel-food route with reliance on Singapore. [VN-direct] Low. Foresight type: estimate.

### 5. Molecular farming (sub-question 5)
- **US regulatory steps.** USDA APHIS found Moolec's "Piggy Sooy" soybean (pork proteins) not subject to its biotechnology regulations (announced April 2024); FDA consultation still required and pending. APHIS also cleared Moolec's pea expressing bovine myoglobin (October 2024) and a safflower expressing bovine chymosin. PoLoPo filed a Regulatory Status Review for potatoes expressing egg ovalbumin (by May 2024). FTB-28, FTB-29, FTB-30 [general] Medium (trade press relaying APHIS decisions). The pea decision is confirmed on an APHIS program update: "Moolec Science, pea with a meat protein in seeds", found "unlikely to pose an increased plant pest risk" (FTB-31 [general] High).
- **No plant-made animal food protein has completed FDA review that we could find** (as of September 2026). [general] Low (absence of evidence; not exhaustively checked).
- **Expression levels (company claims):** Moolec, pork proteins up to 26.6% of total soluble seed protein; IngredientWerks, bovine myoglobin above 10 mg per g of maize kernel. FTB-28, FTB-29 [general] Low.
- **Consolidation:** Moolec merged with Bioceres entities (listed among more than 40 alternative-protein closures, mergers and acquisitions between September 2024 and August 2025: 24 acquisitions, 11 closures, 4 liquidations, 2 bankruptcies, 1 merger; 5 in Asia-Pacific). FTB-32 [general] Medium.
- **Yield per hectare (our estimate).** Protein per hectare = grain yield x seed protein share x soluble share x target share. For a US soybean at 3.5 t per ha, 38% protein, about 80% soluble (assumed), 26.6% target: about 0.28 t of target protein per ha a year before losses. At a lower Vietnamese-type soybean yield (about 1.5 t per ha, our assumption, to be checked against NSO statistics) this falls to about 0.12 t per ha. For maize with myoglobin at 1% of kernel mass and a maize yield of about 4.8 t per ha (our assumption, to be checked), about 48 kg of myoglobin per ha. [general] Low. Foresight type: estimate. Basis: company expression claims plus assumed yields; extraction losses not included.
- **Reading:** molecular farming makes most sense for **low-dose, high-value proteins** (heme and myoglobin at 0.5 to 2% of a product, chymosin, growth factors for cell culture, lactoferrin), not bulk protein: a hectare yields tens to a few hundred kilograms of target protein, and purification from plant tissue is the cost driver. Peer-reviewed critical reviews reach the same judgement for pharmaceutical proteins (Schillberg and Finnern 2021, doi:10.1016/j.jplph.2020.153359, abstract only). FTB-33 [general] Medium.
- **Vietnam relevance.** Vietnam has licensed GM maize for cultivation since 2015 (5 events with biosafety certificates: MON89034, GA21, NK603, Bt11, TC1507; 16 recognised varieties; the 29th country to allow commercial GM cultivation), has gene-editing projects on rice (Bắc Thơm 7, OM5451), and, from January 2026, a notification route for gene-edited organisms without foreign DNA (edition 1.0 ch05). FTB-34 [VN-direct] Medium (2024 government portal article). Molecular farming would need a GM crop cultivation biosafety certificate plus a food or feed GMO certificate, and containment to keep pharma-type or food-protein crops out of commodity chains. We found no molecular-farming project in Vietnam. [VN-direct] Low.
- **Vietnam window (estimate):** 2035 or later, for contained, high-value hosts (duckweed in closed ponds or tanks, rice seed, tobacco-type leaf systems in greenhouses) producing heme, enzymes or growth factors for feed or cell culture, and only after a first US or Singapore food approval shows the route. Duckweed is attractive because Vietnam holds a germplasm collection and a genome (edition 1.0) and contained aquatic culture limits gene flow. [VN-direct] Low. Foresight type: estimate, horizon 2035 to 2040.

### 6. Cell-free protein synthesis for food (sub-question 6)
- **State:** cell-free protein synthesis (CFPS) is a research and pharmaceutical tool. The largest documented scale-up is 1 mL to 100 L for a cytokine in an *E. coli* system (Zawada et al. 2011, doi:10.1002/bit.23103, via FTB-35). A TEA for monoclonal antibodies by CFPS estimated USD 2.8 per mg at 100 L and USD 48 per mg at 1 L (USD 2.8 million per kg at best), and concluded viability needs "a significant increase in the product yield and the recycling of high-cost components such as DNA" (Thaore et al. 2020, doi:10.3390/pr8040454). FTB-35 [general] High (full-text excerpts).
- A low-cost reconstituted (PURE) system reached 156 micrograms per mL at USD 0.09 per microlitre, a 14-fold improvement in cost-normalised yield (Lavickova and Maerkl 2019, doi:10.1021/acssynbio.8b00427), which still implies hundreds of US dollars per milligram. FTB-36 [general] High (abstract; our arithmetic: USD 90 per mL / 0.156 mg per mL, about USD 580 per mg).
- **Distance to food:** food proteins sell at roughly USD 2 to 20 per kg; the best CFPS costs above are about 5 to 8 orders of magnitude higher. We found no food product, pilot plant or peer-reviewed food TEA for CFPS. [general] Medium. Foresight type: estimate.
- **Realistic horizon:** CFPS matters for food through **R&D**, not manufacturing: rapid prototyping of enzymes and proteins inside AI design loops (hours instead of days per design), and decentralised production of tiny amounts (diagnostics, enzymes). Bulk food CFPS before 2050 would need several breakthroughs at once (cheap energy regeneration, DNA recycling, extract reuse) and is best treated as a wildcard. [general] Low. Foresight type: wildcard, horizon 2050.
- **Vietnam:** a CFPS teaching and prototyping capability is cheap (low-cost systems were designed for low- and middle-income labs; Guzman-Chavez et al. 2022, doi:10.1021/acssynbio.1c00342, title and abstract) and fits a design-in-Vietnam strategy. FTB-37 [general] Medium.

### 7. Next-generation biomass fermentation and hybrid products (sub-question 7)
- **Investment and failures.** Meati (US mycoprotein) entered an assignment for the benefit of creditors in May 2025 despite commercial production; its Colorado plant was seized over USD 6.7 million of unpaid taxes and interest; in 2026 Ayana Bio and Zenfold Sustainable Technologies bought fermentation equipment valued at USD 15 to 20 million, "300,000 litres of pilot and production capacity", for USD 75,000 and plan to ship it to India. FTB-38 [general] Medium (trade press, 18 September 2026). GFI links Meati's failure to "labor, electricity, water costs, and local tax environments". FTB-17. Foresight type: signal (distressed fermentation assets moving to lower-cost Asian sites).
- **Continuous mycoprotein:** The Better Meat Co claimed a 30% total cost cut and 68% more mycelium in the same time through continuous fermentation (June 2024, company claim), and holds a Singapore approval for *Neurospora crassa* mycoprotein (edition 1.0 APR-010). FTB-39 [general] Low.
- **Scale-ups in 2025:** Angel Yeast (China) new industrial yeast-protein facility; Typcal (Brazil) industrial mycelium plant; Millow (Sweden) full-scale mycelium-oat process; Planetary (Switzerland) 50,000 L site; Jiangnan University engineered *Fusarium* at 5,000 L. FTB-17 [general] Medium.
- **Enough (UK, Abunda mycoprotein)** raised EUR 40 million (USD 43.6 million) to scale (trade press; date not read); The Protein Brewery raised USD 35.6 million Series B; Planetary raised USD 28 million for fermentation infrastructure and licensing. FTB-40 [general] Low (headlines only). Mixed picture: capital still flows to biomass players with plants and licensing models.
- **Hybrids:** GFI 2026 highlights blended meat (plant or mycoprotein plus conventional meat), a mycelium steak launched by Beyond Meat in US foodservice, and cultivated hybrids (Aleph 10 to 20% cells). FTB-17 [general] Medium. Foresight type: trend. Hybrids are the most plausible volume route for cultivated cells and for mycoprotein in meat-loving markets.
- **Plant cell culture** (Ayana Bio) is a further adjacent family now acquiring fermentation assets. FTB-38 [general] Low. Signal only.
- **Vietnam window:** biomass fermentation for **feed** is already the strongest fit in edition 1.0 (functional microbial ingredients). New in this round: cheap second-hand fermentation capacity is appearing on the market as Western plants fail, and buyers are moving it to Asia. A Vietnamese feed-protein venture could acquire tanks at a fraction of new cost. **Window opens when** a buyer (aquafeed mill) signs offtake and MAE lists microbial biomass for aquafeed; plausible 2027 to 2030. Mycoprotein for food: 2030 to 2035, after a new-food route exists. [VN-direct] Low. Foresight type: estimate.

## Implications by audience
- **Investors.**
  - Treat AI claims as R&D-speed claims, not cost-of-goods claims. Ask for the peer-reviewed or audited baseline.
  - The investable frontier in Vietnam to 2030 is feed-side fermentation. Distressed Western tanks cut its capital cost.
  - Precision fermentation fits only as high-value contract manufacturing.
  - The mid-2030s patent expiries and the 2025 US approval wave for Chinese makers set the competitive clock.
- **Policy makers.**
  - Vietnam has compute and an AI Law, but no biofoundry, no bio-design programme and no synthetic biology strategy, while Thailand, Korea, India and China all have these.
  - Two drafting windows are open now: the biotechnology strategy for 2026 to 2030 (vision 2045), and MAE's biotechnology clusters due by December 2026. Both could add a bio-design and biomanufacturing line linked to the AI Strategy's compute and talent targets.
  - Three moves pay off in every scenario:
    - a shared automated build-test lab, or a funded biofoundry partnership;
    - an open-data rule for publicly funded bio-design projects (FTB-01 names data as the binding constraint);
    - a published timeline for Decree 43/2026 closed-production certificates.
- **Startups.**
  - "Design in Vietnam, build and test abroad" is feasible now.
  - Aquafeed enzymes (phytase is the demonstrated case) and feed-yeast media are the best first targets for AI methods.
  - Cultivated meat and molecular farming are not start-now businesses in Vietnam.
- **F&B and feed manufacturers.**
  - Expect AI-optimised enzymes and functional microbial ingredients in feed before 2030.
  - Precision-fermented dairy proteins may arrive as imports, including from China, well before any Vietnamese production.
  - Hybrid products are the most plausible way cultivated and fungal proteins will reach menus.
- **Research bodies.**
  - Point the growing protein-design groups (VNU-HCM, FPT University, VNU Hanoi, Hue, Thai Nguyen) at food and feed proteins.
  - Build a Vietnamese open dataset: media and strain performance on cassava and molasses streams, and fish-cell growth.
  - Add cell-free prototyping to teaching.
- **International bodies.**
  - Fundable regional public goods:
    - open bio-design datasets for tropical feedstocks;
    - biofoundry access schemes for Southeast Asian teams;
    - a food-grade fish cell line for Mekong species.
