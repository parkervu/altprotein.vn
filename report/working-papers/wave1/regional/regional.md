## Headline findings

1. **Vietnam scores lowest in the region on rules and near the top on cost, but it is not unique on cost.** In the GFI APAC x Hawkwood Biotech "Where to Build" study (October 2025), Vietnam's scores (0 to 100, min-max across nine countries, 100 = best) were: labour 100 (USD 342/month manufacturing wage), utilities 97 (USD 0.08/kWh), construction cost 93 (USD 1,672/m2), business environment 30, sugar capability 26, talent 26, regulatory readiness 0. Indonesia scores the same or better on every cost factor (labour 100, utilities 99, construction 100). Thailand is close on cost (81 to 99) and far ahead on sugar (100), business environment (74) and regulation (50). [VN-direct] [High] (RGN-01)

2. **The study's recommended fix for Vietnam is a rules fix.** Its three priorities for Vietnam: "Operationalise Resolution 36 via funded programs with specific agency mandates"; "Clarify incentive eligibility criteria, timelines, and application processes for biomanufacturing sites"; "Publish regulatory procedures and statutory timelines for novel food approvals." [VN-direct] [High] (RGN-01)

3. **Vietnam's cost advantage acts on a small share of cost.** In the study's own representative plant models, labour is 7% (biomass, 80,000 t/yr) and 17% (precision fermentation, 10,000 t/yr) of production cost, and utilities 4 to 5%. Depreciation (capital cost) is 37 to 56%. The models were run for a US Midwest plant on corn glucose, not for any Asian site. Cheap labour and power alone will not win projects. [general] [High] (RGN-01)

4. **The study's feedstock index is sugar only, which understates Vietnam, but Vietnam's fermentable carbohydrate is already sold to China.** Vietnam scored 26 on "sugar capability". The index ignores cassava starch. Vietnam exported 2.15 Mt of cassava starch worth USD 1.06 billion in 2023, 93.5% to China (Vietnam-reported). In 2024 Vietnam supplied 39% of China's cassava starch imports (1.56 Mt, USD 757 M, China-reported). The starch sells at about USD 0.49/kg. [VN-direct] [High for trade numbers; the interpretation is ours] (RGN-01, RGN-51, RGN-53)

5. **Thailand is responding directly to the same ranking.** Thailand's national Biotechnology Industry Development Framework 2026-2035 (NXPO, Nov 2025) cites the GFI/Hawkwood study. It names "future food and alternative proteins" as one of four target sub-industries, plans a GMP precision fermentation pilot plant and a CDMO network, and budgets 500 million baht per year for 10 years for synthetic biology research. [VN-adjacent] [Medium] (RGN-35)

6. **Singapore has approved 18 novel foods, including the world's first cultivated duck and beef.** The Singapore Food Agency (SFA) list dated 14 August 2026 includes 6 cultivated meat approvals, 6 fungal or bacterial biomass proteins (including Solar Foods' Solein, 29 Sep 2022), 2 microalgae, 3 precision-fermented ingredients and 1 synthetic compound. Aleph Farms' cultivated beef is dated 22 July 2026 on the SFA list (announced 4 Aug 2026). Parima's duck is dated 10 April 2026. [VN-adjacent] [High] (RGN-04, RGN-29)

7. **But Singapore has stepped back from producing alternative protein itself.** "Singapore Food Story 2" (Nov 2025) dropped the "30 by 30" goal. The new 2035 local production targets (20% of fibre, 30% of protein, meaning eggs and seafood) exclude alternative protein "given the nascency of the sector." Singapore now acts as regulator, R&D hub and financier more than as a production base. This leaves room for lower-cost neighbours to host manufacturing. [VN-adjacent] [High] (RGN-08, RGN-09)

8. **Verified: US FDA completed its review of Wildtype's cultivated coho salmon on 28 May 2025.** The FDA inventory lists it as CCC 005 (*Oncorhynchus kisutch*), response date 28 May 2025. It is the first cultivated seafood cleared in the US. The US has five completed cultivated-cell consultations: UPSIDE (Nov 2022), GOOD Meat (Mar 2023), Mission Barns (Mar 2025), Wildtype (May 2025) and Believer (Jul 2025). [general] [High] (RGN-03)

9. **Outside Singapore, no Asian regulator has approved cultivated meat yet, even where a route exists.** Korea opened applications in February 2024 (fee reported at KRW 45 million, review up to 270 working days) and still had no approval in April 2026 reporting. Thailand's first dossier (Aleph Farms, filed December 2024) is still pending. Japan only presented draft safety checkpoints in May 2026. Australia/New Zealand approved Vow's quail in 2025. Having a framework does not by itself make approvals fast. [VN-adjacent] [Medium] (RGN-21, RGN-32, RGN-33, RGN-34, RGN-18)

10. **ScaleUp Bio, the region's reference precision-fermentation CDMO, has changed owners and widened its focus.** It has up to 10,000 L at Tuas and an SFA food manufacturing licence (May 2024). It was a joint venture of Nurasa (Temasek) and ADM. In March 2026, Fusionopolis Ventures (a subsidiary of EDIS, a Singapore state investor) "joined ADM as shareholder". The March 2026 releases do not name Nurasa. The company now also serves pharma, cosmetics, chemicals and energy. [VN-adjacent] [High for facts; Medium that Nurasa has exited] (RGN-11, RGN-12, RGN-13)

11. **The largest verified open pilot fermentation plant near Vietnam is in Thailand, and one Thai private plant may have just been cancelled.** EECi's Biorefinery Pilot Plant (Rayong, run under NSTDA) has 12 fermenters, including two of 15,000 L, and offers contract manufacturing. Its food-grade status is not stated. Separately, BBGI (Bangchak group) posted a stock exchange notice on 22 September 2026 titled "Notification of the Termination of the Biotechnology Plant Construction Project." BBGI was building a synthetic biology plant with Fermbox Bio, linked to Aleph Farms' Thai plans. We could not read the notice's content. [VN-adjacent] [Medium; Low for the BBGI item] (RGN-36, RGN-38)

12. **China is the scale competitor and sets the price to beat.** Angel Yeast's Yichang plant makes 11,000+ t/yr of yeast protein. Fushine Biotech is expanding mycoprotein from 1,200 to 20,000 t/yr. A 20,000 t/yr mycoprotein plant (More Meat with Guangzhou Shuangqiao, a starch sugar maker) began commercial runs in June 2026. Former pharma CDMOs now take foreign food clients. One client said scale-up elsewhere would have taken "two times as long and double the cost." [general] [Medium] (RGN-24)

13. **Vietnam imports few food-grade protein ingredients, and the two customs data sets disagree.** Vietnam reported 2023 imports of USD 22.2 M under HS 3504 (protein substances, including soy protein isolate; China 45%) and USD 34.0 M under HS 2106.10 (protein concentrates and textured protein; US 46%, China 27%). China reported shipping 10,028 t of HS 3504 to Vietnam in 2023 and 25,267 t in 2024, at USD 1.0 to 1.6/kg. That price suggests much of it is feed-grade rather than isolate. [VN-direct] [Medium] (RGN-45 to RGN-50)

14. **Vietnam sells starch to China, not sugar ready for fermenters.** In 2024 China imported USD 114 M of modified starch (HS 3505.10) and USD 104 M of dried cassava (HS 0714.10) from Vietnam, but only USD 0.16 M of glucose (HS 1702.30). The step that turns starch into glucose for fermentation happens in China. Keeping that step in Vietnam is the practical way for Vietnam to "plug in". [VN-direct] [High] (RGN-51, RGN-52, RGN-54, RGN-55)

15. **Halal rulings now exist next door.** In December 2025 Malaysia became the first Muslim-majority country to rule that cultivated meat can be halal. Singapore's Islamic authority has also set out a path. Aleph Farms says it will use capacity in Penang (Malaysia) to supply the wider region. [VN-adjacent] [Medium] (RGN-20, RGN-21, RGN-28, RGN-30)

16. **Korea has combined a law, a fee schedule and a special zone.** Korea changed an existing instrument (the "temporary standards" for new food ingredients) in about nine months (May 2023 to February 2024). It passed a Food Tech Industry Promotion Act (enacted December 2024, in force December 2025) and created a regulation-free special zone for cell-cultured food in Gyeongbuk. A public cultivated meat research centre (KRW 14.5 bn, about USD 10 M) is due to open in Uiseong in 2027. [VN-adjacent] [Medium] (RGN-19, RGN-21, RGN-34)

### 7. The GFI APAC x Hawkwood "Where to Build" study (October 2025)

- **Authors:** Jennifer Morton and Dean Powell (GFI APAC), Tony Day (Hawkwood Biotech). The PDF is in GFI's October 2025 upload folder. A companion GFI article names Thailand, Vietnam and Australia as the "three APAC countries with the right ingredients" (RGN-01, RGN-02).
- **Countries:** Australia, Indonesia, Japan, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, South Korea, Thailand, Vietnam.
- **Drivers scored:** feedstock (sugar), capex, utilities, labour, talent, business environment, regulatory readiness, plus incentives. The text says incentives and feedstock cost "emerged as the two most critical drivers."
- **Assumptions:**
  - Representative plants of 80,000 t/yr (submerged biomass) and 10,000 t/yr (precision fermentation), modelled at a US Midwest site on corn glucose "due to limitations in data and modelling capacity."
  - Sugar index = 2024 exports (double-weighted), production and import reliance.
  - Talent = engineering, manufacturing and construction graduates, vocational enrolment, and R&D researchers per million.
  - Business environment = World Bank Ease of Doing Business (2021 data vintage per table header). That index was discontinued in 2021.
  - Regulatory readiness as described in section 1.
- **Vietnam's inputs and scores:**

| Dimension | Vietnam input | Index (0-100) | Best in region |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sugar capability | 2024 production 1,350 kt; exports 0; imports 110 kt | 26 | Thailand 100 |
| Construction cost | USD 1,672/m2 | 93 | Indonesia 100 |
| Utilities | USD 0.08/kWh; water USD 0.31 per 15 m3/month | 97 | Indonesia 99 (highest printed) |
| Labour | USD 342/month | 100 | Vietnam and Indonesia (USD 350) 100 |
| Talent | EMC graduates 20%; vocational 25%; 779 researchers per million | 26 | Korea 68 |
| Business environment | WB EoDB about 70 | 30 | Singapore 100 |
| Regulatory readiness | No framework, no approvals | 0 | Singapore, Australia 100 |

- **Other countries' scores (same order: sugar, capex, utilities, labour, talent, business, regulation):**
  - Thailand: 100, 81, 84, 99, 36, 74, 50 (sugar production 10,040 kt, exports 5,300 kt; wage USD 431/month)
  - Indonesia: 14, 100, 99, 100, 18, 29, 0 (construction USD 1,487/m2; USD 0.07/kWh; wage USD 350; sugar imports 5,000 kt)
  - Philippines: 31, 94, 75, 97, 17, 0, 0
  - Malaysia: 0, 38, 87, 93, 29, 80, 0
  - Australia: 60, 0, 13, 0, 50, 79, 100
  - Korea: 0, 22, 80, 29, 68, 91, 50
  - Japan: 11, 5, 47, 27, 49, 65, 50
  - Singapore: 0, 25, 27, 26, 55, 100, 100
  - The cost-factor rows (study Figure 18) and enabling-factor rows (Figure 21) were extracted verbatim in separate reads. An earlier machine read gave different numbers for Indonesia and Australia sugar. The verbatim Figure 18 read is the one used here. The business environment index was checked by arithmetic against World Bank Doing Business 2020 scores, and it matches.
  - Reading across: no country combines low cost with regulatory readiness. Thailand is the closest to doing so.
- **Incentives:** the highest return to government and the highest NPV gain to the company both come from a 50% capex grant or non-dilutive funding (reported NPV gain USD 73 M). Next come a 10% feedstock cost cut and a loan guarantee. Tax holidays rank near the bottom.
- **What it says about Vietnam:**
  - Vietnam "presents one of the region's most cost-competitive environments" and "a potential first-mover opportunity."
  - Obstacles: "constraints in feedstock supply, the absence of a clear regulatory pathway for novel foods, and limited experience with industrial-scale fermentation."
  - Recommendations are quoted in headline 2.
  - The study also cites an unnamed "significant investment in a large-scale biomanufacturing facility" in Vietnam, with no reference.
  - It claims Vietnam had "the region's highest sugar yield per hectare" in 2024. We did not verify this.

**Our critique (adversarial):**
- The index rewards Vietnam for low wages and power, which the study's own cost model says make up less than a quarter of cost.
- It penalises Vietnam on sugar while ignoring cassava starch, Vietnam's real fermentable carbohydrate.
- It scores the business environment with a discontinued index.
- It gives no country-specific cost per kg.
- The scores are relative ranks among nine countries. They are not investment-grade cost estimates.

### 8. Lessons: how Singapore, Thailand and Korea built their frameworks

| | Singapore | Thailand | South Korea |

Sources: RGN-04, RGN-19, RGN-32, RGN-34, RGN-44, RGN-18.

**Transferable to Vietnam (our assessment, [VN-adjacent], Medium):**
1. Korea shows a route that does not need a new law. Add a "new food ingredient" safety assessment step inside an existing instrument (for Vietnam, a decree amending Decree 15/2018/ND-CP), with a published fee and a statutory review time. Korea took about nine months.
2. Thailand shows how to borrow assessors. Designate an existing technical body as the safety assessment unit instead of building a new agency. The Vietnamese candidate is for the regulation chapter to name.
3. Singapore shows the value of transparency. Publishing each approval with its specification and risk controls lets later regulators rely on earlier assessments. Vietnam could accept or fast-track dossiers already approved by SFA, FSANZ or US FDA.
4. A framework is not enough. Thailand and Korea have routes but no approvals after 21 to 31 months. Staff capacity and review timelines matter as much as the legal text.

## Implications by audience

- **VCs and investors:**
  - Vietnam is a cost story without a regulatory route. Until a route exists, a Vietnamese food-grade novel protein plant can only be justified for export under another country's approval, or for feed.
  - Price any Vietnamese microbial protein against Chinese yeast protein and mycoprotein made at 11,000 to 20,000 t/yr scale.
- **Policy makers:**
  - The single most-cited regional recommendation for Vietnam is to publish a novel food procedure with a statutory timeline. Korea did the equivalent by amending an existing rule in about nine months.
  - Pair it with capex grants, which the Hawkwood study ranks above tax holidays.
  - Thailand is already acting on the same study.
- **Startups:**
  - For precision fermentation, plan on contract manufacturing in Singapore (ScaleUp Bio, food-licensed), Thailand (EECi, confirm food grade) or China (cheapest, with IP trade-offs).
  - Use an SFA approval as the regional credential. Budget about 9 to 12 months or more after a complete dossier.
- **F&B and feed manufacturers:**
  - Vietnam's imported food-protein ingredients are small (about USD 56 M in 2023) and come mostly from China and the US.
  - Vietnam's big lever is the 2 Mt+ of cassava starch it ships to China each year. Converting part of it to glucose or protein at home is the plug-in point.
- **Academia:** Chinese cassava-to-protein and gas-to-protein feed research (for example CAP trials in shrimp and poultry) is directly relevant to Vietnamese feed. Partnering with Chinese or Thai groups is the fastest way to catch up.
- **International bodies:**
  - Help Vietnam's regulator draft a novel food procedure. Candidate channels: SFA's regulator MOUs (FSANZ, ANSES, SAMR) and the FAO cultivated-food safety work.
  - Consider a Thailand-Vietnam pilot-plant access arrangement (EECi).
