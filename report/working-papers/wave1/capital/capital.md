# M7 Capital: who funds alternative protein, agrifood tech and biomanufacturing in Vietnam and the region

Research agent: CAPITAL. Source prefix: CAP. Accessed 23 September 2026. Edition: September 2026.

**Coverage warning.** The session-wide web search cap was reached early in this workstream. After that, only direct page fetches of known URLs were possible. DFI coverage beyond IFC, Proparco and ADB Ventures, Thai Union/CPF/Wilmar venture arms, Vietnamese food M&A (CJ and Cau Tre, Masan and De Heus), aquaculture-tech and feed-tech deals, and H1 2026 Vietnam VC totals are therefore incomplete. Absence of a deal in this file means "not found", not "does not exist".

Exchange rate used for conversions: USD 1 = VND ~25,380 (implied by VnExpress, March 2026, CAP-32).

## Headline findings

1. **Disclosed venture money into Vietnam-based alternative protein is small and concentrated in one insect company.** Entobel's 2022 to 2023 Series B (USD 25M Mekong Enterprise Fund IV, USD 5M Dragon Capital, USD 2.5M IFC; about USD 32.5M to 33M in total) is the only disclosed round above USD 5M we found in 2015 to September 2026. [VN-direct] High for the Mekong amount, Medium for the others. CAP-01, CAP-02, CAP-04, CAP-08.
2. **The prior lead "Entobel raised ~USD 36M" is not supported.** Documented total: USD 33M Series B (company), USD 32.5M project cost (IFC data). Earlier rounds are undisclosed. IFC's ticket was USD 2.5M, not the "~USD 10M" reported by Petfood Industry. A USD 10M IFC equity proposal (January 2022) does not appear to have closed at that size. [VN-direct] Medium. CAP-03, CAP-04, CAP-07, CAP-08.
3. **We found no disclosed VC round for any Vietnam-based plant-based meat, plant-based dairy, precision fermentation, microalgae or cultivated-meat company.** The only plant-protein fundraising found is Emmay ("Ruoc Nam", mushroom-based products): about USD 250K from three angel rounds plus US foundation grants by 2021 (company claim). [VN-direct] Low (search coverage limited). CAP-15.
4. **Cricket One (edible insects) raised three undisclosed rounds** (seed 2019 led by 500 Startups and Masik; pre-Series A 2020 led by Corecam; Series A 2023 led by Robert Alexander Stone). Each was described only as "seven-figure" or less. [VN-direct] Medium. CAP-11, CAP-12, CAP-13.
5. **Vietnamese strategic money has gone outward, not inward.** Vinh Hoan invested in Singapore's Shiok Meats and Avant Meats (cultivated seafood, 2021). VinaCapital Ventures invested in Korea's INNOHAS (plant-based meat, 2024) so that INNOHAS could enter Vietnam. Strategic investors, not VCs, are the most active Vietnam-linked alt-protein investors. [VN-direct] Low to Medium. CAP-16, CAP-17, CAP-18.
6. **Vietnam VC totals:** USD 529M (2023), USD 398M (2024), USD 509M in 103 deals (2025, up 28%). Private equity hit a record USD 3.96B in 46 deals in 2025. Total private capital was USD 4.5B. H1 2026 totals have not been published in a reliable source. [VN-direct] Medium (industry report, NIC/VPCA/Do Ventures). CAP-20, CAP-21, CAP-22, CAP-24.
7. **Agritech is a rounding error in Vietnamese VC and fell 99% in 2025.** Agriculture VC: USD 8M (2023), USD 74M (2024, mostly the Techcoop deal), USD 1M (2025). That is about 0.2% of 2025 VC. Climate tech got USD 35M (7 deals) in 2025. [VN-direct] Medium. CAP-20, CAP-21.
8. **Consumer staples private equity hit a ten-year high (USD 1.2B in 2025), but there were zero technology IPOs in 2025 or the four years before.** For a food or ingredient company, a realistic exit is a trade sale to a strategic or a PE buyout, not an IPO. [VN-direct] Medium. CAP-21, CAP-22.
9. **New state venture framework.** Decree 264/2025/ND-CP (in force 14 October 2025) lets national and provincial venture funds take equity, act as fund-of-funds, accept up to 50% portfolio loss per investment cycle, and run 10 to 15 year cycles. Managers are exempt from liability for "objective risk" losses. The national fund (minimum VND 500bn initial state capital, VND 2,000bn target within five years) was still at proposal stage in June 2026. [VN-direct] High (decree text). CAP-29, CAP-30, CAP-31.
10. **Local funds are small and just starting.** Ho Chi Minh City's HCM VIF has VND 500bn (~USD 19.7M; 40% state), lists biotechnology as a priority, and plans its first one or two investments in Q4 2026. Hanoi's HVCF has VND 230bn (47.8% state) and caps each investment at 5% of the fund (about VND 11.5bn, or USD 0.45M). High-tech agriculture and biotechnology are among its 12 priority areas. [VN-direct] Medium. CAP-32 to CAP-35.
11. **NATIF's money suits established firms, not startups.** The 2026 Round 1 interest subsidy (50% of loan interest, capped at 6% a year, for up to five years; VND 50bn budget) closed on 20 September 2026. It requires a bank loan and a profit in the last year. NATIF now operates under Decree 77/2026/ND-CP. [VN-direct] Medium. CAP-37, CAP-38.
12. **The largest alt-protein R&D grant open to Vietnamese applicants in 2026 has now closed.** Coefficient Giving's Alternative Protein R&D RFP offered up to USD 30M (USD 0.1M to 5M per award, for-profits eligible, international applicants welcome). Round 1 closed 10 August and Round 2 on 15 September 2026. GFI's Research Grant Program is closed with no reopening date. No Navigation Fund alt-protein RFP could be found. [general] High. CAP-50, CAP-57, CAP-58.
13. **Global alt-protein investment is at a cyclical low and has shifted towards fermentation.** Totals were USD 881M in 2025 (plant-based 450, fermentation 357, cultivated 74) and USD 359M in H1 2026. In Q2 2026, fermentation took about USD 132M of roughly USD 197M. The cumulative total since 2017 is over USD 19.5B. [general] High (GFI page, with an internal inconsistency; see disagreements). CAP-51.
14. **Agrifoodtech overall is flat, not collapsing, and money is moving upstream.** Global funding was USD 16.2B in 2025 (down 3%). Upstream (farm, food production, biological systems) took USD 9B (up 7%) even as deal count fell 12%. Asia-Pacific raised USD 4.2B in January to October 2024 (up 38%, 31% of the global total). [general / VN-adjacent] Medium. CAP-53, CAP-54.
15. **DFIs: IFC is the only DFI with a direct Vietnam alt-protein ticket (Entobel, USD 2.5M).** Its Vietnam agribusiness tickets run from USD 20M to 90M (TTC AgriS USD 40M facility in 2023; Mavin USD 90.6M proposed in 2021), and it bought Vietnam's first blue bond (SeABank, USD 25M, 2024). Proparco reaches Vietnamese consumer and food businesses indirectly, as a USD 15M LP in Mekong Enterprise Fund IV (and so indirectly in Entobel). ADB Ventures (USD 0.1M to 4M tickets) has backed insect protein and microbial oils elsewhere in Asia but has no Vietnam agrifood deal. [VN-direct] High. CAP-04, CAP-41, CAP-43, CAP-44, CAP-45 to CAP-47.
16. **The benchmark lesson.** Vietnam's first industrial alt-protein asset (Entobel, 10,000 t/yr in Ba Ria-Vung Tau, now part of Ho Chi Minh City) was a feed play built with local PE plus DFI support, not venture capital. IFC said in 2023 that private investors were cautious because insect meal had not proven it could compete on scale and price with fishmeal and soy. [VN-direct] Medium. CAP-06, CAP-08.

### 5. Government capital

- **Decree 264/2025/ND-CP** (signed and effective 14 October 2025) on national and local venture funds. National fund: MOST represents the state's capital; at least VND 500bn from the development budget at the start; at least VND 2,000bn within five years including mobilised capital. Investment forms: direct equity, fund-of-funds, co-investment. Loss threshold: 50% of charter capital across the portfolio per investment cycle (Art. 10.4.a). Cycle: 10 years, or 15 for strategic or dual-use tech (Art. 10.1.c). Managers are exempt from liability for losses from "objective risks" (Art. 14). Priority sectors include green and circular economy (Art. 5.3). Local funds invest only in Vietnam, and the provincial People's Committee sets the state share (CAP-29, CAP-30). In June 2026, MOST was still preparing the national fund proposal and preferred a fund-of-funds model (CAP-31).
- **Ho Chi Minh City: HCM VIF.** VND 500bn (VND 200bn state, VND 300bn private), with a target of VND 5 trillion by 2035. It plans to back 50 to 150 startups in 2026 to 2035, had received about 50 proposals, and planned its first one or two disbursements in Q4 2026. Founding private investors include Sovico, Vingroup, VinaCapital, Becamex IDC, VNG, CT Group, Hoa Sen, Lotte Ventures Vietnam and FPT. Priority areas include biotechnology (CAP-32, CAP-33).
- **Hanoi: HVCF.** Established 22 April 2026 (Decisions 2145, 2146 and 2147/QD-UBND). Charter capital VND 230bn, which can rise to VND 600bn. The state share is VND 110bn (47.8%) and is capped at 49%. Each investment is capped at 5% of the fund. The fund has a 10-year life with a review at year 5. Priorities include biotechnology and high-tech agriculture (CAP-34, CAP-35).
- **NATIF** (National Technology Innovation Fund). Reorganised under Decree 77/2026/ND-CP (17 March 2026). It funds technology innovation, IP, startups and interest subsidies. 2026 Round 1 interest subsidy: VND 50bn budget; 50% of interest, capped at 6% a year; up to five years; the borrower must have been profitable last year and have had no bad debt in 24 months; deadline 20 September 2026; partner banks include VPBank and BIDV (CAP-37, CAP-38).
- **Credit.** A bank-funded preferential programme of VND 500 trillion (2025 to 2030; VND 100 trillion in 2025 to 2026) covers strategic infrastructure, high technology and innovation through 21 banks, with no agriculture-specific window found (CAP-40). Decree 156/2025 (in force 1 July 2025) raises unsecured lending limits for agriculture and gives organic and circular agriculture the same credit treatment as high-tech agriculture (CAP-39).
- **Decree 182/2024 Investment Support Fund:** link only per the brief. Not verified by CAPITAL. Its high thresholds make it irrelevant to startups. See the regulation workstream.
- **NIC programmes:** the NIC site did not render. The only NIC product used here is the annual private capital report (co-published with VPCA and Do Ventures). Gap.

### 8. APAC alt-protein and agrifoodtech trends

- Global alt protein: USD 881M in 2025; USD 359M in H1 2026; about USD 197M in Q2 2026, of which fermentation took USD 132M, plant-based USD 41M and cultivated USD 25M (CAP-51). The "since 2017" total is over USD 19.5B. The funding climate is "fundamentally different" from before 2022 (CAP-51). Synthesis Capital says valuations "have reset" (CAP-64).
- Agrifoodtech: USD 16B in 2024 (down 4%) and USD 16.2B in 2025 (down 3%). Upstream got USD 9B in 2025 (up 7%). Investors want "tangible science, real unit economics, and clear paths to revenue" (CAP-53). APAC: USD 4.2B in January to October 2024, up 38%, or 31% of global (CAP-54). Developing markets: up 63% in 2023 to 2024, with Vietnam among the markets where the leading ecosystem grew (no Vietnamese figure given; CAP-55).
- APAC-specific alt-protein totals for 2024 and 2025 (GFI APAC) could not be extracted; the report sits behind a download form (CAP-52). Gap.
- Direction: fermentation (biomass and precision) and upstream ingredients still get funded, while downstream consumer plant-based brands and cultivated meat get much less. This matches the "feed and ingredients first" logic in the prior leads, but in Vietnam that logic has so far been funded only for insects.

## Implications by audience

**VCs and investors**
- There is no track record of venture exits in Vietnamese alt protein, and agritech VC was about USD 1M in 2025. A thesis has to rest on B2B feed or ingredient revenue and a strategic buyer, not on a consumer brand exit.
- The Entobel structure (local PE lead, local co-investor, small DFI upstream ticket, strategic offtake with a seafood exporter) is the only proven template. Replicating it needs a company that can show a path to USD 20M+ of capex.

**Policy makers**
- Decree 264 (50% loss tolerance) and the HCMC and Hanoi funds create the first state equity route for biotech and high-tech agriculture. Their ticket sizes (Hanoi about USD 0.45M per deal) fit pilot plants, not production. Naming "alternative protein / biomanufacturing" as an explicit priority in fund investment rules would cost little.
- NATIF's interest subsidy only reaches profitable borrowers with bank loans. A grant or first-loss window for pre-revenue pilot-scale fermentation would fill the gap the market leaves.

**Startups and founders**
- Realistic first cheques: angels and family offices (the Cricket One pattern), Better Bite and Big Idea (USD 100K to 200K), ADB Ventures (up to USD 4M, climate lens), and in 2026 to 2027 the HCMC and Hanoi funds. Plan around episodic global grants (Coefficient Giving, GFI) and a Singapore holding company, as Entobel did.

**F&B and feed manufacturers**
- NATIF's interest subsidy and the Decree 156 circular-agriculture credit treatment are the cheapest state money for an incumbent adding an alt-protein or side-stream line. Vinh Hoan's early bets show that Vietnamese exporters can hold options on future protein through minority stakes abroad.

**Academia**
- Coefficient Giving's topics (fish flavour characterisation, off-flavours) and ACIAR's commissioned model (Australian partner required) are the most accessible external research money. Vietnamese groups need an international co-applicant and a ready proposal before windows open.

**International bodies**
- IFC's USD 2.5M upstream model and ADB Ventures' insect and microbial-oil precedents can be transferred to Vietnamese side-stream protein. A DFI-backed "first plant" facility or a pooled first-loss tranche for fermentation feed protein would target the gap between grants and PE.
