# M2 Asset map: Vietnam feedstocks and side streams for protein (wave 1, agent FEEDSTOCK)

Edition: September 2026. Accessed dates: all sources accessed 2026-09-23.
Data files in this folder: `feedstocks.csv` (29 rows, one per stream) and `feedstock_prices.csv` (32 price observations).

Terms used:
- **DM** = dry matter. **CP** = crude protein. **Wet** = as produced, before drying.
- **Derived** = we calculated it from a sourced ratio; the ratio and its source are always shown.
- Province names are the post-1 July 2025 names (Resolution 202/2025/QH15, FS-52), with the former province in brackets.
- Scope note: the web-search budget for this session ran out part-way through the wave. Cassava, rice, sugar, beer, soy and ethanol were researched properly. Seaweed, spirulina, duckweed, mushrooms, cashew, coffee, jackfruit, pulses and gas/CO2 were only covered through the peer-reviewed literature tool and are thin. These are listed as gaps, not as findings.

---

## Headline findings

1. **Cassava is Vietnam's largest processable carbohydrate asset, and nearly half of it is imported.** Domestic harvest was 10.24 Mt fresh roots on 499,100 ha in 2025 (down 18,600 ha from 2024; yield 20.5 t/ha). Total factory supply is above 18 Mt a year, of which about 42% (about 8 Mt) comes from Cambodia and Laos. [VN-direct; High for domestic, Medium for imports] (FS-01, FS-14)

2. **Cassava pulp (bã sắn / bã mì) is about 6 Mt wet a year (about 1.3 Mt dry matter) in 2025. This is derived, not measured.** Arithmetic: 2.49 Mt starch (11-month exports of 2.28 Mt scaled to 12 months) x 2.5 t wet pulp per t starch (Thai material balance, 4.4 t roots per t starch, pulp 79-80% moisture). That equals 0.57 t wet, or 0.12 t DM, per t of roots processed. Plausible range 4 to 8 Mt wet. The prior-draft figure of 1.7 to 2.1 Mt wet is too low unless it refers to pressed or partly dried pulp. No government body publishes this number. [VN-adjacent ratio on VN-direct volumes; Low] (FS-01, FS-05, FS-06, FS-53)

3. **Cassava pulp is a carbon source, not a protein source.** Crude protein is 1.5 to 2.6% of DM, starch 37 to 75% of DM, moisture 77 to 85%. All the pulp in Vietnam contains only about 20 to 35 kt of protein. Any protein route needs added nitrogen (fermentation). [VN-adjacent; High for composition] (FS-05, FS-06, FS-17, FS-18, FS-20)

4. **Cassava is no longer cheap.** By April 2026, fresh-root prices were 3,000 to 3,550 VND/kg (30% starch) in Tay Ninh, Dak Lak, Quang Ngai and Gia Lai, about 75% above April 2025. Starch was USD 520 to 540/t FOB Ho Chi Minh City, up from USD 405 to 415/t in January 2026. The causes: farmers left cassava after low 2025 prices, leaf-spot disease, and Cambodian supply running out. [VN-direct; Medium] (FS-11, FS-12, FS-01)

5. **Fuel ethanol is a new competitor for cassava.** E10 gasoline has been sold nationwide since 1 June 2026. Three ethanol plants now run at a combined 830 m3/day (about 277,000 m3/yr): Dung Quat (Quang Ngai) 330 m3/day, the Quang Nam plant (now Da Nang) 300 m3/day and Dong Nai 200 m3/day. Dung Quat is moving part of its feedstock to imported corn from Q3 2026. Quang Ngai Sugar (QNS) plans a 200 m3/day plant at An Khe (Gia Lai) for Q1 2028. [VN-direct; High] (FS-09, FS-10)

6. **The starch industry is large and concentrated, but the factory count is disputed.** Estimates are about 100 industrial mills (MOIT, 2021), about 120 in 27 provinces with 11.3 Mt design and 8.62 Mt actual root capacity (ASEAN Cassava Centre, 2023), and "150+" (Vietnam Cassava Association, October 2025). Tay Ninh alone has 65 to 68 factories and 6.4 Mt/yr root capacity. That makes Tay Ninh the obvious place to site a pulp-based process. [VN-direct; Medium] (FS-02, FS-03, FS-04, FS-07)

7. **Vietnam became China's largest cassava-starch supplier in 2025.** Starch exports were 2.28 Mt in the first 11 months (+70%), a 48.8% share of China's imports after 10 months, ahead of Thailand. Total cassava exports were 3.99 Mt worth USD 1.27 bn in 2025. Because pulp scales with starch output, pulp volume swings from year to year: the 2024 equivalent was only about 3.7 Mt wet. [VN-direct; High] (FS-01, FS-13)

8. **Rice bran is a traded commodity, not a free residue.** 2025 paddy was 43.5 Mt (NSO). Bran is about 4.35 Mt (derived at 10% of paddy). In 2025, 1.2 Mt of bran was exported (double 2024, mostly to China), and about 2.8 Mt of bran plus broken rice went into domestic feed. The An Giang price on 19 September 2026 was 7,300 to 7,650 VND/kg. Full-fat bran has 11 to 17% protein and 12 to 24% oil. Only a small share is de-oiled: in 2018, Calofic and Uni-Bran together took about 10% of Mekong bran. [VN-direct; Medium] (FS-25, FS-27, FS-23, FS-26, FS-29, FS-56, FS-57)

9. **Broken rice is priced close to rice.** In 2025, 0.87 Mt of 100% broken rice was exported (to Africa). On 19 September 2026, fragrant broken rice sold for 8,500 to 8,600 VND/kg in An Giang, and 100% broken for USD 368 to 372/t FOB, against USD 440 to 445/t for 5%-broken rice. Broken rice is a food-grade starch at a commodity price. The total volume of broken rice is not published. [VN-direct; Medium] (FS-23, FS-26)

10. **Sugar is in surplus, and molasses is roughly 0.55 to 0.72 Mt (derived).** The 2025/26 crop crushed 13.3 Mt of cane for 1.298 Mt of sugar. There are about 25 mills with 124,000 t cane/day design capacity. Domestic refined sugar was about 16,505 VND/kg in mid-2026, a three-year low and well below regional prices. The molasses figure uses 42 to 54 kg molasses per t cane, taken from Indian alcohol-yield ratios. No bulk molasses price was found. [VN-direct volumes; general ratio; Low for molasses] (FS-31, FS-33, FS-34, FS-37)

11. **Brewer's spent grain (BSG) is at most 0.8 to 0.9 Mt wet a year, from very few sellers.** Derived: more than 4 to 4.4 billion litres of beer x 20 kg wet BSG per 100 L. That is at most about 160 to 176 kt DM and 32 to 35 kt of protein (about 20% of DM). The true figure is lower because Vietnamese lagers use rice adjunct. Heineken (43%) and Sabeco (33.9%) held about 77% of the 2023 market, so a buyer deals with a handful of counterparties. Fresh BSG spoils in 2 to 3 days. [VN-adjacent ratio; Low] (FS-38, FS-39, FS-40, FS-41, FS-42)

12. **Soy protein is almost entirely imported.** Vietnam grew 42 kt of soybeans on 26,000 ha in MY2024/25 and imported 2.61 Mt in 2025 (Brazil 45.8%, US 43.5%). Domestic crush is rising from 2.5 Mt (MY2025/26) to 3.0 Mt (MY2026/27). The feed sector needs about 7.4 Mt of soybean meal. Soy for food is about 550 kt, so okara is at most about 0.6 to 0.66 Mt wet (derived, 1.1 to 1.2 kg wet okara per kg bean). [VN-direct; High for soy, Low for okara] (FS-24, FS-44, FS-45)

13. **Feed ingredients are about 82% imported.** USDA's 2025 feed-ingredient table shows 23.57 Mt imported out of 28.62 Mt. Domestic supply is 5.05 Mt: rice bran and broken rice 2.8 Mt, corn 1.7 Mt and cassava 0.55 Mt. Imports include 9.2 Mt corn, 7.2 Mt soybean meal (including local crush of imported beans), 1.55 Mt DDGS and 2.58 Mt feed wheat. [VN-direct; High] (FS-23)

14. **Seafood processing creates large, protein-rich by-product streams, but we could not size them.** Aquaculture output was 6.12 Mt in 2025. Vietnamese pangasius filleting uses 3.1 kg fish per kg fillet, so about 68% of the fish is by-product. Shrimp side streams are 45 to 50% of shrimp weight, and shrimp heads carry up to 65% protein on a DM basis. These streams are animal-origin benchmarks and competitors, not alt-protein feedstocks. The often-quoted "~1 Mt/yr" total was not verified. [VN-adjacent; Low] (FS-25, FS-48, FS-49, FS-50)

15. **Coffee pulp is an overlooked Central Highlands stream.** About 450 kt of dried coffee pulp a year (2016 basis) is burned in dryers or used as fertiliser. Pulp is about 28% of dry berry weight, and caffeine (0.5 to 1.3%) limits its use as feed. [VN-direct; Low] (FS-46)

16. **The novel aquatic and fungal biomasses cannot yet be sized from public data.** No reliable national volumes were found for seaweed (Kappaphycus, Gracilaria, Caulerpa, Sargassum), spirulina, duckweed/Wolffia, mushrooms or spent mushroom substrate. For duckweed, we found no evidence of commercial cultivation, only feed trials. Any plan built on these streams starts from primary data collection. [VN-direct; Low] (FS-51, FS-54, FS-55)

Main producing and processing provinces (current name, former name in brackets): Tay Ninh (Tay Ninh; about 60,000 to 61,000 ha, more than 2 Mt a year, second nationally before the merger), Gia Lai (Gia Lai, plus Binh Dinh for the Quy Nhon export port), Dak Lak (Dak Lak), Quang Ngai (Quang Ngai, Kon Tum), Dong Nai (Binh Phuoc), Son La, Thanh Hoa. Sources: FS-03, FS-07, FS-11, FS-16, FS-22. We did not find a ranked provincial table, so shares are not given.

Seasonality: the main harvest runs from about October/November to April. By late March 2026, 70 to 80% of the area had been harvested (FS-12). The starch campaign follows the harvest. Many factories run below capacity outside the season, which is the main reason actual throughput (8.62 Mt) sits well below design capacity (11.3 Mt) (FS-02).

**Starch industry**

- Factory count: ~100 industrial mills (MOIT, 2021; FS-04). ~120 industrial factories in 27 provinces with 11.3 Mt design and 8.62 Mt actual root capacity (ASEAN Cassava Centre, January 2023; FS-02). "150+" factories averaging 300 to 350 t roots/day (Vietnam Cassava Association, October 2025; FS-07). Tay Ninh: 68 factories with 6.4 Mt/yr root capacity (December 2024; FS-03) and 65 factories (October 2025; FS-07). There are also over 4,000 small units (FS-17, citing older data).
- Examples: Dinh Khue (Tan Chau, Tay Ninh), 280 t roots/day (FS-03). Son La Starch Factory: 100,000 t roots in, 30,000 t starch out planned for the 2019/20 season, with dried pulp sold to feed mills in former Ha Nam (now Ninh Binh) and former Bac Giang (now Bac Ninh) (FS-22). Long Giang Thinh: over 50,000 t roots/yr (FS-04). Vina Thanh Hoa (Ngoc Lac, Thanh Hoa) sells dried pulp (FS-21). FOCOCEV (Binh Phuoc, now Dong Nai) supplied pulp to a pelleting trial (FS-17). Hung Duy Group (Tay Ninh) is quoted in FS-07.
- Exports: 2.28 Mt starch worth USD 809.9 M in 11 months of 2025 (+69.8% volume) (FS-01). Total cassava exports were 3.99 Mt and USD 1.27 bn in 2025 (FS-01). China took 92.5 to 94% (FS-01, FS-12, FS-13). Q1 2026: 1.06 Mt, USD 359.4 M, of which starch was 64% of volume (FS-12).
- Domestic starch use (MSG, paper, food, modified starch) is not quantified in anything we read. This is the largest uncertainty in the pulp estimate.

**Cassava pulp: resolving the tonnage question**

Published ratios disagree because they use different moisture bases:

| Ratio | Basis | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.5 t wet pulp per t starch; 4.4 t roots per t starch; pulp 79.4% moisture | Thai factory material balance | FS-05, FS-06, FS-53 | Most transparent. Gives 0.57 t wet or 0.12 t DM per t roots |
| Thailand national: 9.5 Mt pulp and 21 million m3 wastewater a year | national | FS-06 | Consistent with the ratio above |
| 20 to 30% of root weight | review | FS-20 | Moisture basis not stated |
| 10 to 15% of root weight | Laos factory; also cited in VN paper | FS-18, FS-17 | Likely a pressed or DM basis |
| 30 to 100 t roots/day gives 12 to 48 t pulp (0.40 to 0.48) | VN, 2005 | FS-16 | Wet basis |
| >30,000 t pulp from >50,000 t roots (0.6) | VN factory | FS-04 | Wet basis |
| "Pulp = 45% of processing output"; Dinh Khue 3,500 t pulp/yr from 280 t/day roots | VN factory | FS-03 | The 3,500 t figure is far too low for wet pulp, so it is probably dried pulp |

Our position: use the Thai material balance at about 80% moisture. It is the only one with a stated basis, and it agrees with the Vietnamese wet-basis factory figures (0.40 to 0.60). Stated as dry matter, it is about 0.12 t DM per t of roots, which also agrees with the "10 to 15% of root weight" figures if those are on a dry or pressed basis.

Derivation for 2025:
- Starch route: 2.28 Mt exports in 11 months x 12/11 = 2.49 Mt starch. x 2.5 = **6.2 Mt wet pulp**, about 1.28 Mt DM.
- Root route (cross-check): 8.62 Mt actual processing (2023) x 0.57 = 4.9 Mt wet. 13 Mt roots to starch x 0.57 = 7.4 Mt wet.
- 2024 equivalent: 11-month starch exports were about 1.34 Mt (2.28/1.698), about 1.47 Mt for the full year, giving about 3.7 Mt wet.
- Biases: domestic starch use would push the total up. Any re-export of starch made in Cambodia or Laos would push it down. Factories that press pulp to about 65% moisture (FS-17) cut the wet tonnage but not the DM.
- Our number: about 6 Mt wet (range 4 to 8 Mt), about 1.0 to 1.6 Mt DM, in 2025. Confidence Low. It is derived, and the ratio comes from Thailand.

Composition (DM basis unless stated): starch 37 to 75%; cellulose 4 to 25%; hemicellulose 4 to 13%; lignin 1 to 7%; CP 1.5 to 2.6% (one Vietnamese press report says 0.2% wet, which is consistent); ash 2 to 12%; energy about 13 MJ/kg DM (FS-03, FS-05, FS-06, FS-18, FS-19, FS-20). A commercial dried-pulp specification: starch 40 to 50%, moisture 10 to 16%, fibre 14 to 20% (FS-21). Fresh pulp moisture is 77 to 85% (FS-05, FS-17, FS-19).

Current use and buyers: fresh pulp is sold to cattle and pig farms, or sun- or drum-dried and sold to feed mills (for example from Son La to northern feed mills). It is also pelleted, fed to biogas digesters for boiler heat, or used as fertiliser (FS-03, FS-17, FS-22). Storage: fresh pulp ferments on its own in open pits (pH 3.2 to 3.5) and kept its feed value (<9% energy loss) for up to 4 years in a Lao study; only the top 50 cm spoiled (FS-18).

Price: we found no current published gate price for pulp. The only 2024 signal is an implied value: Dinh Khue earns 6 bn VND a year from 3,500 t of pulp, about 1,700 VND/kg, form not stated (FS-03). The last explicit quotes (2005) were 200 VND/kg wet and 800 to 1,000 VND/kg dry (FS-16). A July 2026 cattle-feed reference puts "other by-products (cassava, pumpkin, cane tops)" at 2,000 to 5,000 VND/kg delivered (FS-30). The Vietnam Cassava Association site, which publishes weekly price bulletins, could not be fetched (TLS certificate error).

**Peel, rhizomes, wastewater** (derived, Low): the Thai ratio per t starch is 100 kg peel, 600 kg rhizomes and 170 kg sand (FS-53). At 2.49 Mt starch that gives about 0.25 Mt peel and 1.5 Mt rhizomes. A Vietnamese factory reports over 5,000 t peel from over 50,000 t roots, and over 100,000 m3 wastewater a year, about 2 m3 per t of roots (FS-04). Wastewater is mostly sent to biogas lagoons. This biogas is a possible methane source for gas-fermentation protein, but it is not quantified nationally.

**Chips and ethanol**
- Chip exports were 967,460 t in 10 months of 2025 (FS-13) or 955,670 t in 11 months (FS-01); see the disagreements log. The domestic chip price was 5,800 to 6,200 VND/kg at end-Q1 2026 (FS-12). FOB Quy Nhon was USD 260/t (China) to 315/t (Korea) in April 2026 (FS-11). Conversion: 2.5 t fresh roots per t dry chips (FS-08).
- Ethanol: see headline 5. One source says Vietnam has "6 ethanol plants" (FS-08). Another lists the 3 now operating (FS-09). Our position: 6 were built and 3 operate. June 2026 output of the three plants was 20,875 m3 (FS-09). Stillage and distillers' grains volumes are not published. Dung Quat's research plans include low-carbon methanol from captured CO2 (FS-09), a possible CO2/methanol feedstock lead.
- Cassava in compound feed: 550 kt in CY2025, forecast 645 kt in CY2027 (FS-23).

- Bran oil: Calofic (Can Tho extraction plant with Mekong collection points, since 2004) and Uni-Bran (since 2012). In 2018 they handled about 10% of Mekong bran, and domestic bran-oil consumption was about 7,700 t (2017) (FS-29). This is old; a 2025-2026 update is needed. Several press items mention new rice-oil plants (for example a 100 t/day plant, baodautu), not verified.
- Composition: full-fat bran 11 to 17% CP, 12 to 22% fat, 6 to 11% fibre, 5 to 15% moisture. Defatted bran about 13.8% CP (FS-56). Full-fat bran sampled from Vietnam had lower fat and amino-acid levels than Australian or Thai bran, which suggests partial defatting or dilution in trade (FS-57).
- Prices (19 September 2026, An Giang and Mekong Delta; FS-26): bran 7,300 to 7,650 VND/kg; fragrant broken rice (grade 3-4) 8,500 to 8,600 VND/kg; fresh paddy 5,700 to 6,200 VND/kg; FOB 100% broken USD 368 to 372/t; FOB 5% broken USD 440 to 445/t.
- Broken rice uses: export to Africa (FS-23), feed, rice flour and noodles, beer adjunct. The split was not found.
- Straw: not researched this wave. The prior-draft figure of 43 to 47 Mt is ratio-based and unverified. Straw is the main substrate for straw mushrooms.
- Main provinces (Mekong Delta): An Giang (An Giang, Kien Giang), Dong Thap (Dong Thap, Tien Giang), Can Tho (Can Tho, Soc Trang, Hau Giang), Vinh Long (Vinh Long, Tra Vinh, Ben Tre), Tay Ninh (Long An), Ca Mau (Bac Lieu). This is regional knowledge; province shares were not sourced.

### 3. Sugarcane (mía đường)

- 2025/26 crop: 13.3 Mt cane crushed, 1.298 Mt sugar; most mills finished by end-June 2026 (FS-31, FS-33). 2024/25: 12.4 Mt cane, 1.3 Mt sugar (FS-32). Sugar output has risen four seasons running, to about 180% of 2020/21 (FS-33). About 220,000 farm households (FS-33).
- Mills: 25 operating with 124,000 t cane/day design capacity (2023/24; FS-34). Gia Lai had over 40,000 ha of cane and its mills made 215,000 t sugar. Former Phu Yen (now Dak Lak) had over 26,000 ha (FS-34). QNS (An Khe, Gia Lai) is expanding its bagasse power plant to 135 MW and targets 40,000 ha of cane by 2027/28 (FS-35).
- Market: refined sugar about 16,505 VND/kg in June 2026, a three-year low, against 19,600 to 27,300 VND/kg in China, Indonesia and the Philippines. There is a supply surplus, and HFCS imports were 111,839 t in H1 2026 (FS-31, FS-33). Implication: cheap domestic sugar is itself a candidate fermentation carbon source.
- Molasses: 0.55 to 0.72 Mt in 2025/26 and 0.52 to 0.67 Mt in 2024/25 (derived). Ratio: 10 to 13 L of alcohol per t cane via molasses, divided by 240 L alcohol per t molasses, gives 42 to 54 kg molasses per t cane (Indian data; FS-37). [general; Low]
- Molasses users: MSG and yeast plants, alcohol, feed (from prior leads, not verified). QNS planned a molasses-ethanol plant of 60 M litres/yr targeted for 2026 (FS-35). The later government report gives an An Khe ethanol plant of 200 m3/day for Q1 2028 (FS-09), which is probably the same project delayed.
- Molasses price: only a retail price for 35-kg cans (about 9,970 VND/kg, December 2023; FS-36). The bulk mill-gate price is a gap. The prior lead that Vietnam is a net molasses importer (2022) was not verified.
- Bagasse is burned for power at mills (FS-35). Vinasse was not researched.

### 4. Brewer's spent grain (bã bia)

- Beer volume: over 4 billion litres a year (VIRAC, 2022, cited in FS-40); 4.4 billion litres in 2019 (FS-41). **No 2024 or 2025 volume was found.** The 2019 figure may overstate current volumes (sales fell after 2019 per FS-41's value series: USD 7.6 bn in 2019, 4.6 bn in 2021); check against NSO output data.
- Market shares (2023): Heineken 43%, Sabeco 33.9%, Carlsberg 9.2%, Habeco 7.5%. There are 98 active breweries, most of them microbreweries (FS-41).
- BSG ratio: about 20 kg wet BSG per 100 L beer at about 80% moisture. Protein is about 20% and fibre about 70% of DM (FS-38, FS-39).
- Derived: 4.0 to 4.4 billion L x 0.20 kg/L = **0.80 to 0.88 Mt wet**, 160 to 176 kt DM, 32 to 35 kt protein. Treat this as an upper bound: Vietnamese mass-market lagers use rice adjunct, which lowers the malt grist and so the BSG per litre. The adjunct share was not found.
- Buyers: cattle and pig farms and feed traders (FS-42). Entobel (black soldier fly, factory in Dat Do industrial zone, now Ho Chi Minh City, former Ba Ria-Vung Tau) is said in prior drafts to use Heineken BSG. Its website does not mention feedstock (FS-43), so this is unverified.
- Price: one Hanoi retailer quotes 6,000 to 6,500 VND/kg fresh and about 5,500 VND/kg dried (March 2026; FS-42). This is implausible on a dry-matter basis: it is close to the price of dry rice bran for a product that is about 80% water. Treat it as a retail small-lot price, not a brewery gate price. Low.
- Storage: fresh BSG must be used within 2 to 3 days, or ensiled with salt (FS-42).
- Brewer's spent yeast is a second brewery stream. A Vietnamese group (VNU-HCM) is working on debittering it for yeast extract (FS-40). Volume unknown.

### 5. Soy (đậu tương / đậu nành)

- Domestic: 26,000 ha and 42,000 t (MY2024/25); forecast 41,000 t (MY2025/26) and 40,000 t (MY2026/27). Farmers are switching to fruit and vegetables (FS-24).
- Imports: 2.61 Mt in 2025 (Brazil 45.8%, US 43.5%); forecast 3.2 Mt (MY2025/26) and 3.8 Mt (MY2026/27) (FS-24).
- Crush: 2.5 Mt (MY2025/26), 3.0 Mt (MY2026/27). Crushers added new lines in north and south in Q1 2026; USDA does not name them (FS-24).
- Soybean meal: feed demand 7.4 Mt (MY2025/26) and 7.8 Mt (MY2026/27); imports steady at about 5.7 Mt (FS-24). The cattle-feed reference price is 9,000 to 11,000 VND/kg (July 2026, Low; FS-30).
- Food use: 550 kt (MY2025/26), 570 kt (MY2026/27) (FS-24). This covers tofu, soymilk, sprouts, sauces and more.
- Okara: 1.1 to 1.2 kg wet okara per kg dry soybean for tofu or soymilk; moisture 79 to 84%; up to 40% protein in DM, most commonly 25 to 35% (FS-44, FS-45). Derived upper bound: 550 kt x 1.1 to 1.2 = **605 to 660 kt wet** (about 120 to 130 kt DM). The real figure is lower because not all food soy is made into tofu or soymilk. Current use: pig and cattle feed, or discarded; no price found.
- Vinasoy (a QNS subsidiary) and other soymilk makers: capacities were not verified this wave.

### 7. Seafood by-products and aquaculture effluent

- Output 2025: aquaculture 6.12 Mt (+5.1%), capture 3.83 Mt (-0.2%), total 9.95 Mt (FS-25).
- Pangasius: 3.1 kg of whole fish per kg of fillet in Vietnam and Brazil (FS-48), so about 68% of the fish (heads, frames, skin, viscera, belly fat) is by-product. Indonesian data put by-products at 51 to 55% (FS-48 context).
- Shrimp: side streams are 45 to 50% of shrimp weight, about 70% head and 30% shell. Shrimp head is up to 65% protein, 21% ash and 18% chitin on a DM basis. Seafood discards average 60% protein, 19% fat and 22% ash on a DM basis (FS-49, FS-50).
- National volumes of pangasius and shrimp harvest, and of by-products, were not verified this wave. Current uses (fishmeal, fish oil, pangasius collagen and gelatin, shrimp head meal, chitin) come from prior leads.
- Pangasius pond area and nutrient loads (for duckweed or algae growing on effluent) were not found.

### 8. Aquatic biomass

- Seaweed: Vietnam has 827 recorded seaweed species. The economically important, cultivated or harvested genera are Caulerpa (sea grapes), Sargassum, Gracilaria, Ulva and Kappaphycus, concentrated in Khanh Hoa (Khanh Hoa, Ninh Thuan) (FS-51). No production volume was found. A national seaweed development scheme to 2030 is a lead (not verified).
- Spirulina: only the prior lead (Vinh Hao, former Binh Thuan, now Lam Dong; 5,000 m2 on mineral water). Not verified.
- Duckweed / Wolffia (bèo tấm, bèo trứng / rau bèo): one South Vietnam poultry trial replaced 7% of the diet with Lemna minor. Wolffia is sold commercially as human food in Thailand, among other countries (FS-54). No Vietnamese commercial cultivation found. Azolla not researched.

### 9. Mushrooms

- National output was not verified (prior lead: 250 to 270 kt/yr). General ratio: about 3 kg of spent substrate per kg of mushrooms (FS-55). Substrates are rice straw (straw mushroom) and sawdust (oyster, wood-ear). Vietnamese trials reuse spent oyster sawdust for straw mushroom (FS-55, citing Luu et al. 2022).

### 10. Others (one line each)

- Ethanol stillage and rice-liquor distillers' grains: not quantified. The three cassava/corn ethanol plants (about 277,000 m3/yr) will generate stillage, and Dung Quat's move to corn could produce a DDGS-like stream (FS-09).
- MSG/lysine fermentation residues (Vedan, Ajinomoto): not researched.
- Fruit and vegetable processing waste: not researched.
- Natural gas, biogas, CO2: cassava-starch wastewater biogas is widespread but unquantified (FS-04, FS-06). Dung Quat is studying methanol from captured CO2 (FS-09). Natural gas supply not researched.

## Implications by audience

**VCs, impact investors, DFIs**
- The volume thesis for "cheap cassava residue" holds (about 6 Mt wet pulp a year, clustered in Tay Ninh), but the price thesis is weaker than earlier drafts assumed. Root prices roughly doubled in a year, E10 has created a new buyer for cassava, and pulp is already sold to farms. Model pulp at a positive price (the only signal is an implied ~1,700 VND/kg) and stress-test for cassava price cycles.
- Year-to-year pulp volume depends on the Chinese starch export market (2024 about 3.7 Mt vs 2025 about 6.2 Mt wet). Supply contracts need multi-factory sourcing or pulp storage (pulp ensiles well in pits).

**Policy makers (MAE, MOIT, provinces)**
- Nobody publishes national data on starch output, pulp, BSG, okara, molasses or seafood by-products. A one-page annual by-product return for starch factories, breweries, sugar mills and seafood processors (through MAE or provincial Departments of Agriculture and Environment) would make the circular-economy and alt-protein cases investable.
- Tay Ninh (65 to 68 starch factories) is a natural pilot province for a by-product valorisation cluster.

**Startups and founders**
- For cassava-pulp fermentation, start in Tay Ninh. Negotiate per-tonne DM prices, not wet prices, and budget for nitrogen: pulp has almost no protein.
- BSG comes from essentially two groups (Heineken, Sabeco) plus Carlsberg and Habeco, spoils in 2 to 3 days, and is capped at under 0.9 Mt wet nationally. It suits one plant next to one brewery, not a national play.
- Surplus domestic sugar (16,500 VND/kg, three-year low) and broken rice (USD 368 to 372/t FOB) are clean, food-grade carbon sources to compare against residues.

**F&B and feed manufacturers**
- Local protein-rich side streams are small: BSG protein at most about 35 kt a year, okara at most about 40 kt of protein a year (derived), against 7.4 Mt of soybean meal demand. Local side streams cannot replace imported soybean meal at scale. Microbial conversion of carbohydrate streams (cassava, sugar, rice) is the only route with the volume to matter.
- Rice bran is increasingly exported to China (1.2 Mt in 2025). Expect upward pressure on domestic bran prices.

**Academia**
- Priority measurements: a Vietnamese pulp material balance (wet and DM per t roots across 10+ factories and seasons); okara and BSG composition from Vietnamese plants; molasses composition and flows; seaweed and duckweed productivity on Vietnamese sites.

**International bodies**
- A public by-product atlas for Vietnam (volumes, locations, prices, composition), updated yearly, is a fundable public good that no one currently provides.
