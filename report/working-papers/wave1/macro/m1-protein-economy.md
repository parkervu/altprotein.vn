# M1. Vietnam's protein economy: production, feed, import dependence, risks and outlook

Agent: MACRO (wave 1). Source prefix: MAC. Edition date: 23 September 2026.
Data files: `macro_indicators.csv` (87 rows), `feed_imports.csv` (24 rows).

Terms used:
- "Live weight" (thit hoi) is the weight of the live animal at slaughter. Vietnam's statistics office (NSO, formerly GSO) reports meat this way. "Carcass weight" (thit xe) is lower, about 45% to 78% of live weight depending on species.
- "CY" is calendar year. "MY" is a USDA marketing year. For Vietnam soybeans and soybean meal, USDA's "MY2024/25" runs January to December 2025, so it equals CY2025 (checked in the PSD table header, MAC-04).
- "Industrial feed" means compound feed from feed mills. Vietnamese official figures for industrial feed cover livestock and poultry only. Aquafeed is counted separately (see 3.1).
- MAE is the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment, formed in 2025 from MARD and MONRE. The livestock department is now the Department of Livestock Production and Animal Health (Cuc Chan nuoi va Thu y). Both names appear in the 2025/2026 sources read.

## Headline findings

1. **Meat.** In 2025 Vietnam produced 5.39 Mt of pork and 2.60 Mt of poultry meat (both live weight), plus 0.50 Mt of beef and 0.13 Mt of buffalo meat. Pork was 63% of live-weight meat output. Pork grew 3.9% and poultry 5.9% on 2024. [VN-direct, High, MAC-14, MAC-12]
2. **Eggs, milk, H1 2026.** In 2025 Vietnam produced 21.4 billion eggs (+5.3%) and 1.3 Mt of fresh milk (+5.5%). In H1 2026 pork output was 2.86 Mt (+4.8%) and poultry meat 1.3 Mt (+5.6%). [VN-direct, High, MAC-14, MAC-18]
3. **Fish.** Fisheries output reached 9.95 Mt in 2025: 6.12 Mt from aquaculture (+5.1%) and 3.83 Mt from capture (-0.2%). Farmed pangasius was 1.94 Mt, whiteleg shrimp 0.99 Mt and black tiger shrimp 0.30 Mt. [VN-direct, High, MAC-15]
4. **Feed output.** Vietnam's feed mills made about 22 Mt of livestock and poultry feed in 2025 (+3%), after 21.5 Mt in 2024. Pig feed was 55% and poultry feed 41% of the 2024 total. Output in H1 2026 was 12.43 Mt (+7.5%). [VN-direct, High for 2024/2025, Medium for H1 2026; MAC-01, MAC-28, MAC-03]
5. **Aquafeed is uncertain.** Estimates run from 3.9 to 4.8 Mt (industry estimate by species) to 6.5 Mt (USDA). The prior lead of 3.4 to 4.0 Mt matches only a capacity-times-utilisation calculation (5.5 Mt x 60 to 70%), not a production figure. [VN-direct, Low to Medium, MAC-06, MAC-01]
6. **Corn.** Vietnam imported 11.91 Mt of corn worth USD 2.95 bn in 2025. That is about 74% of domestic supply. Argentina supplied 46.5% and Brazil 34.8%. Domestic corn output is flat to falling at about 4.1 Mt on 810,000 ha. [VN-direct, High, MAC-08, MAC-01]
7. **Soy.** Vietnam imported 2.61 Mt of soybeans in 2025 (USD 1.21 bn), 98.4% of supply. Domestic output was only 42 to 43 kt. It also imported 5.70 Mt of soybean meal, 79% of meal supply. Domestic crushers make their meal from imported beans, so about 99% of the soy protein in Vietnamese feed comes from abroad. [VN-direct, High, MAC-04, MAC-09]
8. **Import bill.** Vietnam's gross import bill for feed grains and feed ingredients was about USD 10.2 bn in 2025. This is our sum of four customs lines: corn USD 2.95 bn, soybeans USD 1.21 bn, wheat USD 1.46 bn and the "animal feed and ingredients" group USD 4.58 bn. It includes some milling wheat and food soybeans. The livestock department's narrower 2024 figure is 22.4 Mt and USD 7.7 bn. [VN-direct, Medium, MAC-07, MAC-08, MAC-09, MAC-10, MAC-28]
9. **Share imported.** Official and industry sources put the imported share of feed raw materials at 65% to 85%, depending on definition. For the two ingredients that set feed cost, corn and soybean meal, dependence is 74% and about 99% respectively. [VN-direct, Medium, MAC-25, MAC-27, MAC-28]
10. **Feed cost.** Feed is 60% to 70% of livestock production cost (industry, 2026) and more than 70% of pig production cost (2024 review). In pangasius, feed was 70% to 90% of operating cost (studies from 2007 to 2011). Feed prices rose 10% to 14% between January and April 2026. The poultry association blamed the Middle East conflict. [VN-direct, Low to Medium, MAC-24, MAC-43, MAC-42]
11. **Disease shocks.** African swine fever (ASF) killed or forced the culling of about 6 million pigs in 2019, more than 20% of the national herd. ASF returned from Q3 2025, and 1.2 to 1.3 million pigs were culled in 2025. [VN-direct, High for 2019, Medium for 2025; MAC-37, MAC-01, MAC-12, MAC-13]
12. **Consumption.** Pork consumption was about 39 kg per person in 2025, up from about 30 kg in 2021 (MOIT market bureau). Our check from production agrees: 52.7 kg live weight per person, or about 39.5 kg carcass at a 75% yield. Output per person was 209 eggs and 12.7 kg of domestic fresh milk. [VN-direct; Low for the reported figure, High for the arithmetic; MAC-21, MAC-14, MAC-19]
13. **Targets.** The Livestock Development Strategy (Decision 1520/QD-TTg, 2020) sets these 2030 targets: 6.0 to 6.5 Mt carcass meat, 58 to 62 kg meat per person, and 30 to 32 Mt industrial feed output from 40 to 45 Mt of mill capacity. On our rough conversion, 2025 meat output was already about 5.9 to 6.5 Mt carcass. The egg target for 2025 was exceeded. The milk target (1.7 to 1.8 Mt in 2025) was missed by about 25%. [VN-direct, High for targets, Low for our conversion; MAC-23, MAC-14]
14. **Outlook.** USDA expects total feed demand (animal plus aquafeed) to rise from 28.6 Mt in 2025 to 30.6 Mt in 2027. In August 2026 it raised its forecast of corn imports for MY2026/27 to 15 Mt. It also expects soybean meal feed use to reach 7.8 Mt by 2027. Every increment of demand growth is met by imports. [VN-direct, Medium, MAC-01, MAC-03, MAC-04]
15. **Greenhouse gases.** Agriculture emitted 104.5 MtCO2e in 2020, 19.8% of Vietnam's total, according to the 2022 NDC. In the 2016 inventory, livestock (enteric fermentation plus manure) emitted 18.5 MtCO2e, 5.8% of the national total, against 49.7 MtCO2e from rice. Vietnam has pledged to cut methane 30% below 2020 levels by 2030. [VN-direct, High for NDC, Medium for 2016 inventory; MAC-32, MAC-33]
16. **Exports.** Vietnam exported USD 11.3 bn of seafood in 2025 (+12.4%), including shrimp USD 4.6 bn and pangasius USD 2.2 bn. Seafood exports reached USD 8.0 bn in January to August 2026 (+11.8%), and China and Hong Kong became the largest market. Vietnam is a large net exporter of animal protein as seafood, but a large net importer of the plant protein that feeds it. [VN-direct, High for 2025 total, Medium for the rest; MAC-15, MAC-01, MAC-34]
17. **Antibiotics.** In 2015 an estimated 71.7% of all antimicrobials used in Vietnam went to animals: pigs 41.7% of the national total, aquaculture 21.9% and chickens 4.8%. [VN-direct, Medium, MAC-36]
18. **Fishmeal pool.** From sourced inputs, the fishmeal that non-animal proteins could displace in Vietnamese aquafeed is roughly 80 to 270 kt a year. That is below the 350 to 450 kt in earlier drafts. The inclusion rates behind it are global averages, because no Vietnam-specific rates were found. [VN-direct volumes with general inclusion rates, Low, MAC-06, MAC-39, MAC-41]
19. **Macro context.** In 2025 GDP grew 8.02%, GDP per person was USD 5,026, population averaged 102.3 million and 38.6% of people lived in cities. GDP grew 8.18% in H1 2026. USDA puts food-processing revenue at USD 88 bn in 2025 (+11%). [VN-direct, High for NSO, Medium for USDA; MAC-19, MAC-17, MAC-31]

### 2. Consumption per person, trends and projections

- **Pork.** About 39 kg per person in 2025, 37 kg in 2024, 33.8 kg in 2023, 32 kg in 2022 and about 30 kg in 2021 (MOIT market bureau via VTV and VietnamNet, MAC-21, MAC-22, Low; weight basis not stated). Vietnam is said to rank fourth in the world for pork consumption. Pork is "over 63%" of livestock-product consumption.
- **Our check from production** (derived, High for the arithmetic; the yields are our assumption). Live-weight pork per person was 52.7 kg in 2025. At a 75% carcass yield that is 39.5 kg, which matches the reported 39 kg. Net pork trade is small: imports were 292 kt in 2024 (MAC-22). Poultry was 25.4 kg per person live weight.
- **Eggs.** 209 eggs per person produced in 2025. Decision 1520 targets 180 to 190 by 2025 (exceeded) and 220 to 225 by 2030 (MAC-23).
- **Milk.** Domestic fresh milk was 12.7 kg per person. Decision 1520 targets 16 to 18 kg (2025) and 24 to 26 kg (2030), but we could not confirm whether these targets are on a production or consumption basis. Vietnam imports large volumes of milk powder, so domestic output understates consumption.
- **Fish.** Fisheries output was 97 kg per person (live weight), but a large share is exported. We found no current per-capita fish consumption figure (gap).
- **Projections.**
  - Decision 1520/QD-TTg (6 October 2020) sets 2030 targets of 6.0 to 6.5 Mt carcass meat (pork 59% to 61%, poultry 29% to 31%, ruminants 10% to 11%), 58 to 62 kg meat per person, and exports of 15% to 20% of pork and 20% to 25% of poultry output (MAC-23, High).
  - Our rough conversion puts 2025 carcass meat at 5.9 to 6.5 Mt. On that basis, output already exceeds the 2025 target (5.0 to 5.5 Mt) and is near the 2030 range. Treat this with caution: the dressing yields are assumptions, and the 1520 baseline may use different conversion factors.
  - The OECD-FAO Agricultural Outlook 2025-2034 publishes Vietnam projections, but its website blocked automated access. Its Vietnam per-capita projections are an open gap.
  - USDA projects feed demand to 2027 (section 3).

- **The 21.5 Mt excludes aquafeed.** The species split (pig 55.3%, poultry 40.9%, other livestock 3.7%) leaves no room for aquafeed, which runs to several million tonnes. This settles a question from earlier drafts: 21.5 Mt (2024) and 22 Mt (2025) cover livestock and poultry feed only.
- **Aquafeed, two incompatible estimates:**
  - USDA Post: 6.2 Mt (2024) and 6.5 Mt (2025). Method not disclosed (MAC-01, MAC-02).
  - Aqua Culture Asia Pacific (June 2026), industry estimates: shrimp feed "up to 920,000 tonnes" and fish feed "3.0-3.9 million tonnes" in 2025. The fish feed splits into pangasius 2.0 to 2.5 Mt, tilapia 0.4 to 0.5 Mt, snakehead up to 0.3 Mt and marine fish 0.08 to 0.10 Mt. The total is 3.9 to 4.8 Mt. The same article gives installed aquafeed capacity "up to 5.5 million tonnes" at 60% to 70% utilisation, which implies 3.3 to 3.85 Mt (MAC-06).
  - The article does not give fishmeal inclusion rates by species. The prior lead that attributed inclusion rates to this article is not supported by it.
- **Feed and production numbers do not add up.** The article says 920 kt of shrimp feed supported "nearly 800,000 tonnes" of shrimp. NSO reports 1,291 kt of farmed whiteleg plus black tiger shrimp. Black tiger is mostly semi-intensive and uses little pelleted feed, but NSO whiteleg alone (994 kt) would need more than 920 kt of feed at any feed conversion ratio (FCR) above 0.93. The same problem arises for pangasius: 2.0 to 2.5 Mt of feed for 1.94 Mt of fish implies an FCR of only 1.03 to 1.29. Either the feed estimates are low, part of the feed is farm-made, or official output is overstated. We cannot resolve this. It matters for any feed-market sizing.
- **Industry structure.**
  - More than 260 feed companies operate. FDI firms make "more than 60%" of industrial feed (Vietnam Report survey, December 2025, via Tuoi Tre, MAC-25, Low). A 2017 MARD figure was 60% to 65% from about 218 mills, 71 of them FDI (MAC-29, dated).
  - Vietnam Report's 2025 top 10: De Heus, GreenFeed Vietnam, Cargill Vietnam, Japfa Comfeed, Sunjin Vina, VNF, Hong Ha Nutrition, Lai Thieu Feed, Mekong Aquafeed and Vina-Ha Nam Feed (MAC-25). CP Group is described elsewhere as the market leader with 9 plants (MAC-30, undated). We found no current volume-based market shares.
  - Top shrimp feed producers in 2025: Sheng Long, CP Vietnam, Uni-President Vietnam, Tongwei, Grobest and Gromax, with a combined 640 kt (MAC-06).

#### 3.3 Domestic corn and soybean, and the soybean crush reconciliation

- **Corn.** Domestic production was 4.105 Mt on 820,000 ha (MY2024/25), 4.1 Mt on 810,000 ha (MY2025/26) and a forecast 4.0 Mt on 800,000 ha (MY2026/27), per USDA Post. Feed and residual use was 14.4 Mt in MY2024/25 (MAC-01).
- **Soybeans.** Output was 45 kt (2024) and 43 kt (2025) on 26,300 ha, with 41 kt forecast for 2026. Farmers are shifting to fruit and vegetables (MAC-04, MAC-05).
- **Reconciliation of the crush figures.** For CY2025 (USDA's MY2024/25):
  - Soybean supply was beginning stocks 460 kt, production 42 kt and imports 2,609 kt.
  - Uses were crush 1,900 kt, food 540 kt and feed/waste 220 kt, leaving ending stocks of 451 kt. The balance closes.
  - Crushing 1,900 kt gives 1,482 kt of meal, a 78% extraction rate.
  - Meal supply was 678 kt of opening stocks, 1,482 kt produced and 5,703 kt imported. Meal uses were 283 kt exported, 95 kt food and 7,200 kt feed, leaving 285 kt of closing stocks. The balance closes.
  - The earlier drafts' problem came from mixing years: they combined the CY2026 forecasts (41 kt produced, 3.2 Mt imported) with a CY2025 forecast of 7.0 Mt feed use and CY2025 actual meal imports of 5.7 Mt.
  - Even if every domestic soybean were crushed, it would supply only 2.2% of the crush. Domestic "crush" therefore does not reduce import dependence. It shifts imports from meal to beans.
  - Crushing capacity is growing. Four plants operated in 2025 (one in the north, three in the south). New lines opened in Q1 2026. USDA forecasts crush of 2.5 Mt in 2026 and 3.0 Mt in 2027 (MAC-04, MAC-05). USDA does not name the crushers.

### 4. Aquafeed and fishmeal

- **Volumes by species (2025, industry estimates, MAC-06, Low):**
  - Shrimp feed up to 920 kt, mostly for intensive whiteleg shrimp. Black tiger is mostly semi-intensive.
  - Pangasius 2.0 to 2.5 Mt.
  - Tilapia 0.4 to 0.5 Mt.
  - Snakehead up to 0.3 Mt.
  - Marine fish 0.08 to 0.10 Mt.
  - US tariffs on Chinese tilapia have pushed northern feed millers, including Dabaco, into tilapia and carp feed.
- **Inclusion rates.** We found no Vietnam-specific published rates. General literature:
  - Freshwater fed fish (carps, tilapia, catfish): global fishmeal inclusion "dropped ... to 1-2%" by 2017 (Naylor et al. 2021, MAC-39, [general], High).
  - Shrimp: fishmeal was 19% to 40% of feed in 2000 and 11% to 23% in 2014, with about 6% projected for 2025 (Salin et al. 2018, cited in MAC-41, [general], Medium). "Traditional" shrimp feeds use 20% to 30% (MAC-40, [general]).
  - Marine finfish rates fell "by one-half to two-thirds" over 1997 to 2017, but no single current value is given (MAC-39).
- **Displaceable fishmeal pool.** This counts only species with both a sourced feed volume and a sourced inclusion range. It is an estimate, Low confidence:
  - Shrimp: 920 kt x 6% = 55 kt (low) to 920 kt x 23% = 212 kt (high)
  - Pangasius: 2,000 kt x 1% = 20 kt to 2,500 kt x 2% = 50 kt
  - Tilapia: 400 kt x 1% = 4 kt to 500 kt x 2% = 10 kt
  - Total: about 79 to 272 kt of fishmeal a year
  - Excluded because their inclusion rates are unsourced: snakehead (carnivorous, likely high inclusion, up to 300 kt of feed) and marine fish (80 to 100 kt of feed). If snakehead feeds contained 20% to 40% fishmeal, they would add 60 to 120 kt. That is a hypothesis, not a finding.
  - Position: the 350 to 450 kt in prior drafts is not supported by sourced inputs. The pool is more likely in the low hundreds of kilotonnes, and the shrimp inclusion rate is the swing factor. Vietnamese feed millers (Sheng Long, UPV, Grobest) could close this with one call.
- **Substitutes already in use.** In 2025 the fishmeal price gap "accelerated the use of poultry byproduct meal as a partial fishmeal substitute, as well as fermented soy, soy protein concentrate and rapeseed meal" (MAC-06). New non-animal proteins would compete with these incumbents, not only with fishmeal.
- **Domestic fishmeal production, imports and trash fish.**
  - Gap: we found no sourced figure for 2024 to 2026.
  - Pangasius processing by-products are 62% to 67% of whole fish (Minh 2014, cited in MAC-45), which is a large domestic base for fishmeal and fish oil.
  - The use of trash fish (low-value fish) as direct feed was documented in 2003 to 2004 surveys (Edwards, Le Anh Tuan and Allan 2004). We did not use it as a current figure.
- **Fishmeal prices.** International prices were USD 1,600/t (Q1 2025 low) and USD 1,825/t (end-2025), driven by Peru (MAC-06). Vietnamese domestic prices are a gap.

### 5. Shocks and resilience

- **ASF.**
  - First confirmed in February 2019 in Hung Yen and Thai Binh (2019 province names). The 2025 merger combined these two into the present Hung Yen province; we did not re-verify this.
  - By October 2019 it had reached all 63 provinces then existing. About 6 million pigs died or were culled, more than 20% of the herd (MAC-37, MAC-38, High).
  - It came back in April 2020 and continued through 2022 (MAC-38).
  - It resurged from Q3 2025, with 1.2 to 1.3 million pigs culled in 2025 (MAC-01, MAC-12). NSO described the herd as falling sharply (MAC-13). In H1 2026 outbreaks were "less severe ... more dispersed, sporadic" (MAC-03).
  - The modern, biosecure sector was less affected in 2019 and even gained (MAC-37).
- **Poultry disease and disasters.** 3.68 million poultry died or were culled in 2025 (MAC-12). In 2024, 5.4 million livestock and poultry died in natural disasters (NSO, MAC-20).
- **The 2022 feed price spike.** Wheat import prices peaked at USD 424/t (September 2022, +37% y/y) (MAC-44). A 2024 review says COVID-19 and the Russia-Ukraine war "greatly disturbed" feed markets and forced about 30% of small pig farmers out (MAC-43). We did not find an official 2022 feed price index. The feed industry contracted for two years before recovering in 2024 (Vietnam Report, MAC-26).
- **The 2026 shock.** Feed prices rose 10% to 14% between January and April 2026. The poultry association chair said they could rise 20% to 25% if the Middle East conflict continued, and advised farmers not to expand herds (MAC-24). Farm-gate eggs sold at VND 1,200 to 1,300 against a production cost of about VND 1,600. The deputy director of the livestock department said rising import-dependent feed costs are weakening domestic competitiveness (MAC-24). USDA's August 2026 report does not mention this price rise. It records live hog prices down about 20% and broiler prices down 11% to 19% in H1 2026 (MAC-03), a cost-price squeeze.
- **Currency and tax.** In 2025 the USD/VND rate rose about 3% to 3.5%. Feed became VAT-exempt from 1 July 2025, so the 5% VAT paid on inputs can no longer be deducted, which raises millers' costs (MAC-25).
- **Share of feed in cost.** Livestock 60% to 70% (MAC-24, industry claim). Pigs more than 70% (MAC-43, review). Pangasius 70% to 90% of operating cost (MAC-42, older studies). Shrimp: gap.
- **Official targets on feed self-reliance.**
  - Decision 1520/QD-TTg encourages domestic feed-ingredient processing from by-products (brewers' grain, pineapple residue, cassava pulp, fish offal) and biological products that replace antibiotics. It sets no numerical target for localising raw materials (MAC-23).
  - We could not verify these in this wave, so they remain gaps: Decision 300/QD-TTg (2023) on food systems transformation, any 2025/2026 directive on feed raw materials, and the methane action plan.
- **Other policy.** Vietnam required ethanol blending in all gasoline from early June 2026 (MAC-03). This affects demand for cassava and corn and the supply of DDGS. It is a lead for M2 and M3.

### 6. Environment

- **National GHG** (NDC update, October 2022, MAC-32, High):
  - Agriculture emitted 89.8 MtCO2e in 2014 (31.6% of 284.0) and 104.5 MtCO2e in 2020 (19.8% of 528.4). It is projected at 112.1 MtCO2e under business as usual (BAU) in 2030 (12.1% of 927.9).
  - Agriculture's committed reductions are 12.4 MtCO2e unconditional (1.3% of national BAU) and 50.9 MtCO2e conditional (5.5%).
  - Economy-wide targets are 15.8% unconditional (146.3 MtCO2e) and 43.5% conditional (403.7 MtCO2e).
  - Livestock measures listed: improved ruminant rations, treatment of livestock waste and by-products, and biogas.
  - Global Methane Pledge: methane 30% below 2020 by 2030, with emphasis on wet rice and livestock waste.
- **Livestock within agriculture** (BUR3, 2016 inventory, MAC-33, Medium): enteric fermentation 12.42 MtCO2e and manure management 6.09 MtCO2e, together 18.5 MtCO2e or 5.8% of 316.7 MtCO2e. Rice cultivation emitted 49.69 MtCO2e (15.7%) and direct soil N2O 7.75 MtCO2e. So rice, not livestock, dominates agricultural methane. Any case for alternative protein on climate grounds in Vietnam rests mainly on avoided imported-feed footprint and manure pollution, not on national methane totals.
  - Currency flag: a newer inventory in the first Biennial Transparency Report (due end-2024) and an NDC 3.0 (2025) may exist. We did not check either.
- **Land footprint of feed imports** (illustrative, [general], Low; assumptions unsourced).
  - 2025 soy imports equal about 9.9 Mt of soybeans: 2.61 Mt of beans plus 5.70 Mt of meal / 0.78 extraction (MAC-04).
  - At an assumed 3.0 to 3.5 t/ha that needs about 2.8 to 3.3 Mha. Corn imports of 11.9 Mt at an assumed 6 to 8 t/ha need about 1.5 to 2.0 Mha.
  - Together that is roughly 4 to 5 Mha of cropland abroad, several times Vietnam's own corn plus soybean area (about 0.84 Mha).
  - Use this only as an order of magnitude until yields are sourced.
- **Livestock waste and pollution.** Gap. Decision 1520 requires all farms and slaughterhouses to have environmental controls but gives no numerical waste-treatment target (MAC-23).
- **Antimicrobial use.**
  - In 2015 Vietnam used about 3,838 t of antimicrobials, 71.7% of them in animals. By share of the national total: pigs 41.7%, aquaculture 21.9%, chickens 4.8%.
  - Use per kg of animal biomass was 247 mg, against 152 mg in the EU (Carrique-Mas et al. 2020, MAC-36, Medium; estimates are "approximate").
  - Pangasius farmers mix their own medicated feed, and product quality is poor (Phu et al. 2015, not given an ID; lead).

### 7. Trade position

- **Seafood.**
  - Exports were USD 11.3 bn in 2025 (+12.4%, NSO) (MAC-15). USDA gives shrimp USD 4.6 bn (+19%) and pangasius USD 2.2 bn (+8%) (MAC-01).
  - January to August 2026: USD 8.00 bn (+11.8%), including shrimp USD 3.29 bn (+12.3%), pangasius USD 1.5 bn (+9%) and tuna USD 622 M.
  - Markets: China and Hong Kong USD 2.03 bn (+33%), USA USD 1.24 bn (flat), Japan USD 1.11 bn and the EU USD 750 M (MAC-34).
  - Shrimp exports in January to August 2026: whiteleg more than USD 2 bn. VASEP reports a US Section 301 tariff of 12.5% on Vietnamese shrimp from July 2026, against 10% for Ecuador, India and Indonesia (MAC-35, Low until checked against USTR).
  - Export volumes for 2025: gap.
- **Meat and feed.**
  - Livestock-sector exports were more than USD 2.1 bn in 2025 (+17.4%). Livestock products were about USD 628 M and animal feed about USD 1.5 bn (MAC-12). USDA gives feed exports of USD 1.44 bn (+40%), mainly to China and Southeast Asia, plus 1.2 Mt of rice bran and 4 Mt of cassava (MAC-01).
  - Pork imports were 292 kt (USD 460 M) in 2024 (MAC-22). Total meat and offal imports for 2025: gap.
- **Net position.** Vietnam exports about USD 11 bn of seafood and about USD 1.5 bn of feed a year. It imports about USD 10 bn of feed grains and ingredients. It is a protein-processing economy: it imports plant protein and energy and exports animal and aquatic protein.

### 8. Macro context

In 2025 Vietnam's GDP grew 8.02% to about USD 514 bn, or USD 5,026 per person, for an average population of 102.3 million. 38.6% of people lived in cities. Agriculture, forestry and fisheries made up 11.64% of GDP and grew 3.78%. Manufacturing grew 9.97%. FDI disbursed was USD 27.62 bn, against USD 38.42 bn registered (MAC-19). GDP grew 8.18% in H1 2026, and processing and manufacturing grew 10.23% (MAC-17). USDA estimates food-processing revenue at USD 88 bn in 2025 (+11%) and consumer-oriented food imports at USD 16.3 bn (MAC-31). Leaders are seeking double-digit growth for 2026 to 2030 (MAC-01). We did not find a figure for FDI in food processing specifically (gap).

## Implications by audience

- **VCs and investors**
  - Demand for feed protein is large (about 7.2 Mt of soybean meal equivalent), almost fully imported and growing about 3% to 5% a year. Any domestic protein that beats landed soybean meal or fishmeal on price has a real B2B market. But the incumbent substitutes (domestic crush expansion, poultry by-product meal, soy protein concentrate) are expanding too.
  - Size theses on the smaller fishmeal pool (about 80 to 270 kt), not on total aquafeed tonnage.
- **Policy makers**
  - Feed import dependence is structural: domestic corn and soybean area is falling while feed demand rises. Decision 1520 has capacity and output targets but no numerical target for localising feed raw materials.
  - The 2026 price shock and the 2025 VAT mismatch show two levers: a feed-security indicator, and the tax treatment of inputs.
  - Livestock is a small share of national GHG (5.8% in 2016), so the policy case for alternative protein rests more on feed security, trade balance, AMR and pollution than on methane.
- **Startups**
  - The buyers are concentrated. Six firms make 640 kt of shrimp feed, and FDI mills make more than 60% of livestock feed. A first customer is likely one of about 10 to 15 feed groups.
  - Shrimp feed is where fishmeal inclusion, and so willingness to pay, is highest.
- **F&B and feed manufacturers**
  - Corn origins shifted in H1 2026 (Argentina 37%, Brazil 36%, US 13%), and a new wheat supplier appeared (Argentina 58% in H1 2026). Diversification is under way but stays within the Americas and Australia.
  - Domestic soybean crushing is expanding (2.5 to 3.0 Mt by 2026 to 2027).
- **Academia**
  - Priority data gaps: Vietnam-specific fishmeal inclusion rates by species, aquafeed volumes and FCRs consistent with NSO output, and a current national AMU estimate.
- **International bodies**
  - Fundable public goods: an independent national feed balance (all ingredients, all species), an updated AMU survey, and livestock-waste statistics.

## Open questions and gaps (with the cheapest way to close each)

1. **Fishmeal: domestic production, imports and domestic prices, 2023 to 2026.** Get Vietnam Customs HS 2301.20 data through ITC Trade Map, or ask the Vietnam Animal Feed Association (VAFA, Hiep hoi Thuc an chan nuoi Viet Nam) or VASEP (for pangasius by-product meal).
2. **Vietnam-specific fishmeal inclusion rates by species.** One call each to the technical managers at Uni-President Vietnam, Sheng Long and Grobest, or ask the Research Institute for Aquaculture No. 2 (RIA2).
3. **Official aquafeed output by species.** MAE's Department of Fisheries and Fisheries Surveillance publishes an annual review; request the feed chapter.
4. **Per-person consumption of fish, milk and meat by type, on a consumption basis.** Use the Vietnam Household Living Standards Survey (NSO) and FAOSTAT food balance sheets. The FAOSTAT site could not be read by automated tools.
5. **OECD-FAO Agricultural Outlook 2025-2034 projections for Vietnam** (meat, fish, feed to 2034). The OECD site blocked automated access. Download the data from the OECD Data Explorer manually.
6. **Latest GHG inventory** (first Biennial Transparency Report) **and NDC 3.0** (2025). Check the UNFCCC submissions page.
7. **Decision 300/QD-TTg (2023), the methane action plan, and any 2025/2026 directives on feed raw materials.** Read the full texts on thuvienphapluat.vn.
8. **Current FDI market share and volume-based rankings of feed mills.** VAFA, or Vietnam Report's full ranking report.
9. **Meat and offal imports in 2025 (volume and value); seafood export volume in 2025.** Customs annual bulletin; VASEP annual report.
10. **Import values for soybean meal and DDGS separately.** Customs HS 2304 and 2303 detail (ITC Trade Map).
11. **Share of feed in shrimp production cost.** Shrimp farm cost surveys (Can Tho University; VASEP).
12. **Livestock waste volume and treatment rates.** MAE environment reports; the national state-of-environment report.
13. **An official feed price index for 2021 to 2023** (the 2022 spike). NSO producer and consumer price sub-indices for animal feed.
14. **FDI in food processing.** The foreign investment agency now under MOF (formerly MPI) publishes FDI by sector. Check whether food and beverage is broken out.
