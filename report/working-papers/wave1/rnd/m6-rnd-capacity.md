# M6 Knowledge base: research capacity, talent, labs and funding for alternative protein in Vietnam

Agent: RND (wave 1). Edition: September 2026. Date of research: 23 September 2026.
Files in this folder: `m6-rnd-capacity.md` (this note), `publications.csv` (76 papers), `institutions.csv` (31 institutions), `research_funding.csv` (16 programmes).

**How to read this note.** "VN-affiliated" means at least one author works at a Vietnamese institution, judged from Scite records, affiliation filters and author groups. Paper counts are **lower bounds**. They come from the Scite database (RD-01), whose affiliation data is incomplete and which only partly indexes Vietnamese-language journals and the journal *Livestock Research for Rural Development* (LRRD). A "probable" attribution means the institution was inferred from the author group and not read from the paper header.

**Tool limits in this wave.** The session's web search budget was used up before this agent started. Web evidence therefore comes only from fetching known pages directly (9 pages loaded; several failed). The bibliometric picture rests on 30 Scite queries run by this agent (Appendix A). Facility inventories, graduate numbers and grant sizes are the weakest parts of this note.

## Headline findings

1. **Vietnam's peer-reviewed base for alternative protein is thin and scattered. Where it exists, it sits next to alternative protein rather than inside it.** Across eight topic areas we screened more than 150 VN-affiliated records from 2015 to 2026 and tabulated 76. Only 2 papers are on plant-based meat or hybrid meat products. **None** are on texturisation or extrusion, mycoprotein, tempeh-type food fungi, or the technology of cultivated meat. [VN-direct] Medium (search-based lower bound). RD-01, RD-26, RD-27.

2. **Vietnam's five real research strengths that alternative protein can borrow are:**
   - aquafeed nutrition trials (Can Tho University, Nha Trang University);
   - recombinant protein expression in *Pichia pastoris* (VNU-HCM University of Science);
   - human stem cell culture, including serum-free media (Vinmec, VNU-HCM Stem Cell Institute);
   - fungal genetic tools for koji mould and *Cordyceps* (VNU Hanoi University of Science);
   - microalgae cultivation (Institute of Biotechnology VAST, Vietnam National University of Agriculture).

   [VN-direct] Medium. RD-01, RD-33 to RD-41, RD-46 to RD-49.

3. **Aquafeed is the deepest applied field. Replacement levels measured in Vietnam are useful benchmarks for feed-first plays:**
   - soy protein concentrate replaced 40% of fishmeal protein in snakehead with no loss of growth (60 to 100% replacement reduced growth) (2016);
   - fermented soybean meal had 88.8% crude protein digestibility in whiteleg shrimp, with an optimum fishmeal replacement of 253.6 g/kg (2018);
   - spent brewer's yeast replaced up to 60% of fishmeal protein in giant freshwater prawn without loss of growth or survival (2019).

   All results are measured, but only in lab or tank trials. [VN-direct] High. RD-46, RD-47, RD-48.

4. **HUST leads food-grade side-stream protein work:**
   - Rice-based dried distillers grain (DDG) from Vietnamese ethanol plants holds 55 to 80% protein (dry matter). Cassava DDG holds only 13 to 16% (preprint, 2024).
   - Alkaline extraction recovered 90% of the protein. Enzymatically extracted protein was 77.1% digestible in vitro (2025).
   - Spent brewer's yeast protein was recovered at 84.9% and made into a 14.9 g-protein energy bar (2024).

   [VN-direct] High (2025, 2024), Medium (2024 preprint). RD-22, RD-23, RD-32.

5. **Vietnam already makes the costliest ingredients of cultivated-meat media, but not for food.** VNU-HCM groups have expressed the growth factors FGF-2, KGF/FGF7 and PDGF-BB in *Pichia* (2020 to 2021). Vinmec has tested serum-free and xeno-free media for stem cells (2021 to 2022). No Vietnamese group has published on cells from food animals for food, and **no fish or shrimp cell line developed in Vietnam was found**. The only livestock stem-cell paper is on porcine germline stem cells (2019). [VN-direct] Medium. RD-36 to RD-40, RD-42.

6. **Microbial protein from cassava residues is almost unstudied in Vietnam's peer-reviewed literature.** We found only:
   - one 2025 preprint, in which oyster-mushroom mycelium on cassava plus soy residue raised protein 1.84-fold;
   - mushroom cultivation on cassava peel;
   - one 2016 paper isolating a methane-oxidising bacterium for single cell protein, with no follow-up found.

   A 2023 single cell protein paper (bamboo cellulose) was **retracted**. [VN-direct] Medium. RD-28, RD-29, RD-30, RD-64.

7. **Duckweed is a clear Vietnamese advantage that nobody has used for protein.** A survey collected more than 100 wild *Spirodela*, *Lemna* and *Wolffia* samples nationwide and keeps them in the lab (2022). A Vietnamese-led team assembled a duckweed genome (2020). Yet we found no protein extraction, food trial or feed trial with Vietnamese duckweed from 2015 to 2026. The FiBL researcher named in prior leads (T. Stadtlander) has 9 duckweed records in Scite, all from European work. [VN-direct] Medium. RD-44, RD-71, RD-59.

8. **The prior-draft claim that "no Vietnamese study has reported RNA reduction, mycotoxin testing or amino acid digestibility" is too strong.** HUST studied nucleic acid reduction in spent-yeast hydrolysate (2018). A 2025 paper in the NIFC journal tested aflatoxin in 38 traditional fermented soy (*tương*) samples. HUST measured in vitro digestibility of rice DDG protein (2025). What remains true is narrower: none of this has been applied to a novel microbial biomass, and **no Vietnamese DIAAS or PDCAAS study was found**. [VN-direct] Medium. RD-31, RD-61, RD-23, RD-13.

9. **Research money is national and small-grant based:**
   - NAFOSTED is the funder most often acknowledged in the relevant Vietnamese literature (255 Scite records match NAFOSTED with protein, fermentation or aquafeed terms).
   - VINIF mostly funds people. Its 2018 to 2023 totals were about 1,300 Master and PhD scholarships, 180 postdoctoral fellowships and 117 projects, with a Big Data focus.
   - Typical grant sizes were not established.

   [VN-direct] Medium. RD-05, RD-06, RD-12.

10. **International money is present but not aimed at alternative protein:**
    - ACIAR's Vietnam programme is AUD 5.4 million in FY2025-26 across 23 projects. The closest to our scope is manufactured feed for grouper (FIS/2022/148).
    - The GFI Research Grant Program reports USD 27M+ in 141 grants in 26 countries since 2019. The page we fetched showed no grantee in Vietnam or elsewhere in Southeast Asia, and the programme was not accepting submissions.

    [VN-direct / general] High for the figures, Medium for "no VN grantee". RD-02, RD-03, RD-04.

11. **The Law on Science, Technology and Innovation No. 93/2025/QH15 (issued 27 June 2025) adds tools a research consortium can use.** The page we read shows controlled testing, or a sandbox (Articles 21 to 23), exemption from liability for research risk when procedures are followed (Article 9(3)), and specialised funds with state capital (Article 6). [VN-direct] Medium (read through a machine summary; confirm against the official text). RD-08.

12. **FIRI (MOIT, Hanoi) is the only public institute we found with all three pilot-relevant assets:** a pilot-scale food production and technology transfer centre, an enzyme and protein technology department, and an online microbial culture collection. This makes it the obvious first call for pilot-scale fermentation partners. Equipment sizes are not published. [VN-direct] High for the structure, Low for capacity. RD-07.

13. **Existing international links are mostly in aquaculture nutrition and traditional fermentation, not alternative protein:**
    - Wageningen: pangasius digestion work; a 2022 seaweed-for-methane seed study.
    - SLU Sweden: brewer's-yeast feed trials at Can Tho University.
    - Deakin University: barramundi diets with Nha Trang University.
    - The Erasmus+ AsiFood project and the Tropical Fermentation Network (Agreenium, France) with HUST.
    - Japan's SATREPS: biomass energy and indigenous pig genetics.

    [VN-direct] Medium. RD-56, RD-57, RD-58, RD-49, RD-53, RD-01.

14. **The only Vietnamese paper we found on cultivated meat is a legal analysis of EU and Singapore rules (2024).** It is not a technical paper. [VN-direct] High. RD-43.

15. **Counting method matters.** With the affiliation filter, Scite returned only 3 papers on replacing fishmeal. Term searches found at least 10 Vietnamese aquafeed replacement papers. Any Vietnam bibliometric figure should therefore be read as a floor. OpenAlex-based counts are recommended for the next wave. [VN-direct] High (method observation). RD-01.

### C. Talent pipeline

- **Programmes.** Food technology and biotechnology degrees exist at HUST, VNUA, Nong Lam, Can Tho, Nha Trang, Hue, Danang, IUH, Van Lang, HCMUT, IU and HCMUS. Aquaculture degrees exist at CTU, NTU, Nong Lam, VNUA and Hue. [VN-direct] Medium (institution websites and agent knowledge; CTU confirmed on RD-10).
- **Annual graduates: NOT established.** No published graduate numbers were retrieved. Cheapest fix: each university's 2025 and 2026 admissions plans (*Đề án tuyển sinh*) list quotas by programme code (food technology 7540101, biotechnology 7420201, aquaculture 7620301). Summing the quotas for the named universities gives an intake estimate in one afternoon.
- **Skills picture (inference from the literature, Low confidence).** The gap is specialist rather than general:
  - Vietnam produces fermentation, enzyme, nutrition and cell-culture researchers.
  - The literature shows almost no one working on extrusion or texturisation, food-grade downstream processing of microbial biomass, sensory and structure work on meat analogues, protein-quality scoring (DIAAS), or cell culture of fish and livestock for food.
- **Vietnamese researchers abroad: NOT mapped.** Scite returns only the first three authors and no affiliations, so a diaspora map could not be built reliably. Two recent reviews had Vietnamese first authors, but their affiliations were not established: plant-based fish analogues (10.1111/jfpe.70365, 2026) and plant and algae protein (10.55677/ijlsar/v03i6y2024-10, 2024). One Vietnamese researcher published antibody production in *A. oryzae* with Japanese co-authors (10.1186/s40694-020-00098-w, 2020), which shows fungal expression skills in the diaspora or among returnees. We name no individuals as diaspora without verified affiliations.

### F. Gaps Vietnam is well placed to fill (VN-advantaged research questions with no published work found)

Each gap below meets three tests: (1) Vietnam holds a scarce raw material, species or skill; (2) we found no peer-reviewed work on it by anyone in Vietnam; (3) a named Vietnamese group already has the adjacent skill.

1. **Cell lines from striped catfish, whiteleg and black tiger shrimp, Asian seabass, grouper and snakehead for cultivated seafood.** Vietnam runs the world's largest pangasius industry. Fish and shrimp cell lines developed in Vietnam: none found. Adjacent skills: Vinmec and the VNU-HCM Stem Cell Institute (culture, serum-free media), aquatic virology labs.
2. **Low-cost recombinant growth factors for fish-cell media.** FGF-2, KGF and PDGF-BB are already expressed in *Pichia* at VNU-HCM. Nobody has tested them on fish cells or costed them per gram.
3. **Fungal biomass protein from cassava pulp and peel with a food-grade safety package** (cyanide, mycotoxins, RNA, digestibility). Only a Pleurotus preprint for feed exists. Adjacent skills: the A. oryzae toolkit at VNU HUS; RNA reduction in yeast at HUST; aflatoxin analysis at NIFC.
4. **Koji (*tương*) strains as mycoprotein chassis.** Indigenous A. oryzae strains are held at VTCC and FIRI, and genetic tools exist. No biomass-protein study was found.
5. **Rice DDG and broken-rice protein texturisation.** Extraction is published (HUST), but extrusion and functionality in meat analogues are not. Vietnam has rice-ethanol side streams and 1.5 to 2.5 Mt of broken rice (prior lead, unverified).
6. **Protein extraction and feeding trials with Vietnamese duckweed clones**, especially *Wolffia* for food, using the collection of more than 100 wild samples.
7. **Microalgae protein ingredients from salt-tolerant Spirulina** (48.7% protein measured) grown on seawater or brackish water in the south-central coast. Only cultivation physiology is published.
8. **Seaweed protein** (Ulva, Caulerpa, Kappaphycus) for food and feed. Only small shrimp postlarvae trials exist.
9. **Methanotroph or biogas-derived bacterial protein** from pig-farm and cassava-starch biogas. One isolation paper (2016), no follow-up.
10. **A DIAAS/PDCAAS reference dataset for Vietnamese protein ingredients** (rice DDG, mung bean, gac seed, spent yeast, catfish side-stream isolates, spirulina). Nothing found. This is a cheap public good that every plant-based and feed play needs.
11. **Bacterial SCP and fungal meal in pangasius and whiteleg shrimp diets.** Soy, yeast, BSF and seaweed have been tested. Novel microbial meals have not.

## Implications by audience

**VCs and investors**
- Feed-first theses can find credible trial partners today: CTU and NTU for digestibility and growth trials, HUST for side-stream protein, and VNU-HCM for *Pichia* enzymes. Ask founders for CTU/NTU trial data as a diligence gate.
- There is no domestic academic pipeline in extrusion, mycoprotein or cultivated meat. Startups in those areas will import know-how and people, which adds cost and key-person risk.

**Policy makers (MOST, MAE, MOIT, MOH, provinces)**
- The binding research constraint is **shared pilot infrastructure**, not ideas. Three items would unlock most gaps: a pilot extrusion line, a food-grade fermentation train (roughly 50 to 500 L with downstream processing), and a food cell-culture lab.
- A directed NAFOSTED call, or a national programme using the Law 93/2025 sandbox, on "protein from Vietnamese side streams and species" would convert scattered groups into a network.
- Fund a national protein-quality (DIAAS) reference lab, or add it to NIFC or FIRI NACEFA. Regulators will need these data for any novel food dossier.

**Startups**
- Practical partner map:
  - FIRI (pilot plant, culture collection);
  - VTCC (strains);
  - VNU HUS (fungal engineering);
  - VNU-HCM US (Pichia);
  - HUST (side-stream protein);
  - CTU and NTU (feeding trials);
  - Vinmec and the VNU-HCM Stem Cell Institute (serum-free culture);
  - NIFC (reference analysis).
- Do not assume any Vietnamese partner owns extruders or 100 L+ food-grade fermenters until confirmed by phone.

**F&B and feed manufacturers**
- Rice DDG protein (55 to 80% protein on a dry basis) and spent brewer's yeast are two domestic protein side streams with published Vietnamese extraction data. Brewers and ethanol plants can commission HUST to scale these up.
- Feed mills can commission species-specific digestibility trials for novel proteins at CTU, NTU or the RIAs. Published Vietnamese benchmarks already exist for SPC, FSBM and brewer's yeast.

**Academia**
- The 11 gaps in section F are open and Vietnam-advantaged. The quickest to publish are: the DIAAS dataset, duckweed protein from the national collection, rice DDG texturisation, and Pichia growth factors tested on fish cells.
- Publish in indexed English-language journals or with DOIs. Much applied Vietnamese work is invisible to international partners.

**International bodies (GFI APAC, ACIAR, FAO, JICA, KOICA, EU)**
- Vietnam has had no GFI research grant. The fundable public goods are: a pangasius or shrimp cell line, a Vietnamese protein-quality database, duckweed germplasm protein screening, and a safety dossier template for cassava-residue fungal protein.
- ACIAR's grouper-feed project and the Can Tho-Wageningen and Can Tho-SLU links are ready channels for alternative-protein aquafeed work.
