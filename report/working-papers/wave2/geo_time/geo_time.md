## Headline findings

1. **Vietnam has 34 provincial-level units from 1 July 2025: 28 provinces and 6 centrally governed cities.** Resolution 202/2025/QH15 (12 June 2025) merges 52 former units into 23 new ones. It leaves 11 unchanged: Hanoi, Hue, Cao Bang, Dien Bien, Ha Tinh, Lai Chau, Lang Son, Nghe An, Quang Ninh, Thanh Hoa, Son La. The six cities are Hanoi, Hue, Hai Phong, Da Nang, Ho Chi Minh City and Can Tho. [VN-direct] High. GT-01.
2. **All 24 former units named in the brief (19 merger questions) are confirmed against the resolution text.** Long An went into **Tay Ninh**, not HCMC. Binh Duong and Ba Ria-Vung Tau went into HCMC. Every other assignment in the brief is also correct (see table A2). [VN-direct] High. GT-01.
3. **In 7 merged provinces the name comes from one former unit and the capital from another.** Tay Ninh is run from Long An ward (former Tan An). An Giang is run from Rach Gia (former Kien Giang). Dong Thap is run from My Tho (former Tien Giang). Gia Lai is run from Quy Nhon (former Binh Dinh). Quang Tri is run from Dong Hoi (former Quang Binh). Lao Cai is run from Yen Bai. Bac Ninh is run from Bac Giang. This matters for company addresses and licensing offices. [VN-direct] High. GT-02, GT-03.
4. **The district level ended on 1 July 2025.** Local government now has two tiers, province and commune. Resolution 60-NQ/TW (12 April 2025) set the end of district-level units and a 60 to 70% cut in commune-level units. [VN-direct] High. GT-02.
5. **The ministry mergers took effect on 1 March 2025.** Resolution 176/2025/QH15 (18 February 2025) set 14 ministries and 3 ministerial-level agencies. MARD and MONRE became the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment (MAE; Decree 35/2025). MOST absorbed MIC (Decree 55/2025, 2 March 2025). MOF absorbed MPI (Decree 29/2025, amended by Decree 166/2025). MOIT (Decree 40/2025) and MOH (Decree 42/2025) kept their names. [VN-direct] High. GT-04 to GT-09.
6. **Standards now sit with a "National Committee for Standards, Metrology and Quality" under the merged MOST.** The same ministry owns the STI law sandbox, the high-tech lists and IP. So one ministry now holds the standards, science-policy and IP levers a novel-protein firm needs. [VN-direct] High. GT-06.
7. **E10 date resolved: E10 gasoline is mandatory nationwide from 1 June 2026, not 1 January 2026.** Circular 50/2025/TT-BCT (7 November 2025) took effect on 1 January 2026. It allows E5 RON92 until 31 December 2030. Biodiesel (B5, B10) is voluntary only. Cassava now has a regulated fuel buyer, and that demand grows after 2030. [VN-direct] High. GT-10.
8. **Revised PDP8 (Decision 768/QD-TTg, 15 April 2025) targets 28 to 36% non-hydro renewable electricity by 2030 and 74 to 75% by 2050.** Other 2030 targets: solar 46.5 to 73.4 GW, onshore wind 26.1 to 38.0 GW, coal steady at 31.1 GW, LNG 22.5 GW. Nuclear is 4.0 to 6.4 GW in 2030 to 2035, at the former Ninh Thuan sites, now in Khanh Hoa. The plan assumes GDP growth of about 10% a year in 2026 to 2030. [VN-direct] High. GT-11.
9. **UN WPP 2024 (medium variant) puts Vietnam at 104.3 M people in 2030 and 106.5 M in 2035, peaking at 110.0 M in 2049.** Headcount rises only about 4.9% from 2025 to 2035. Demand growth to 2035 will come mainly from income and diet, not from more people. The official GSO 2025 estimate (102.3 M) is about 0.7 M above the UN figure. [VN-direct] Medium (read through Our World in Data). GT-12.
10. **The two 2030 livestock targets agree with each other.** Decision 1520 sets 58 to 62 kg of meat per person. Times the UN 2030 population, that gives 6.05 to 6.46 Mt, which matches its 6.0 to 6.5 Mt output target. The feed target (30 to 32 Mt by 2030) is harder. From about 21.5 Mt in 2024 (unverified base), it needs 5.7 to 6.9% growth a year. [VN-direct] Medium (arithmetic). GT-12, REG-54.
11. **Fishmeal hit USD 2,500/t in August 2026 on the World Bank series.** That is up from USD 1,706/t (2025 average) and USD 1,699/t (2024). Soybean meal was USD 403/t. The fishmeal to soybean meal price ratio widened from 3.8 (2024) to 6.2 (August 2026). This improves the economics of fishmeal replacers faster than wave 1 assumed. [general] High (measured monthly price; may revert). GT-15.
12. **The World Bank forecasts stop at 2027.** April 2026 forecasts: soybean meal USD 359/t (2026) and 363/t (2027); maize USD 211/t and 214/t. Maize had already reached USD 224/t by August 2026. The Bank expects overall commodity prices to rise 16% in 2026 on energy and fertilizer. No official 2030 or 2035 feed price paths were found, so any long-run price must be an explicit scenario assumption. [general] High. GT-14, GT-15.
13. **The legal calendar puts almost all regulatory change for alternative protein in 2025 to 2027.** It runs: STI Law and new CIT Law (1 October 2025), E10 (1 June 2026), high-tech lists (1 July 2026), and the Food Safety Law vote (second 2026 session). Entry into force is possibly 1 January 2027. The carbon market pilot runs to end-2028, with full operation in 2029. The 2030 targets come from Resolutions 36 and 57 and Decision 1520. [VN-direct] Medium to High. See milestones.csv.
14. **Vietnam's NDC 3.0 (2035 target) could not be verified with the tools allowed.** Climate Action Tracker (updated 28 October 2025) still rates the 2022 NDC: 878 Mt CO2e unconditional and 629 Mt conditional for 2030. [VN-direct] Low (gap). GT-16.

- **Resolution 202/2025/QH15** of the National Assembly, 12 June 2025, signed by Chairman Tran Thanh Man. Article 1 has 23 merger clauses and clause 24 lists the 11 unchanged units. Article 2 says the new provincial authorities "officially operate from 1 July 2025". [VN-direct] High. GT-01.
- **Resolution 60-NQ/TW** (Party Central Committee, 11th plenum, 12 April 2025) decided the mergers and the location of each political-administrative centre. It ended district-level units from 1 July 2025 and set a 60 to 70% cut in commune-level units. The two-tier local government model (province and commune) followed from it. [VN-direct] High. GT-02.

#### A3. Size of the new units that matter most for protein supply (Resolution 202 figures)
- **Ho Chi Minh City:** 6,772.59 km2; 14.0 M people. It now holds the Binh Duong industrial parks and the Ba Ria-Vung Tau port and petrochemical cluster (for example the Entobel insect plant site).
- **Tay Ninh (with Long An):** 3.25 M people. It holds much of the cassava starch cluster (wave 1: largest pulp cluster) and the food plants of Long An.
- **Dong Nai (with Binh Phuoc):** 4.49 M people. It holds the Bien Hoa fermentation plants (Vedan, Ajinomoto) and the Binh Phuoc cashew and cassava areas.
- **An Giang (with Kien Giang):** 4.95 M, now the largest province by population. It holds pangasius and shrimp farming.
- **Lam Dong (with Binh Thuan and Dak Nong):** 24,233 km2, the largest by area. It holds the Binh Thuan coast (Vinh Hao spirulina) and highland crops.
- **Can Tho (with Soc Trang and Hau Giang):** 4.20 M. It holds shrimp in Soc Trang and the Can Tho university research base.
- **Ca Mau (with Bac Lieu):** 2.61 M. It is the core shrimp belt.
- **Khanh Hoa (with Ninh Thuan):** 2.24 M. It has the planned nuclear sites and the Nha Trang fisheries research base (the field audit city).
[VN-direct] High for figures; the sector notes come from wave 1. GT-01.

#### A4. Practical rules for this report
- Name locations as "new unit (former X)". Example: "Tay Ninh (former Long An)".
- **Provincial statistics before 2025 use the 63-unit map.** To compare with post-2025 data, add up the former units using the `former_units` column. NSO series for 2025 onward are published on the 34-unit basis (agent knowledge; Medium).
- **There are no longer district names in addresses.** Company addresses now read "ward/commune, province". Older addresses that name a district are not wrong for historical documents but should be converted.

### C. Milestones 2025 to 2035
See `milestones.csv` (30 rows). Key resolutions:

- **E10 (question in the brief):** Circular 50/2025/TT-BCT was issued 7 November 2025 and took effect 1 January 2026. ### E. Scenario scaffold: three plausible states of Vietnamese alternative protein in 2035
**This is scenario thinking, not a forecast.** Each state is internally consistent with the milestones above. Signposts are observable events that would tell a reader which path is unfolding. Probabilities are deliberately not given.

**Scenario 1: "Status quo imports" (price-taker)**
- *Picture in 2035:* Vietnam's alternative protein is mostly imported. It buys Chinese SCP, yeast protein and TVP, and Singapore- or Thai-approved food ingredients. Domestic activity is do chay (traditional vegetarian) processing, tofu and a few insect-meal plants. Feed protein imports grow with the 30 Mt-plus feed industry.
- *What drives it:* the Food Safety Law passes without a "new food" route, or with one that has no workable procedure. Chinese scale keeps landed prices of SCP and TVP below Vietnamese cost. Fishmeal falls back from its 2026 peak. Agritech VC stays near zero.
- *Signposts:* (1) the Food Safety Law and its decree contain no defined "new food / new ingredient" procedure by end-2027; (2) customs data show rising imports of HS 2102 (yeasts), 2106 and 2309 from China; (3) no new domestic extruder or fermentation plant above pilot scale is announced by 2028; (4) fishmeal below USD 1,800/t for a full year.

**Scenario 2: "Feed-first domestic build-out" (side-stream processor)**
- *Picture in 2035:* several Vietnamese plants turn side streams into feed protein for shrimp and fish feed. The side streams are cassava pulp in Tay Ninh (former Long An/Tay Ninh), brewers' spent grain in HCMC and Hai Phong, and seafood by-products in Ca Mau, An Giang and Can Tho. The products are insect meal, yeast or bacterial SCP, and duckweed. Food applications stay small.
- *What drives it:* fishmeal stays high (above about USD 2,000/t). MAE keeps adding new ingredients to the permitted feed lists. The Decision 23/2026 high-tech status and the CIT Law 67/2025 incentives (10% rate for 15 years) are granted to fermentation plants. DFIs repeat the Entobel structure (PE plus DFI plus offtake). E10 demand makes cassava costly, which pushes processors toward pulp and other residues rather than roots.
- *Signposts:* (1) a second insect or SCP plant above 5,000 t/yr reaches financial close by 2028; (2) MAE list updates name SCP or new microbial proteins for aquafeed; (3) a major feed mill (for example a top-5 aquafeed producer) signs a multi-year offtake; (4) fishmeal averages above USD 2,000/t in 2027; (5) MAE reports feed output on track for 30 Mt in 2030.

**Scenario 3: "Regulated food innovation hub" (regional producer)**
- *Picture in 2035:* Vietnam has a working novel-food approval route that relies on Singapore, Korea and other reference regulators. It hosts contract fermentation (precision fermentation and biomass) for ASEAN brands. HCMC (including former Binh Duong) and Dong Nai are the manufacturing base. Domestic brands sell hybrid and fermented products.
- *What drives it:* the Food Safety Law (2026/27) defines new food and allows reliance on reference regulators. An implementing decree for the Law 93/2025 sandbox names MOH or MOST as the approver for food pilots. MOST high-tech certification works for precision fermentation. Resolution 57 R&D money flows into a national protein platform. PDP8 renewables and nuclear make low-carbon power available.
- *Signposts:* (1) the new Food Safety Law or its decree contains a "thực phẩm mới" (new food) definition and procedure; (2) the first sandbox approval for a food product under Law 93/2025; (3) a foreign precision-fermentation or CDMO firm registers an investment certificate in Vietnam; (4) Vietnam joins or aligns with an ASEAN novel-food reliance mechanism; (5) the Thai GMP precision-fermentation pilot plant is built first and draws the same investors (a negative signpost for Vietnam).

**Cross-cutting uncertainties** (the axes that separate the three):
1. Does the food law provide a workable novel-food gate (yes or no)?
2. Where does the fishmeal and soybean meal price gap sit (wide or narrow)?
3. Can Vietnam match Chinese price at scale?
4. Does the state help with money and approvals, not only strategy text?

These are the variables to monitor, not the scenarios' outcomes.

## Implications by audience
- **VCs and investors:** the key regulatory events land in 2026 to 2027 (Food Safety Law, high-tech lists, E10). A thesis can be tested against them within 18 months. The fishmeal to soybean meal ratio (6.2 in August 2026) is a leading indicator for feed-protein deals. Track it monthly on the World Bank Pink Sheet.
- **Policy makers:** after the mergers, one ministry (MAE) holds feed, aquafeed, GMO biosafety, genetic resources and carbon. One ministry (MOST) holds standards, the sandbox, high-tech status and IP. This makes a one-window pilot for novel protein administratively simpler than before 2025. The Food Safety Law window is the one that is closing.
- **Startups:** use the new names (for example "Tay Ninh (former Long An)") in licences and investor documents. Expect provincial officials, not district officials, to handle more feed procedures. The E10 mandate is making cassava roots expensive, so build on residues, not roots.
- **F&B and feed manufacturers:** the 2030 feed target (30 to 32 Mt) implies 5.7 to 6.9% a year of growth from an unverified 21.5 Mt base. That means 8.5 to 10.5 Mt more feed a year by 2030 than in 2024, a large protein-input requirement that could be met by imports or by domestic novel inputs.
- **International bodies:** provincial statistics before and after 2025 are not comparable without re-aggregation. A published 63-to-34 crosswalk (this `provinces.csv`) is a public good. So is support for the novel-food drafting window and a Vietnam NDC 3.0 agriculture annex linking alternative protein to methane targets.
