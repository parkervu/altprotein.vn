# State of the science by technology family (wave 2, SCIENCE agent)

Edition: September 2026. Source prefix: SCI. Tools: Scite (peer-reviewed search and full text), two WebFetch attempts on primary regulatory pages. No WebSearch used.
Companion data file: `science_facts.csv` (43 rows, one per verified or refuted claim, with DOI, full-text flag and retraction check).
Retraction check: every paper cited here was checked for Scite editorial notices. None of the cited papers carries a retraction, correction or expression of concern. One preprint seen in passing (lactoferrin TEA, 10.21203/rs.3.rs-8767901) was withdrawn and is not used.

Evidence labels: [general] = global science, [VN-adjacent] = tropical Asian or same-species work with a transfer assumption, [VN-direct] = Vietnamese data (none found in this wave). Confidence: High = I read the primary text or abstract; Medium = reputable secondary statement; Low = unclear basis or unverified.

## Headline findings

1. **The ">50 g/L titer" benchmark is real, but it is an expert assertion, not a calculated threshold.** Source: Nielsen, Meyer and Arnau, *Annual Review of Food Science and Technology* 15:173-187 (online 2023, issue 2024). Exact wording: "The cost-efficiency requirements involve production titers above 50 g/L". The same group repeats "Protein titers well above 50 g/L are needed" in a 2025 paper. Neither shows the cost model behind the number. [general] High (SCI-01, SCI-02)
2. **Best published leghemoglobin titers are 5 to 15 times below that benchmark.** Secreted leghemoglobin in *Pichia*: 3.5 g/L (Shao 2022). Intracellular leghemoglobin in *Kluyveromyces marxianus*: 7.27 g/L (Tian 2024), now 10.1 g/L in 5 L bioreactors (Chen 2026). Heme proteins are used at only 0.5 to 2% of a product, so the bulk-protein benchmark binds less for them. [general] High (SCI-03 to SCI-05)
3. **OPENPichia is "licence-free" only in a loose sense.** The strain and toolkit come under "liberal end-user distribution licences" that allow royalty-free commercial manufacture, distributed by VIB with the NCYC culture collection. Users still accept a licence. [general] High (SCI-08)
4. **The ICAR-NBFGR striped catfish thymus line "PHT" is real (accession NRFC-078), but it is a disease-research line, not a food line.** It grows in L-15 medium with 20% fetal bovine serum, is epithelial and adherent. A striped catfish gill line exists (2022), but the name "PHG" could not be confirmed. [VN-adjacent] High (SCI-10, SCI-11)
5. **The shrimp line "PmLyO-Sf9" is real, but it is a black tiger shrimp x insect (Sf9) hybrid from Cochin University (CUSAT), not ICAR-NBFGR.** It contains insect genetic material and is used for virus work. No true continuous shrimp cell line was found. The prior lead was partly wrong. [general] High (SCI-12, SCI-13)
6. **Growth-factor cost depends entirely on the price assumption.** Humbird (2021) models growth factors at only USD 3 to 4 per kg wet cells at 100,000 t/yr scale. At today's prices, estimates put growth factors at up to 95% of serum-free medium cost. Both statements are true; they describe different futures. [general] High/Medium (SCI-14, SCI-15)
7. **Best documented cultivated-meat density in animal-free medium is about 28 million cells/mL (chicken cells, Pasitka 2024), below the 140 to 195 g/L wet cells in Humbird's model.** No comparable data exist for fish or shrimp cells. [general] Medium (SCI-14, SCI-16)
8. **The "118.5 g/L A. oryzae biomass" from Karimi et al. (2019) is not a broth concentration and must not be used as a productivity input.** The flask held 100 mL of 5% vinasse with about 21 g/L COD. 11.85 g of dry biomass from 2.1 g of COD is impossible. The figure is most plausibly per litre of undiluted vinasse, i.e. about 5.9 g/L in the flask (my arithmetic). The paper does not state the basis and is internally inconsistent. [general] High (SCI-18)
9. **A. oryzae cannot make aflatoxin, but it can make cyclopiazonic acid (CPA) and 3-nitropropionic acid.** Modern industrial strains carry a deletion that removes both the aflatoxin and CPA gene clusters. Kojic acid is not classed as a mycotoxin. A koji play needs a characterised, deletion-type or tested strain, not a wild starter. [general] High (SCI-19)
10. **Mycoprotein RNA is cut from about 10% to about 2% of dry weight by a 68 C, 20 minute heat step.** This step is described as industrially expensive and is a fixed cost of any fungal or yeast food protein. [general] High (SCI-20)
11. **The "Candida utilis 49 to 57% protein on cassava peel hydrolysate" claim is correct (47.5% glucose, 49.1% acid, 56.7% enzymatic hydrolysate), but these are crude protein values (N x 6.25) that include nucleic-acid nitrogen, from a low-tier journal.** [VN-adjacent] Medium (SCI-22)
12. **In whiteleg shrimp, gas-fermentation protein (CAP) replaces about 30% of fishmeal without growth loss; halving fishmeal with CAP cut growth unless a bile acid was added.** Methanotroph meal had lower protein digestibility than the control diet and reduced growth at 30% dietary inclusion in one Chinese trial. [general] High (SCI-23, SCI-24)
13. **A 2025 preprint reports mixed-culture SCP made from soybean-processing wastewater replacing up to 90% of fishmeal in whiteleg shrimp without growth loss.** Not yet peer reviewed, but directly relevant to Vietnam's tofu and soy-milk wastewater. [VN-adjacent] Medium (SCI-25)
14. **EFSA could not establish the safety of whole Wolffia globosa powder (2021) or mixed water-lentil powder (2021) because of manganese intake. It found a Lemna protein concentrate safe (2023).** Protein extraction solved the problem that blocked whole-plant powders. [general] High (SCI-28 to SCI-30)
15. **The claim that fresh Wolffia plants were deemed "not novel" in the EU could not be verified, and the "PDCAAS 0.89" duckweed figure was not found in any peer-reviewed source.** Both should be dropped until a primary source is shown. [general] Low (SCI-32, SCI-34)
16. **Measured DIAAS (pigs): soy isolate 97, mung bean 86, pea concentrate 70, brown rice concentrate 42 (older child and adult pattern).** For young children: soy 83, mung bean 68, pea 60. In children measured directly, mung bean digestibility was about 20% lower than pig values. [general/VN-adjacent] High (SCI-40 to SCI-42)
17. **No MEASURED rice plus legume blend with DIAAS of 100 or more was found.** Claims of 100 come from calculations (pea plus rice PDCAAS 1.00 on the adult pattern) or meal-level estimates. [general] Medium (SCI-42, SCI-43)
18. **Thermotolerant yeasts growing at 40 to 45 C are well documented (e.g. K. marxianus SS106 isolated from Thai cassava pulp: 39 to 46 g/L biomass at 40 C on raw cassava starch), but no published study quantifies the cooling cost saved in a tropical plant.** [VN-adjacent] High for strains; gap for cost (SCI-44, SCI-45)
19. **Black soldier fly meal is a weak fishmeal substitute in whiteleg shrimp: 20% fishmeal replacement had no growth effect, 30% reduced growth.** [general] High (SCI-27)

## Detailed findings by technology family

### 1. Precision fermentation

**State of the science (2026)**
- Target bulk food proteins need titers "above 50 g/L" according to the leading review (SCI-01). This is an assertion from Novonesis/21st.BIO-linked authors, not a derived TEA threshold. The same authors state cost targets of USD 10/kg by 2025 and USD 1/kg by 2035 as "envisaged" (SCI-02).
- Industrial *A. oryzae* strains (Novozymes lineage) are the most mature hosts. A 2025 study on bovine beta-lactoglobulin raised product amount by 79% and carbon yield by 73% through pH, temperature and feed optimisation, but kept the absolute titer confidential. The strain is being used for a US GRAS notice (SCI-02).
- Leghemoglobin: 3.5 g/L secreted in *Pichia* (SCI-03), 7.27 g/L intracellular in *K. marxianus* (SCI-04), 10.1 g/L in 5 L bioreactors (SCI-05). EFSA assessed soy leghemoglobin from GM *K. phaffii* as a colour additive in 2024 (SCI-09).
- *Trichoderma reesei*: VTT produced beta-lactoglobulin and ovalbumin (Aro et al., Food Research International 163:112131, dated 2023). I could not read the titers (full text denied; the Europe PMC abstract fetch was rate-limited). VTT separately claims more than 80 g/L of one heterologous protein as "unpublished data" and reports brazzein at 1.3 g/L (SCI-06, SCI-07).
- OPENPichia (Claes et al., Nature Microbiology 9:864-876, 2024): genome-sequenced type strain NCYC 2543 with a HOC1 truncation that restores transformability. Near-identical to the patent strain NRRL Y-11430. No strain was consistently better at protein production. Liberal licence, royalty-free commercial manufacture, toolkit free of third-party material transfer agreements (SCI-08).

**What it means for Vietnam**
- A Vietnamese precision-fermentation startup would start 5 to 10 times below the bulk-protein titer benchmark with public strains. High-value, low-dose ingredients (heme proteins, sweet proteins, enzymes) are the only near-term fit.
- OPENPichia removes one freedom-to-operate barrier for FIRI, VNU-HCM and Vabiotech groups that already work with *Pichia*. Product-specific patents (e.g. on leghemoglobin constructs) still apply.
- *K. marxianus* links precision fermentation to thermotolerance (see family 7), which suits Vietnam's climate.

**Corrections to prior claims**
| Prior claim | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ">50 g/L titer needed (Nielsen et al. 2023)" | Right, with caveat | Nielsen MB, Meyer AS, Arnau J, Annu Rev Food Sci Technol 2024 (online 2023). It is an assertion, not a model output. |
| Shao 2022 leghemoglobin 3.5 g/L Pichia | Right | Confirmed via citing paper; original closed-access. |
| Tian 2024 leghemoglobin 7.27 g/L K. marxianus | Right, but superseded | Chen et al. 2026 report 10.1 g/L. |
| Aro 2022 T. reesei BLG/ovalbumin | Partly right | Paper exists (dated 2023, online 2022). Titers not verified. |
| OPENPichia "licence-free" | Partly right | Liberal licence allowing royalty-free commercial use; not literally licence-free. |

### 2. Cultivated seafood

**State of the science (2026)**
- Striped catfish (*Pangasianodon hypophthalmus*) cell lines exist from ICAR-NBFGR (India): thymus line PHT (NRFC-078, 52 passages, 20% FBS, 28 C, epithelial) (SCI-10); a gill line (Sathiyanarayanan et al. 2022) (SCI-11); a caudal fin line (Soni et al. 2018, Acta Tropica) (SCI-47). A skin epithelial line was published from Taiwan in 2025 (SCI-46). All were built for virology and toxicology.
- PmLyO-Sf9 is a *Penaeus monodon* lymphoid x insect Sf9 hybrid (Anoop et al. 2021, CUSAT). It supports WSSV gene expression and IHHNV replication (SCI-12, SCI-13). It is not a shrimp food cell line.
- Humbird (2021): absolute maximum density 258 g/L wet cells; 110 g/L fed-batch before ammonia inhibition; 140 g/L with one perfusion filter; 195 g/L oxygen-limited. With inefficient metabolism, inhibition caps density at 7 to 20 g/L. Growth factors add only USD 3 to 4/kg at 100 kt/yr (assumed bulk prices). Conclusion: economics "would likely preclude" affordability as food (SCI-14).
- At current prices, growth factors can be up to 95% of serum-free medium cost, and medium about 98% of raw-material cost at scale (Negulescu et al. 2023, cited in SCI-15).
- Best documented animal-free result: chicken cells at 28 million cells/mL over 20 days of repeated harvest, medium cost cut 38% (Pasitka et al. 2024). A companion analysis modelled USD 13.67/kg for a 50:50 cell and plant hybrid product (SCI-16, SCI-17). If cells weigh 2,000 to 3,000 pg, 28 million cells/mL is roughly 56 to 84 g/L wet (my arithmetic), below Humbird's 140 to 195 g/L.

**What it means for Vietnam**
- Vietnam has no food-grade fish or shrimp cell line to start from. The Indian catfish lines are a useful research tool (same species as Vietnamese tra) but would need serum-free adaptation, suspension growth and myogenic or adipogenic character.
- Any shrimp cultivated-seafood claim built on PmLyO-Sf9 should be treated as misinformed: the line is part insect.
- The prior conclusion "watch, don't build" is supported by the science.

**Corrections to prior claims**
| Prior claim | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ICAR-NBFGR PHT (thymus) line | Right | NRFC-078 confirmed. Not a food line. |
| ICAR-NBFGR PHG (gill) line | Partly right | A gill line is published (2022); the name "PHG" is not confirmed in what I read. |
| PmLyO-Sf9 shrimp hybrid (grouped with NBFGR) | Partly wrong | Exists, but from CUSAT, black tiger shrimp, part insect. |
| Growth factors dominate media cost | Depends on price assumption | Up to 95% today; USD 3 to 4/kg in Humbird's scaled model. |

### 3. Fungal biomass (mycoprotein, koji, yeast SCP)

**State of the science (2026)**
- Karimi et al. 2019 (Fermentation 5(4):99): 250 mL flasks, 100 mL of diluted molasses vinasse, 35 C, 72 h, biomass sieved, washed and oven-dried. Reported "yields" 34.3 to 118.5 g DM/L. Medium COD about 21 g/L at 5% vinasse. COD removal is reported on the as-received vinasse basis (835 g/L), and the paper cites related work in "g per litre of vinasse". Mass balance rules out 118.5 g/L in the flask. Protein (N x 6.25): *A. oryzae* 44.7%, *N. intermedia* 57.6%, *R. oryzae* 50.9%. *F. venenatum* and *M. purpureus* did not grow (SCI-18).
- *A. oryzae* safety: no aflatoxin (disabling mutations); CPA and 3-nitropropionic acid possible; industrial strains carry a deletion covering both aflatoxin and CPA clusters; reports of aflatoxigenic "A. oryzae" are misidentified *A. flavus* (SCI-19).
- Mycoprotein: RNA about 10% of dry weight reduced to about 2% by 68 C for 20 min (SCI-20). Protein of *F. venenatum* mycoprotein about 30 to 45% of dry weight (secondary, SCI-21).
- *Candida utilis* on cassava peel hydrolysate: crude protein 47.5 to 56.7%, biomass yield 0.44 to 0.52 g/g sugar, maximum growth temperature 41 C (SCI-22).

**What it means for Vietnam**
- Fungal biomass on cassava or molasses residues is scientifically plausible, but published yields per litre must be read with care. Pilot data from Vietnamese substrates are needed before any cost model.
- A koji route that relies on the tuong tradition still needs strain characterisation (CPA, 3-NPA). Industrial deletion strains exist but are proprietary.
- RNA reduction and drying are unavoidable cost lines for food use; feed use tolerates higher RNA.

**Corrections to prior claims**
| Prior claim | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 118.5 g/L A. oryzae biomass on vinasse | Wrong as a titer | Probably per litre of undiluted vinasse (about 5.9 g/L broth). |
| C. utilis 49 to 57% protein on cassava peel | Right | Crude protein; low-tier journal. |
| A. oryzae metabolite burden (CPA, kojic acid, 3-NPA, aflatoxin) | Partly right | No aflatoxin; CPA and 3-NPA real; kojic acid not a mycotoxin. |

### 4. Microbial protein in aquafeed

**State of the science (2026)**
- Whiteleg shrimp: CAP (84% crude protein) replaces about 30% of fishmeal without growth loss (Aquaculture Reports 2021, cited in SCI-23). At 50% fishmeal replacement (25% to 12.5% of diet) growth fell unless 0.06% chenodeoxycholic acid was added (SCI-24).
- In a digestibility screen at 30% dietary inclusion, CAP protein digestibility was higher than the control, methanotroph meal (*Methylococcus capsulatus* Bath) lower; growth fell with methanotroph meal, BSF and mealworm meals but not with CAP or *Chlorella* (SCI-23). This single trial is not the last word on methanotroph meal; FeedKind-specific shrimp trials were not retrieved in this wave.
- Mixed-culture SCP from soybean-processing wastewater: up to 90% fishmeal replacement in shrimp without growth loss (preprint, SCI-25).
- Catfish and tilapia: brewer's yeast replaced 45% of fishmeal in hybrid "Thai Panga" catfish with better growth (review citation, SCI-26); *S. cerevisiae* used without harm in Nile tilapia (SCI-26).

**What it means for Vietnam**
- Shrimp feed is the right target for bacterial and yeast SCP, but the evidence supports partial (about 30%) fishmeal replacement per ingredient, not full replacement.
- Pangasius and tilapia diets already use little fishmeal, so SCP there competes with soybean meal on price, a harder benchmark.
- Tofu and soy-milk wastewater SCP is a Vietnam-relevant research lead worth a local trial.

### 5. Duckweed and algae

**State of the science (2026)**
- Wolffia protein 20 to 30% of freeze-dry weight in 11 lab-grown species (SCI-34); 44% crude protein in Thai Wolffia, 22% in Lemna, 18% in Spirodela; in vitro standardized ileal protein digestibility 72% (Lemna), 69% (Wolffia) (SCI-35). Drum-dried Wolffia had 32 to 34% protein and in vitro digestibility 81 to 85% (SCI-48).
- Manganese is the documented food-safety issue: 78 to 431 mg/kg dry weight in Wolffia (SCI-34). EFSA could not establish safety of whole Wolffia powder or mixed water-lentil powder for this reason; a Lemna protein concentrate was judged safe (SCI-28 to SCI-30).
- A 2026 review states that Wolffia globosa powder was authorised in the EU by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/2223 and that Thai FDA treats it as traditional food (SCI-31). I could not verify the EU regulation (EUR-Lex fetch blocked).
- Productivity: about 30 t DM/ha/yr is the average cited in a 2026 review; 33 t DM/ha over 8 months in domestic wastewater; "up to 100 t DM/ha/yr" is an extrapolated upper bound (SCI-36, SCI-37).
- Spirulina and microalgae: no DIAAS for microalgal biomass was available as of a 2021 review (SCI-39). Spirulina protein and digestibility figures were not verified in this wave.

**What it means for Vietnam**
- Duckweed grown on aquaculture effluent fits feed; for food, the EU record shows that mineral control (Mn) and controlled water are the gate, and that a protein concentrate is an easier regulatory path than whole-plant powder.
- Planning yields should use 10 to 30 t DM/ha/yr until Vietnamese field trials exist.

**Corrections to prior claims**
| Prior claim | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| EFSA opinion on Wolffia globosa powder (Mankai) | Right that an opinion exists; content is negative | Safety not established (Mn), 2021. |
| Fresh Wolffia not novel in the EU | Unverified | Not found in the EC consultation list as fetched. |
| Duckweed PDCAAS 0.89 | Unsupported | No source found. |

### 6. Plant protein quality

**State of the science (2026)**
- Pig ileal DIAAS (older child and adult pattern): soy isolate 97, pea concentrate 70, brown rice concentrate 42; young-child pattern: soy 83, pea 60 (SCI-40). Cooked mung bean 86 (older) and 68 (young child) (SCI-41).
- In Indian children, true ileal digestibility of mung bean was 65.2%, rice 78.5%, egg 87.4%; mung bean was about 20% lower than pig estimates (SCI-42).
- No measured rice plus legume blend DIAAS of 100 or more found; only calculated values (SCI-43).

**What it means for Vietnam**
- Mung bean is a strong local legume by pig DIAAS but weaker in humans; rice protein alone is poor (lysine-limited).
- A Vietnamese DIAAS reference dataset (flagged as a gap in wave 1) is justified by these gaps.

### 7. Tropical fermentation

**State of the science (2026)**
- *K. marxianus* SS106, isolated from Thai cassava pulp and starch-factory soil, grows at 35 to 45 C and reached 39 to 46 g/L cell mass at 40 C on raw cassava starch in 5 L reactors (SCI-44).
- The genome of *K. marxianus* DMKU 3-1042 explains its thermotolerance; high-temperature fermentation is "expected" to reduce cooling cost and contamination (SCI-45). No quantified cooling-cost analysis for a tropical plant was found.

**What it means for Vietnam**
- The strain toolkit for 40 to 45 C fermentation exists and matches cassava feedstocks. The cooling-cost saving remains an estimate (wave 1 cost agent: USD 70 to 180/t biomass for chilled water), not a published measurement.

### 8. Insects (benchmark only)

- Black soldier fly larvae meal: 20% fishmeal replacement in whiteleg shrimp without growth loss; 30% reduced growth (SCI-27). BSF meal had lower protein digestibility than control in a shrimp screen (SCI-23).
- Implication: as a shrimp-feed benchmark, insect meal displaces only a small share of fishmeal. Microbial proteins such as CAP match or exceed it on digestibility.

## Implications by audience

- **VCs and investors:** Discount any precision-fermentation plan that assumes bulk-protein economics at published titers (best 10 g/L vs 50 g/L target). Treat cultivated seafood in Vietnam as pre-science for food use. In feed, back microbial protein for partial fishmeal replacement in shrimp, not full replacement.
- **Policy makers:** The EU duckweed record shows what a regulator will ask: mineral limits (Mn), controlled cultivation, allergenicity. Vietnam can set these specifications ahead of applications.
- **Startups:** Use thermotolerant yeasts (*K. marxianus*) on cassava streams; budget for RNA reduction and drying for food; demand strain safety data (CPA, 3-NPA) for any *Aspergillus* process.
- **Feed manufacturers:** Plan inclusion trials at 20 to 30% fishmeal replacement per novel ingredient in shrimp; expect bile-acid or blend strategies beyond that.
- **Academia:** Four quick public goods: (1) a DIAAS dataset for Vietnamese proteins; (2) serum-free adaptation of *P. hypophthalmus* cells; (3) SCP from tofu wastewater in shrimp trials; (4) duckweed Mn and heavy-metal profiling on Mekong water.
