# Wave 7, step 1: investors and startups against Part VI

**Actor group:** investors and startups. **Source prefix:** AIS. **Date:** 25 September 2026.
**Files:** `actors.csv` (20 actors), `questions.csv` (25 questions), `candidate_lines.csv` (14 lines), `sources.csv` (48 sources), `new_facts.csv` (17 facts).

Tags: `[evidence label, confidence, source, demand evidence type]`. Demand types: stated, revealed, tested, inferred. "Our calculation" marks our own arithmetic.

## How we worked

We read the Part VI chapter summaries and the sections that matter to this group (ch23.4 to 23.7, ch24, ch25, ch26, ch28, ch29), the investor and startup briefs, and the data files `launches_exits.csv`, `investors.csv`, `deals.csv`, `demand_moves.csv` and the Part VI open questions. We then opened primary pages for each actor: investor sites and reports, press releases, company pages and trade press from 2024 to 2026 where they exist. Older statements (2021 to 2023) are used only for an actor's mandate and are marked. Several pages were read through a fetch summary; Vietnamese pages were translated that way; paywalled pages are used for headlines only and marked. The session's web-search budget ran out near the end, so two checks (preferential tariffs, Vinh Hoan's 2025 annual report) are left as candidate lines.

## Headline findings

1. **Investors now say what they fund, and it is transactional.** In 2025 to 2026 investors and analysts cite "strong offtake agreements", "contracted revenue", "real unit economics" and "clearer unit economics" [general, Medium, AIS-01; AIS-02; AIS-47, stated]. Vietnam's own private-capital report says the same: "capital is still available, but investors are concentrating on stronger founders, clearer unit economics, and more mature opportunities" [VN-direct, Medium, AIS-15, stated]. Part VI gives demand pools and inferred buyer lists but no transaction evidence (no letter of intent, trial order, qualification time or margin). This is the largest gap for this group.

2. **Regional startups have already made Part VI's pivot, and it is revealed, not stated.** TiNDLE (Singapore-founded) announced in November 2025 a move to private label only and the divestment of its US business; its co-founder said "there is no real underlying consumer demand to shift away from meat" and its CEO that products were "still 50% more expensive" than meat [general, Medium, AIS-28; AIS-29, revealed]. Vow moved to co-manufacturing in 2026 [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-37, revealed]; Avant and Umami Bioworks now earn from skincare and pet food [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-34; AIS-35; AIS-36, revealed]; INNOHAS, the one plant-based company a Vietnamese VC backed, is an OEM and private-label maker [VN-direct, Low, AIS-16, revealed]; Meatiply chose co-development with manufacturers over its own brand [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-46, stated]. The shift is structural: B2B firms rose from 26% (2021) to 75% (2022) of new Asia-Pacific alternative-protein startups, and one investor said "We wouldn't invest in a company purely servicing the cultivated meat industry" [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-04, revealed]. This strengthens Part VI's core conclusion with firm behaviour rather than consumer surveys.

3. **The Southeast Asian survivors sell through foodservice and familiar dishes, and so did Vietnamese founders.** Green Rebel raised USD 12.5 million (PPTI) or USD 10.5 million (DealStreetAsia) in early 2026, and "foodservice partnerships have played a central role in the company's expansion" [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-30; AIS-31, revealed]. VMEAT earned 70% of revenue from F&B in 2021, and its founders also run a chay noodle chain [VN-direct, Low, AIS-41, revealed]; Emmay took 80% from wholesale and supermarkets in 2022 [VN-direct, Low, AIS-40, revealed]; Kashew and Ong Tempeh sell to hotels, restaurants and caterers [VN-direct, Low, AIS-42; AIS-43, revealed]. Part VI does not use this founder evidence, although it supports ch25.7.

4. **No regional alternative-protein investor we read names Vietnam as a market in 2024 to 2026.** Big Idea Ventures' 2026 site, Better Bite's statements, AgFunder's 2026 report page, TiNDLE, Green Rebel, Growthwell and Thai Union's strategy do not mention Vietnam [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-11; AIS-12; AIS-02; AIS-28; AIS-30; AIS-33; AIS-23]. Vietnam appears only as a production location ("Australia, Thailand, and Vietnam have all the right ingredients" for fermentation, GFI APAC) or generically (Temasek portfolio companies "see opportunities to invest in ... Vietnam") [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-05; AIS-07, stated]. Clean negative: AgFunder has published no Asia-Pacific report since the 2024 edition [general, Medium, AIS-03].

5. **Regional suppliers are already inside the B2B channels Part VI recommends.** Seven CP Meat Zero *chay* SKUs, including 1 kg packs, are listed on Kamereo, a B2B supplier to more than 4,000 restaurants, cafes and hotels, at VND 244,000 per kg (minced) and VND 35,000 per 200 g (roast) [VN-direct, Low, AIS-25; AIS-26, revealed; metadata only]. Angel Yeast's Vietnamese distributor launched yeast protein to local food makers in May 2025 [VN-direct, Medium, AIS-38, revealed]. Part VI frames competition as Chinese commodity plant protein and records Meat Zero as not found (`launches_exits.csv` LE-09).

6. **Part VI upgrades T1 without the numbers an investor needs.** Ch29.3 raises domestic textured protein to market pull 5, but the only cost evidence is Part I's ex-factory USD 792 to 2,065 per t with "no verified extrusion capex", which straddles the Chinese border price of USD 1.24 per kg (2025) [VN-direct, Low, Part I ch09.4; ch26.2, inferred]. A 1 t per hour line needs about 7,000 t a year of offtake (ch28.6), about USD 9 million of revenue at that price (our calculation). Part VI cites only MFN duties (3 to 10%); the package lists ACFTA and ATIGA rates as "not verified". An investor cannot underwrite margin, payback or tariff protection from the package.

7. **Part VI's own model says the lead thesis may fail impact screens.** Import substitution (route R1, about 60% of benchmark demand in 2035) displaces no meat (ch28.2). ADB Ventures expects "80% of the investments ... to have a strong climate mitigation and/or adaptation impact" [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-22, stated]. Brief-investors proposes a development-finance first-loss tranche without saying that DFI and climate money fits hybrids, canteens and feed better than T1 (our inference).

8. **Strategic buyers have stepped back, so the trade-sale exit is thinner than the package implies.** Thai Union's Strategy 2030 puts alternative proteins under "exploring new frontiers" [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-23, revealed]; Monde Nissin halted a fifth Quorn fermentation plant ("It's a declining market ... we built all the capacity") and Quorn revenue fell a further 2.9% in 2025 [general, Medium, AIS-44; AIS-48, revealed]; Beyond Meat suspended China operations in 2025 [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-45, revealed]; CJ's 2026 Vietnam push is K-food through Bach Hoa Xanh with no plant-based mention [VN-direct, Medium, AIS-27, revealed]. Part VI does not ask who would buy a Vietnamese ingredient company.

9. **Vietnamese seafood exporters' cultivated options look stale.** Avant's Singapore unit declared voluntary winding-up in January 2026 [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-34, revealed]; Umami (with Shiok) sells pet food and skincare ingredients, not seafood [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-35; AIS-36, revealed]. Ch24.6 still tells investors that "Vinh Hoan and Minh Phu are the domestic strategic co-investors for seafood".

10. **Blends have a Temasek-linked retail launch but no sales data.** Temasek's Nurasa co-developed Q Protein, soy-enhanced beef and chicken sold through a meat importer in Singapore (September 2025), and GFI APAC lists large-scale blended rollouts in Asian foodservice as a 2026 watch item [VN-adjacent, Medium, AIS-10; AIS-06, revealed]. This supports TPP-03, and shows the partner model (meat brand plus protein supplier) that Part VI recommends but has no Vietnamese partner for.

11. **Vietnam's one plant-based VC now runs the city's public venture fund.** VinaCapital operates the VND 500 billion HCM VIF and Hoang Duc Trung, the VinaCapital partner quoted on the 2024 INNOHAS deal, is its CEO; the fund targets Series A and B and plans its first one or two investments in Q4 2026 [VN-direct, Medium, AIS-16; AIS-17; AIS-18, revealed]. INNOHAS shows no Vietnam launch two years on (package). The first public venture money that could reach a protein venture will be screened by the investor whose Vietnam plant-based thesis has not yet produced a buyer.

## Coverage verdict

Of 25 questions, Part VI answers 1, partly answers 19, does not answer 3 and treats 2 as out of scope. Of the 13 questions with high decision value, 11 rest on weak or no evidence in Part VI.

**Where Part VI serves this group well.**
- Its core thesis (ingredients inside familiar foods, sold to firms) matches what investors now say they fund and what regional startups actually did (findings 1 to 3). An investor reading ch26, ch28 and ch29 gets the right shape of opportunity.
- The top-down pool is traceable: about USD 87 to 109 million a year of plant-protein imports and about USD 57 million of benchmark ingredient value in 2035 (ch26.8, ch28.3), with a clear warning against untraceable market sizes (ch28.1). Q12 is partly answered with moderate evidence: the whole 2035 pool is small, which tells a venture investor to look for regional or export revenue.
- The price ladder (ch26.2) and target product profiles with kill tests (ch29.2, ch29.3) are directly usable in diligence.
- Displacement factors by route (ch28.2, ch28.4) answer the impact question (Q15) that DFIs and climate funds ask, even though the briefs do not draw the conclusion.

**Where Part VI fails this group.**
- **No transaction evidence (Q02, Q03).** First customers are inferred from labels and trade data; there is no offtake letter, trial order, minimum order or qualification time. Investors now ask for exactly these.
- **No unit economics (Q01, Q25).** The T1 upgrade to market pull 5 is not matched by margin, capex or tariff evidence; the competitor's duty-free access is unverified.
- **Competition inside the recommended channels is missing (Q06, Q11, Q19).** Regional finished chay (Meat Zero), regional OEMs (Growthwell, INNOHAS) and Chinese yeast protein are already selling or marketing into the same buyers.
- **Founder base rates are unused (Q07, Q13).** Part VI tracks brands on shelves, not firm outcomes; the Vietnamese and regional founder evidence in findings 2 and 3 would strengthen its channel advice.
- **Exit demand is not asked (Q04).** Who buys a Vietnamese ingredient company, when regional strategics are retreating, is outside Part VI and weakly covered in Part I.
- **Stale corporate signals (Q10).** Ch24.6's advice on seafood co-investors needs updating.

## Candidate lines, ranked

Most lines are a different kind of question from wave 6: they ask what firms and markets have done or will commit to, not what consumers say.

1. **Priced offer test (AIS-L01), calls and field.** Send a specification and a price at Chinese parity to 10 to 15 named buyers and ask for conditional trial orders. Three or more keep T1 at 5 and give investors the offtake evidence they ask for; none returns T1 to 4. Also yields qualification time and minimum orders.
2. **T1 and T10 unit-economics card (AIS-L02), desk now.** Quoted capex, inputs, landed competitor prices including ACFTA and ATIGA duty, margin at parity, payback. A gross margin under about 15% at parity makes T1 an incumbent or debt project, not a venture thesis.
3. **Founder base-rate register (AIS-L04), desk now.** Firm-level first channel, revenue mix, funding and status for about 17 startups in Vietnam and the region. Tests Part VI's "business customer before brand" advice with revealed firm outcomes.
4. **Competitor price scan inside B2B channels (AIS-L05), desk now plus quotes.** HoReCa platforms, distributors and imported chay, in VND per 100 g of protein. Adds a competitor price to the channel table and the price targets of TPP-02, TPP-04 and TPP-06.
5. **Revealed non-entry (AIS-L03), calls plus desk.** Why no one has built a textured-protein line despite about 40,000 t of food-grade imports. Cost reasons downgrade T1; capital or scale reasons point to a co-investment with chay makers.
6. **Monthly trade seasonality (AIS-L11), desk now.** Lunar-calendar peaks in monthly imports would give founders working-capital advice and a first split of food from feed behind the 26,000 t protein pool.
7. **Impact-screen mapping (AIS-L06), desk now.** Displacement and CO2e per tonne for each target product profile against DFI criteria; likely moves DFI money from T1 to hybrids, canteens and feed.
8. **Supplier-side pilot tracking (AIS-L10), calls.** Distributors' records of Vietnamese trials of yeast, fungal and pulse proteins could replace the ch26.6 clean negative.
9. **Acquirer appetite scan (AIS-L07), desk now.** Replaces "exits are trade sales" with evidence.
10. **Investor threshold calls (AIS-L09), calls.** Whether any investor has Vietnam in mandate and what demand proof unlocks a round.
11. **Private-label interviews (AIS-L08), calls.** Whether OEM supply to Vietnamese retailers is a founder route.
12. **Foodservice procurement interviews (AIS-L12), calls; deeper.**
13. **Vinh Hoan stake status (AIS-L13), desk now; deeper.**
14. **Export buyer interviews through existing exporters (AIS-L14), calls; deeper.**
