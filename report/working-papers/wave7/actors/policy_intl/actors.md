# Wave 7, step 1: policy makers, research bodies and international organisations

**Actor group:** policy makers, research bodies and international organisations. **Source prefix:** APR. **Date:** 25 September 2026.

**What this paper does.** It checks Part VI (the demand side, edition 1.2) against what 20 real actors in this group say they care about, in their own documents. For each actor it derives the demand questions they would need answered before acting on alternative protein in Vietnam, checks whether Part VI answers them, and proposes research lines. It also records new facts that change Part VI.

**Tags.** Claims carry `[evidence label, confidence, source; demand evidence type]`. Demand evidence types follow the package: stated, revealed, tested, inferred. A government rule or guidance is tagged "stated" (a stated specification by an institutional buyer or regulator). "Our calculation" marks derived numbers; inputs are shown in section 6.

**Files in this folder.** `actors.csv` (20 actors), `questions.csv` (33 questions), `candidate_lines.csv` (12 lines), `sources.csv` (45 sources, APR-01 to APR-45), `new_facts.csv` (20 facts).

## 1. Headline findings

1. **No Vietnamese plan sets a target on meat intake, plant protein or diet shift.** The operative text of the National Nutrition Strategy (Decision 02/QĐ-TTg, 2022) contains no word for protein, meat, plant or legumes. The food systems action plan (Decision 300/QĐ-TTg, 2023) sets stunting, overweight, export and a 10% emissions cut, not protein. Resolutions 71 and 72 (2025) and the dairy strategy (2026) are silent on protein sources. Part V's "no protein norm" finding stands. [VN-direct, High, APR-01; APR-02; APR-05; APR-06; APR-11; stated]

2. **But current health guidance already makes room for plant protein, and Part VI missed it.** The Ministry of Health's Ten tips to 2030 (November 2024) say eat fish, poultry and nuts, seeds and legumes daily and eat red meat in moderation. Its disease-prevention guidance (Decision 1982/QĐ-BYT, 1 July 2026) tells adults to use diverse animal and plant protein, prioritising fish, lean meat, eggs, milk and legumes. Part VI cites only a 2021 press figure for the meat recommendation. [VN-direct, High, APR-17; APR-16; APR-18; stated]

3. **Part VI's school conclusion rests on an expired target.** Part VI says school meals are "guided by an old target to raise the animal share of children's protein" (Decision 1340, a school milk programme to 2020). The Ministry of Health's school-meal guidance of 25 December 2025 (Decision 3958/QĐ-BYT) has no animal-share target. It sets protein at 13 to 20% of energy, animal protein at least 4 to 5 days a week, and **plant protein (legumes, tofu) at least twice a week**, and it lists processed meats and sausages among foods to limit. The plant-protein slot exists; today tofu and legumes fill it. [VN-direct, High, APR-04; stated]

4. **The rules for public meals are being written now, and that is the demand-side policy window.** The School Health Programme 2026 to 2035 (Decision 973/QĐ-TTg, 1 June 2026) requires rules on bán trú (school lunch and midday care) meals; the education ministry plans a unified national school menu; Resolution 71 requires legal rules on school nutrition; UNICEF's 2027 to 2031 programme will support "strengthened school food and complementary food standards". These will decide whether processed plant or blended proteins are welcome or restricted in schools. Part VI does not mention any of them. [VN-direct, Medium, APR-12; APR-13; APR-06; APR-27; stated]

5. **The Food Safety Law timetable has moved.** On 24 September 2026 the National Assembly Standing Committee agreed first comments in October 2026 and a vote in May 2027. The draft (76 articles) adds risk-based control, traceability for high-risk foods and duties for non-commercial collective meal providers. Part VI and the briefs still say the key step is the October to November 2026 session. [VN-direct, Medium, APR-07; APR-08; stated]

6. **Public-meal money sits at the plant floor of the price ladder.** Border-commune lunch support is VND 450,000 a month, about VND 20,500 to 22,500 a school day. The school guidance implies about 14 to 43 g of protein per primary-school lunch, about 25 g at the midpoint. If protein items take 30 to 50% of the cash, protein costs about VND 24,000 to 45,000 per 100 g (USD 0.92 to 1.73) (our calculation). Tofu fits; eggs and chicken are at the edge; a branded retail analogue (VND 112,000, Part VI) does not. [VN-direct, Low, APR-09; APR-04; inferred]

7. **For children, protein is a micronutrient question that Part VI does not address.** Zinc deficiency affected 53.3% of children aged 6 to 59 months in 2020; the school guidance's own appendix rates zinc absorption low when diets contain little animal protein; stunting is 32% among ethnic minority children (2023). A plant or blended product for schools or stunting programmes needs fortification and protein-quality evidence before NIN or UNICEF could endorse it. [VN-direct, Medium, APR-42; APR-04; APR-27; inferred]

8. **A new fiscal risk for plant milks.** From 2027 an 8% excise (10% from 2028) applies to sugary "nước giải khát" above 5 g of sugar per 100 ml; milk and dairy are exempt. The sources we read do not say whether sweetened soy and nut milks are taxed. If they are, Part VI's finding that plant milk sells at or below dairy prices weakens. [VN-direct, Medium, APR-19; APR-20; APR-22; stated]

9. **Ministries pull in different directions on protein.** The livestock strategy targets 58 to 62 kg of carcass meat per person by 2030 (FAO supply was already 60.5 kg in 2023); the dairy strategy targets about 40 litres of milk per person by 2030 and "school meals with fresh milk"; the health ministry advises moderating red meat. Part VI shows the health side only. [VN-direct, High, APR-10; APR-11; APR-17; stated]

10. **No international body in this group names plant or alternative protein in its Vietnam documents.** Their entry points are different: school food standards (UNICEF), NAP-FST monitoring and food balance sheets (FAO, CGIAR), sugary-drink and salt policy (WHO), nutrition-tagged agrifood lending (ADB), rice methane (World Bank), market access for US beef, dairy, soybeans and nuts (USDA), nutrition-sensitive smallholder research (ACIAR). [VN-direct, Medium, APR-27; APR-25; APR-21; APR-31; APR-29; APR-34; APR-35; stated]

11. **International routes for school-meal support are thin.** Vietnam is not listed as a School Meals Coalition member; we found no current WFP Vietnam country page; GAIN lists no Vietnam programme; the EU's 2026 Global Gateway package is for transport and energy. Part VI's advice to funders to "fund channel trials" names no funder with a mandate. [VN-adjacent, Low, APR-38; APR-39; APR-40; APR-37; APR-32; revealed]

12. **A cheap diet-tracking source Part VI did not use.** The Global Diet Quality Project's Vietnam round (December 2021 to January 2022) found that on the previous day 40% of adults ate pulses and 18% ate processed meats, against 92% who ate meat, poultry or fish. It is a repeatable instrument that could carry a chay-day or plant-protein item. [VN-direct, Medium, APR-36; revealed]

## 3. Actor-by-actor notes

### Vietnamese government

**APR-A01 Ministry of Health (MOH).** The actor with the most direct say over whether new protein enters public meals. Its stated goals are the Nutrition Strategy targets (stunting below 15%, below 23% in mountain areas by 2030; overweight caps; salt below 7 g a day) [VN-direct, High, APR-01; stated]. In the past ten months it issued school-meal guidance with a plant-protein minimum (APR-04), adult and shift-meal guidance favouring legumes among other proteins (APR-16) and the Ten tips (APR-17). It leads the Food Safety Law. None of these documents names alternative, novel or plant-based products; they name legumes and tofu. **Demand questions:** can processed plant or blended dishes fill the plant slot at tofu-like cost with adequate zinc and iron (APR-Q01); what supplier documents and claim rules will apply (APR-Q07, APR-Q33). Part VI does not answer the first and partly answers the second.

**APR-A02 National Institute of Nutrition (NIN).** The technical gatekeeper: recommended intakes (Decision 116/QĐ-VDD, 12 February 2026, cited in APR-16), food composition data, menu tools, national surveys, and strategic partner of CGIAR's diet programmes [VN-direct, High, APR-23; APR-24]. It signs MoUs with firms for research and communication (a supplement distributor, 9 September 2026), which is a route an ingredient maker could use [VN-direct, Low, APR-42; revealed]. Its director still cites 2020 survey figures. **Demand questions:** protein quality and micronutrient bioavailability of candidate dishes for children (APR-Q04); how a new ingredient gets into composition tables and school menu software (APR-Q05). Part VI answers neither.

**APR-A03 Ministry of Education and Training (MOET).** Runs the school system that buys the meals. Its 2026 to 2035 plans include a unified national nutrition menu and rules on bán trú meal organisation [VN-direct, Medium, APR-12; APR-13; stated]. The Ajinomoto School Meal Project, run with MOET and MOH, says its menu software was used in 4,262 primary schools by March 2023 [VN-direct, Low, APR-41; stated, company claim]; any new ingredient would need to be in such tools. HCMC schools could not start bán trú in September 2026 because of post-merger paperwork, a reminder that operations, not only trust, limit the channel [VN-direct, Low, APR-45; revealed]. **Demand questions:** national bán trú numbers and protein spend (APR-Q02); parent acceptance after the pork case (APR-Q03); what a unified menu will allow (APR-Q08).

**APR-A04 Ministry of Agriculture and Environment (MAE).** Leads the food systems action plan, the FST Partnership (47 partners at signature in October 2024) and the International Support Group [VN-direct, Medium, APR-25; revealed]. Decision 300 gives it the food balance sheets "at national, regional and provincial level" [VN-direct, Medium, APR-02; stated]. Its sector goals push meat per person up or flat (58 to 62 kg by 2030) and emissions per unit down [VN-direct, High, APR-10; stated]. **Demand questions:** can the food balance sheets carry protein by source (APR-Q10); how the next livestock strategy treats meat per person (APR-Q12). Part VI answers neither; they matter more for the policy brief than for products.

**APR-A05 Politburo, National Assembly and Government.** Sets the windows. Resolution 72 (9 September 2025) asks for nutrition "suited to Vietnamese physique, culture and economic conditions" and new laws on disease prevention and food safety [VN-direct, High, APR-05; stated]. Resolution 71 (22 August 2025) asks for legal rules on school nutrition [VN-direct, High, APR-06; stated]. The Law on Disease Prevention (114/2025/QH15) came into force on 1 July 2026 and encourages school meals with food safety and rational nutrition [VN-direct, Medium, APR-14; APR-15; stated]. Parliament passed the sugary-drink excise in June 2025 [VN-direct, Medium, APR-19; stated]. The Government's Decree 339/2025 funds border-commune lunches [VN-direct, High, APR-09; revealed]. **Demand questions:** APR-Q07, APR-Q08, APR-Q09.

**APR-A06 Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT).** Under the Nutrition Strategy it must develop markets for fortified and "healthy" foods [VN-direct, High, APR-01; stated]; we found no criteria or instrument. The dairy strategy (Decision 309, 2026) promotes "school meals with fresh milk" and says nothing about plant milks [VN-direct, Medium, APR-11; stated]. Low demand-side interest in alternative protein. **Demand questions:** APR-Q13, APR-Q14.

**APR-A07 Ministry of Science and Technology (MOST).** Under the Nutrition Strategy it funds S&T tasks on nutrition and food and sets food standards [VN-direct, High, APR-01; stated]. We found no stated demand-side interest beyond this. Its likely question is whether a funded project has a named adopter (APR-Q15), which Part VI's buyer map partly answers. Our web-search budget ran out before its 2026 programme documents could be checked.

**APR-A08 Provinces and cities.** They pay for and supervise school meals and run provincial FST pilots (Dong Thap and Son La) [VN-direct, Medium, APR-23; revealed]. Part VI covers Hanoi's budgets well. It does not cover stunting-hotspot provinces, where Decree 339 now fixes lunch money. **Demand questions:** protein spend and cost-effectiveness in upland schools (APR-Q16); whether provincial FST plans include protein or legume actions (APR-Q17).

### Research bodies

**APR-A09 VAAS, IPSARD (now ISPAE) and Can Tho University.** The national partners of CGIAR's diet work: VAAS and IPSARD as strategic partners; Can Tho University leading the food-systems training rollout [VN-direct, Medium, APR-23; revealed]. IPSARD co-authored Vietnam's Food-EPI assessment [VN-direct, High, APR-26]. We found no stated interest in alternative protein. **Demand questions:** domestic legume supply for public meals (APR-Q18); which food-environment levers shift protein choices (APR-Q19).

**APR-A10 CGIAR in Vietnam (Alliance of Bioversity and CIAT, IFPRI, ILRI).** Vietnam is one of three target countries of the Better Diets and Nutrition programme; its coordinator supports provincial FST plans and M&E [VN-direct, Medium, APR-24; revealed]. The Food-EPI study found 74% of 35 food-environment indicators low or very low, school food policies mostly non-mandatory and workplace and hospital meal guidelines not binding [VN-direct, Medium, APR-26; inferred]. ILRI's food-safety group set 2026 to 2030 priorities around traditional markets and small slaughterhouses [VN-direct, Medium, APR-30; stated]. **Demand questions:** a Vietnamese sustainable healthy reference diet (APR-Q20); whether pork scares shift institutional buyers (APR-Q21).

### International organisations

**APR-A11 FAO Vietnam.** Supports the NAP-FST and a "green, low-emission and climate-resilient" agrifood transition; its representative calls 2026 to 2030 "decisive" [VN-direct, Medium, APR-25; stated]. It would engage with protein only through the NAP-FST: an indicator in the food balance sheets or M&E (APR-Q10). We did not read the FAO Country Programming Framework in this session.

**APR-A12 UNICEF Vietnam.** The clearest international stakeholder in public meals. Its 2027 to 2031 programme reports stunting at 18% (2023) and 32% among ethnic minority children, and will prioritise "strengthened school food and complementary food standards, and marketing restrictions" [VN-direct, High, APR-27; stated]. It would ask whether processed plant or blended products count as healthy or as processed foods to limit (APR-Q23) and whether they are cost-effective against eggs and milk for stunting (APR-Q24). Part VI answers neither.

**APR-A13 WHO Vietnam.** Its diet agenda is fiscal and nutrient-based: it pushed the sugary-drink excise as a "win-win" [VN-direct, Medium, APR-21; stated]. **Demand questions:** sodium and sugar in chay foods, analogues and plant drinks (APR-Q25); whether plant milks fall under the excise (APR-Q09).

**APR-A14 World Bank.** Its 2024 agrifood climate report frames diet shifts for high-income countries and asks middle-income countries to cut livestock and rice emissions [general, Medium, APR-28; stated]. In Vietnam its climate operation is low-methane rice and carbon credits [VN-direct, Medium, APR-29; revealed]. Low demand relevance; its question would be cost per tonne CO2e (APR-Q26), which Part VI leaves to Part V.

**APR-A15 ADB.** Commits USD 40 billion to food security in 2022 to 2030, with about a quarter of the new USD 26 billion for nutrition (page metadata, paywalled article) [VN-adjacent, Low, APR-31; stated]. Its Vietnam director cites "integrating nutrition and healthy diets" [VN-direct, Medium, APR-25; stated]. **Demand question:** an investable nutrition-tagged protein chain with public buyers (APR-Q27).

**APR-A16 European Union.** The March 2026 Global Gateway package (EUR 560 million) is for transport and clean energy; the country page lists ecological agriculture in the Mekong Delta but no food or nutrition programme [VN-direct, Medium, APR-32; APR-33; stated]. Its demand relevance runs through EVFTA buyers, which Part VI ch27 covers.

**APR-A17 USDA Foreign Agricultural Service.** Promotes US beef, dairy, whey proteins, soybeans and tree nuts; notes tree nut imports of USD 3.9 billion in 2025, up 40%, partly for plant-based milk demand among younger consumers [VN-direct, Medium, APR-34; revealed]. That is revealed evidence that strengthens Part VI's plant-milk finding.

**APR-A18 ACIAR.** Priorities include "nutrition-sensitive agriculture, food safety and One Health" and smallholder livestock [VN-direct, Medium, APR-35; stated]. A legume project with a public buyer (upland schools) is the only plausible fit (APR-Q30).

**APR-A19 GAIN and the Global Diet Quality Project.** GAIN lists no Vietnam programme [VN-direct, Low, APR-37; revealed]. Its Diet Quality Questionnaire does cover Vietnam (headline 12) and is the cheapest national tracker Part VI could use (APR-Q31).

**APR-A20 School Meals Coalition and WFP.** Vietnam is not listed as a member and was not among the member states at the 2024 Southeast Asia summit; WFP's Vietnam country page returned "not found" [VN-adjacent, Low, APR-38; APR-39; APR-40; revealed]. No international school-meal finance route to Vietnam found.

**Not examined in depth.** GIZ, JICA, UNDP and IFC. We found no retrievable GIZ or JICA document on food or nutrition in Vietnam in this session; the UNDP NDC page was blocked; IFC is covered by the funders group.

## 4. Coverage verdict: where Part VI serves this group, and where it fails

**Where Part VI serves policy makers, research bodies and international organisations well.**
- The protein price ladder and the canteen budget arithmetic are exactly what a public-meal specifier needs, and they are revealed evidence (ch21.3, ch25.3).
- "Two protein problems" (urban excess, upland deficit) is the right framing for UNICEF, WHO and the Ministry of Health (ch21.2).
- The refusal to use consultancy market sizes, and the small displacement benchmark (0.2% of meat protein by 2035), give ministries and the World Bank an honest basis for not over-claiming climate benefits (ch28).
- The cheap-rules list for the Food Safety Law window (chay definitions, blend labelling, neutral names, GMO status) is concrete (ch29.4).

**Where Part VI fails this group.**
1. **It misreads current public-meal policy.** It relies on a 2016 school-milk target that expired in 2020 and does not cite the December 2025 school guidance, the July 2026 shift-meal guidance, the Ten tips or the School Health Programme. Its advice to "base public-meal guidance on protein quality and cost rather than an animal share" (DMV-17) is partly overtaken; the live question is how the plant slot is filled and at what cost.
2. **It treats policy makers mainly as label-rule setters, not as buyers and specifiers of public meals.** The biggest demand lever for this group is public procurement standards (schools, canteens, boarding schools), and Part VI has no national count, no provincial costs outside Hanoi and no view of the rules being drafted.
3. **It has nothing on children's nutrition quality.** No protein quality, zinc, iron or sodium analysis, so NIN and UNICEF cannot use it.
4. **It misses the fiscal side.** The sugary-drink excise could change plant-milk pricing from 2027.
5. **It does not engage the food-systems machinery.** The NAP-FST, its partnership of more than 40 partners, provincial pilots, M&E framework and food balance sheets are where FAO, CGIAR and MAE would take up protein diversification. Part VI does not mention them.
6. **Its advice to international funders has no named funder.** The school-meal and nutrition funders we checked either have no Vietnam programme or work on other topics.
7. **Its policy timing is out of date** (Food Safety Law vote now May 2027).

## 6. Derived numbers (our calculations)

**Protein per primary-school lunch.** Inputs from Decision 3958 [APR-04]: daily energy for 6 to 11 year olds 1,460 to 2,150 kcal (moderate activity); lunch 30 to 40% of daily energy; protein 13 to 20% of energy; 4 kcal per g. Low end: 1,460 × 0.30 × 0.13 ÷ 4 = 14.2 g. High end: 2,150 × 0.40 × 0.20 ÷ 4 = 43.0 g. Midpoint (8 to 9 years, mean 1,775 kcal, 35% lunch, 16.5% protein): 25.6 g. [VN-direct, Low; inferred]

**Cash per school day, border communes.** VND 450,000 a month ÷ 20 to 22 school days = VND 20,500 to 22,500 a day; rice is given separately in kind (8 kg a month) [APR-09]. [VN-direct, Low; inferred]

**Protein budget.** If protein items take 30 to 50% of the cash: about VND 6,000 to 11,000 a lunch; for 25 g of protein that is about VND 24,000 to 45,000 per 100 g of protein (USD 0.92 to 1.73 at 26,000 VND per USD). Against Part VI's ladder (ch21.3): tofu VND 19,600 to 30,000; eggs 34,300; chicken thigh 39,000; lean pork 70,900; branded textured analogue 112,000 (ch25.3). The 30 to 50% share is our assumption; menu data (APR-L01) would replace it. [VN-direct, Low; inferred]

## 7. Limits

- The session's web-search budget ran out part way through. Documents for MOST's 2026 programmes, GIZ, JICA, UNDP and the FAO Country Programming Framework were not retrieved.
- Decision 300, Decision 1520, the Law on Disease Prevention and the ADB article were read through a fetch tool that extracts or summarises pages; quoted passages were requested verbatim but are Medium confidence. Decision 02, Decision 3958, Decision 1982, Resolutions 71 and 72, the dairy strategy text and the UNICEF programme document were read in full.
- Decision 973 is known from a Ministry of Health newspaper report, not the decision text.
- The status of plant milks under the sugary-drink excise is unresolved; we flag it as a risk, not a finding.
- Negative findings on international programmes (School Meals Coalition, WFP, GAIN) rest on web pages and could be overturned by a call.
