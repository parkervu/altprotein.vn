# Wave 7 actor-interest check: Vietnamese food manufacturers, retailers and caterers

**Actor group:** incumbents and first buyers (source prefix AIB). **Date:** 25 September 2026. **Checked against:** Part VI of "Alternative protein in Vietnam: a supply-side scoping study", edition 1.2 (chapters 21 to 29, appendices AA to AI, data files and OQ-156 to OQ-269).

**Method.** We read the 2025 annual reports or 2026 AGM documents of the listed incumbents where they are public (Vinamilk annual report 2025; QNS board report to the 2026 AGM and annual report 2025; Charoen Pokphand Foods 56-1 One Report 2025; Masan and Vissan AGM releases), company product and strategy pages, and 2025 to 2026 press on AGMs and launches, in Vietnamese and English. For caterers we read the Ministry of Health school-meal guidance of December 2025, Dispatch 64/CĐ-TTg, the Ho Chi Minh City education department's follow-up, the trade union's shift-meal conclusion, one public hospital patient-meal tender and the 2025 military ration circular. Every source in `sources.csv` was opened; press summaries of AGMs are marked Medium. The session's web-search budget ran out part-way through, so some checks were made by opening known pages rather than by searching; gaps this caused are listed at the end.

**Tags.** `[scope, confidence, source; demand evidence type]`. Scope is VN-direct, VN-adjacent or general. Demand evidence type is stated, revealed, tested or inferred.

## 1. Headline findings

1. **No incumbent in this group states a plant-based, alternative-protein or protein-diversification target in its 2025 annual report or 2026 AGM material. Where incumbents diversify protein, they add animal protein.** Vinamilk's chilled-beef plant (Vinabeef Tam Dao, 10,000 t a year, 51% Vinamilk) and cattle farm are operating, and its high-protein push is dairy (Green Farm Cao Đạm); Vissan is building its own 32,000-head pig farm (VND 230 billion); Masan MEATLife is moving into marinated and processed branded meat, which it says holds only about 2% of the meat market [VN-direct, High, AIB-01; AIB-04; AIB-12; AIB-15; stated and revealed]. This strengthens Part VI's clean negative on novel protein (ch26.6). But Part VI treats incumbents only as buyers. Several are also vertical integrators or competitors for the same "high-protein" claim.

2. **Plant milk is the one plant category incumbents invest in, and 2025 results confirm it.** Vinasoy sold 264 million litres in 2025 (up 4%) with revenue up 14%; Veyo nut milks grew 50%, premium Fami 36%, e-commerce 135% and exports 126% [VN-direct, High, AIB-05; AIB-06; revealed]. Vinamilk reports "impressive" plant-milk revenue growth, launched an oat milk and two plant yoghurts in 2025, and writes that plant milk "không còn được xem là sản phẩm thay thế sữa động vật" (is no longer seen as a substitute for animal milk) but is positioned as a nutrition solution for specific health goals [VN-direct, High, AIB-01; stated and revealed]. QNS flags rising marketing costs for plant nutrition as a 2026 risk [VN-direct, High, AIB-05; stated]. The US agricultural attaché reports that Vietnam imported USD 3.9 billion of tree nuts in 2025 (up 40%), partly for plant milk bought by younger consumers [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-40; stated]. This strengthens ch23 and play T10.

3. **The buyer Part VI names for a local non-soy isolate also sells dairy protein against plant protein.** Vinamilk promotes Green Farm Cao Đạm (25 g of protein in two 250 ml cartons) as containing milk protein that is "dễ hấp thu hơn so với đạm có nguồn gốc thực vật" (more easily absorbed than plant-origin protein) [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-03; stated]. Its annual report gives supplier criteria as price competitiveness, quality and "other appropriate standards", plus geographic diversification of supply [VN-direct, High, AIB-01; stated]. A domestic isolate must be pitched on soy-free function, cost against Western pea protein and supply security, not on protein quality. Part VI does not say this (ch26.3, TPP-05).

4. **National school-meal guidance changed in December 2025 and Part VI missed it.** Decision 3958/QĐ-BYT (25 December 2025) sets protein at 13 to 20% of energy, lunch at 30 to 40% of daily energy, animal-source protein on at least 4 to 5 days a week, "xen kẽ đạm có nguồn gốc thực vật (đậu các loại, đậu phụ) ít nhất 2 lần mỗi tuần" (alternating with plant protein such as beans and tofu at least twice a week), and no animal share of protein [VN-direct, High, AIB-39; revealed (rule)]. Part VI says school meals are "guided by an animal-protein target" (ch25 summary, ch25.3, from Decision 1340/2016, whose targets ran to 2020) and proposes moving guidance off an animal share (DMV-17). The current rule is a day-count rule that already reserves plant-protein days. Those days are in the baseline, like chay days: an ingredient sold into them replaces tofu and beans, not meat.

5. **After Dispatch 64, school supply is governed by visible documents, not only by price.** Dispatch 64/CĐ-TTg (13 September 2026) says provinces must "không lựa chọn đơn vị cung cấp thực phẩm chỉ dựa trên tiêu chí giá thấp" (not choose food suppliers on lowest price alone) and control food origin [VN-direct, High, AIB-41; revealed (rule)]. Ho Chi Minh City's education department now requires schools to publish daily the caterer, ingredients, purchase invoices, menus and photos of pupils' actual meal trays, and bars one supplier from serving more schools than its real capacity [VN-direct, High, AIB-42; revealed (rule)]. Invoice-level traceability that parents can see is now the entry ticket for any protein ingredient into school kitchens.

6. **Public hospitals and the army are price-set channels with published specifications, and one hospital already treats tofu as a full substitute for meat.** The Hospital for Tropical Diseases' 2025 patient-meal tender (about 81,800 rice meals and 20,500 porridge meals a year) sets at least 1,800 kcal and 60 g of protein a day, and per meal "Thịt nạc/cá/gà/thủy hải sản: tối thiểu 60g. Hoặc Đậu hũ (Thay thế hoàn toàn cho đạm động vật): tối thiểu 120g" (lean meat, fish, chicken or seafood at least 60 g, or tofu as a full replacement for animal protein at least 120 g) [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-44; revealed]. The army's basic food allowance is VND 72,000 (USD 2.77) per soldier per day under Circular 96/2025/TT-BQP [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-45; revealed]. Part VI found no hospital or military data (ch25.3, AD.4, OQ-220).

7. **Retailer private label is a route Part VI does not examine for the home market.** Saigon Co.op sells more than 2,500 private-label SKUs made with more than 100 Vietnamese manufacturers, 15 to 30% below comparable brands [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-28; AIB-29; stated], and already sells Co.op Select chay dumplings with wheat protein [VN-direct, High, AIB-30; revealed]. WinCommerce aims for 25 to 50 "Mega House Brands", about 6,100 stores by end 2026 and 13,000 by 2030 [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-12; AIB-13; stated]. A higher-protein chay or blended product can reach shelf through a co-packer and a retailer's label, without a consumer brand. Part VI discusses private label only for export (TPP-08, ch27).

8. **Factory catering is bigger, more tiered and more client-driven than Part VI shows.** Haseca says it serves more than 200,000 meals a day from more than 120 kitchens for more than 90 multinational clients, in five tiers from VND 18,000 to 45,000 (USD 0.69 to 1.73), with ISO 22000 and RBA compliance [VN-direct, Low, AIB-36; stated]. The trade union's floor is VND 25,000 a meal when a caterer is hired in regions I and II and VND 20,000 when the firm cooks itself [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-43; revealed (rule)]. Chay meals appear only as a "personalisation" option [VN-direct, Low, AIB-37; stated]. Because caterers sell compliance with client codes (RBA, ISO audits), the FDI client is a decision-maker that Part VI never asks about (ch25.3, TPP-04) [VN-direct, Low; inferred].

9. **Multinationals in this group bring no Vietnamese alternative-protein pull.** Charoen Pokphand Foods sells Meat Zero "for flexitarian consumers" and registered a petty patent for a soy "PLANT-TEC" texturing process, but in Vietnam it reports retort sausages and owns a caterer, Dzine Food Solutions [VN-adjacent, High, AIB-19; stated]. CJ's plant-based push (KRW 200 billion of sales targeted by 2025) named the US, UK, Germany and Japan, not Vietnam [general, Medium, AIB-17; stated]. Nestlé Vietnam's sustainability pull is regenerative sourcing (50% of key ingredients, including soybeans and meat, by 2030) and net zero by 2050, not protein diversification [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-22; stated]. Sodexo's "33% of our planned menu offerings plant-based" by 2025 is published by its US arm, and we could not confirm a current Vietnamese operation [general, Low, AIB-38; stated].

10. **Noodles and snacks are still not protein markets.** Acecook's July 2025 strategy moves it towards being a "comprehensive" food supplier with ready-to-eat foods and meal-replacing snacks, adds "real meat and vegetables" to instant pho and bun and fortifies with calcium and vitamin B12, with no protein claim [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-18; stated]. Mondelez Kinh Do talks about snacking moments, social-media discovery and "real ingredients", not protein [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-25; stated]. This strengthens ch26.3 and the low ranks of segments 5 and 7 in ch26.7.

11. **Several store counts and incumbent figures in Part VI are out of date.** AEON plans 30 malls by fiscal 2030 (from 9 now), with Vietnam taking 60% of AEON's ASEAN investment [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-33; stated]. Lotte Mart has 17 stores and is moving into industrial towns, targeting factory workers, with ready-meal kitchens in 7 stores [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-34; stated]. Central Retail runs 43 GO! hypermarkets, 16 mini go!, 9 Tops and 23 Lan Chi stores [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-31; revealed]. Cholimex Food's revenue was about VND 4,050 billion (USD 156 million) in 2025, against the 2021 figure of VND 2,513 billion used in the buyer map [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-21; revealed].

## 2. Actor-by-actor notes

Each note gives what the actor says, what it buys or builds, what it would need to see before buying or launching a new protein ingredient or product (our derivation, inferred), and whether Part VI tells it.

### AIB-A01 Vinamilk (dairy, plant milks, beef)
- **Says.** Its annual report 2025 (signed 19 March 2026) sees adult demand moving to "các sản phẩm cao cấp giàu protein, ít béo và ít đường" (premium high-protein, low-fat, low-sugar products) and plant milk growing on low-sugar, high-protein demand, "no longer a substitute for animal milk" [AIB-01]. Traceability and label compliance are becoming "yêu cầu bắt buộc thay vì lợi thế cạnh tranh" (mandatory, not a competitive advantage) [AIB-01]. Net Zero by 2050; farm emissions down 7% in 2025 [AIB-01].
- **Buys and builds.** 2025 plant launches: oat milk, 9-nut plant yoghurt, oat and passion-fruit plant yoghurt [AIB-01]. The high-protein nut milk uses pea protein (Part VI, BUY-16). Green Farm Cao Đạm dairy at 5 g of protein per 100 ml, promoted against plant protein [AIB-03]. Vinabeef chilled beef, 10,000 t a year [AIB-04]. 2026 plan: revenue VND 66,477 billion, profit VND 9,828 billion; 2026 is the last year of the 2022 to 2026 strategy [AIB-01; AIB-02].
- **Would need to see.** A soy-free isolate that is UHT-stable and neutral, priced between Chinese and Western pea protein, with non-GMO and glyphosate-free documents, from a supplier that passes a Vinamilk audit and diversifies its supply geographically; evidence that the high-protein nut milk is a growth line, not a niche beside dairy.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** Partly. Price band and function specification are in ch26.4 and TPP-05. Part VI does not see Vinamilk as a competitor on protein claims, does not know how big the nut-milk line is, and has nothing on audit or qualification (OQ-233, OQ-236).

### AIB-A02 Quang Ngai Sugar (QNS) and Vinasoy (soy milk, nut milks)
- **Says.** "Khẳng định vị thế của Vinasoy là một nhãn hàng dinh dưỡng từ thực vật" (establish Vinasoy as a plant-nutrition brand) [AIB-05]. May 2026 repositioning: "Kiến tạo dưỡng chất lành từ đậu nành và hạt" (creating wholesome nutrition from soybeans and seeds) [AIB-07]. 2026 plan: renew core soy milk and "mở rộng ngành hàng mới" (expand into new categories); invest in soybean sourcing areas; group revenue target VND 10,500 billion, down 5% [AIB-05].
- **Buys and builds.** Whole soybeans; own soybean research centre (2,072 lines in its gene bank; variety VINASOY 05 yields more than 2.5 t per ha in Cu Jut) [AIB-06]. Three plants. School soy-milk donations: 13.8 million boxes to 906,848 pupils since 2016, and 1 million boxes to 16,700 pupils in 30 schools for 2026 to 2027 [AIB-06; AIB-08].
- **Would need to see.** A reason to buy protein rather than make it: a protein that lifts nut milks above 5 g per 100 ml without soy, or a use for its own soy (okara, soy base) as an ingredient for others.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** No. Part VI lists Vinasoy as a plausible buyer of a rice or mung bean isolate (ch26.7), on inference. It does not consider that Vinasoy's integrated, whole-bean, non-GMO model may make it a supplier or partner rather than a buyer.

### AIB-A03 TH Group (TH true MILK, TH true NUT, TH true OAT)
- **Says and builds.** Nut milks since March 2018 sweetened with dates; oat milk from 2023 with coconut and chocolate variants in 2024; positioning on low sugar, organic and "clean" milk [AIB-09]. Its walnut nut milk contains cow's milk (Part VI, NOV-58). No protein claim found.
- **Would need to see.** A protein that fits its natural, short-label story.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** Partly (TPP-05). TH is private and publishes no annual report; we found no stated interest in plant protein. Low demand relevance.

### AIB-A04 Nutifood (nutrition powders, medical nutrition)
- **Says and builds.** Research institute NNRIS in Sweden; clinical studies with Military Hospital 175 (Part VI, BUY-19). Its Värna Life ready-to-drink "sữa hạt công thức" (formulated nut milk) is soy-extract based, with 2.35 g of protein per 100 ml, marketed as lactose-free with plant protein for people with diabetes [AIB-10].
- **Would need to see.** Clinical and regulatory dossiers for any new protein, and GMO status settled for fermentation-derived proteins.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** Partly (ch24.2, ch26.3, TPP-07). The time and cost of a dossier are unknown (OQ-233, OQ-240).

### AIB-A05 Masan Group: Masan MEATLife and Masan Consumer
- **Says.** MEATLife is "moving away from the traditional meat producer model toward a modern consumer protein platform"; innovation products were about 31% of category revenue in 2025, up from 18% in 2024 [AIB-11]. At the 2026 AGM: branded meat holds about 2% of the market; MML moves into "các sản phẩm thịt tẩm ướp và chế biến" (marinated and processed meat); 2026 targets MML revenue up 20%, Masan Consumer up 15% with 16 "Mega Brands" [AIB-12]. No mention of plant-based, chay or alternative protein [AIB-11; AIB-12].
- **Would need to see.** An extender that lowers cost per kg against lean pork without harming a brand that sells on meat content (Ponnie "88% meat", Part VI BUY-13), and a label rule for blends.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** Partly (ch24.4, TPP-03). Part VI has no processor cost model and no test data. Its "consumer protein platform" is animal protein.

### AIB-A06 WinCommerce (WinMart, WinMart+)
- **Says.** About 6,100 stores by end 2026, 70% of new stores rural; 13,000 stores by 2030 with at least 1,500 openings a year; 25 to 50 "Mega House Brands" [AIB-12; AIB-13]. MML holds about 61% of the protein category in its stores (Part VI, BUY-10).
- **Would need to see.** A product that fits a house brand or an MML test, with margin and supply capacity for thousands of minimarts, much of it rural.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** Partly (ch25.1 on store counts). Part VI's 2030 target (15,000 stores, inferred) is superseded by the AGM figure. Private label and the MML tie are not analysed.

### AIB-A07 Vissan (meat processing, chay line)
- **Says.** 2026 AGM: revenue VND 3,270 billion, processed food 20,350 t, pork 7,730 t, beef 538 t; two projects, a relocated slaughter and processing plant and a pig farm in Binh Duong [AIB-14]. Management accepted lower margins to keep prices stable; packaging and imported inputs rose 10 to 40%; live hogs averaged VND 71,000 per kg in Q1 2026; pig farm VND 230 billion for 32,000 head; exports at least 300 t [AIB-15]. No chay target at the AGM [AIB-14; AIB-15]. Launched two chay dumpling SKUs for Vu Lan on 27 August 2026 through its own shops and online [AIB-16].
- **Would need to see.** A binder or extender cheaper than lean pork at 2026 hog prices with gel and retort stability; for chay, a textured protein at Chinese parity with certificates.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** Partly (ch26.3, ch26.7, AE.5 label audit is strong). OQ-181 (a 2026 chay target) can now be closed as a negative.

### AIB-A08 CJ Foods Vietnam (CJ Cau Tre, bibigo)
- **Says and builds.** bibigo Healthy Mandu Chay claims "100% Protein từ thực vật" (Part VI, LE-25). The parent planned KRW 200 billion of plant-based sales by 2025 under PlanTable, citing animal-disease limits on meat exports, and named the US, UK, Germany and Japan as markets [AIB-17]. We found no PlanTable launch in Vietnam.
- **Would need to see.** A local textured protein for chay mandu at import parity; export markets where chay versions avoid animal-health barriers.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** Partly (ch26.7). The export animal-health angle is not in ch27.

### AIB-A09 Acecook Vietnam (instant noodles)
- **Says.** Strategy "Đổi Mới Nâng Tầm Hạnh Phúc" (July 2025): become a comprehensive, sustainable food supplier; expand into seasonings, ready-to-eat foods and snacks; calcium in Hao Hao, vitamin B12 in Mien Phu Huong, "real meat and vegetables" in instant pho and bun; 54% of factories on biomass fuel, 80% by 2030 [AIB-18].
- **Would need to see.** A protein garnish or ready-meal component at Chinese textured-soy parity that survives its process; a consumer reason to pay for protein in noodles.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** Yes, as far as demand exists (ch26.3, OQ-237). Low relevance now.

### AIB-A10 C.P. Vietnam (CPV Food; parent Charoen Pokphand Foods)
- **Says and builds.** The group calls itself a "Protein Expert", sells Meat Zero alternative proteins "for flexitarian consumers", and holds a petty patent for a soy "PLANT-TEC" texturing technology; in Vietnam it reports retort sausages "designed for long shelf life without refrigeration" and owns Dzine Food Solutions (restaurants and mobile catering) [AIB-19]. No Vietnamese plant-based product found (Part VI, LE-09).
- **Would need to see.** A group decision to localise Meat Zero, or a hybrid line for its catering and shop network.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** Partly. Part VI does not know CP is also a caterer in Vietnam, or that it already has shelf-stable formats for the traditional trade.

### AIB-A11 Industrial chay SMEs (An Nhien, Au Lac, Thoai An, LC Foods, SG Food and others)
- **Says and builds.** Owner-managed; import textured soy, soy concentrate and gluten (Part VI, FORM-01). SG Food's own profile lists fresh porridge, baby meals, seafood and prepared meals, four frozen plants and ISO 9001, HACCP, BRC and FSSC 22000, and supplies Co.opmart, AEON and WinMart; it does not list a chay line [AIB-20]. We found no 2025 to 2026 strategy documents from these firms.
- **Would need to see.** Landed price at or below Chinese textured protein, granules and slices to spec, non-GMO and halal certificates, 20 to 25 kg bags, reliable supply at lunar peaks.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** Mostly yes (ch22, ch26.7, TPP-01, TPP-02). Part VI is strongest here. Qualification time and minimum order are still unknown.

### AIB-A12 Cholimex Food (sauces, frozen spring rolls and dim sum, chay seasoning kits)
- **Says.** 2025 revenue about VND 4,050 billion and profit VND 356 billion; 2026 targets about VND 4,500 billion and VND 390 billion; small exports incl. Middle East; the parent (40.72%) is not planning to sell [AIB-21].
- **Would need to see.** Domestic textured protein for frozen chay dim sum and export spring rolls with EU-grade documents.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** Partly. Its revenue in the buyer map is from 2021 (AE.3).

### AIB-A13 Nestlé Vietnam
- **Says.** Reduce greenhouse gas emissions 50% by 2030 and reach net zero by 2050; source 50% of key ingredients (milk, coffee, cereals, soybeans, vegetables, cocoa, palm oil, sugar, fish, meat) from regenerative farms by 2030; school nutrition activities through MILO sport programmes [AIB-22]. Globally, Nestlé frames 2026 innovation around protein and fibre density for GLP-1 users, with no plant-based or Vietnam mention [AIB-23].
- **Would need to see.** A local, lower-carbon protein that counts towards regenerative or emissions targets.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** No, and it does not need to: Nestlé Vietnam shows no demand-side interest in alternative protein. Low relevance.

### AIB-A14 Ajinomoto Vietnam (seasonings, frozen food, school-meal menus)
- **Says and builds.** Since 2012, with the Ministry of Education and the National Institute of Nutrition, its School Meal Project supplies menu software with "120 thực đơn sẵn có với trên 360 món ăn" (120 ready menus with more than 360 lunch dishes); by November 2022 it covered 62 provinces and 4,262 registered schools [AIB-24]. Parent partners with Solar Foods (Part VI, RGN-40).
- **Would need to see.** Dishes that meet Decision 3958 norms at school budgets, including plant-protein dishes for the twice-weekly slots.
- **Does Part VI tell it?** No. Part VI treats Ajinomoto only as a seasoning buyer (ch26.7) and never asks who writes school menus. Ajinomoto may be the single largest influence on what protein dishes Vietnamese schools serve.

### AIB-A15 Mondelez Kinh Do (biscuits, cakes, snacks)

### AIB-A17 Saigon Co.op (Co.opmart, Co.op Food, Co.opXtra)

The Saigon Times names Dussmann among large caterers and a Tan Long and Nikkotrust joint venture (October 2023) serving meals at VND 28,000 to 100,000 [AIB-35]. ### AIB-A20 Vietnamese contract caterers for factories, schools and hospitals (Haseca, Viet Nam EOC, Hoang Kim, Vinastory, Global Catering, school and hospital kitchens)
- **Say.** Haseca: more than 200,000 meals a day, 120 kitchens, five tiers of VND 18,000 to 45,000, 40-day non-repeating menus, ISO 22000 and RBA [AIB-36; AIB-37]. The Saigon Times: Viet Nam EOC 120,000 meals a day, Hoang Kim 50,000, Vinastory 38,000; Global Catering 15,000 for a hospital, a university and GS25 stores; common price VND 29,000; food 60 to 80% of meal value; commissions of VND 500 to 1,000 per meal [AIB-35]. Rules: union floors of VND 20,000 to 25,000 (regions I and II) and "4 food groups: protein, green vegetables, starch and fat" [AIB-43]; school rules in Decision 3958 and Dispatch 64 [AIB-39; AIB-41; AIB-42]; hospital tenders with gram norms and tofu substitution [AIB-44]. Our calculation from Decision 3958: for a pupil aged 6 to 7 (1,460 to 1,570 kcal a day), a lunch at 30 to 40% of daily energy with 13 to 20% of energy from protein holds about 14 to 31 g of protein [VN-direct, Low, AIB-39; inferred], consistent with Part VI's 20 g per protein dish.
- **Would need to see.** A protein ingredient that costs less per 20 g than eggs or chicken, tolerates bulk cooking, and arrives with invoices and certificates that a school or FDI client can audit; client or parent acceptance.
- **Does Part VI tell them?** Partly (ch25.3, TPP-04). The price arithmetic is useful. Part VI has no caterer menu or cost data (OQ-215), no hospital or military data, and uses superseded school guidance.

## 3. Coverage verdict

### Where Part VI serves this group well
- **The core thesis fits what incumbents do.** No incumbent plans a branded meat analogue; all buy protein as an ingredient; the only proven modern plant category is plant milk. Our reading of 2025 annual reports and 2026 AGMs finds nothing that contradicts ch26 or ch29.1.
- **Chay makers and meat processors get usable numbers.** The border price ladder (ch26.2), the Vissan label audit (AE.5), the buyer map (AE.3) and TPP-01 and TPP-03 are what a purchasing manager would ask for first.
- **The clean negatives are right and now stronger.** No Vietnamese incumbent pilots mycoprotein, precision-fermented protein or a declared novel-protein blend (ch26.6), and none states a target (this paper).
- **The canteen price arithmetic** (ch25.3, TPP-04) is the right frame: only an ingredient fits a VND 7,500 to 14,000 protein-dish budget.

### Where Part VI fails this group
1. **It sees incumbents only as buyers.** Vinamilk (dairy protein claims, beef), Vinasoy (own soy breeding), Vissan (own pig farm) and Masan (retailer and processor in one) are integrators and competitors. Part VI never asks whether they would make rather than buy, or whether they would resist a claim that plant protein equals animal protein. That changes who a startup should approach and how.
2. **It misses the retailer's own label.** Co.op's 2,500 private-label SKUs and WinCommerce's house-brand plan are a cheaper route to shelf than a startup brand or an incumbent brand. Part VI's channel ranking (ch25.6, AD.6) does not include it.
3. **Its school analysis rests on superseded rules.** Decision 3958/QĐ-BYT (December 2025) and the post-Dispatch 64 disclosure rules change the school verdict from "do not start in schools" towards "plant-protein days already exist; sell a certified ingredient for them". Part VI's displacement logic should also treat school plant-protein days like chay days.
4. **It does not ask who writes menus.** In schools, menu software (Ajinomoto with the Ministry of Education and the National Institute of Nutrition), caterers and education departments; in factories, caterers under FDI client codes and union oversight; in hospitals, nutrition departments under tender specifications. The best lever may be a menu bank, not a caterer.
5. **It has no client-side view of canteens.** FDI clients set catering contracts through RBA, ISO and brand supplier codes. Whether any client asks for plant-forward or lower-carbon meals is the question that decides whether canteens are a price-only channel.
6. **Several numbers are stale.** WinCommerce's 2030 target, AEON, Lotte and Central Retail store counts, Cholimex revenue and the largest caterer's scale all changed or were superseded in 2025 to 2026 sources.
7. **Hospitals and the army are left blank** although both publish specifications: a hospital tender that accepts tofu at 120 g for 60 g of meat is revealed institutional acceptance of plant protein that Part VI does not have anywhere else.
