# Funders, philanthropies and field-builders: does Part VI answer their demand questions?

Wave 7, step 1. Actor group: funders, philanthropies and field-builders (source prefix AFN). Working paper, 25 September 2026.

**Scope.** We identified 20 real actors that fund, build or shape the alternative-protein and diet-change field and could act in Vietnam. For each we read their own RFPs, strategy pages, benchmarks and programme pages (2024 to 2026 where possible), derived the demand-side questions they would need answered before allocating money to Vietnam, and checked each question against Part VI (chapters 21 to 29, Appendices AA to AI, `demand_moves.csv`, `open_questions.csv`). Files: `actors.csv` (20 actors), `questions.csv` (30 questions), `candidate_lines.csv` (10 lines), `new_facts.csv` (19 facts), `sources.csv` (50 sources, AFN-01 to AFN-50). Question numbers in this paper (Q01 and so on) are AFN-Q01 and so on; actor codes are AFN-A01 and so on.

**Tags.** [evidence label, confidence, source, demand evidence type]. "Stated" here often means a funder's stated priority, which is revealed evidence of what the funder will pay for but stated evidence about the market.

## 1. Headline findings

1. **No major alternative-protein funder names Vietnam as a priority.** Nova Vista wants more policy staff in "high-innovation Asian economies (including but not limited to Japan and South Korea)", Germany and the Nordics. GFI APAC's 2025 report card does not mention Vietnam and its team page lists nobody covering Vietnam. The Bezos Centre at NUS targets "East and Southeast Asia" from Singapore. The 2026 ReRoot Asia diet-change fund is not open to Vietnam, ACE's July 2025 Movement Grants had no Vietnamese grantee, and Vietnam is not a School Meals Coalition member. [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-05; AFN-11; AFN-12; AFN-15; AFN-29; AFN-28; AFN-33, revealed]

2. **Market-shaping funders count institutional decisions as demand, not consumer attitudes.** Nova Vista funds "public procurement of innovative protein products specifically", advance market commitments and retailer protein-ratio targets, and judges goals as "outcomes in the world" with "an identifiable decision-maker, a reason they might move, and a window". ARE and FAIRR count published sourcing targets and disclosed alternative-protein shares of sales. Part VI sizes channels and prices well but has no map of decision-makers and windows. [general, High, AFN-05; AFN-18; AFN-20, stated]

3. **Part VI's cheapest displacement route is out of scope for the newest market-shaping funder.** Nova Vista excludes "interventions primarily focused on whole-food plant-based options", "culture change and consumer awareness campaigns", "individual diet change" and "work whose likely effect is to shift consumption from larger animals to smaller ones". Part VI tells funders to test canteen chay days and chay promotion (ch28.6) and projects poultry-led meat growth (ch21.1). Neither fits. [general, High, AFN-05, stated; VN-direct, Medium, our inference]

4. **Diet-change funders do pay for institutional menu shifts, but judge them in units Part VI never reports.** ACE rates Sinergia's institutional programme at "3.4 meals replaced per dollar"; Humane World reports 2.3 million meals a year moved to plant-based in 2025; Lever reports 29 million corporate meals shifted in Asia. Part VI reports displaced meat protein in tonnes only, with no animals spared, no CO2e and no cost per meal replaced. [general, Medium, AFN-26; AFN-23; AFN-38, revealed and stated]

5. **Asian public-meal precedents exist and Part VI's benchmark appendix misses them.** As of 2024 all 17 Korean education offices recommend plant-based school meals once or twice a month; Seoul's plant-based menus carried 26.7 g of protein against 29.9 g for regular menus (standard 13.8 g). Chinese hotel groups committed 30 to 70% of menus to plant-based. India's ORF and GFI India are designing alternative protein into school feeding on protein quality, with budgets under INR 10 per student per day. Appendix AG.4 covers only the US, UK, Denmark and France, and OQ-261 says no Asian trials were found. [VN-adjacent, High, AFN-42, revealed; VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-38; AFN-14, stated]

6. **Vietnam's school-meal system has a new framework that Part VI does not mention.** Decision 973/QĐ-TTg (1 June 2026) approved the School Health Programme 2026 to 2035, which scales a school meal model built by MOET, NIN and TH Group: 400 standard menus and one carton of fresh milk a day. Ajinomoto, NIN and MOET run a second menu project (120+ menus, 360+ dishes). The gatekeeper for protein in schools is the menu template, and both templates are built with food companies; one is dairy-anchored. [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-44; AFN-45; AFN-46, revealed]

7. **Corporate protein-diversification pressure reaches Vietnam, thinly.** ARE's Asia Protein Buyers 100 (February 2026) scored five Vietnamese buyers (Vinamilk, Masan, Vinh Hoan, Minh Phu, Nam Viet); Vinamilk reached Tier 3. Across 100 Asian buyers the protein diversification score averaged 7.4% and only 2 publish alternative-protein sourcing targets. FAIRR's 2024 index puts Vinamilk's plant-based products at 1.30% of its range, with no timebound target. Accor's policy of 50% vegetarian or plant-based dishes by 2030 covers all its hotels, and it ran 43 in Vietnam in 2024. [VN-direct, High, AFN-18; AFN-21, revealed; VN-direct, Medium, AFN-40; AFN-41, stated]

8. **Research funders fund taste, cost and scale-up, not demand.** Coefficient Giving's USD 30 million RFP says "taste and price represent the biggest barriers" and asks for "a credible pathway to industry uptake"; GFI's grants target organoleptic quality, cost and scale-up. Part VI's price ladder answers the price half for Vietnam (revealed); the taste half in Vietnamese dishes is untested. The fish-flavour topic names catfish and tilapia, species Vietnam farms. [general, High, AFN-01; AFN-08, stated]

9. **New evidence strengthens Part VI's displacement view.** A Stanford lab analysis of 1.2 million transactions in seven restaurants (US, Canada, Australia; August 2026), funded by Coefficient Giving, found "essentially no evidence" that modern alternative proteins reduced meat purchases. [general, Medium, AFN-07, revealed]

10. **GFI APAC is steering Asian corporates towards exactly the route Part VI recommends.** It expects "Asia's first large-scale rollouts" of meat enhanced with plant proteins in 2026, foodservice first and at price parity; a Temasek-backed range launched in Singapore in September 2025. No Vietnamese processor partner is named. [VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-11; AFN-13, stated]

11. **Vietnam's organised field is small, welfare-led and partly unseen by wave 6.** Humane World works on cage-free hens in Vietnam and in 2025 included Viet Nam in plant-based chef and nutritionist training, which corrects the wave-6 note that it has no plant-based work there. Vietnam Animal Welfare Watch tracks cage-free pledges. Dharma Voices for Animals runs a pagoda-based plant-based programme in Hanoi. We found no Vietnamese or regional foundation funding sustainable protein. [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-23; AFN-37; AFN-36, revealed and stated]

## 3. Actor-by-actor notes

**AFN-A01 Coefficient Giving.** The Farm Animal Welfare fund's alternative-protein RFP offers up to USD 30 million over two rounds (USD 0.1 to 1 million; USD 1 to 5 million), closed on 10 August and 15 September 2026, with decisions by 30 November and 31 December 2026 [general, High, AFN-01, stated]. Topics are off-flavour reduction, fat alternatives, egg replacement and fish flavours in tilapia, milkfish, carp and catfish. It frames the problem as taste and price and asks for a pathway to uptake. The fund lists movement building "especially in emerging economies" [general, High, AFN-02, stated]. *Demand questions:* is there a Vietnamese buyer who would adopt a fish-flavour or off-flavour solution (Q05); is taste or price binding in Vietnamese dishes (Q06); how many animals would each route spare (Q25). Part VI answers the price part well (ch21.3) and nothing on animals.

**AFN-A02 Nova Vista Foundation.** A family foundation, "hits-based and risk neutral", with a USD 10 million RFP, grants of USD 100,000 to 750,000 a year, phase 1 closed on 15 September 2026 [general, High, AFN-04; AFN-05, stated]. Its market-shaping ideas are "less mature" and "mostly under-explored": procurement of innovative protein products specifically, advance market commitments ("the field lacks a designed mechanism"), retailer and corporate protein-ratio targets and follow-through, blended finance and prizes. Its case for protein is climate plus "domestic food security and production capacity, supply chain resilience ... and economic competitiveness", which matches AltProtein Vietnam's framing. Its exclusions (whole-food plant-based, culture change, individual diet change, shifts to smaller animals, direct R&D) rule out several Part VI moves as written. *Demand questions:* Q01, Q04, Q15, Q30. This is the actor Part VI serves worst: it has sizes but not decision-makers, windows or instrument designs.

**AFN-A03 The Navigation Fund.** Invitation-only farm animal welfare grants with "institutional impact" as the first priority (policies of food corporations, government and public institutions) [general, Medium, AFN-06, stated]. No alternative-protein RFP and no region named, as Part VI ch07 found. The brief's "Nova Vista Foundation / Navigation Fund" RFP is Nova Vista's alone on every page we read.

**AFN-A04 The Good Food Institute.** Research grants (USD 27 million plus, 141 grants, 26 countries) are closed with no reopening date; the 2025 round funded fermentation functionality and cell lines [general, High, AFN-08; AFN-09, stated]. Giving Green lists GFI among its five top climate nonprofits [general, Medium, AFN-30, stated]. GFI's State of the Industry page gives global plant-based meat and seafood retail of USD 6.6 billion in 2025 and says nothing about Southeast Asia [general, High, AFN-10, revealed]. Part VI already uses GFI's US and European data well.

**AFN-A05 GFI APAC.** Its 2026 watch list puts "conventional meat enhanced with plant proteins" at number five, foodservice first, at price parity, with net-zero goals as the corporate motive, and "protein maximisation" at number six [VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-11, stated]. Both points are close to Part VI (blends as the entry route, ch24.4; protein density, ch21.8), though Part VI warns against pitching "more protein" to adults. Vietnam is absent from the report card and the team page [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-11; AFN-12, revealed]. *Demand questions:* Q07, Q08, Q09.

**AFN-A06 GFI India with ORF.** An August 2026 roundtable with FSSAI, the Department of Biotechnology, the World Bank and WFP set out alternative protein from pulses, millets, oilseed meals and broken grains for PM-POSHAN and ICDS, on protein-quality grounds and within budgets "under 10 rupees per student each day" [VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-14, stated]. This is the clearest transfer model for Part VI's "second protein problem" (ch21.2): cheap, high-quality protein for children in poor upland districts. Part VI names the problem but not a buyer, a specification or a programme (Q18).

**AFN-A07 Bezos Earth Fund and the Bezos Centre at NUS.** USD 30 million (September 2024) for fermentation, cultivated, plant and hybrid proteins "affordable, nutritious, and widely accepted", including tofu by-product protein; platforms for nutrition in Asian populations and consumer acceptance [VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-15; AFN-16, stated]. With Enterprise Singapore it offers up to SGD 1 million per growth-stage startup at TRL 7 to 9 [VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-17, stated]. *Demand questions:* Q10 (co-run Vietnamese acceptance studies), Q11 (Vietnamese startups at TRL 7 to 9: Part VI's clean negative is adequate).

**AFN-A08 ProVeg Incubator.** Up to USD 300,000 including USD 75,000 in kind; no Asian programme or Vietnamese company found [general, Medium, AFN-48, revealed]. It publishes no demand evidence standard. Low relevance to Part VI.

**AFN-A09 Asia Research and Engagement.** The APB 100 scores 100 listed Asian buyers on ten themes including protein diversification, whose four indicators are: alternative proteins in strategy (average 25%), published 2030 sourcing targets (2%), disclosure of alternative protein as a share of protein sales (2%) and reporting on the GFI and FAIRR framework (0%) [VN-direct, High, AFN-18, revealed]. Five Vietnamese buyers are scored; Vinamilk is one of 26 Tier 3 companies. ARE's platform names engagement in China, Thailand, India, Japan and Singapore, not Vietnam [VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-19, stated]. *Demand questions:* Q02, Q03, Q27.

**AFN-A10 FAIRR Initiative.** Its protein diversification engagement (73 investors, USD 11.5 trillion) covers 20 Western manufacturers and retailers [general, Medium, AFN-20, stated]. Its producer index scored Vinamilk 45 of 100 on alternative proteins, with plant-based at 1.30% of the product range and "no timebound target" [VN-direct, High, AFN-21, revealed]. That figure puts Part VI's finding that plant milk is "the category that sells" (ch23.5) in proportion inside Vietnam's largest dairy company.

**AFN-A11 Humane World for Animals.** In Viet Nam its page lists dog and cat meat, wildlife, cage-free hens (90,000 hens freed from cages in 2024) and disasters [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-22, revealed]. Its 26 November 2025 summary says it "trained over 370 chefs, as well as more than 570 nutritionists and other professionals across Brazil, Canada, India, South Africa, Thailand, the UK and Viet Nam" in plant-based menu work [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-23, stated]. Forward Food itself lists no Vietnamese location [general, Medium, AFN-24, revealed]. It is the only international diet-change implementer with staff in Vietnam that we found (Q13, Q22).

**AFN-A12 Lever Foundation.** Reports 175+ Asian corporate commitments by early 2025, including IHG Greater China (30% plant-based menus by 2025) and Orange Hotels (70% across 750 hotels), and 29 million corporate meals shifted [VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-38, stated]. In July 2026 it expanded plant-based work to India and Thailand, "shifting protein procurement ratios" with hotels and foodservice; its Thai lead says the job is "to help institutions formalise and expand something that's already culturally present" [VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-39, stated]. That sentence describes chay in Vietnam, yet Vietnam is not in its programme. Accor's own policy already binds its Vietnamese hotels to 50% vegetarian or plant-based dishes by 2030 [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-40; AFN-41, stated] (Q20).

**AFN-A13 Animal Charity Evaluators and its diet-change charities.** Sinergia Animal's institutional programme replaced an estimated 998,000 meals in 2023 in Argentina, Colombia and Indonesia, at 3.4 meals per USD by ACE's estimate [general, Medium, AFN-26, revealed]. Good Food Fund certifies Chinese institutions that are at least 60% plant-based and aligns with the Chinese Dietary Guidelines [VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-27, stated]. No recommended charity works on diet in Vietnam [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-25; AFN-28, revealed]. *Demand questions:* Q12, Q14, Q25.

**AFN-A14 Animal Alliance Asia (ReRoot Asia Fund).** The 2026 round, supported by Stray Dog Institute, funds "projects that will bring diet change", including institutional menus, in Taiwan, the Philippines, Indonesia, Nepal, Pakistan and Japan; Vietnam is not eligible [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-29, revealed].

**AFN-A15 Giving Green and Founders Pledge.** Giving Green recommends GFI for climate donors ("livestock production is responsible for at least 10% of global emissions") [general, Medium, AFN-30, stated]. Founders Pledge's Climate Fund impact page shows no food grants [general, Low, AFN-31, revealed]. Both would need CO2e avoided per USD (Q24), which Part VI does not give.

**AFN-A16 Rockefeller Foundation and the School Meals Coalition.** Rockefeller pledged USD 100 million in March 2025 for school meals, with a Bangkok office and a focus on local and regenerative procurement; Vietnam is not named [general, Medium, AFN-32, stated]. Vietnam is not a School Meals Coalition member, unlike Cambodia, Indonesia, Lao PDR, the Philippines, Thailand and Timor-Leste [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-33, revealed] (Q29).

**AFN-A17 WWF.** Its retailer methodology (November 2025) measures plant and animal protein-rich food sales by weight, "not protein content", towards 60% plant, and counts tofu, tempeh, seitan and legumes [general, Medium, AFN-34, stated]. Applied in Vietnam, tofu and low-protein chay (median 4.7 g per 100 g, ch22.5) would count in full, so a ratio target could be met by incumbents with no novel protein (Q19). WWF-Viet Nam's home page shows no diets work [VN-direct, Low, AFN-35, revealed].

**AFN-A18 Dharma Voices for Animals.** A Hanoi programme with Phap Van Pagoda: films, plant-based cooking classes, dharma talks, free lunches, a donation-based restaurant; it aims at 15,000 pagodas and "approximately 60 million lay Buddhists" (its framing) [VN-direct, Low, AFN-36, stated]. The only organised attempt we found to add meat-free occasions through pagodas, which is the route Part VI calls cheapest on paper (ch28.4). No dates or outcomes are published.

**AFN-A19 Vietnam Animal Welfare Watch.** Tracks company cage-free pledges and exposes inaction [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-37, stated]. A commitments-tracking capacity that could extend to protein commitments (Q23).

**AFN-A20 School-meal field-builders (Ajinomoto Vietnam, TH Group, with NIN and MOET).** The Decision 973 model (400 menus, a daily milk carton) was piloted in 10 provinces in 2020 to 2021 and is now to be scaled nationally [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-44; AFN-45, revealed]. Ajinomoto's project offers 120+ menus and 360+ dishes [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-46, revealed]. These are corporate field-builders rather than funders, but for any funder interested in public meals they are the first people to call (Q16, Q17).

## 5. Coverage verdict

**Tally.** Of 30 questions, Part VI answers none fully, answers 19 in part, does not answer 10 and treats 1 as out of scope. Of the 12 High-value questions, 7 are partly answered and 5 are not answered (Q02 corporate commitments, Q15 who pays for each move, Q17 menu-template owners, Q25 animals spared, Q30 what would put Vietnam on funders' lists). Better answers would change the actor's decision for 14 questions and might for 15.

**Where Part VI serves this group well.**
- *Price and product targets.* The protein price ladder (ch21.3), the canteen protein budget (ch25.3) and the ten target product profiles (ch29.2) are exactly what an R&D funder needs to judge "a credible pathway to industry uptake".
- *Honest demand evidence.* The stated, revealed and tested tags, the rejection of untraceable market sizes and the displacement arithmetic (ch23.7, ch28) protect funders from the forecast errors of 2019 to 2021. New evidence (AFN-07) supports this line.
- *A costed menu of public goods.* The 19 demand moves with indicative costs (ch29.4, `demand_moves.csv`) are the kind of list funders ask for.
- *The blends route.* Part VI's blends conclusion (ch24.4) matches GFI APAC's 2026 strategy (AFN-11).

**Where Part VI fails this group.**
1. *It sizes channels but does not map decisions.* Market-shaping funders need a decision-maker, a reason and a window. Part VI has Dispatch 64, Decision 1340 and the Hanoi fee ceiling, but not Decision 973/QĐ-TTg, the menu-template owners (NIN with TH Group and with Ajinomoto), or any decision calendar. Hospitals and the army are blank (ch25.3).
2. *It recommends moves without checking who would pay.* ch28.6 and brief-international point funders at chay-day tests and chay promotion; the market-shaping RFP we read excludes both, and the diet-change regranter that would fund them excludes Vietnam.
3. *It speaks in protein tonnes only.* Welfare funders allocate on meals replaced per USD and animals spared; climate recommenders on CO2e per USD. Part VI gives neither, and does not split displacement by species even though poultry leads growth.
4. *It has no corporate-commitments lever.* ARE and FAIRR already score Vinamilk and four other Vietnamese buyers, and Accor's target already binds its Vietnamese hotels. Part VI's channel and buyer chapters treat companies as buyers of ingredients, never as holders of commitments.
5. *Its institutional benchmarks are Western.* AG.4 lists US, UK, Danish and French cases; the Korean, Chinese, Indian and Indonesian public-meal precedents are missing, including the Seoul evidence that plant-based school menus lose protein, which bears directly on TPP-04.
6. *It does not ask why Vietnam is off funders' maps.* Funders name Japan, Korea, Singapore, Thailand, India and China. Part VI does not say what Vietnam-specific fact would change that, or that Vietnam lacks implementers who could absorb diet-change money.

## 8. Limits and clean negatives

- The EA Forum returned HTTP 403; we read the Nova Vista post and the Stanford report through the ea.greaterwrong.com mirror and read the full Nova Vista RFP from its published document.
- Coefficient Giving's grants database could not be queried (HTTP 403 and 404), so we cannot say whether it has made grants in Vietnam.
- Our web search allowance ran out late in the work. We could not verify the number of schools using the Ajinomoto or TH menu sets, Seoul's own policy documents, or Vietnamese-language coverage of Humane World's 2025 training.
- Clean negatives: no Vietnamese or regional foundation funding sustainable protein found; no Vietnam mention on the GFI APAC report card, team or policy pages (AFN-11; AFN-12; AFN-50), the Bezos Centre pages, the Rockefeller school meals page or the School Meals Coalition member list; no Vietnamese ACE Movement Grant (July 2025 round); ReRoot 2026 not open to Vietnam; no food grants on Founders Pledge's Climate Fund impact page; no diets work on WWF-Viet Nam's home page; GAIN's workforce nutrition programme (factory canteens) does not list Vietnam [general, Medium, AFN-49, revealed].
- Lever, Humane World and Dharma Voices for Animals figures are organisation claims.
