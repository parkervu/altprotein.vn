# Public meals: the rulebook, who writes the menus, and what schools actually serve

Line: W7-L1 PUBLIC MEALS: RULEBOOK, MENU AUTHORITY AND A REVEALED MENU AUDIT (wave 7). Source prefix: PMR. Date: 25 September 2026.

## Verdict

Part VI's school logic rests on the wrong rule, and the actor check's correction is only half right. National guidance (Decision 3958/QĐ-BYT, December 2025) has no animal share and asks for plant protein (legumes, tofu) at least twice a week. But the menu software that schools and caterers use (MOET, Ajinomoto and NIN) still prints a standard of "animal protein at least 48% of protein", and in 48 published school-weeks from 22 primary schools in Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi and Hai Phong only 15% of lunch days carried any plant protein, only 9 school-weeks met the twice-weekly rule, one day in 239 had no animal protein, and no dish used textured soy or mock meat. Plant protein enters school lunches as tofu (sometimes peanuts or sesame) served beside meat, so an ingredient sold into the plant slot replaces tofu, not meat; the only school route that removes meat is a blend in the minced-meat and fish-cake dishes that appear on 28.5% of days. Yield class: **changed a conclusion** (the school verdict, route R4's school displacement logic, DMV-17 and TPP-04 change), plus **added a constraint** (supplier documents, ward-level caterer selection, daily public disclosure); the benchmark headline of about 19,000 t of protein by 2035 does not move.

## Headline findings

1. **The rule.** Decision 3958/QĐ-BYT asks schools to combine animal protein "ít nhất 4 - 5 ngày trong tuần, xen kẽ đạm có nguồn gốc thực vật (đậu các loại, đậu phụ) ít nhất 2 lần mỗi tuần" (on at least 4 to 5 days a week, alternating with plant protein, legumes and tofu, at least twice a week), fish or seafood 2 to 3 times a week, protein at 13 to 20% of energy and no animal share. It limits processed meat and sausages as fat sources, names no processed plant protein, and its 139-item food table lists tofu, soybeans, soy milk, legumes, peanuts and sesame but no textured soy, gluten or analogue. It is guidance with no sanction and excludes preschools. [VN-direct, High, AIB-39; revealed]
2. **The software standard.** Nutrition sheets from the "Phần mềm Xây dựng Thực đơn Cân bằng Dinh dưỡng" (balanced menu software) of MOET, Ajinomoto Vietnam and NIN, published by a Hanoi primary school for five weeks in March to May 2026, print a standard of animal protein at least 48% of total protein per lunch. It is not enforced: 9 of 25 published days fall below it (27.2% to 36.2% on fish and beef-with-tofu days). [VN-direct, Medium, PMR-26; revealed]
3. **What schools serve.** In 239 lunch days (48 school-weeks, 22 schools, three provinces, March to September 2026) 36 days (15.1%) had plant protein in a protein dish: tofu on 30, peanuts on 3, sesame salt on 2, white beans with beef on 1. Ho Chi Minh City 5.6% of days, Hanoi 28.7%, Hai Phong 17.6%. Nine of 22 schools served none in the weeks read. [VN-direct, High, PMR-10 to PMR-31; revealed]
4. **How the plant slot is filled.** On 27 of the 36 plant days tofu is a second dish beside a meat or fish main (the northern two-dish lunch); on 8 it is mixed into a meat dish (ribs braised with tofu, tofu stuffed with pork, crab noodle soup); one Ho Chi Minh City day served Sichuan-style tofu alone, a dish usually cooked with minced pork. Pork leads 38% of days. No textured soy, *chay* (vegetarian) mock meat or branded analogue appears in 333 protein dishes. [VN-direct, High, PMR-10 to PMR-31; revealed]
5. **Compliance is low.** Only 9 of 48 school-weeks (19%) met the plant rule (10 if tofu soups count) and 17 of 48 (35%) the fish rule. Both rules are guidance, and menus are pork, chicken and egg led. [VN-direct, High, PMR-10 to PMR-31; AIB-39; revealed]
6. **Protein per lunch.** The software sheets imply 31.8 g of protein per lunch on average (21.2 to 38.6 g, rice included), 17.5 g of it animal (our calculation). Tofu days carry more protein (33.4 g) but less animal protein (14.8 g against 19.3 g) than other days: in the two-dish format tofu takes the place of a second animal dish such as eggs or pork loaf. [VN-direct, Medium, PMR-26; revealed and inferred]
7. **Who writes the menus.** In Hanoi a ward evaluation council assigns caterers and food, milk and water suppliers to each school (one ward notice covers 14 public schools from preschool to lower secondary), the caterer writes the menu with grams and kcal and the principal co-signs; one caterer (Hoa Sua) wrote near-identical menus for three schools and was assigned five schools in one ward. In Ho Chi Minh City menus come from school kitchens or caterers approved by the principal; in Hai Phong the caterer writes a 20-day menu under the tender. The Ajinomoto menu bank (120+ menus, 360+ dishes) sits behind a school login and asks each school for its lunch fee. [VN-direct, High, PMR-05; PMR-06; PMR-07; PMR-18; PMR-20; PMR-22 to PMR-24; AFN-46; revealed]
8. **The price and the tender.** Lunch costs VND 35,000 in Ho Chi Minh City (2025 to 2026) and VND 40,000 in Hanoi for 2026 to 2027, of which the city pays VND 28,000 (Resolution 84/2026/NQ-HĐND). A Hai Phong school tender fixes the price at the regulated norm and scores caterers on capability and on the extra portion value they add; it asks for a food-safety certificate, revenue of at least VND 3 billion, three school or industrial-zone contracts and certified food suppliers, and allows ready-processed foods only with clear origin, compliant packaging and a use-by date. [VN-direct, High, PMR-06; PMR-07; PMR-32; PMR-33; revealed]
9. **The rule window.** Decision 973/QĐ-TTg (School Health Programme 2026 to 2035) orders a MOET circular on *bán trú* (school lunch and midday care) meal organisation in 2026 and MOET digital tools to build balanced menus in 2027 to 2028; its text names no company, menu count or milk. Directive 33/CT-TTg (14 August 2026) orders supplier listing and certificate checks and daily public disclosure of caterer, ingredients, invoices, menus and tray photos, and restricts no food type. [VN-direct, High, PMR-01; Medium, PMR-02; revealed]
10. **Cost of 25 g of protein** (our calculation, retail prices of 24 September 2026): tofu VND 4,900 to 7,500; eggs 8,575 to 10,000; chicken thigh 9,750; pangasius 16,675; lean pork 17,725; textured soy as an ingredient 925 to 2,675 (ex-factory) or 1,235 (TPP-01 target); branded retail textured analogue 28,000. A textured-soy ingredient giving the protein of an 80 g tofu portion costs VND 320 to 930 against VND 1,700 to 2,600 for the tofu, before oil, sauce and labour. [VN-direct, Low, DIE-05; DIE-23; COST-39; COST-40; COST-41; FORM-01; inferred]
11. **Hospitals and canteens.** The hospital tender accepts tofu at 120 g for 60 g of meat (about 13.1 g against 11.4 g of protein, our calculation) and asks for a tofu menu version among three sample menus. Shift-meal rules (Decision 1982 chapter V; union Conclusion 03) set floors, four food groups and two menus per meal but no plant slot; no factory canteen menu with grams was found. [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-44; AIB-43; APR-16; PMR-34; revealed]

## 1. The rulebook, read from the primary texts

The rule-by-rule extraction is in `data_public_meal_rules.csv` (13 instruments).

**Decision 3958/QĐ-BYT (25 December 2025), read in full including all seven appendices** [AIB-39].
- *Scope*: general schools that serve meals, boarding and semi-boarding schools. Preschools follow the MOET preschool programme instead.
- *Energy and macronutrients*: lunch 30 to 40% of daily energy; protein 13 to 20%, fat 20 to 30%, carbohydrate 55 to 65% of energy; protein grams computed as share times energy divided by 4.
- *Protein sources*: meat, poultry, fish, shrimp, eggs, milk described as high biological value; "các loại hạt giàu đạm như đậu, đỗ, lạc, vừng" (protein-rich seeds such as beans, peanuts, sesame) as plant protein; "phối hợp hợp lý" (combine sensibly).
- *Frequency rules*: fish or seafood 2 to 3 times a week; animal protein at least 4 to 5 days a week; plant protein (legumes, tofu) at least twice a week; menus not repeated within two weeks.
- *Processed foods*: limit saturated and trans fat, naming "thịt chế biến, xúc xích, lạp xưởng" (processed meat, sausages, Chinese sausage) and fried foods. Nothing on processed plant proteins, mock meat or textured soy.
- *Portion and cost norms*: vegetables 80 to 120 g (primary), salt under 4 g and sugar under 15 g a day; no protein-food gram norms and no cost norm.
- *Micronutrients*: iron absorption is rated 10% when the diet holds 30 to 90 g of meat or fish a day and 15% above 90 g; zinc absorption is rated poor (15%) with "ít hoặc không có chất đạm động vật hoặc cá" (little or no animal protein or fish). These appendices give any reviewer a reason to keep meat or fish on the plate every day.
- *Food table (Appendix 7)*: 139 foods. Plant protein entries: black beans, cowpeas, peas, soybeans, mung beans, peanuts, sesame, tofu (10.9 g protein per 100 g) and soy milk. No textured soy, gluten or analogue. Pangasius fillet is listed at 9.7 g of protein per 100 g.
- *Supplier documents*: none beyond the Food Safety Law. *Menu authority*: the school; MOET is asked to "rà soát, bổ sung, hoàn thiện các phần mềm xây dựng thực đơn" (review and complete the menu software) to match the guidance.
- *Anything restricting processed plant protein?* Not explicitly. The practical barriers are indirect: the food table that menu software uses has no textured soy entry, and the micronutrient appendices favour animal foods.

**Decision 973/QĐ-TTg (1 June 2026), full text** [PMR-01]. The programme asks to "nhân rộng mô hình bữa ăn học đường" (scale up the school meal model) with menus that meet the preschool programme and MOH school-meal guidance. Appendix II assigns MOET a circular on school nutrition education and *bán trú* meal organisation (due 2026), the scale-up (ongoing) and digital tools for balanced menus (2027 to 2028), and the Ministry of Finance a revision of Circular 14/2007 on school health funding (2026). It targets 100% of schools with meals meeting food-safety conditions by 2030 (93.1% in 2026) and allows funding from enterprises and international aid. It says nothing on protein, milk or TH Group; the "400 menus and a daily milk carton" model reported for this programme [AFN-45] comes from press coverage, not the decision.

**Decision 1982/QĐ-BYT (1 July 2026), chapter V on shift meals** [APR-16]. Shift meals must ensure rational nutrition and food safety following the Ten tips, the 2026 recommended intakes and NIN shift-meal guidance; no plant slot, no protein norm, no cost norm (read through a summary, Medium). The general adult advice is "sử dụng đa dạng các nguồn thực phẩm giàu đạm từ động vật và thực vật; ưu tiên cá, thịt nạc, trứng, sữa và các loại đậu đỗ" (use diverse animal and plant protein; prefer fish, lean meat, eggs, milk and legumes). We did not find the NIN shift-meal guidance on NIN's policy page.

**Other instruments.** MOET Decision 2195/QĐ-BGDĐT (2022) asks for at least 10 foods, at least 5 of 8 food groups and "sữa tươi và các chế phẩm từ sữa" (fresh milk and dairy) in menus, with no legume or animal-share rule [PMR-03]. Directive 33/CT-TTg (14 August 2026) is the national source of the daily-disclosure regime that Ho Chi Minh City's Document 9632 implements [PMR-02; AIB-42]. The union's Conclusion 03 sets shift-meal floors of VND 20,000 (regions I and II) and VND 18,000 (regions III and IV), four food groups and two menus per meal [AIB-43]. Decision 1340/QĐ-TTg, the animal-share target Part VI cites, is a school-milk programme whose targets ran to 2020 [CHN-33].

A menu-bank partnership is a real lever but its content is closed to desk research.

## 3. Revealed menu audit

**Sample.** 48 school-weeks (239 lunch days, 333 protein dishes) from 22 public primary schools: Ho Chi Minh City 12 schools and 25 weeks (September 2026), Hanoi 6 schools and 16 weeks (March to May and September 2026), Hai Phong 4 schools and 7 weeks (September 2026). All weeks postdate Decision 3958. Sources are school websites on the provincial school-site platforms; menus were read from text, PDF, spreadsheets and images. Day-level coding is in `data_school_menu_audit.csv`, weekly results in `data_school_week_summary.csv`.

**What fills the plant days.** Fried or braised tofu in tomato sauce (*đậu sốt cà chua*), often with a few grams of minced pork (the Ngoc Lam sheet lists 80 g tofu, 7 g pork, 15 g tomato), tofu with spring onion, Sichuan-style tofu, ribs braised with tofu, tofu stuffed with pork, crab and tofu noodle soup, peanuts roasted with salt and sugar, peanuts with sesame salt, sesame salt with pork. The one possibly animal-free day is a single Sichuan-tofu main in Ho Chi Minh City. [VN-direct, High, PMR-10; PMR-24; PMR-31; revealed]

**Robustness.** Per school, the average plant-day share is 12.7% (median 10%). Excluding the one school with six weeks of sheets gives 11.5%. The September 2026 weeks (all three cities) give 10.6%; the March to May weeks are all from Hanoi and give 32%, so season and city are confounded. Hanoi's higher share comes from its two-dish format: a second dish is where tofu goes. Caterer clustering matters: Hoa Sua menus for three schools are near-identical, so the Hanoi evidence is closer to four independent menu writers than six. [VN-direct, High; inferred]

**Prices published.** VND 35,000 a lunch at two Ho Chi Minh City schools in March and April 2026 [PMR-32; PMR-33]; VND 35,000 including VAT at a Hanoi school in spring 2026 [PMR-26]; VND 40,000 in Hanoi for 2026 to 2027 with VND 28,000 from the city [PMR-06]. No canteen or hospital meal price was published in what we read.

**Hospitals and canteens.** Beyond the one hospital tender [AIB-44] and Haseca's list of sample dish types, which includes stuffed tofu and soft tofu but no days, grams or prices [PMR-34], we found no published factory-canteen or hospital menu. This is a clean negative for desk research on canteen menus.

## 4. Computations

**Protein per lunch** (our calculation from 25 software sheets, `data_school_nutrition_sheets.csv`; protein grams = kcal × protein share ÷ 4). Mean 31.8 g (21.2 to 38.6 g), of which 17.5 g animal and 14.2 g plant (mostly rice: 110 g raw rice gives about 8.7 g). Tofu days: 33.4 g total, 14.8 g animal. Other days: 30.7 g total, 19.3 g animal. Fish days are the lowest (23.4 g) partly because the software uses 9.7 g of protein per 100 g of pangasius. The Hoa Sua sheets give the portions behind these numbers: 85 to 95 g raw pork or chicken, 67 to 70 g fish, 80 g tofu, one egg or 4 to 5 quail eggs per dish. Part VI's assumption of 20 g of protein per protein dish (DMA-083) is consistent with this. [VN-direct, Medium, PMR-24; PMR-26; inferred]

**Implied protein budget per lunch** (our calculation). Meal VND 35,000 to 40,000; food 60 to 80% of the meal [AIB-35]; protein items 30 to 50% of food (our assumption) gives VND 6,300 to 16,000 a lunch, or VND 25,200 to 64,000 per 100 g for 25 g of protein. At retail prices an observed tofu day (fish 70 g, tofu 80 g, pork 7 g) costs about VND 10,500 for 20.5 g of protein; an observed pork day (pork 90 g plus pork 65 g) about VND 20,500 for 29.5 g. Tofu days are the cheap days that balance the budget. [VN-direct, Low, PMR-23; PMR-24; COST-39; DIE-15; DIE-23; inferred]

**Would a plant ingredient in the rule-required slot displace meat or tofu?** Tofu. The observed plant slot is a second dish beside meat (27 of 36 plant days) or tofu cooked into a meat dish (8 of 36); no school replaced the meat main with a plant dish. An ingredient sold into that slot competes with tofu at VND 1,700 to 2,600 per 80 g portion and removes no meat, exactly as a sale into an existing *chay* day removes no meat. Two things would displace meat: more plant days, where tofu replaces a second animal dish (on the software sheets, tofu days carry 4.5 g less animal protein than other days), and blends in minced dishes. [VN-direct, Medium, PMR-10 to PMR-31; PMR-26; inferred]

**Size of the school opportunities** (our calculation, using Part VI's 0.8 billion school lunches a year, range 0.5 to 1.2 billion, DMA-081). The plant slot as served today: 0.8 billion × 15.1% × 8.7 g = about 1,050 t of protein a year (660 to 1,580 t); if every school met the twice-weekly rule (40% of days), about 2,780 t (1,740 to 4,180 t). This is tofu protein that a new ingredient could take but that displaces no meat. A 25% blend in every minced dish, replacing about 2 g of meat protein per dish (our assumption: 25% of about 8.6 g in a 45 g raw minced portion), on 28.5% of days would remove about 460 t of meat protein a year (285 to 685 t). For comparison, Part VI's D-BENCH path has schools delivering 80 t in 2035 and all institutional meals displacing 650 t. [VN-direct, Low; inferred]

## 5. Re-assessment of Part VI

**School verdict (ch25 summary and 25.3; ch29.5 "Do not start in schools").** The conclusion "not first" survives, but for different reasons. Schools are not guided by an animal-share target in national rules; they are guided by caterers, prices and habit, with a software default that prints a 48% animal floor and a national plant rule that most menus ignore. The plant slot exists, is small (about 1,000 t of protein a year), and is filled by tofu at VND 4,900 to 7,500 per 25 g. A school entry is a caterer ingredient that beats tofu on cost and matches it on children's acceptance, after canteens; a branded product or a "replaces meat" pitch has no place in it. Trust is now managed through documents: Directive 33 puts every invoice and menu in front of parents daily.

**Route R4 displacement logic (ch28, DMA-099 to DMA-114).** Part VI applies one displacement factor (0.8) to canteen and school adoption and bases school adoption on Decision 1340. For schools, protein sold into the plant slot should count as delivered but not displaced (factor 0, like existing *chay* days); only blends in meat dishes, or added plant days that replace a second animal dish, displace meat. In D-BENCH 2035 school adoption delivers 0.08 kt, so correcting this lowers R4 displacement by at most 0.06 kt (0.65 to about 0.58 kt) and total displacement from 2.35 to about 2.29 kt; the 19,000 t headline and the 0.2% displacement share do not change (our calculation from `demand_outputs.csv`).

**TPP-04 (canteen protein ingredient).** Still canteen-first. Add school and hospital caterers as later buyers with a sharper price target for the school plant slot (beat 80 g of tofu, VND 1,700 to 2,600 for about 8.7 g of protein) and the documents caterers must hold (food-safety certificate, clear origin, labelled packaging with a use-by date). The profile's "about 20 g per dish" is consistent with observed portions.

**DMV-05 (canteen trial).** Add a school arm with one caterer that writes one menu for several schools, testing a textured-soy or blended dish in the plant slot and in one minced dish, measuring served portions and plate waste. Published daily menus under Directive 33 give a free control series.

**DMV-17 (base public-meal guidance on protein quality, not an animal share).** Partly overtaken and mis-aimed. The national guidance already has no animal share. The live targets are the menu software's 48% animal-protein default and food list, the MOET *bán trú* circular due in 2026 and the digital menu tools due 2027 to 2028. Supplier documents are now set by Directive 33 and local tenders, so the second half of DMV-17 is largely done.

## Next-wave candidates

1. **The software's animal-share default and food list** (different kind: a document request, not desk research). What is the basis of "animal protein at least 48% of protein" in the MOET, Ajinomoto and NIN software, is it a default or a hard check, and does the food list include textured soy? Desk cannot close it (school login). If the floor is a hard check or textured soy is absent, a plant ingredient cannot be written into software-built menus and DMV-17 becomes the first policy move for schools; if it is a soft default, the school route depends only on caterers.
2. **The MOET *bán trú* circular due in 2026** (different kind: rule tracking). Desk can check when a draft appears on the MOET portal. A clause on processed foods, supplier documents or menu software would open or close the school route and set TPP-04's document list.
3. **Caterer cost sheets** (deeper; 3 calls to Hoa Sua, Ngoi Sao Xanh and Tu Anh). Wholesale prices of tofu, pork, chicken and eggs and the food share of a VND 35,000 to 40,000 meal would replace our retail-based budget and fix TPP-04's school price target.
4. **A two-week plate-waste trial** with one caterer serving several schools (different kind: tested evidence; part of DMV-05). It would show whether children eat a textured-soy dish in the plant slot and a 20 to 30% blend in *xíu mại* or *thịt viên*. A good result moves schools to a second route for hybrids.
5. **Ten hospital patient-meal tenders** (deeper; desk possible on hospital sites). Would show whether tofu substitution and tofu menu versions are standard; it would strengthen hospitals as a small, specification-led plant channel. Low priority.
6. **Preschool menus** (different kind; desk possible on the same platforms). Preschools sit outside Decision 3958; a 20-menu audit would show whether their protein mix differs. Low priority.

**Where to stop.** More primary-school menu weeks from the same platforms would be noise: 48 school-weeks give a clear pattern and the remaining uncertainty is about software rules and caterer costs, which need calls. Further desk searching for factory-canteen menus would also be noise: caterers do not publish them.

## Limits

- Convenience sample: schools were found by probing school-site names on provincial platforms, not drawn at random, and only schools that publish menus are included. Menus are plans, not what was served or eaten.
- Three provinces only; no upland, stunting-hotspot or border-commune school was reached, and Da Nang and most provincial portals were unreachable. The Hanoi weeks cluster around two caterers and one school with six weeks.
- Coding from dish names: minced pork inside tofu dishes and tofu inside noodle soups are inferred from the usual recipe. Mixed dishes are counted once.
- Protein grams come from software values printed on menu sheets (with the software's composition data, including 9.7 g of protein per 100 g of pangasius), not from measurement.
- Costs use retail prices of 24 September 2026; institutional prices are lower. Textured-soy dish costs exclude oil, sauce and labour.
- Directive 33, Decision 1982 chapter V, Decision 2195 and the hospital tender were read through a fetch tool that summarises pages (Medium). The Ajinomoto menu bank, Circular 96/2025/TT-BQP, Hai Phong's fee resolution and the NIN shift-meal guidance were not reached. A single OpenAlex search found no Vietnamese peer-reviewed study of school-lunch composition (Low-confidence negative).
