# Domestic textured protein (T1): an investor card, and why nobody has built it

Line: W7-L5 Investor card for domestic textured protein (T1) and why nobody has built it (wave 7). Source prefix: TIC. Date: 25 September 2026.

## Verdict

Capital is not what stops a Vietnamese textured-protein line. Shandong makers list complete dry textured vegetable protein (TVP) lines of 0.5 to 1.5 t an hour, with dryer, at USD 11,000 to 100,000. That is a fifteenth or less of the USD 1.5 to 4.0 M of equipment Part I assumed, and a whole plant can be installed for about USD 0.3 to 1.4 M. The real funding need is USD 0.6 to 3.5 M of working capital. At price parity with Chinese product landed at USD 1.29 to 1.44 per kg, the margin runs from minus 22% to plus 47%. One input we could not verify sets its sign: the delivered price of food-grade, low-temperature defatted soy flour. That flour is 63 to 85% of cost and comes from the same countries that export the finished product. The pool a soy line can serve is about 12,800 t a year, not 40,000 t, and fragmented. That, a lowest-grade Chinese price and almost no tariff shelter explain the non-entry better than capital or technology do. T1 is a debt-financed SME project or an add-on for an incumbent, not a venture thesis. Yield class: **changed a conclusion** (T1's deal type, its capex, its kill test and "three to four lines by 2035"), plus **added a constraint** (flour supply and price).

## Headline findings

1. **Chinese dry TVP lines are cheap and on offer in Vietnam.** Listings from eight Shandong makers with stated capacities price complete dry-TVP lines (mixer, twin- or single-screw extruder, dryer, cooler) at USD 11,000 to 100,000 per set for 200 to 1,500 kg/h. Examples: Jinan Saibainuo SLG85 and SLG95 (500 and 1,000 kg/h) at USD 30,000 to 80,000; Jinan Dayi SLG115 (600 to 800 kg/h) at USD 30,000. A Ho Chi Minh City dealer offers Chinese soy lines of 100 to 2,000 kg/h [general, Low, TIC-01; TIC-03; TIC-05; FORM-34, revealed]. These are list prices, not quotes, and ranges often span several models.
2. **Installed capex is USD 0.28 to 1.0 M for a 0.5 t/h line and USD 0.32 to 1.38 M for a 1 t/h line.** This allows up to three times the list price, plus auxiliaries, fit-out, certification and a 15% contingency (our calculation). Working capital of 3 to 4 months of revenue at parity is USD 0.6 to 3.5 M, the larger need [VN-direct, Low, TIC-02; TIC-07; COST-21, inferred].
3. **Chinese textured protein lands in Vietnam at USD 1.29 to 1.44 per kg duty-paid.** The basis is the 2025 FOB unit value of USD 1.237 (10,201 t), plus 3 to 8% freight and insurance, 0% or 5% duty and USD 15 to 40 per t for port and inland (our calculation). USD 1.24 is China's lowest price to any of eight Asian and US partners (world average USD 1.95). India shipped 2,372 t at USD 0.98, which lands at about USD 1.08 to 1.15 [VN-direct, Medium, TIC-12; TIC-14; BUY-02, revealed].
4. **The domestic full cost of flour-based TVP is USD 761 to 1,294 per t for a 1 t/h line at 90% utilisation, and USD 858 to 1,573 for a 0.5 t/h line at 50%.** That is VND 19,800 to 40,900 per kg, or VND 3,800 to 7,900 per 100 g of protein. Raw material is 63 to 85% of the cost [VN-direct, Low, TIC-12; TIC-13; COST-01; COST-19; COST-20; COST-21, inferred].
5. **Flour price sets the sign of the margin.** At Chinese parity, the low-cost case earns plus 33 to 47% and the high-cost case minus 22 to plus 10%. To earn 15% at a 1 t/h line running 70 to 90% of the time, delivered flour must cost less than USD 700 to 1,076 per t. The observed anchors are USD 627 to 925 per t: China's soybean-meal exports to Vietnam at USD 604 per t FOB (grade unknown), and Indian and Dutch soy flour at USD 783 to 896 per t [VN-direct, Low, TIC-12; TIC-13, inferred].
6. **The pool a soy extrusion line can serve is about 12,800 t a year.** In 2025 Vietnam imported 12,832 t of HS 2106.10 from its three low-cost origins, China, India and Serbia. That is about 30% of the 40,000 t R1 pool; gluten and isolates need other plants. China-origin 2106.10 grew 54% from 2019 to 2025. It did not triple: the tripling is mostly HS 3504 at about USD 1 per kg [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-02; TIC-12, revealed].
7. **One efficient line needs most of that pool.** A 1 t/h line at 90% (7,200 t) must displace 56% of 2025 textured-type imports, or 71% of China-origin 2106.10. That is 26 to 131 buyers the size of Vissan's illustrative soy-protein use (55 to 280 t a year). The benchmark path supports about 5,700 t of domestic soy texturate by 2035. That is one 1 t/h line or two 0.5 t/h lines, not "three to four" (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, BUY-02; BUY-04; BUY-05, inferred].
8. **T1 is not a venture thesis.** Its return is a commodity spread that Chinese exporters can compress: in 2025, China's textured-protein price to Vietnam was about USD 630 per t above its soybean-meal price. T1 has no intellectual property, its funding need is mostly working capital, its exit is a trade sale, and it displaces almost no meat. It fits a debt-financed SME project or an incumbent add-on. Candidate owners are a large *chay* (vegetarian food) maker, a group of chay makers, an ingredient distributor or a soy processor (our inference) [VN-direct, Low, TIC-12; AIS-01; AIS-15, inferred].
9. **Several reasons explain the non-entry, and capital is not one of them.** The pool is small and fragmented. The main input is imported from the competing countries. Vietnam buys China's lowest-priced grade, and India lands lower still. The net shelter from freight and duty is only about USD 30 to 130 per t. Buyers ask for non-GMO and for several shapes. One founder said domestic raw materials had "two problems, one is quality, two is price that is not yet competitive" (our translation) [VN-direct, Low to Medium, TIC-14; FORM-10; FORM-37; BUY-38; FORM-01, revealed and stated]. We found no evidence either way on credit.
10. **Domestic craft protein shows the same pattern.** Distributors sell Chinese vital gluten as *bột mì căn* (gluten powder for *mì căn*, wheat-gluten seitan) and tell buyers to mix it with water instead of washing flour. My Hoa (Vinh Long) still makes about 7 t of *tàu hũ ky* (tofu skin) a day, by hand, in 27 households, mostly from imported soybeans [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-37; FORM-14, revealed].
11. **We found no domestic extruder and no dormant project.** No Vietnamese textured-protein extruder was named in wave 1 or here. The national legal database holds customs classifications of soy isolates and a Brenntag-imported soy-gluten texturate (2106.10.00), but no investment approval for a soy or textured-protein plant [VN-direct, Medium, TIC-18; TIC-19, revealed].
12. **T1's lead in the rankings is fragile.** T1 leads the balanced preset by 0.05 (3.95 against 3.9). A one-point fall in market pull or defensibility hands first place to the cassava sugar hub (T4). Our proposed changes (capital efficiency 4 to 5, Vietnam advantage 3 to 2) leave the balanced score unchanged (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, plays.csv, inferred].

## 1. What we tested

Part VI raised T1's market pull from 4 to 5. Investors in the actor check say they now fund "real unit economics", offtake and contracted revenue [general, Medium, AIS-01; AIS-02; AIS-15, stated]. The package gave only an indicative ex-factory cost with assumed capex. We asked four questions:

- What does a 0.5 or 1 t/h line cost?
- What does it cost to run in Vietnam?
- What margin and payback does it earn at price parity with Chinese product?
- Why has no one built one?

We did no web searches. The evidence comes from equipment makers' marketplace listings, the UN Comtrade public API, Vietnamese legal and company pages, Scite and OpenAlex, and the package's own sources.

**Reading.**

- **The two cases give opposite answers.** The low case gives margins of 33 to 47% and payback in under a year. The high case gives losses, or margins up to 10% with payback of 3 years or more. Investors in the actor check set a threshold: a margin under about 15% at parity makes T1 an incumbent or debt project (AIS-L02). The high case falls below it; the low case clears it easily.

## 8. What kind of deal T1 is

- **Not a venture thesis.** There is no intellectual property. The product is a commodity with cheaper imports, including India's. The margin is a spread that Chinese exporters can narrow: about USD 630 per t between their 2025 textured-protein and soybean-meal prices to Vietnam (our calculation from TIC-12). Most of the funding need is working capital. The exit would be a trade sale. For impact funds, route R1 displaces almost no meat (ch28.2), as the parallel funder line (W7-L3) also finds.
- **A debt-financed SME project or an incumbent add-on.** The economics favour an owner that already has buyers, distribution or flour access:
  - a large chay maker, or two to three makers pooling offtake;
  - an ingredient distributor that now imports Chinese textured soy (Brenntag imports a soy-gluten texturate under 2106.10.00 [VN-direct, Medium, TIC-18, revealed]);
  - a soy processor (tofu or soy milk);
  - a Chinese textured-protein maker locating in Vietnam (inference; no evidence of intent).
- **How to finance it.** Bank debt and trade finance for flour and receivables fit better than equity (inference).
- **The role for a startup.** A founder can operate a line with an anchor buyer or distributor as co-owner and a signed flour supply. Raising venture money to build a line alone does not fit (inference).

## 9. Why nobody has built it: the revealed non-entry

We ranked the explanations by the strength of the evidence. The full table is `data_t1_non_entry_evidence.csv`.

1. **Scale and fragmentation (strong).** The textured-type pool is about 12,800 t a year and grows about 7.5% a year for China-origin product. One efficient line must take 30 to 60% of it from many small buyers served by distributors [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-02; TIC-12; FORM-37; BUY-36; BUY-37, revealed].
2. **No input advantage (strong).** Vietnam crushes 2.6 Mt of soybeans a year for feed meal at Phu My, Ho Chi Minh City (former Ba Ria-Vung Tau), and has no food-grade defatted flake stream [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-10, revealed]. Its soybean-meal imports are feed grade: 3.92 Mt from Argentina at USD 311 per t in 2025 [VN-direct, Medium, TIC-15, revealed]. A Vietnamese line would buy 63 to 85% of its cost from the countries whose product it replaces.
3. **Low competitor prices (strong).** China's 2025 price to Vietnam was its lowest in the region (USD 1.24 against 1.34 to 3.29), and India lands lower still [VN-direct, Medium, TIC-14; BUY-02, revealed].
4. **Little tariff shelter (moderate).** MFN duty is 5% on textured protein against 2% on food-grade flour. The preferential rate for China is probably 0% (pending W7-L2). The net natural shelter after freight and duty on both product and input is about USD 30 to 130 per t (our calculation) [VN-direct, Medium, REG2-20; REG2-21, revealed].
5. **Specification and quality (moderate, partly stated).** An Nhien declares Serbian soy protein concentrate and markets non-GMO soy [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01, revealed]. The founder of VMEAT, a plant-based startup, said over 90% of its raw materials came from the United States, Canada and Europe. He said domestic materials had "two problems, one is quality, two is price that is not yet competitive" and that even domestic soybeans cost "more than double" imported ones (our translation) [VN-direct, Low, BUY-38, stated].
6. **Capital (argues against).** Chinese lines list at USD 11,000 to 100,000 and are sold in Ho Chi Minh City [VN-direct, Low, FORM-34; TIC-11, revealed]. VMEAT built its own workshop for about VND 3 billion of paid-in capital (2018) [VN-direct, Low, BUY-38, stated].
7. **Credit (not tested).** We found no evidence on credit constraints for chay makers or would-be extruders.

**Craft analogues point the same way.**

- *Mì căn* makers have moved from washing flour to mixing imported gluten powder. Distributors sell Chinese vital gluten in 25 kg bags as *bột mì căn* and describe the recipe [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-37; FORM-12, revealed].
- We found no gluten wet-milling plant. Each tonne of gluten comes with several tonnes of wheat starch, which would have to compete with Vietnam's cheap cassava starch (inference).
- Tofu skin, by contrast, is still made domestically by hand. My Hoa in Vinh Long makes about 7 t a day in 27 households with over 200 workers, from mostly imported soybeans [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-14, revealed]. Domestic soy texturising survives where the product is craft-made and not traded as a low-price commodity (inference).

**Clean negatives.**

- No Vietnamese textured-protein extruder, past or present, was found.
- The legal database searches for *protein đậu nành* (soy protein), *đạm thực vật* (plant protein) and "textured" returned customs classifications only. They found no investment approval or factory project [VN-direct, Medium, TIC-19, revealed].
- The company-registry aggregator returned 403, so small or closed firms may be missed.
- One 2026 paper from the Industrial University of Ho Chi Minh City works on high-moisture meat analogues. We saw only the metadata, so we do not know where its extrusion was done [VN-direct, Low, TIC-21, revealed].

## Next-wave candidates

1. **Delivered flour and TVP quotes.** A deeper version of this line. Collect five written quotes for food-grade, low-temperature defatted soy flour (GMO and non-GMO), CIF Cat Lai, and three for Chinese plain TVP. Desk tools cannot close it; it takes emails from a partner buyer, tens of hours in all. The result decides T1's kill test:
   - flour below about USD 700 per t keeps T1 as the lead play, with an SME or incumbent structure;
   - flour above about USD 940 per t removes T1 as a standalone plant and leaves it as an incumbent option only.

   This is the most valuable next step.
2. **Priced offer test with conditional orders (AIS-L01).** A different kind of question: revealed commitment, not cost. Calls and field outreach are needed. Three or more orders totalling 2,000 t or more would confirm market pull 5; none would return it to 4.
3. **Declaration-level customs data for HS 2106.10 (DMV-02, OQ-231).** A deeper version of the pool question. It needs a customs or vendor dataset, which we did not have. The textured share of 2106.10 and the importers' names would fix the pool (12,800 t is an upper bound) and name the distributors a line must displace or recruit.
4. **Make-or-buy interviews.** A different kind of question. Talk to three chay makers, two extruder vendors and one regional maker such as Growthwell (AIS-L03, the calls half). This would test whether scale and specification, as we infer, or credit, which we could not test, drive the non-entry. Only calls can close it.
5. **Firm equipment quotes.** A deeper version of this line: requests for quotation (RFQs) to three Shandong makers and one European maker. They would move capex from Low to Medium but are unlikely to change any conclusion, because the capital charge is 1 to 6% of full cost with Chinese equipment. **Further desk work on equipment listings would be noise.**
6. **Preferential duty (W7-L2).** Already running. It moves the parity price by about 5% and settles which margin column applies. It should not reopen this line.

## Limits

- Equipment prices are marketplace list prices, not quotes. Ranges often cover several models, and the same marketplace shows placeholder prices for other goods.
- The flour price anchors are Comtrade unit values of mixed grades. None is verified as low-temperature, food-grade or non-GMO. Chinese soybean meal to Vietnam at USD 604 per t may be fermented or specialty feed meal.
- The composition of HS 2106.10 (textured protein against concentrate powder and premix) is unknown, so the pool of 12,832 t is an upper bound for textured protein. It covers all uses, food and feed.
- Freight, working-capital terms, interest rates, fit-out, auxiliaries, staffing and overhead are assumptions. We retrieved no China to Vietnam container rate and no State Bank lending rate.
- Preferential duty rates were not checked here (W7-L2). India's preferential rate was not checked.
- Electricity tariffs were not re-verified after July 2026. The bag price is Part I's assumption.
- Buyer volumes are anchored only on Part VI's illustrative Vissan range. No chay maker publishes volumes.
- The search for Vietnamese extruders was limited: no web search, and the company registry aggregator was blocked. Small or closed workshops may be missed.
- The freeze-alignment TEA was read through the publisher page summary, not the full text.
- All margins are before tax. They ignore Chinese price responses, product-mix changeovers and lunar-calendar seasonality, beyond stock in working capital.
