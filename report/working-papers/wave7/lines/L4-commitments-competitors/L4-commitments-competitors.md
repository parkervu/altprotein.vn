# Commitments, private label and competitors inside the recommended channels

Line: W7-L4 Commitments, private label and competitors inside the recommended channels (wave 7). Source prefix: CPC. Date: 25 September 2026.

## Verdict

The corporate-commitments lever is a clean negative for volume. Only Accor's target of 50% vegetarian or plant-based dishes by 2030 binds business units in Vietnam, no Vietnamese protein buyer publishes a protein-diversification target, and the slow Asian delivery of older cage-free pledges shows how such pledges land here. Retailer private label is a real route for *chay* (Buddhist vegetarian food) that is already in use: all five Co.op Select frozen chay dishes come from one certified co-packer, GN Foods, and Lotte Mart has its own chay label; but the products are starch and vegetable led, carry no protein claim, and we found no chay private label at WinCommerce or AEON. Inside the business-to-business channels Part VI recommends, the imported branded analogue has already been tried and dropped (CP Meat Zero on Kamereo, its minced product at 1.5 to 2.1 times CP's own minced pork per kilogram, now not orderable), while tofu and domestic dry soy-gluten pieces sell to kitchens at or below the cost of eggs and chicken per gram of protein, and a Chinese yeast protein is being marketed to Vietnamese makers through ACC. Yield class: **added a route** (private label through existing co-packers) and **added constraints** (the canteen rung and the functional-protein rung already have cheaper or earlier competitors), with one correction (launches_exits.csv LE-09) and a **clean negative** on commitments; the core conclusion and the demand model do not change.

## Headline findings

1. **One commitment binds Vietnamese units, and it counts dishes, not protein.** Accor's Good Food Policy (November 2024) commits all managed and franchised hotels to "50% of vegetarian or plant-based dishes in menus" by 2030, measured as an average over the hotel's whole food offer, with Novotel at 25% by 2026; Accor had 43 hotels (10,662 keys) operating in Vietnam in October 2024 [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-40; AFN-41; AFN-18, stated]. The metric can be met with vegetable, tofu and noodle dishes, so it creates no requirement for a new protein ingredient [VN-direct, Low, AFN-40, inferred].
2. **Asian delivery of the previous generation of pledges is slow.** Accor's 2025 cage-free pledge was "not yet achieved in Asia" (February 2026); IHG's East Asia and Pacific hotels were 4.1% aligned with its 2025 global cage-free commitment in 2024 (global 70.3%); Hilton's APAC managed hotels were at 42% in 2025 against 100% by end 2025; Minor Hotels' Asia Pacific and Indian Ocean region, which includes Vietnam, was at 13% in 2025 against 100% by 2027 [VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-18; CPC-04; CPC-05; CPC-07, revealed].
3. **No Vietnamese protein buyer publishes a diversification target.** FAIRR's 2024 index puts Vinamilk's plant-based products at 1.30% of its range (up 0.05 points since FY2022), with an alternative-proteins score of 45 of 100 and no timebound target [VN-direct, High, AFN-21, revealed]. ARE's Asia Protein Buyers 100 (February 2026) places Vinamilk in Tier 3, Masan in Tier 4, Vinh Hoan in Tier 5 and Minh Phu and Nam Viet in Tier 6; only 2 of 100 Asian buyers publish alternative-protein sourcing targets, and company-level theme scores are not published [VN-adjacent, High, AFN-18, revealed].
4. **Neither the pledge-makers nor the organisations that turn pledges into procurement are working on Vietnam.** Lever Foundation's country programmes do not include Vietnam; Marriott's 2026 to 2030 goals contain no food goal; Sodexo's group target is 70% of main dishes labelled low-carbon by 2030 and its Vietnamese operation is unconfirmed; CP Foods sells Meat Zero with no quantified target; Unilever sold The Vegetarian Butcher in March 2025 as "less scalable" in its portfolio [general and VN-adjacent, Medium to High, CPC-10; CPC-01; CPC-08; AIB-38; AIB-19; CPC-09, stated and revealed].
5. **Retailer private label for chay already runs through one certified co-packer.** Co.op Online names GN Foods (Long Hau Extended Industrial Park, Can Giuoc, Tay Ninh; BRCGS, ISO 22000, HACCP, halal) as the maker of all five Co.op Select frozen chay dishes, and Thai Corp Inter.VN and Vi Nguyen as makers of its tofu [VN-direct, High, CPC-15; ECO-35, revealed]. Co.op's supplier portal answers a product pitch within 10 working days [VN-direct, Medium, CPC-18, revealed].
6. **But today's private-label chay is not a protein product, and two large chains show none.** Co.op Select chay sells at VND 112,048 to 141,500 per kg and Lotte Mart Choice L chay at VND 107,800 to 117,250 per kg; their protein ingredients are wheat protein, soybean or none, with no protein claim [VN-direct, High, CPC-15; FORM-01, revealed]. WinCommerce's online chay assortment at its default Hanoi store returned 27 items and no house brand; AEON's online search showed no Topvalu chay or tofu [VN-direct, Low, CPC-19; CPC-20, revealed].
7. **The imported branded analogue has been tried in B2B foodservice and has stalled.** Kamereo lists eight CP Meat Zero SKUs imported from Thailand at VND 175,000 to 378,000 per kg, against CP's own frozen minced pork at VND 114,450 per kg on the same platform; the eight SKUs show 386 units sold in total against 24,769 for the minced pork, and none could be ordered on 25 September 2026 [VN-direct, Medium, AIS-25; CPC-12, revealed]. This corrects launches_exits.csv LE-09 ("none found").
8. **Kitchens already buy cheap plant protein, so the canteen rung is not empty.** On Kamereo, loose white tofu shows 78,058 units sold, and domestic dry soy-isolate and gluten rib pieces (Panko) sell at VND 135,000 per kg, marketed to chay eateries, school kitchens and restaurants [VN-direct, Medium, CPC-12, revealed and stated]. Per 20 g of protein (our calculation): dry soy-gluten pieces about VND 4,000 to 5,100 (on a borrowed protein range of 53.3 to 66.7 g per 100 g), tofu VND 7,200 to 9,500, eggs VND 8,800, chicken breast VND 9,600 and minced pork VND 13,900 [VN-direct, Low, CPC-12; FORM-01; DIE-12, inferred].
9. **The HoReCa (hotel, restaurant and catering) distributors carry domestic chay and tofu, not imports.** Kamereo's 6,176 product URLs include 37 tofu SKUs and no dry textured soy, gluten or Western analogue; MM Mega Market's 13,591 product URLs include about 39 chay prepared foods from An Nhien, Au Lac, Cau Tre, Cholimex and others and 31 tofu items, and no Meat Zero or Western analogue (names only) [VN-direct, Medium and Low, CPC-13; CPC-14, revealed].
10. **A fermentation-derived protein is already being sold to Vietnamese makers.** ACC (Asia Ingredients Group) has promoted Angel Yeast's yeast protein since May 2025 for bakery, beverages, dairy, plant-based dairy and meat alternatives, claiming 55 to 90% protein and PDCAAS 1.0; about 100 people attended its seminar; no catalogue SKU, price or named user is public [VN-direct, Medium, AIS-38; CPC-21; CPC-22, revealed]. TPP-06 (fungal biomass priced against egg and dairy protein) enters against this product, not an empty rung.
11. **Regional OEMs are not in Vietnam, and C.P. Vietnam is a feed and farm business.** Growthwell's site names Singapore, Malaysia and Taiwan and no Vietnam; INNOHAS's 2026 site shows only Korean plants, two years after its Vietnam plan [VN-adjacent, Medium, CPC-24; VCO-31; AIS-16, revealed]. CPF's Vietnamese revenue was THB 101.3 billion in 2025, of which food was THB 8.2 billion [VN-direct, High, AIB-19, revealed].

### 2.2 What the lever can and cannot do in Vietnam

- **It is not a volume lever.** No Vietnamese company has a target, and the investor benchmarks that could create one either do not cover Vietnam (FAIRR's engagement names no Asian company, AFN-20) or publish tiers across ten themes rather than protein scores (AFN-18) [VN-adjacent, High, AFN-18; AFN-20; AFN-21, revealed].
- **Where a target binds, it binds dishes.** Accor's metric is a share of dishes. In a cuisine with a chay tradition, tofu, vegetables and noodles can fill that share at no cost in new ingredients [VN-direct, Low, AFN-40, inferred]. Hotels stay a showcase, as Part VI concludes (ch25.4), but they now have a named buyer with a reason to meet a supplier.
- **Delivery in Asia lags.** Every hotel group whose cage-free pledge we could read reports Asian progress far below its global figure (IHG EAPAC 4.1% against 70.3% globally; Hilton APAC 42% against 93% to 96% elsewhere; Minor Asia Pacific hotels 13%), and ARE records "an escalation of public campaigns" in 2025 against companies "not meeting, delaying or obfuscating" cage-free pledges [VN-adjacent, Medium, CPC-04; CPC-05; CPC-07; AFN-18, revealed]. A plant-based menu pledge would face the same fragmented, property-level procurement that Minor describes [VN-adjacent, Low, CPC-07, inferred].
- **The organisations that convert pledges into purchase ratios are elsewhere.** Lever Foundation lists programmes in Malaysia, the Philippines, Thailand, Indonesia, Hong Kong and Taiwan, and Korea, and extended plant-based policy work to India and Thailand in July 2026, not Vietnam [VN-adjacent, Medium, CPC-10; AFN-39, stated].

- **AEON Vietnam's** Topvalu page makes no plant-based or welfare statement (AIB-32), and an online search showed no Topvalu chay, tofu or plant-based product [VN-direct, Low, CPC-20, revealed].
- **Bach Hoa Xanh** pages returned no content to our requests, and **GO!**'s online shop was not located. Both are gaps.

### 3.3 What a higher-protein private-label chay product would have to beat

On today's shelf, private-label chay costs VND 107,800 to 141,500 per kg at roughly 4 to 6 g of protein per 100 g (archetype medians, FORM-01). A 10 g per 100 g version at the same price per kg would cost roughly VND 108,000 to 142,000 per 100 g of protein, still above tofu and eggs, so the pitch must rest on taste, convenience and a numeric protein claim, not on protein value (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, CPC-15; FORM-01, inferred].

- **Meat Zero cannot compete on protein cost in this channel.** To cost less per gram of protein than CP's minced pork, the 1 kg pack would need more than 35.2 g of protein per 100 g and the 220 g pack more than 25.6 g (our calculation: price per kg divided by VND 69,364 per 100 g of protein). - **The domestic dry chay product that kitchens can buy is cheap per gram of protein.** Panko rib pieces list soy protein isolate, corn starch, wheat gluten, fibre and salt, keep 12 months at room temperature, and are described as a familiar choice of chay eateries, school kitchens and restaurants [VN-direct, Medium, CPC-12, stated]. If their protein lies in the 53.3 to 66.7 g range of comparable dry pieces, 20 g of protein costs VND 4,048 to 5,066, below tofu (VND 7,206 to 9,539), eggs (VND 8,790), chicken breast (VND 9,621) and minced pork (VND 13,873) on the same platform (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, CPC-12; FORM-01; DIE-12, inferred].

### 4.2 MM Mega Market

MM Mega Market's online shop has categories for frozen chay, chay foods and plant-origin foods. Its sitemap lists about 39 chay prepared foods from An Nhien, Au Lac, Pham Gia Phat, Cau Tre, Cholimex, Tri Hue and others, 31 tofu items (some under a "We Are Fresh" label whose owner we did not verify), three items named vegan and no Meat Zero or Western analogue [VN-direct, Low, CPC-14, revealed]. Product pages are assembled by script and the site's data service reset our connection, so we have names, not prices.

### 4.3 Ingredient distributors

- **ACC and Angel Yeast.** ACC's 18 June 2025 article presents yeast protein as "55 to 90%" protein with a PDCAAS of 1.0, non-GMO and suitable for sports nutrition, plant-based dairy, infant and senior nutrition and meat alternatives, and offers "end-to-end support" from supply to commercialisation; its catalogue online lists only coconut products and no protein price [VN-direct, Medium, CPC-21; CPC-22; AIS-38, revealed].
- **Brenntag Vietnam** lists plant-based dairy, meat and seafood alternatives as a market segment and proteins in its range, with no Vietnamese product list or price [VN-direct, Low, CPC-23, revealed]. **VMC and TDGIMEX** sell Chinese soy isolate and gluten in 20 to 25 kg bags at prices on request (package, FORM-11; BUY-36; BUY-37). **Barentz Vietnam's** page could not be reached.

### 4.4 Regional OEMs

Growthwell names Singapore, Malaysia and Taiwan and supplies chefs and food makers, with no Vietnam mention [VN-adjacent, Medium, CPC-24, revealed]. INNOHAS offers OEM and ODM plant-based meat and dumplings from Korean plants; its site does not mention Vietnam two years after VinaCapital's investment to bring products here [VN-adjacent, Medium, VCO-31; AIS-16, revealed]. We found no regional OEM supplying Vietnamese retailers or caterers.

### 4.5 What the competitor scan means for the recommended channels

- The channel Part VI ranks first for an ingredient (chay makers) already sells a dry, shelf-stable soy-gluten product into foodservice. A domestic textured protein would supply these makers (TPP-01); it would not reach caterers as a new product category (TPP-04) unless it is cheaper per gram of protein or performs better in bulk cooking [VN-direct, Low, CPC-12; CPC-13, inferred].
- The imported branded analogue has already stalled in B2B foodservice distribution, on price per kilogram inside the same catalogue as the meat it imitates [VN-direct, Medium, AIS-25; CPC-12, revealed].
- The functional-protein rung (priced against egg white and dairy proteins) has an entrant with distribution and application support: Angel Yeast through ACC [VN-direct, Medium, CPC-21, revealed].
- C.P. is both a named first buyer for extenders (TPP-03) and the owner of the analogue that stalled; its Vietnamese business is mainly feed and farming (food THB 8.2 billion of THB 101.3 billion in 2025) [VN-direct, High, AIB-19, revealed].

## 7. Limits

- **Search.** No web search: we reached pages by known addresses, sitemaps and site search. Some groups' policies were not reachable (Hyatt), some retailers returned no content (Bach Hoa Xanh) or were not located (GO! online, Barentz Vietnam), and several hotel groups and caterers were not checked.
- **Commitments.** APB 100 gives tiers across ten themes, not company protein-diversification scores, so we cannot report those scores for the five Vietnamese companies. Hilton's figures came through the fetch tool's reading of a page assembled by script (Medium). Accor's Vietnamese hotel count dates from October 2024.
- **Prices.** Listed prices on one day, VAT-inclusive where shown. Kamereo's units-sold counter has no stated period and may include small test orders; it ranks products, it does not measure volume. MM Mega Market prices were not retrievable. WinCommerce and AEON assortments were read for one online store each.
- **Protein.** No B2B page showed protein per 100 g except Kamereo's generic tofu table. Panko's protein is a borrowed range (Low). Tofu uses the same-SKU label from FORM-01 where available, otherwise FCT 10.9 g; tofu labels vary from 3.8 to 9.3 g. Meat Zero conversions are break-even values, not measured protein.
- **Scope.** Private-label evidence covers what is listed, not supplier terms, margins or listing fees (OQ-213 stays open). The cage-free figures are used as evidence about how pledges are delivered in Asia, not as a protein measure.
