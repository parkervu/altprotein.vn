# Fiscal and tariff rules that set ingredient and plant-milk economics

Line: W7-L2 Fiscal and tariff rules that set ingredient and plant-milk economics (wave 7). Source prefix: FTR. Date: 25 September 2026.

## Verdict

Chinese, ASEAN and Korean textured protein, soy isolate, pea protein and gluten enter Vietnam at 0% duty under ACFTA, ATIGA, AKFTA and RCEP in 2025 to 2027, so the most-favoured-nation (MFN) duties that Part VI cites (5%, 3%, 10%) apply only to non-FTA origins such as the United States, and a domestic maker has no tariff protection against China at all. From 1 January 2027 drinks above 5 g total sugar per 100 ml pay an 8% excise (10% from 2028), and since 1 January 2026 they have lost the 2% VAT cut; dairy is exempt. On a plain reading of the standard the law cites, sweetened cereal milks are inside the tax and soy and nut milks probably are, but nobody has ruled on it. Every core Fami soy milk we checked carries 6.5 to 9.3 g of sugar per 100 ml, yet even if taxed, mass soy milk stays well below dairy on price. Yield class: **added a constraint** (plant-milk design and pricing from 2027), with **corrections** to ch26.2, TPP-01, the T1 kill test and three appendices, and one open question closed (OQ-150); no Part VI conclusion is overturned.

## Headline findings

1. **Chinese protein ingredients enter duty-free.** HS 2106.10 (textured protein and concentrates), 3504 (soy isolate, pea protein), 1109 (wheat gluten) and 1208.10 (soy flour) are at 0% under ACFTA, AKFTA and RCEP-China in every year from 2024 to 2027, and 0% under ATIGA. MFN rates of 5%, 3%, 10% and 8% apply only to non-FTA origins. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-32; FTR-33, revealed]
2. **Part VI's price benchmark for T1 carries a duty that Chinese product does not pay.** At 2025 China unit values, Chinese textured protein lands at about USD 1.28 to 1.36 per kg (USD 1.96 to 2.10 per kg of protein) with 0% duty, against USD 1.34 to 1.43 if the 5% MFN rate applied. For gluten the gap is about USD 0.14 per kg (our calculation). [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-02; FTR-32; FTR-38, inferred]
3. **The sugary-drink excise turns on a product standard, not on the word "milk".** Law 66/2025/QH15 taxes "*nước giải khát* (soft drinks) under the national standard with sugar above 5 g per 100 ml". Decree 360/2025/ND-CP names that standard as TCVN 12828:2019 and defines sugar as the *đường tổng số* (total sugar) printed on the label. The only exclusions are in the standard's scope: milk and milk products, liquid foods for nutritional purposes, mineral and bottled water, juices and nectars, and cocoa products. [VN-direct, High, FTR-01; FTR-02; FTR-05; APR-22, revealed]
4. **Cereal milks are named inside the standard, and soy milk is not dairy.** TCVN 12828:2019 lists "*các loại đồ uống từ ngũ cốc*" (cereal drinks) in its scope. TCVN 11216:2015 defines milk as a mammary secretion, so soy and nut milks cannot use the dairy exclusion on its face. Soy milk has its own standard (TCVN 12443:2018). Vinasoy declares every Fami and Veyo product under QCVN 6-2:2010/BYT, the regulation for non-alcoholic beverages, and customs classes soy milk under heading 2202 (non-alcoholic beverages). Our reading: oat, rice and corn milks above 5 g are taxable; soy and nut milks are probably taxable, but this is unresolved. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-05; FTR-06; FTR-07; FTR-08; FTR-17; FTR-32, inferred]
5. **No one has asked for, or received, a ruling on plant milks.** We found no official letter on plant milks in the legal database. We found eight 2025 to 2026 tax and customs letters on sugary drinks: the four we read name no milk, soy, nut or cereal drink, and the titles of the other four do not either. The industry submissions by VBA and VCCI (March 2025) and the 2025 annual reports of QNS and Vinamilk do not mention the tax. This is a clean negative as of 25 September 2026. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-09 to FTR-15; AIB-01; AIB-05; AIB-06, revealed]
6. **Most mainstream sweetened plant milks are above the threshold.** Label total sugar: Fami Nguyên Chất 9.3 g, Fami Go 8.5 g, Fami brown-sugar 9.0 g (re-declared 30 June 2026), Veyo Smarty 7.3 g and Fami "less sugar" 6.5 g per 100 ml. Vinamilk's high-protein nut milk "less sugar", the TPP-05 reference product, lists 6.5% added sugar. Only Fami Green Soy (3.9 g), no-sugar variants, TH true NUT walnut and Oatly Barista (3.4 g) are below it. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-17 to FTR-30; FORM-01, revealed]
7. **If the tax applies and is passed on in full, mass soy milk stays below dairy.** Fami at VND 22,900 per litre would rise to about VND 24,700 in 2027 and VND 25,200 in 2028. That is still 24 to 44% below cow milk at VND 33,000 to 45,000 (2023 prices). For the soy-milk business, the upper bound of the excise is about VND 384 to 480 billion a year (USD 15 to 18 million), or 17 to 22% of QNS's 2025 pre-tax profit (our calculation). [VN-direct, Low, CON-26; RDS-036; CON-35; AIB-05, inferred]
8. **Plant milks gain no shelter from the 30% MFN tariff.** Soy milk drinks (2202.99.20) and other ready-to-drink plant drinks (2202.99.50) carry 30% MFN, but 0% under ACFTA, ATIGA, AKFTA and VKFTA, 0% under CPTPP and 3.7% under EVFTA in 2026. Customs classes soy milk as a beverage, not as dairy. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-32, revealed]
9. **VAT is neutral between processed plant foods and processed meat, and 3 to 5 points against both relative to fresh meat.** Tofu, chay foods, plant-based meat and processed meat pay 8% until 31 December 2026 and 10% after. Fresh meat pays 5% at the trading stage and nothing at the farm gate or at import. We found no VAT, excise or income-tax concession for plant-based or "healthy" foods. [VN-direct, High, COST-24; FTR-37, revealed]
10. **The dairy strategy excludes plant milks from school milk.** Decision 309/QD-TTg (2026) has no mention of plant, nut or soy milk or of sugar. It says that for *sữa học đường* (school milk), programmes use "only fresh milk". [VN-direct, High, FTR-31, revealed]
11. **Chinese single-cell protein also enters duty-free.** HS 2102 lines are at 0% under ACFTA, ATIGA and RCEP, so the "7% small edge for local SCP" in Appendices G and H holds only against non-FTA origins. This closes OQ-150. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-32, revealed]

## A. The excise and plant milks

### A.1 What the rules say

| Instrument | What it says on sugary drinks | Source |
|---|---|---|

| Decree 360/2025/ND-CP, Art 3(4) (31 Dec 2025) | The drink is "nước giải khát theo ... TCVN 12828:2019"; sugar is "đường tổng số ghi trên nhãn sản phẩm" under MOH labelling rules; importers of unlabelled goods self-assess. | FTR-02 |
| Circulars 158/2025/TT-BTC and 136/2026/TT-BTC | Nothing on drinks (dossiers, biofuel refunds, export documents). | FTR-03; FTR-04 |
| TCVN 12828:2019 Nước giải khát, scope and definition 3.1 | Covers flavoured drinks (energy, sports, electrolyte, other special drinks), coffee, tea, herbal and fruit-containing drinks and "các loại đồ uống từ ngũ cốc". Excludes "Sữa và sản phẩm từ sữa; Thực phẩm dạng lỏng dùng với mục đích dinh dưỡng; Nước khoáng thiên nhiên và nước uống đóng chai; Nước rau, quả và nectar rau, quả; Sản phẩm từ cacao". Definition: "Sản phẩm pha sẵn để uống với mục đích giải khát, được chế biến từ nước, có thể chứa đường, phụ gia thực phẩm, hương liệu, có thể bổ sung các thành phần nguyên liệu có nguồn gốc tự nhiên, vitamin và khoáng chất, có ga hoặc không có ga". | FTR-05; APR-22 |
| Tax office answer on the MOF Q&A portal (Khanh Hoa Tax Office 2, question 158420, 19 Dec 2025 question) | Quotes the scope and definition above. Drinks that a hotel mixes for guests are outside the tax if they are not ready-to-drink products under TCVN 12828:2019, or if they are at or below 5 g. | APR-22 (answer images read in this line) |
| MOF Letter 17380/BTC-CST (7 Nov 2025), Dong Nai 1751/DON-QLDN1, Can Tho 2846/CTH-QLDN1, Customs 46146/CHQ-NVTHQ | MOF 17380 and Dong Nai 1751: sugary drinks above 5 g are excise goods from 1 Jan 2026, so they lose the 2% VAT cut for the whole of 2026 (10%, not 8%). Two practical points follow. The test is the **total sugar on the label**, so intrinsic sugars count (oat hydrolysis, fruit) and sweeteners do not (our reading). And the answer on hotel drinks implies that a café mixing its own oat-milk coffee is outside the tax, but the packaged plant milk it buys is taxed at the factory if the product is in scope.

### A.2 Is *sữa đậu nành* (soy milk) dairy, a soft drink or neither?

| System | Treatment of soy milk | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Dairy vocabulary standard TCVN 11216:2015 | Milk is "obtained from ... the mammary glands of milking animals". Soy milk is not milk. | FTR-07 |
| Product standard | Soy milk has its own TCVN 12443:2018 *Sữa đậu nành*, drafted by the nutrition and dietetic foods committee (F6), with at least 2.0 g protein per 100 ml; a lower-protein "soybean drink" is also defined. It is neither in the dairy family (F12) nor in the soft-drink standard (F9). | FTR-06 |
| Food-safety declarations | Vinasoy declares every Fami SKU under QCVN 6-2:2010/BYT (non-alcoholic beverages) plus TCVN 12443:2018. It declares Veyo nut and oat milks under QCVN 6-2:2010/BYT, as *thực phẩm bổ sung* (supplemented food). | FTR-16 to FTR-29 |
| Customs nomenclature | 2202.99.20 "Đồ uống sữa đậu nành" (soya milk drinks), inside heading 2202, other non-alcoholic beverages. Dairy drinks are separate (2202.99.10, chapter 4). | FTR-32 |
| Excise law | Not named. | FTR-01; FTR-02 |

In standards, soy milk is neither dairy nor a soft drink; it is its own product. In food-safety and customs practice, it is treated as a non-alcoholic beverage. The tax asks only whether a product fits TCVN 12828:2019 and escapes its exclusions. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-05 to FTR-08; FTR-32, inferred]

### A.4 Has industry sought clarification?

We found no evidence that it has.
- The legal database's official letters on soy milk cover VAT (2012, 2017), customs classification of a production line (2016) and essential goods (2021), but nothing on the excise. We found eight 2025 to 2026 letters on sugary drinks; the four we read (FTR-09 to FTR-12) name no milk, soy, nut or cereal drink, and the other four titles (bird's nest drinks in Tay Ninh, VAT answers in Dong Nai and Can Tho, a Gia Lai policy notice) do not either. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-13; FTR-09 to FTR-12, revealed]
- VBA, VCCI and two other associations asked in March 2025 to delay the tax or phase it in by 5% steps. They did not raise plant milks in the pages we read. VBA and a US business council later asked about VAT on sugary drinks and got FTR-09. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-14; FTR-15; FTR-09, stated]
- QNS's 2025 annual report and 2026 AGM board report do not mention the excise. Their only legal-risk text is generic. Vinamilk's 2025 annual report notes rising demand for low- and no-sugar plant milks but not the tax. [VN-direct, High, AIB-06; AIB-05; AIB-01, stated]
- The revealed signal is mixed. Vinasoy re-declared Fami brown-sugar soy milk on 30 June 2026, six months before the tax starts, at 9.0 g of total sugar (9.3 g in 2025). It has also carried two Green Soy SKUs below the threshold since January 2025. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-21 to FTR-24, revealed]
- We could not search the MOF Q&A portal (captcha). The Vietnam Dairy Association site timed out. These are gaps.

Our calculation, assuming full proportional pass-through of the excise on the producer price. If distributors keep fixed margins in VND and the factory price is 70% of the pre-VAT shelf price (an assumption, not sourced), the rises are 5.6% and 7.0% instead. **The 2026 VAT test.** If sweetened plant milks count as *nước giải khát*, they should already have been invoiced at 10% VAT in 2026 while dairy paid 8%, about 1.9% on the shelf price (our calculation). Which rate Vinasoy and Vinamilk actually invoice is the cheapest revealed test of how the industry reads the law (open question 2).

**Exposure.** At about VND 4,800 billion of 2025 soy-milk revenue, an 8% to 10% excise on every litre would cost VND 384 to 480 billion a year (USD 14.8 to 18.5 million at 26,000 VND per USD). That is 17 to 22% of QNS's 2025 pre-tax profit of VND 2,212 billion (our calculation). It is an upper bound: exports are not taxed and below-threshold SKUs are excluded. [VN-direct, Low, CON-26; AIB-05, inferred]

### A.7 What this means for demand for new protein

- Plant milk remains the one modern plant category at scale, and mass soy milk stays cheaper than dairy even if taxed. The Part VI conclusion stands. [VN-direct, Medium, inferred]
- New plant-milk products, including the drinks a TPP-05 isolate would go into, should be designed at or below 5 g of total sugar per 100 ml. The reference product for T10 (Vinamilk's high-protein nut milk, less sugar) is above the line. Its no-sugar sister is below it. [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-01; FTR-02, inferred]
- Rice-based drinks, the natural host for a rice protein (T10), are cereal drinks and so the clearest taxable case. Enzymatic processes that create sugars count against the threshold. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-05, inferred]
- A likely incumbent response is reformulation below 5 g. Removing sugar removes solids and body, and protein, fibre (Vinamilk already uses inulin) and oil replace them. That could add a small pull for soluble plant protein. The evidence for this is inference only. [VN-direct, Low, FORM-01, inferred]

### B.3 What this means for T1 and T10

- **T1, domestic textured protein.** A domestic maker competes with Chinese product that pays no duty, so its tariff protection is zero, not 5%. Its own inputs from China also enter free, and full-fat soy flour from the United States pays 8% MFN. Against the United States, the largest supplier of 2106.10 by value, it keeps a 5% edge. The price to beat in 2025 is about USD 1.28 to 1.36 per kg delivered to a Vietnamese port. The 5% duty that ch26, app-ae and TPP-01 add is worth about USD 0.06 to 0.07 per kg. The direction of Part VI's conclusion is unchanged; the benchmark is stricter. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-32; BUY-02, inferred]
- **T10, non-soy isolate.** Chinese pea protein (0%, ACFTA and RCEP), French pea protein (0%, EVFTA) and Canadian pea protein (0%, CPTPP) all enter free. Only US pea pays 3%. The TPP-05 band (USD 2 to 8.5 per kg) is unprotected at both ends. Mung beans for a local isolate enter free from Myanmar, China and Australia. The 15% MFN applies only to non-FTA origins. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-32, inferred]
- **Feed (T2, T3).** Chinese single-cell protein pays 0%, not 7%, so local SCP has no tariff edge over the Chinese by-product protein named in brief-manufacturers. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-32, inferred]
- **Whether importers claim the preference** (a Form E certificate) is not known. If some do not, they pay MFN. Declaration-level customs data would show it (open question 5).

### B.4 Plant-milk imports

The 30% MFN on soy and other plant drinks protects local plant-milk makers only against the United States and other non-FTA origins. Drinks from Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore, China and Korea enter at 0%, drinks from Australia at 0% under CPTPP, and EU drinks at 3.7% in 2026. The excise, if it applies, falls equally on imports, so it does not favour local makers. [VN-direct, Medium, FTR-32, revealed]

- **Decision 309/QD-TTg (dairy strategy, 2026).** It is led by MOIT and has no mention of plant, nut or soy milk or of sugar. Its fiscal tools are corporate income tax, import and export duty policy, land fees and VAT, all for dairy. School milk is to use "only fresh milk". The consumption target is about 40 litres per person by 2030 and about 100 litres by 2045. Plant milk is outside the strategy and the school channel. [VN-direct, High, FTR-31; APR-11, revealed]
- **Nut inputs got cheaper.** Decree 73/2025 cut MFN duty on shelled almonds from 10% to 5% and on in-shell macadamia from 15% to 5% (31 March 2025). This fits the 40% rise in tree-nut imports in 2025. [VN-direct, Medium, REG2-21; AIB-40, revealed]

## Next-wave candidates

1. **A written ruling on plant milks.** Are soy milk (TCVN 12443:2018) and nut, oat and rice milks above 5 g total sugar "*nước giải khát*"? This is a *deeper* version of this line. Desk research cannot close it: no ruling exists yet, and the MOF Q&A search needs a captcha. A question to the MOF Tax Policy Department, or to QNS investor relations, could close it. If "yes", ch23 states the excise as fact and TPP-05 makes sub-5 g design a hard requirement. If "no", the constraint shrinks to cereal milks.
2. **The 2026 VAT invoice test.** Do Vinasoy and Vinamilk invoice sweetened plant milks at 8% or 10% VAT in 2026? This is a *different kind* of question: revealed industry behaviour, not law. One distributor invoice or a retailer buyer could close it, so it cannot be done from the desk. A 10% rate would show that industry already treats plant milks as sugary drinks.
3. **Form E use.** What share of 2106.10, 3504 and 1109 imports claims ACFTA or RCEP preference? This is a *deeper* question that rides on DMV-02 (declaration-level customs data), and the desk tools cannot reach it. Low utilisation would put part of the MFN duty back into the T1 benchmark.
4. **Reformulation.** Will incumbents move plant milks below 5 g before 2027, and do sub-5 g recipes use more protein and fibre? This is a *different kind* of question (buyer R&D). It needs interviews plus a comparison of 2027 self-declarations on the QNS site; the latter is desk-feasible from January 2027. A clear move to protein-fortified sub-5 g drinks would raise T10's pull. No move would leave it at 4.
5. **Export side (OQ-252).** What duties do China and Korea charge on Vietnamese 2106.10, 2106.90 and 2202.99? This is a *different kind* of question and partly desk-feasible through Chinese and Korean customs tariff sites. It matters only for the export routes in ch27.

Further desk work on Vietnam's import tariffs would be noise: every relevant line is at 0% from the main suppliers, and the remaining uncertainty is utilisation, not rates.

## Limits

- Tariff rates come from MOIT's VNTR database, not from the decree appendices. The ACFTA, ATIGA, AKFTA and RCEP decrees' schedules did not render. VNTR's ATIGA year data stop in 2022 (final rate 0%), and its MFN column is out of date for lines amended in 2025. We checked the MFN amendment chain by decree title and summary, except for Decree 21/2025, which we did not identify.
- The excise reading is ours. Vietnamese law, decree, circulars and eight official letters were read. The National Assembly's explanatory reports and the MOF Q&A archive (captcha) were not searched. No court or tax ruling on plant milks exists that we could find.
- TCVN texts were read through summaries and through the verbatim quotation in a tax office answer, not from the purchased standards.
- Label values come from QNS self-declaration files and are partly from low-resolution images checked against energy. Veyo oat and Veyo 5-nut total sugars are ambiguous between two readings. Vinamilk's pages were blocked (403), so Vinamilk values rest on the retail audit's ingredient lists. We have no sugar data for Nutifood's rice and corn milks.
- Freight and insurance of 3 to 10% is an assumption. The Vietnam-reported quantities in the mirror data are estimated, and the 2106.10 and 3504 mirror values disagree by 28 to 38%.
- Price effects assume full pass-through. Cow milk prices are from 2023, and plant-milk prices are from the September 2026 convenience-sample audit.
- The Vietnam Dairy Association site timed out; its position is unknown.
