# Funder units and funder fit: what Part VI's demand routes are worth to the people who would pay for them

Line: W7-L3 Funder units and funder fit (wave 7). Source prefix: FUF. Date: 25 September 2026.

## Verdict

Restated in the units funders allocate by, Part VI's benchmark path (D-BENCH) spares about 2.7 million animals a year by 2035, 94% of them chickens and ducks, and avoids about 0.14 Mt CO2e a year (0.08 to 0.25); import substitution, which is 60% of the protein delivered, spares no animals and avoids about no CO2e. The funder-fit audit shows that Part VI's advice to funders does not match who actually pays: only 4 of the 19 demand moves have an external funder whose stated scope fits and that accepts Vietnam; the chay-day and canteen-menu work that ch28.6 points funders at is excluded by the two funders with open or recent calls (Nova Vista, and ACE Movement Grants, whose September 2026 call drops diet change) and is out of geography for the four that fund it elsewhere; and Part VI's lead play, domestic textured protein (T1, TPP-01), has no clean fit with any welfare, climate or market-shaping funder. A cost per meal shifted for a Vietnamese canteen programme spans USD 0.006 to 1.7, too wide to rank Vietnam against ACE's benchmark; the robust result is structural: a Vietnamese canteen meal spares about 0.02 animals against 0.13 in ACE's Sinergia estimate unless chicken dishes are the ones replaced. Yield class: **changed a conclusion** (the funder recommendations in ch28.6, ch29.4 and brief-international) and **added a constraint**; Part VI's core demand conclusion (ingredients sold to incumbents) is unchanged.

## Headline findings

1. **Animals spared are almost all poultry.** On D-BENCH the four displacing routes (added chay days, hybrids, canteens and schools, household analogues) spare about 0.39 million animals a year in 2030, 2.7 million (2.4 to 3.0 million) in 2035 and 6.8 million in 2050; on D-STRETCH 3.4, 10.6 and 25.3 million. Chickens and ducks are 92 to 95% of the head count; pigs are about 142,000 in 2035 on D-BENCH (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, FUF-03; demand_outputs.csv; balance_outputs.csv, inferred]. For scale, poultry eaten in Vietnam rises from about 1.08 billion birds (2025) to 1.71 billion (2035) on the S-BASE path, so D-BENCH spares about 0.15% of the birds eaten in 2035 (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, FUF-03; balance_outputs.csv, inferred].

2. **CO2e avoided is small and half of it is beef.** Net of the replacing protein, D-BENCH avoids about 0.024 Mt CO2e a year in 2030, 0.14 Mt (0.08 to 0.25) in 2035 and 0.36 Mt (0.20 to 0.65) in 2050; D-STRETCH 0.19, 0.55 (0.33 to 0.99) and 1.35 Mt. Beef and buffalo are 8.5% of meat eaten in 2035 but about 47% of the gross CO2e displaced (our calculation) [VN-adjacent, Low, FUF-04; FUF-05; FUF-07; FUF-08, inferred]. For scale only (different boundary), Vietnamese agriculture emitted 104.5 MtCO2e in 2020 [VN-direct, High, MAC-32, revealed].

3. **Import substitution scores zero in funder units but has other value.** R1 displaces no meat, spares no animals and avoids about no CO2e (soy protein replaces soy protein). Its value is an import bill of about USD 5 to 7 million a year replaced in 2030, USD 16 to 23 million in 2035 and USD 62 to 89 million in 2050 on D-BENCH (gross, our calculation), about 0.2% of the USD 10 billion feed-ingredient import bill in 2035. The supply-security gain is partial because 98.4% of soybeans are imported [VN-direct, Low, BUY-02; MAC-04; MAC-09, inferred].

4. **Per tonne of protein, canteens spare the most animals and hybrids avoid the most CO2e.** A tonne of protein delivered through canteens at the 2035 national dish mix spares about 1,090 animals and avoids about 45 t CO2e; through hybrids about 540 animals and 57 t CO2e; replacing a chicken dish spares about 2,760 birds (19 t CO2e), a pork dish about 81 pigs (24 t CO2e). Household analogues give about 270 animals and 5 t CO2e; import substitution, upgrades on existing chay days and plant-milk protein give none (our calculation) [VN-adjacent, Low, FUF-03; FUF-04; FUF-05, inferred].

5. **D-BENCH's canteen assumption is far above what diet-change programmes deliver.** It implies 36.6 million canteen meal-equivalents a year in 2035 and 4.0 million school ones, rising to 98.4 and 16.0 million in 2050. That is about 37 times Sinergia Animal's 998,000 meals (three countries, 2023) and about 16 times the 2.3 million meals a year Humane World secured worldwide in 2025, so it can only come from caterers' own commercial use of plant or novel protein [general, Medium, AFN-26; AFN-23, revealed; VN-direct, Low, our calculation, inferred]. MOH school-meal guidance asks for animal protein at least 4 to 5 days a week, which caps animal-free school days at about one in five [VN-direct, High, APR-04, revealed].

6. **A Vietnamese canteen meal spares far fewer animals than ACE's benchmark.** At 20 g of protein per dish and 0.8 net displacement, a meal shifted spares about 0.020 animals at the national meat mix (0.0016 for a pork dish, 0.055 for a chicken dish), against about 0.134 in ACE's estimate for Sinergia's programme (134,000 animals for 998,000 meals) [VN-direct, Low, FUF-03; DMA-083; DMA-114, inferred; general, Medium, AFN-26, revealed].

7. **Cost per meal shifted is too uncertain to be useful.** Four labelled cases give USD 0.006 to 1.7 per meal, USD 0.3 to 85 per animal at the national mix (USD 0.1 to about 1,000 across dish types) and USD 6 to about 1,800 per t CO2e, against ACE's USD 0.29 per meal and USD 2 per animal for Sinergia. The unknown is uptake, not cost: a plant protein dish costs the caterer VND 740 to 2,140 against a protein-dish budget of VND 7,500 to 14,000, so a programme pays for persuasion and support, not subsidy [VN-direct, Low, demand_moves.csv; TPP-04; AFN-26, inferred].

8. **A new constraint: ACE Movement Grants now exclude diet change.** The 12th round (announced 24 September 2026, deadline 18 October 2026) prioritises Africa and Asia but will not fund "diet change and meat reduction programs ... in any region" or alternative-protein work except policy; grants are USD 100,000 or less, for organisations under three years old or with budgets under USD 500,000 [general, High, FUF-01; FUF-02, stated]. With ReRoot Asia excluding Vietnam and Lever, Sinergia and Good Food Fund working elsewhere, no funder we found both funds institutional menu change and covers Vietnam [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-26; AFN-27; AFN-29; AFN-39; FUF-01, stated].

9. **Only 4 of 19 demand moves have a fitting external funder that accepts Vietnam.** DMV-03 and DMV-04 fit the Bezos Centre at NUS (consumer-acceptance platform); DMV-15 and DMV-16 fit Nova Vista (fair naming, novel-food pathways). Humane World fits DMV-05 and DMV-10 as implementer, not funder. DMV-01, called "the single cheapest piece of missing evidence" (ch29.4), has no philanthropic payer; DMV-17 has seven partial fits and no full fit; DMV-06, DMV-09, DMV-11 and DMV-19 have none. Among the product profiles, TPP-01 has eight partial fits and no full fit; TPP-03, TPP-05 and TPP-06 fit R&D funders (two of three now closed) and TPP-03 fits ADB Ventures' climate screen [VN-direct, Medium, AFN-05; AFN-15; AFN-16; AIS-22; FUF-01; FUF-02, stated].

10. **The market-shaping route in Vietnam is procurement and rules, not chay.** Nova Vista funds "innovative proteins, by which we primarily mean plant-based meat, fermentation-derived protein, and cultivated meat", funds "globally on solutions to climate change", excludes whole-food, culture-change and individual diet-change work and shifts to smaller animals, and names public procurement and corporate protein-ratio commitments. Textured soy and blends are not named [general, High, AFN-05, stated]. None of Part VI's routes shifts consumption towards smaller animals; the poultry-led growth is in the S-BASE baseline (poultry per person up 52% from 2025 to 2035, pork 10%) [VN-direct, Medium, balance_outputs.csv; QNT-01, inferred].

11. **Clean negatives.** We found no Vietnamese cradle-to-carcass LCA for pork or poultry: the one Vietnamese pork LCA reports farm emissions per pig per month [VN-direct, Medium, FUF-09, revealed]. We found no cost per meal replaced for any Asian programme other than ACE's multi-country Sinergia figure; for Good Food Fund in China ACE reports people reached per USD and could not estimate animal impact [general, Medium, AFN-27, revealed].

## 1. Method

The demand model (`tools/demand_model.py`) reports protein delivered and meat protein displaced by route. We converted its D-BENCH and D-STRETCH outputs for 2030, 2035 and 2050 into funder units with `calc_funder_units.py` in this folder (standard library; reproduces all `data_` files).

- **Species split.** Displaced meat protein is split by route. Added chay days (R2), canteens and schools (R4) and household analogues (R5) take the national consumption mix of the balance model's S-BASE path: pork 57.4%, poultry 33.9%, ruminant 8.7% of carcass in 2030; 53.6%, 37.9%, 8.5% in 2035; 51.6%, 39.1%, 9.2% in 2050 [VN-direct, Medium, balance_outputs.csv; QNT-01, inferred]. Hybrids (R3) take our assumption of 75% pork, 15% poultry and 10% ruminant, informed by the Vissan label audit, where pork is the first-listed meat in 50 of 80 non-*chay* (non-vegetarian) products, beef in 18, chicken in 6 and fish in 6 (SKU counts, not volumes) [VN-direct, Low, BUY-05, inferred].
- **Animals.** Displaced protein / 0.15 kg protein per kg carcass (the package factor) = carcass displaced; carcass / FAOSTAT carcass weight per animal for Viet Nam, 2022 to 2024: pig 66 kg (65 to 67), chicken 2.1 kg (1.83 to 2.36), duck 1.2 kg (1.2 to 1.34), cattle 148 kg (140 to 148), buffalo 196 kg (171 to 196); poultry meat is 88% chicken and 12% duck, ruminant meat 80% cattle and 20% buffalo (FAOSTAT 2024 production) [VN-direct, Medium, FUF-03, revealed]. Counts are slaughter avoided per year, including animals raised abroad for imported meat; they are not welfare-weighted.
- **CO2e.** Gross = carcass displaced x GLEAM life-cycle intensity for East and Southeast Asia, humid zone (reference year 2005): pork 6.15 kg CO2e per kg carcass (5.37 to 7.94), broilers 5.18 (4.19 to 6.84), mixed beef 54.5 (40.1 to 81.0) [VN-adjacent, Medium, FUF-04; FUF-05, revealed]. Net = gross minus the replacing protein at 8.36 kg CO2e per kg protein (Poore and Nemecek's "other pulses" as a labelled proxy; range 1.31 for feed-grade soy concentrate to 19.75 for tofu) [general, Low, FUF-07; FUF-08, inferred]. The high bound uses Poore and Nemecek's per-protein means (pig 76, poultry 57, beef 499 kg CO2e per kg protein) where they exceed GLEAM's upper values [general, Medium, FUF-07, revealed]. For R2 the replacing protein is 12 g a day on each added chay day (DMA-026).
- **Meals.** A meal-equivalent is one protein dish of 20 g of protein supplied by plant or novel protein instead of meat (DMA-083); canteen and school counts come from DMA-079 to DMA-113.
- **Fish and eggs are not counted.** The model counts meat only; added chay days would also avoid fish and eggs, so R2's animal count is a floor.

Factors with sources: `data_conversion_factors.csv`.

**What this means for the routes.** Even the pork-heavy hybrid route spares about seven birds for every pig, because a bird carries about 2 kg of carcass and a pig about 66 kg. For a welfare funder counting head, the ranking is chay days, then canteens, then hybrids; per tonne of protein it is canteens, then hybrids (section 3). For a climate funder, all three are small and depend on how much beef they touch.

A plausibility check on D-BENCH itself: 36.6 million canteen meal-equivalents in 2035 is equivalent, under a one-plant-protein-day-in-six format, to about 732,000 meals a day switching, or about 24 caterers of 30,000 meals a day (our calculation from DMA-079 and CHN-41). Diet-change NGOs deliver one to two orders of magnitude less (finding 5).

## 4. Import substitution: what R1 achieves

- **In funder units: zero.** R1 replaces imported plant protein with domestic plant protein; no meat, no animals and about no CO2e (freight and process energy differ; sign not computed) [VN-direct, Low, demand_outputs.csv, inferred].
- **Import bill: modest.** USD 16 to 23 million a year of imports replaced in 2035 on D-BENCH and USD 35 to 51 million on D-STRETCH (gross; 17.9 and 38.9 kt of product at 65% protein x USD 0.9 to 1.3 per kg) (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, BUY-02, inferred]. That is about 0.2% of the USD 10 billion feed-ingredient import bill Part I cites. The net saving is lower because the feedstock would still be imported soybeans or meal.
- **Supply security: partial.** Vietnam imports 98.4% of its soybeans, mostly from Brazil and the United States [VN-direct, High, MAC-04; MAC-09, revealed]. A domestic textured-protein industry moves the dependency from Chinese processed protein to imported beans; it gains processing capacity and control of specification, not raw-material independence. Only a domestic legume or non-soy feedstock (TPP-05 rice and mung bean) changes that.
- **Other value.** Industrial capacity (about 3.5 lines of 1 t per hour by 2035 on D-BENCH, ch28.3), jobs and value added, and a platform that could later supply hybrids and canteens. These are the arguments for development finance and industrial policy, not for welfare or climate money.

## 5. What a Vietnamese canteen programme might cost per meal and per animal

**Assumptions, all labelled.** 300 meal days a year (DMA-079). 20 g of protein per dish, 0.8 net displacement (DMA-083, DMA-114). Programme cost: USD 45,000 for a pilot (midpoint of DMV-05's USD 30,000 to 60,000) or USD 100,000 a year for a programme (about Sinergia's 2023 spend per country: 998,000 meals / 3.4 meals per USD = about USD 293,500 across three countries, our calculation from AFN-26). Reach: caterers serve 30,000 to 120,000 meals a day (CHN-41). Share shifted: one day in six (a weekly plant-protein day), two days a month, or about one a month (the Korean school pattern, AFN-42). Other benchmarks publish reach, not cost: Humane World reports 2.3 million meals a year moved to plant-based in 2025 [general, Medium, AFN-23, stated] and Lever 29 million corporate meals shifted in Asia (cumulative, organisation claim) [VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-38, stated]; for Good Food Fund in China ACE reports 0.3 to 2.5 people reached per USD and no meals or animals [VN-adjacent, Medium, AFN-27, revealed]. None gives a cost per meal, so Sinergia is the only cost benchmark we could use.

**Honest reading.** The range is USD 0.006 to 1.7 per meal (about 300-fold), USD 0.3 to 85 per animal at the national mix, and USD 0.1 to about 1,000 per animal across dish types. That is too wide to say whether Vietnam beats or trails Sinergia's USD 0.29 per meal. Three things are robust:

1. **Uptake decides everything; cost barely matters.** A plant-protein dish costs the caterer far less than the meat dish it replaces (VND 740 to 2,140 against a VND 7,500 to 14,000 protein-dish budget, TPP-04), so the programme buys persuasion and support, not subsidy. If one large caterer adopts a weekly plant-protein day, the cost per meal is a few US cents; if only one factory kitchen adopts a monthly one, it is over a dollar. DMV-05 is the test that narrows this, and it is cheap.
2. **Per meal, Vietnam spares fewer animals.** About 0.02 animals per meal at the national mix against about 0.13 in ACE's Sinergia estimate. Only a programme that replaces chicken dishes (0.055) approaches it. A welfare funder should specify chicken-dish replacement; this pushes towards larger animals, not smaller, so it does not conflict with Nova Vista's exclusion.
3. **Per t CO2e it is not a climate buy** unless uptake is strong: USD 222 per t in the modest case and USD 1,849 in the weak case (our calculation).

Further desk work cannot narrow this range; only a Vietnamese trial with a named caterer can.

## Next-wave candidates

1. **Canteen dish species and protein per dish.** A *deeper* version of this line, and the one that matters most: animals per meal differ about 34-fold between pork and chicken dishes, and protein per dish (20 g) is unmeasured. Desk research can close part of it (caterers' published sample menus); the rest needs menus from five caterers or the DMV-05 log. Result that changes a conclusion: if canteen dishes are mostly chicken, Vietnamese canteens approach ACE's animals-per-meal benchmark and become a credible welfare case; if mostly pork, the welfare case for Vietnam rests on chay days alone.
2. **Who would pay for a Vietnamese canteen trial.** A *different kind* of question (funder calls, not desk). Three calls: Humane World Viet Nam, Coefficient Giving's emerging-economy movement building, the Navigation Fund. If one will fund with Humane World as implementer, DMV-05b becomes fundable; if none, ch28.6 should say the welfare route has no payer today.
3. **Does textured soy, a blend or a canteen ingredient count as "innovative protein", and would Vietnam be considered?** *Different kind* (one email to Nova Vista). Decides whether TPP-01, TPP-03 and TPP-04 procurement asks are fundable; it would move TPP-01 from partial to fits or to excluded.
4. **Fish and eggs on added chay days.** *Deeper*; not closable by desk (needs a recall item in DMV-01). Could multiply R2's animal count, since fish are far smaller than birds.
5. **School food standards window.** *Different kind* (calls to UNICEF Viet Nam and MOET); overlaps AFN-L01. Decides which version of DMV-17 to pursue and when.

Further desk work on emission factors, per-bird yields or cost per meal would be **noise**: better factors would move CO2e by perhaps 20% and would not change any ranking or recommendation, and the cost range cannot narrow without a Vietnamese trial.

## Limits

- Everything inherits the demand model's Low confidence: these are what-if conversions of scenario results, not forecasts.
- Species mixes are assumptions: the national mix for canteens, chay days and analogues, and our 75/15/10 mix for processed meat. The R3 CO2e figure roughly halves if processed meat contains no beef.
- GLEAM intensities refer to 2005; Vietnamese pork and poultry have since intensified. No Vietnamese cradle-to-carcass LCA was found (FUF-09 is farm-level). FAOSTAT's farm-gate intensities (FUF-10) exclude feed and are used only as a check.
- The package's 0.15 kg protein per kg carcass is above GLEAM's implied values; the package's 2025 poultry carcass production (1,821 kt) is about 28% above FAOSTAT's 2024 chicken and duck meat (1,424 kt). Both could shift head counts by 15 to 25%.
- Fish and eggs are not counted; head counts are not welfare-weighted (ACE uses suffering-adjusted days).
- No food-grade textured-soy LCA was found; the replacing-protein footprint uses a pulses proxy with a wide range. Poore and Nemecek values were read through Our World in Data, not the paywalled paper.
- The four cost cases are illustrative; no Vietnamese programme cost exists. ACE's Sinergia figures are ACE's estimates, and its animal count used an external expert whose method we did not read.
- Fit classes are our reading of public pages; funders decide case by case, and a partial can become a fit in conversation. Coefficient Giving's grants database could not be searched (AFN limits). ACE's application page was read through a fetch summary; the older guidelines URL returned HTTP 404.
- The FAOSTAT API returned an authorisation error; we used FAO's public bulk files.
