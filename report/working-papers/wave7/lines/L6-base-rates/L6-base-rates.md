# Base rates from firms, diet surveillance and trade seasons

Line: W7-L6 Base rates from firms, diet surveillance and trade seasons (wave 7). Source prefix: BRD. Date: 25 September 2026.

Tags: `[evidence label, confidence, source, demand evidence type]`. "Our calculation" marks our own arithmetic. USD unless stated; 26,000 VND per USD where we convert.

## Verdict

Part VI infers demand from surveys, shelves, annual trade and assumptions. This line tested three revealed base rates against it. Firm outcomes do not support the advice "win a first business customer before building a consumer brand" as a survival rule: firms that launched without a brand of their own, selling an ingredient, technology or OEM product, were operating in 2026 in 2 of 6 cases, firms that put their own brand on restaurant menus in 5 of 12, and firms that sold their own brand in shops or their own outlets in 4 of 5. In practice "business customer first" meant restaurant menus for a branded analogue or business-to-business sales of cultivated technology, and only one plant-protein firm (INNOHAS, an OEM) took the ingredient or private-label route at launch. What does hold is the direction of travel (every pivot we found moved towards private label, co-manufacturing or non-food ingredients) and a pattern Part VI already predicts: low-capital makers of familiar foods survived, while every Vietnamese meat-analogue startup we found went quiet. National diet surveillance (the Diet Quality Questionnaire, n = 1,007, November to December 2021) caps *chay* (lunar-day vegetarian eating) person-days at about 8% and so supports the central chay assumption but not the top of its range; it cannot test the processed-meat assumption, because its Vietnamese processed-meat item leaves out *giò* and *chả* (pork rolls and loaves). Monthly trade shows a Vietnam-specific rise in textured protein shipments before the Vu Lan month, and no chay signature at all in the 2024 jump in HS 3504, which is priced at less than half China's average for the code and is mostly not recorded as 3504 by Vietnamese customs. **Yield class: added a constraint** (three constraints and one reworded recommendation); the core conclusion and the benchmark path stand.

## Headline findings

1. **Starting with a business customer did not predict survival.** Of 24 alternative-protein startups and scale-ups that entered, tested or left Vietnam and Southeast Asia from 2019 to 2026, 7 are active, 4 pivoted, 2 exited and 11 are dormant (no evidence of activity found after their last dated item). In three mutually exclusive groups, firms operating in 2026 (active or pivoted) were: 2 of 6 that launched with no brand of their own (B2B ingredient, technology or OEM), 5 of 12 that launched their brand on restaurant or cafe menus, and 4 of 5 that launched their brand in shops, online or their own outlets (our calculation). On the overlapping definitions the brief asks for, 7 of 18 "business customer first" firms and 9 of 17 "started B2C" firms were operating. [VN-adjacent, Low, data_firm_base_rates.csv; AIS-28; AIS-30; AIS-34; AIS-39; BRD-08; BRD-09; BRD-10, revealed]
2. **"Foodservice first" mostly meant a branded analogue on restaurant menus, and it did not protect anyone.** Of 12 firms that launched through restaurants or cafes, 2 are active (Green Rebel, Oatside), 3 pivoted (TiNDLE to private label, GOOD Meat to a 3% cultivated retail blend, Vow to co-manufacturing) and 7 are dormant (VMEAT, Is It Meat?, OmniFoods, Next Meats, Karana, Float Foods, Phuture). [VN-adjacent, Low, data_firm_outcomes.csv; AIS-28; AIS-37; NOV-12; BRD-08; BRD-09; BRD-10; BRD-15, revealed]
3. **Every pivot moved away from the consumer brand.** Four firms moved to private label, co-manufacturing or non-food ingredients (TiNDLE, Vow, UMAMI Bioworks, Avant's parent), and GOOD Meat moved to a low-inclusion blend; none moved from supplying businesses to a consumer brand. TiNDLE's founders gave the reason: "no real underlying consumer demand to shift away from meat" and a category that "has become increasingly price-driven". [general, Medium, AIS-28; AIS-29; AIS-34; AIS-35; AIS-36; AIS-37, revealed]
4. **In Vietnam, format predicted survival better than channel.** All three Vietnamese makers of traditional or whole-food formats are active in 2025 to 2026 (Emmay's mushroom floss under the Nam Tuoi Cuoi brand, Kashew's fermented cashew cheese, Ong Tempeh's tempeh); none of the four Vietnamese meat or seafood analogue startups shows activity after 2022 (VMEAT, Is It Meat?, Yukino Foods, Bien Phuong). Emmay, a survivor, started as a consumer brand in supermarkets (80% of 2022 revenue). [VN-direct, Low, AIS-39; AIS-40; AIS-42; AIS-43; ECO-15; ECO-17; ECO-19; BRD-18, revealed]
5. **Clean negative: no startup in the register has tried Part VI's recommended route.** We found no startup whose first revenue came from selling a protein ingredient to chay makers, meat processors or caterers. The firms on that route are incumbents or large suppliers (Growthwell, a vegetarian maker since 1989; CP Meat Zero sold as chay on a HoReCa platform; Angel Yeast through a Vietnamese distributor). The advice is untested by any founder, not disproved. [VN-adjacent, Low, AIS-25; AIS-33; AIS-38; data_firm_outcomes.csv, revealed]
6. **On a normal day in late 2021, 7.9% of Vietnamese adults ate no meat, poultry or fish** (95% CI 5.3 to 11.0%), from a national Gallup telephone sample of 1,007 adults (13 November to 12 December 2021, no festival month). This caps chay-day person-days. Part VI's central chay assumption (30% keeping 2.5 days a month, 2.5% of person-days) uses about a third of that ceiling; its high case (6.7%) would need almost every flesh-free day to be a chay day (our calculation). [VN-direct, Medium, APR-36; BRD-01; BRD-03, revealed]
7. **The incumbent reaches 40% of adults a day.** 40.0% (35.2 to 44.9%) ate dried beans, soybeans, tofu, soy milk or bean flour on the previous day, with no urban and rural gap (40.5% and 39.2%) and more women than men (43.7% and 36.3%). [VN-direct, Medium, APR-36; BRD-01, revealed]
8. **Diet surveillance cannot test the processed-meat assumption.** 18.1% of adults ate processed meat on the previous day, against 76.1% for unprocessed red meat, but the Vietnamese item names *lạp xưởng*, sausage, canned meat, pate and bologna and leaves out *giò*, *chả* and *nem chua*, the core of Part VI's R3 category; it also counts days, not grams. The public files give sex and urban or rural only, not age or income. [VN-direct, Medium, APR-36; BRD-01; BRD-04, revealed]
9. **Textured protein imports carry a Vu Lan signature; wheat gluten and HS 3504 do not.** China's monthly shipments of HS 2106.10 to Vietnam run 33% above the monthly average in the two months before the Vu Lan month (5 of 6 years, 2019 to 2024), against 4% for the same code to the world, 2% to Thailand and minus 3% for feed preparations to Vietnam. Gluten rises mid-year to every destination, so it cannot be attributed. HS 3504 to Vietnam runs 21% below average before Vu Lan. [VN-direct, Medium, BRD-05; BRD-07, revealed]
10. **The 2024 jump in HS 3504 does not behave like a chay food ingredient.** China shipped 25,267 t to Vietnam in 2024 at USD 1.03 per kg, against USD 2.44 for all China's 3504 exports and USD 2.38 to Thailand; the step began in October 2023. Before then, Vietnam's recorded 3504 imports from China matched China's records (ratio 0.84 to 1.01, 2019 to 2022); in October to December 2023 Vietnam recorded as 3504 only 38% of the value China reported. The flow is lumpier than feed preparations (monthly coefficient of variation 0.44 against 0.19) and has no lunar timing (our calculation). [VN-direct, Medium, BRD-05; BRD-06; BUY-02, revealed]
11. **The seasonal swing for an ingredient supplier is modest.** Upstream, textured protein imports rise about a third before Vu Lan and fall to 38% of an average month in the *Tết* (lunar new year) month, when all Vietnam-bound flows, feed included, dip; the retail swing of two to three times in ch22.2 does not reach the ingredient order book at that scale (our calculation). [VN-direct, Medium, BRD-05; BRD-06, revealed]

## A. Firm outcome register

### What we recorded

The register (`data_firm_outcomes.csv`, 31 rows) covers every Vietnamese and regional firm named in the brief, in `launches_exits.csv` and in the actor files for which we could find a first channel and a dated status. We reused the investor and startup sources (AIS) and the package sources, and added trade-press archives and company pages (BRD-08 to BRD-19). Status in 2026 uses four classes: **active** (trading in 2025 or 2026 in its launch model), **pivoted** (trading, with a changed revenue model), **exited** (wound up, absorbed or left the market) and **dormant** (no evidence of activity after the date shown; not proof of closure). Corporate brands entering Vietnam (Beyond Meat, Heura, Verdino, Unconventional, CP Meat Zero) are kept for the Vietnam lens only. Insect firms (Entobel, Cricket One) are listed as benchmarks and never counted. We left out incumbents' plant milks and chay lines (Vinasoy, Vinamilk, TH, Nutifood, CJ bibigo), plant milks of established foreign firms, a canned mock-meat import whose owner is unverified (LE-11), a salad chain (SaladStop!), Deep Blue Island (owner not found) and brands never seen in Vietnam (Impossible, Meatless Farm, Zhenmeat, Starfield). ### Testing "win a business customer before building a consumer brand"

The advice appears in the executive summary, ch29.6 and ch25.7 ("a signed channel partner ... is worth more than a brand plan"). Against firm outcomes:

- **Not supported as a survival rule.** Firms that launched with no brand of their own were operating in 2 of 6 cases, restaurant-first brands in 5 of 12 and shop-first brands in 4 of 5. Without the cultivated firms, the no-brand group shrinks to one plant-protein firm (INNOHAS, active in Korea but with no Vietnam launch), so the route Part VI recommends has almost no observations. With 24 firms, selection towards firms that made the trade press, and "dormant" meaning only "no news found", the honest reading is "no measurable advantage for business-customer-first", not "a disadvantage". [VN-adjacent, Low, data_firm_base_rates.csv, revealed]
- **The category was wrong, not the channel.** Restaurant menu placements of a branded analogue (TiNDLE, Karana, Phuture, Float, OmniFoods, GOOD Meat, Vow) are a consumer-brand strategy sold through a business; 3 of the 10 plant-based ones are operating. Part VI's argument is different: sell an ingredient that incumbents put inside familiar foods. No startup in the register did that, so it is untested (finding 5).
- **The direction is supported.** Every pivot moved towards private label, co-manufacturing, B2B or non-food, under financial pressure; TiNDLE's reason (price and absent consumer demand) is Part VI's reason. [general, Medium, AIS-28; AIS-29, revealed]
- **Format and capital are the better predictors in this sample.** Survivors are low-capital makers of foods people already know (floss, cheese, tempeh, oat milk, regional dishes) or existing vegetarian manufacturers (Growthwell) and OEM suppliers (INNOHAS). This fits Part VI's "familiar foods" thesis (ch23.9, ch22.7) better than its "business customer" advice. [VN-adjacent, Low, data_firm_outcomes.csv, revealed]

- **ch29.6, executive summary and brief-startups:** reword the advice (see the change table). Keep the direction; drop the implication that a business customer is a proven survival route; distinguish ingredient or private-label supply from menu placements of a branded analogue.
- **ch25.7:** qualify "a signed channel partner ... is worth more than a brand plan" in the same way.

## B. Diet surveillance: the Diet Quality Questionnaire

### The source

The Global Diet Quality Project (GAIN, Harvard and Gallup) asked a nationally representative sample of 1,007 Vietnamese adults aged 15 and over, by mobile telephone in Vietnamese, whether they ate each of 29 food groups on the previous day. Fieldwork ran from 13 November to 12 December 2021; the design effect is 2.56 and the maximum margin of error 4.9 points [VN-direct, High, BRD-03]. Vietnam has one round only in the 2021 to 2024 dataset. The public results (Harvard Dataverse, and the country CSV) give weighted estimates for all adults, women, men, urban and rural; there is no age, income or education split, and we found no downloadable microdata [VN-direct, High, APR-36; BRD-04]. The country web page shows the fieldwork a month later (13 December 2021 to 12 January 2022), which is also how APR-36 records it; the data file and Gallup's methods table agree on November to December, and we use those (logged in `disagreements.csv`). The window holds the 15th of the 10th lunar month and the 1st of the 11th, so it is an ordinary month with two principal chay days (our calculation, BRD-07).

The Vietnamese questionnaire matters for reading the numbers [VN-direct, High, BRD-01; BRD-02]:

### FAO food balance sheet cross-check

FAOSTAT (the same file as DIE-07) gives Vietnam's food supply of soybeans (including tofu and soy milk in bean equivalent) as 5.03 kg per person in 2021 and 5.31 kg in 2023, and of other pulses 3.61 and 4.19 kg; together they supply 6.94 g of protein per person a day in 2021 and 7.57 g in 2023, against 21.80 and 25.09 g from meat [VN-direct, High, DIE-07, revealed]. Spread over the 40% of adults who eat legumes on a given day, supply is about 17 g of legume protein per eating day (6.94 / 0.40; our calculation, supply not intake). That is the same order as the 12 g of protein Part VI assumes on a chay day (DMA-026), so the diet data and the model are consistent, though neither measures a chay day. Full series in `data_diet_fbs_legumes.csv`.

## C. Trade seasonality

### Data and method

We downloaded monthly UN Comtrade data through the public API: China-reported exports (FOB) to Vietnam, to the world and to Thailand for HS 2106.10, 3504, 1109 and a feed control, HS 2309.90 (animal feed preparations), January 2019 to December 2024 (BRD-05); and Vietnam-reported imports (CIF) from China and the world for the same codes, 2019 to 2023 (BRD-06). China's monthly data for 2025 and Vietnam's for 2024 and 2025 are not in Comtrade. The China-reported monthly tonnages sum exactly to the package's annual totals (18,375 t in 2019; 28,324 t in 2023, against 28,323 t in BUY-02; 51,130 t in 2024), a useful check. Vietnam's quantities are flagged as estimated, so we use its values.

For each year we divided each month by that year's monthly mean, so trend drops out, and averaged across years (index 1.00 = an average month). Because the lunar calendar moves, we also aligned months to two events: the Gregorian month holding lunar 1/1 (*Tết*, lunar new year) and the month holding lunar 7/15 (Vu Lan, the chay peak of the 7th lunar month) (BRD-07):

| 2019 | 5 Feb | 1 Aug | 15 Aug |
| 2020 | 25 Jan | 19 Aug | 2 Sep |
| 2021 | 12 Feb | 8 Aug | 22 Aug |
| 2022 | 1 Feb | 29 Jul | 12 Aug |
| 2023 | 22 Jan | 16 Aug | 30 Aug |
| 2024 | 10 Feb | 4 Aug | 18 Aug |

The test for chay use is a rise in the two months before each event that is **specific to Vietnam**: larger than in China's shipments of the same code to the world and to Thailand, and absent from the feed control. China's own new-year shutdown falls on the same dates as Tết, so the Tết test is weak by design; Vu Lan has no Chinese counterpart of that size, so it is the cleaner test.

- **Textured protein and concentrates (2106.10) carry a Vu Lan signature.** Shipments to Vietnam rise a third above average in the two months before the Vu Lan month in five of six years, while the same code to the world and to Thailand is flat, and Vietnam's share of China's exports rises by a quarter. This is revealed evidence that part of 2106.10 is bought for chay production on the lunar calendar. The excess is about 0.66 of an average month, roughly 5.5% of annual tonnage, or about 550 t in 2024 (our calculation): it shows chay use exists, not how large the food share is.
- **Wheat gluten (1109) rises before Vu Lan too, but everywhere.** China ships more gluten to all destinations in June and July, and Vietnam's share of that trade shows no Vu Lan rise. Vietnamese clearances do peak in the Vu Lan month (1.35 from China and from all origins), which is consistent with chay use but cannot be separated from China's own cycle.

On the Vietnamese side, 2106.10 clearances from China run 25% above average in the two months before Tết (3 of 4 years) against 11% for feed: a weak hint, not a finding.
- **HS 3504 has no chay timing.** It runs below average before Vu Lan in five of six years and below average in the Vu Lan month on the Vietnamese side (0.93).

The answer to the brief's question is **neither steady like feed nor seasonal like chay food**. The new 3504 flow is cheap, lumpy, has no lunar timing and is declared under another heading on arrival. That fits a feed, pet-food or technical protein bought by a few industrial importers better than a food isolate for chay makers, which strengthens ch26.1's "fits feed-grade proteins better than food isolates (not verified)". We checked seven other headings under which Vietnam might record it (2309.90, 2309.10, 2106.90, 2303.10, 2301.20, 2304 and 1208.10; imports from China, August to December 2023 against November and December 2022): none rises consistently by the USD 1 to 2.5 million a month needed to absorb the gap in October and November 2023, and soybean oilcake jumps in December 2023 only. The receiving heading and the importers remain unidentified and need declaration-level data (DMV-02).

### What Part C changes

- **ch26.1:** add the monthly evidence: 2106.10 shows chay timing; the 2024 jump in 3504 does not, is priced at about USD 1 per kg against China's USD 2.44 average, and was mostly not recorded as 3504 by Vietnamese customs. Upgrade the feed-grade reading from "not verified" to "supported by price, timing and mirror records; importer not identified".
- **DMA-004 (R1 ingredient pool):** the 40% food share for 3504 applied to the 2025 partner-reported 27.0 kt probably overstates food use. If the post-2023 Chinese increment is not food, food-grade 3504 is about the non-China supply (2,590 t in 2025) plus China's pre-jump level (2,716 t a year, 2019 to 2022), about 5,300 t or 20% of 27.0 kt (our calculation, from IMP-161 to IMP-170). This is weak evidence; we suggest a note and a lower bound, not a new central value, until declaration data are read.
- **ch22.7 and brief-startups ("build for the calendar"):** add the upstream swing: an ingredient maker should plan for about a third more demand in the two months before the Vu Lan month and a deep trough in the Tết month, not the two to three times swing seen in retail chay sales.
- **ch28:** the three central assumptions it names as most influential are unchanged by this part.

## Next-wave candidates

1. **Lunar-date tabulation of the existing DQQ interviews.** *Deeper* version of Part B. Gallup holds interview dates; comparing the flesh-free share when the recall day was lunar 1st or 15th with other days gives the first revealed estimate of chay-day keeping. About 1,007 interviews over 30 days give roughly 65 recall days on the two principal chay days, enough to detect a doubling, not a small effect. Desk research cannot close it; it needs a request to the Global Diet Quality Project or Gallup. If the flesh-free share on chay days is about 25% or more against about 6% on other days, R2's 30% keeper share is supported; if there is no difference, R2 and ch22's 2.5% avoided meat protein should be cut sharply. High value, low cost.
2. **Add *giò*, *chả* and a chay item to the next Vietnam DQQ round or the next NIN nutrition survey.** *Different kind* (instrument design). Not desk-closable. It is the cheapest way to get national processed-meat and chay-day frequencies; it would test R3's order of magnitude and DMV-09.
3. **Who imports the new 3504 flow, and under which Vietnamese heading?** *Deeper* version of Part C, and the same as DMV-02. Declaration-level data are not reachable with our tools; Comtrade could show which Vietnamese headings rose in late 2023 once its server is stable (a few calls). If the flow is a feed or pet-food protein, DMA-004's 3504 food share falls to about 20% and the R1 pool to about 35 kt; if it is a food isolate, ch26.1's feed reading is wrong and the pool stands.
4. **Has any founder sold a protein ingredient to chay makers, processors or caterers?** *Different kind* (calls). Five calls to chay makers (for example An Nhien, Au Lac, Thoai An) and the ingredient distributor ACC would say whether the recommended route has been tried and what blocked it. A report of trials that failed on price would move the advice from "untested" to "tested and hard"; interest at Chinese parity would support it.
5. **Where to stop.** More desk case-finding of regional startups would add names but not change the base rates: selection towards firms covered by trade press, and "dormant" meaning "no news", limit what a larger desk sample can show. Business-registry checks of the dormant firms (Vietnam's national business registration portal; Singapore's company registry) would firm up 11 statuses but, whichever way they fall, would not change the conclusion that channel did not predict survival. We regard further desk work on Part A as noise.

## Limits

- **Part A:** 24 firms is a small, press-visible sample. "Dormant" means no evidence found in the sources we could read, not closure; several statuses rest on one trade-press archive's search listing (Low) or on company sites that did not respond, which the proxy cannot distinguish from blocks. First channels for OmniFoods, Kashew and Growthwell are mixed and our classification is a judgement. Funding figures are headline amounts. We did not contact founders.
- **Part B:** one DQQ round (late 2021, during the COVID-19 recovery), telephone mode with an urban-heavy raw sample (weighted), n = 1,007 and a design effect of 2.56; the rural subgroup (238) is thin. The flesh-free share bounds chay days but cannot identify them. No age or income breakdown and no microdata are public. FAO balance sheets measure supply, not intake.
- **Part C:** Comtrade has no 2025 monthly data for China and no 2024 or 2025 monthly data for Vietnam, so the 2025 Vu Lan season is untested. China records the month of export and Vietnam the month of clearance. Six years (China) and five years (Vietnam) are short series for seasonal inference; indices are simple means with no significance tests. The public API returned many server errors and throttling responses; all 72 China months and 60 Vietnam months were eventually retrieved, but the heading check lacks October 2022. Vietnamese quantities are estimates, so the Vietnam side uses values.
- The lunar calendar comes from a software table (BRD-07) whose dates we checked against the Tết dates used in Vietnam and the dates of the package's 2024 Vu Lan press reports.
